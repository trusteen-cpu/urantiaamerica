In-Depth Summary: Evil as the Absence of Light and the Active Practice of Good

Preface: The Cosmic Dialectic of Light and Shadow

In the intellectual history of humankind, the inquiry into the origin and nature of evil and its solution has continued unceasingly under the name of theodicy. The question "Why does evil exist in a universe where an almighty and good God exists?" has been a central problem of the philosophy of religion from ancient times to the present. This essay examines the thesis stated above, namely the insight that "God did not create evil; evil is not a reality but a state like darkness in which light (truth, beauty, and goodness) is lacking; and the way to overcome it lies not in struggle but in receiving the light and practicing good," through the cosmology and theological system of The Urantia Book.

It surveys the classification of evil presented in The Urantia Book (error, evil, sin, and iniquity), sheds light on the conflict between false liberty and true liberty through the Lucifer rebellion, and sets forth the way of "⟦Q 194:3.12⟧overcoming evil with good⟦/Q⟧" shown in the life and teachings of Jesus.

1. Divine Perfection and the Structure of a Finite Universe

1.1 The Nature of God, the First Source and Center, and the Absence of Evil

The great premise of The Urantia Book is the absolute goodness and perfection of God. God is the personal source of truth, beauty, and goodness, and the book says that "⟦Q 130:1.5⟧there is absolutely no place in him for negative evil⟦/Q⟧." This strongly supports the first thesis, that "God did not create evil."

If God is absolute light, evil cannot come from him. The central universe of Havona, created by God, is perfect and eternal, and evil is contained neither in the divine nature nor in the original intention of creation. Evil is a by-product arising not from the Creator but in the experiential evolutionary process of imperfect creatures. God did not create evil; he created the opportunity for beings with free will to progress by their own efforts, and in this process the possibility of potential evil, imperfection, was opened up.

1.2 The Inevitability of Potential Evil and the Relativity of Time and Space

The Urantia Book strictly distinguishes between potential evil and actual evil. Potential evil is inherent in the imperfection that inevitably arises when the infinite and eternal is expressed in limited form in time and space. When the infinite God created a finite universe and placed within it beings of finite intelligence, that very finiteness already harbored imperfection, and this becomes the soil of potential evil.

From this point of view, evil is not an independent reality but a shadow arising from the relativity of reality. The very fact that the part stands before the whole constitutes the relativity of reality, and this creates the need for intellectual choice. God desired not mechanical robots but personal beings who could choose and love of their own accord. For freedom of choice to exist, the possibility of choosing the divine will (good) must logically be accompanied by the possibility of choosing what is not the divine will (the potential of evil). Therefore the possibility of evil is the price of the freedom given to free-willed creatures and the necessary background of cosmic growth.

1.3 Darkness as the Absence of Light: The Unreality of Evil

The insight that "evil is not a reality but a state like darkness in which there is no light" agrees with the ontology of The Urantia Book. In Paper 130, Jesus explains evil as immature choosing and an unthinking misstep, and emphasizes that God is so positively good that there is no room for negative evil.

Darkness is not an antiparticle of light particles. Physically speaking, darkness is simply the absence of photons. In the same way, in the cosmology of The Urantia Book, reality consists of truth (the intellectual dimension), beauty (the physical and artistic dimension), and goodness (the moral and spiritual dimension), and these three values are attributes of God. Therefore to move away from God (the light) or to reject him is to move away from reality and to enter the realm of unreality, that is, of shadow and darkness.

Evil originates in the misuse, distortion, and perversion of the finite. Evil is not a self-existent force but a derivative phenomenon that arises when finite beings obscure or distort the divine light. The clearer the divine reality, the more distinctly the act of rejecting it shows up as darkness, but that shadow can never overcome the light, and when the light shines it vanishes at once.

2. The Four Stages of Negativity: Error, Evil, Sin, and Iniquity

The Urantia Book does not speak of evil in a lump but distinguishes four stages, error, evil, sin, and iniquity, according to the intention and spiritual maturity of the creature. This classification is a decisive basis for upholding the thesis that "God did not create evil" and for clarifying human responsibility.

2.1 Error: The Lack of Intellectual Insight

Error, the most basic stage, arises not from deliberate rejection of truth but from a lack of intellectual keenness or from misunderstanding and distorting reality. Evolving human beings by nature start from ignorance and advance toward wisdom. The errors of judgment and immature understanding in this process are not objects of moral condemnation but part of the cosmic learning process.

Nature: intellectual immaturity
State: placed in the conflict between truth and untruth
Responsibility: no moral guilt, but correction is needed

2.2 Evil: The Lack of Wisdom and Disharmony

Evil is the partial realization of, or maladjustment to, universe realities and signifies a lack of wisdom. Imperfections in the natural order, misuse of plans, and maladjustment of techniques belong here.

Evil is inherent in the natural order of this world. This does not mean that God made evil; it means that, because evolution in time and space is itself a process of advancing from imperfection to perfection, its intermediate stages inevitably include discord. A lion hunting its prey may be a natural disharmony, but it is not a moral sin. Likewise, when a person causes others pain through unintended immaturity, this is classified as evil, but it does not thereby mean spiritual disconnection (sin).

Nature: cosmic maladjustment, lack of wisdom
State: a by-product of incomplete evolution
Significance: a background for growth and something to be overcome

2.3 Sin: The Deliberate Rejection of Light

From here on, "the state in which there is no light" combines with free will to bring about fatal spiritual consequences. Sin is conscious and deliberate transgression of divine law. If evil is a lack of wisdom, sin is spiritual poverty.

The deliberate choosing of evil constitutes sin. Sin is the attitude of knowingly choosing darkness and rejecting spiritual progress while knowing what is right and wrong. God allowed the possibility of evil for the sake of free will, but sin arises wholly when the creature resolves to shut out the light in his own mind. Sin is the product of a knowing mind dominated by an unsubmissive will.

2.4 Iniquity: The Disintegration of Personality and Cosmic Insanity

Iniquity, the most serious stage, is the continued pursuit of sin and error and the loss of personality control. This is not a mere mistake but open and persistent rebellion against cosmic reality.

A being sunk in iniquity places himself above the laws of the universe and finally severs every connection with reality (God). The Urantia Book describes this as a state close to cosmic insanity. It is the stage at which one goes beyond rejecting the light to believe that darkness is reality and to become one with the darkness. When this stage is reached, that personality loses its reality and ends in nonexistence (see Section 6).

Comparison of the Four Stages

Error: distortion of reality, misunderstanding rather than rejection of truth / cause: lack of intellectual keenness / intellectual immaturity
Evil: partial realization of, or maladjustment to, universe reality / cause: lack of wisdom / cosmic disharmony
Sin: deliberate transgression of divine law / cause: spiritual poverty, disobedience / intentional rebellion
Iniquity: continued pursuit of sin, persistent rejection of reality / cause: loss of personality control / cosmic insanity, self-destruction

3. Liberty and License: The Origin of Evil Seen in the Lucifer Rebellion

The most powerful example demonstrating historically the thesis that "God did not create evil" is the Lucifer rebellion. This event shows how a being created perfect produced evil by choosing darkness of his own accord.

3.1 The Fall of Lucifer: The Arrogance of Self-Assertion

Lucifer was originally a primary Lanonandek Son outstanding in wisdom, brilliance, and efficiency, and the Sovereign of the system of Satania. He was created perfect, yet, as the record says, "⟦Q 53:1.1⟧You were perfect in all your ways from the day you were created till unrighteousness was found in you⟦/Q⟧," he walked the path of downfall by his own free will.

The central cause of the rebellion was not outside temptation or any defect but self-assertion and the sophistry of false personal liberty that began within Lucifer himself. He refused loyalty to the Universal Father and the Creator Son and began to place the self at the center of the universe. This is the prototype of the act of turning one's back on the light and producing darkness.

3.2 False Liberty (License) and True Liberty

Lucifer justified the rebellion by his so-called declaration of liberty. He asserted that the Universal Father was a myth who did not exist and agitated that every intelligent being should have the liberty of self-determination. The Urantia Book, however, defines this as license.

True liberty: associated with genuine self-respect and the fruit of self-control. It recognizes cosmic relationships and is realized through love and service.
False liberty (license): the counterpart of self-admiration and the mask of unbridled self-assertion. It infringes on the rights of others, ignores cosmic obligations, and ends in abject bondage.

Lucifer misused the free will that God had given him and produced disharmony and rebellion in the name of liberty. Evil is not a creation of God but a distorted state produced by creatures through the misuse of their own liberty.

3.3 God's Response: Permission Rather Than Immediate Suppression

When Lucifer rebelled, the universe authorities did not immediately suppress him by force. Instead of destroying evil by force, Michael chose a wise delay so that the rebellion would reveal its own contradictions. This accords with the thesis that "fighting evil is not the solution." By letting evil reveal its own falsity and destructiveness, God enabled every being in the universe to distinguish good from evil and to choose the light for himself.

4. The Phenomenology of Light: Truth, Beauty, and Goodness and the Unreality of Evil

4.1 Truth, Beauty, and Goodness

The Urantia Book explains cosmic reality in terms of the three dimensions of truth, beauty, and goodness.

Truth: the recognition of God on the intellectual level
Beauty: harmony on the physical and artistic level
Goodness: divine personality on the moral and spiritual level

Truth, beauty, and goodness come from God, who is the personality of love. Therefore to live in these three values is to live in reality, in the light. Evil, on the other hand, is the state in which these three values are lacking. Evil is merely the distortion of truth (error), the destruction of beauty (ugliness), and the rejection of goodness (sin); it is not a reality in its own right.

4.2 The Analogy of the Shadow: Fear without Substance

As in the analogy that "⟦Q 195:5.12⟧the black patches of evil which you see are shown against a white background of ultimate good⟦/Q⟧," a shadow is merely a phenomenon that arises when light is blocked by an object; the shadow itself has no power to extinguish or attack the light.

Many ancient religions and dualistic philosophies have raised evil to a power equal to good, but The Urantia Book firmly rejects this. What we feel as evil is a relative deficiency that arises when the infinite light of God does not fully shine into the lives of finite mortals. Therefore the right attitude toward evil is not to fence with the shadow but to shine light into that place.

5. The Way of Overcoming Evil with Good

The central claim that "the way to overcome evil is not to fight evil but to receive the light of God and do good" is gathered up in the teachings of Jesus. This is not passive avoidance but the most powerful and active spiritual attitude.

5.1 Jesus' Method: A New Love Drives Out the Old

Jesus taught not to yield passively to evil but to respond swiftly and actively with good in order to overcome evil with good. This resembles what Thomas Chalmers called "the expulsive power of a new affection." If one tries forcibly to drive out the darkness within the mind, the mind becomes empty, and evil easily takes up residence again in an empty mind. If instead one fills the mind with the love of God and good deeds, evil naturally loses its place.

What is truly good is always stronger than the most malignant evil. Jesus taught, "Resist not evil," warning against becoming tainted unawares with the ways of evil (hatred, anger, violence) in the course of fighting it. Instead he emphasized that the way of the citizens of the heavenly kingdom is to render evil powerless by the overwhelming positiveness of practicing good.

5.2 The Sin of Omission and Spiritual Stagnation

The Urantia Book emphasizes that it is not enough merely to refrain from doing wrong. This is a warning against failing to do good when one could.

Primitive religion stopped at the negative goodness of keeping taboos, but the religion of Jesus is founded on the positive command "Go and do good." Spiritual stagnation is a shortcut to evil. As water that does not flow goes stale, a soul that stops and does no good loses its inner light and is eaten away by darkness.

The way out of inaction is to exercise the power of living faith. The Urantia Book calls not for recluses who hide to avoid evil but for believers in the kingdom who actively serve and practice love in the midst of the world. At the very moment one does good, the evil in that place disappears.

5.3 The Armor of Faith

Faith is an effective armor against sin and iniquity. The Urantia Book says that fallen spirits such as Satan and Caligastia have no power to invade or harass the faith sons of God.

When we receive the light of God (truth, beauty, and goodness) and abide in faith, we gain spiritual immunity to evil. It is not that we defeat outward evil by fighting it, but that the inner light becomes so strong that the outer darkness cannot seep in. Because God is love, there is no place in him for negative evil. When we become one with God, there is no longer any place for evil to dwell in us either.

Two Responses to Evil

Basic attitude: the worldly, dualistic way acknowledges evil as a reality and opposes it / Jesus' way regards evil as a shadow and does good
Method: resistance, revenge, repression, criticism / forgiveness, service, love, receiving the light
State of mind: anger, fear, tension / peace, assurance, joy
Result: a cycle of evil, exhaustion, further evil / the disappearance of evil, spiritual growth, salvation

6. The End of Evil: Adjudication and Extinction

What becomes of evil that rejects the light to the end? The Urantia Book denies the doctrine of eternal hellfire and presents extinction instead. This confirms, finally, the thesis that evil is unreal.

6.1 The Loss of Reality and Nonexistence

Beings who persist in evil and iniquity cut themselves off from cosmic reality (God). Since God alone is the source of life, a being who has severed himself from that source loses the power to continue existing eternally. Complete identification with iniquity is equivalent to nonexistence.

Such beings are not actively punished by God; they meet the extinction of their existence as the inevitable consequence of their own choice. As a shadow disappears in a place full of light, those beings who rejected the light "become as though they had not been." Evil is not a reality that opposes good eternally but a temporary and illusory state.

6.2 The Harmony of Mercy and Justice

Yet until such extinction takes place, God in his infinite mercy gives every being the opportunity to return to the light. God loves the wicked and is always kind even to the ungrateful. Salvation is open to all who receive the light of God (faith) and reflect that light by doing good to their neighbors (service).

Conclusion: The Active Choice for Light Is Salvation

The nature of creation: God is perfect goodness and did not create evil. Evil is a potential side effect, a state of deficiency of light, arising in the evolutionary process of finite creatures endowed with free will.

The unreality of evil: Evil is only the distorted shadow of the cosmic realities of truth, beauty, and goodness; it is not a self-existent reality.

The way of salvation: The true way to overcome evil is not a war of attrition against darkness but the active practice of love, receiving the light of God within and doing good without.

Do not fear the shadow; walk toward the sun. When we walk toward the sun, the shadow falls behind us of its own accord. To receive the light of God and to do good: this is the secret of the final victory over evil and the way that leads to eternal life.

Q: Minority salvation vs. majority salvation
A:
The Timing and Nature of Salvation: Minority Salvation, Majority Salvation, and a Third Way

1. Introduction: The Central Issue in the Doctrine of Salvation

1.1 The Issue

Among readers of The Urantia Book there is an important doctrinal debate over when and how the ultimate destiny of mortals is decided. This debate can be summed up broadly as two views: minority salvation and majority (universal) salvation.

Minority salvation (the this-life determinism view): It holds that the eternal destiny of the mortal, that is, survival or extinction, is decided entirely by the choices made and the attainment of faith during the material life in this world. A mortal who fails to qualify for survival in this life cannot be resurrected at all, and his existence is simply extinguished.

Majority salvation (the universal resurrection view): It holds that, with very few exceptions, nearly all mortals are first resurrected into the morontia world. Life in this world is merely a preliminary stage, and a second chance, or the real chance, to choose one's ultimate destiny is given in the morontia world.

1.2 A New Hypothesis: A Third Way

This essay adopts a third perspective as a framework for critically examining the two extreme views. In the light of a Paradise journey that may extend to hundreds of millions of years, the minority-salvation claim that a fleeting life of a hundred years in this world, spent in confusion and ignorance, decides eternal extinction has a fundamental problem of proportionality. Such a conclusion does not seem plausible in view of the circumstances of divine justice.

The core of this hypothesis is that resurrection is not the completion or reward of salvation but the beginning of judgment, or of a new opportunity. This also accords with the biblical concepts of "the resurrection of judgment" and "⟦Q 188:3.9⟧the resurrection of life⟦/Q⟧." From this perspective, extinction is not a momentary failure in this world but the accumulated result of a conscious and final rejection of survival made over a sufficient period of time, including life in the morontia world.

1.3 Purpose and Scope

This essay examines the textual grounds and logical limitations of minority salvation and majority salvation respectively, points out the limitations of the two views, and then sets out the texts and reasoning that support the third way, that "resurrection is a continuation of opportunity, and extinction is a final choice."

2. The Grounds and Limitations of Minority Salvation (This-Life Determinism)

2.1 Textual Grounds

Minority salvation rests on several passages suggesting that mortal survival is not automatic or universal but conditional, and that its conditions must be met during this life.

Conditional survival: 40:5.19 clearly states the conditions of survival: "⟦Q 40:5.19⟧All souls of every possible phase of mortal existence will survive provided they manifest willingness to co-operate with their indwelling Adjusters and exhibit a desire to find God and to attain divine perfection, even though these desires be but the first faint flickers⟦/Q⟧..." Proponents of majority salvation focus on the minimal standard of "⟦Q 40:5.19⟧the first faint flickers⟦/Q⟧," but proponents of minority salvation emphasize that the heart of this passage lies in the clear preconditions of willingness and attitude. Survival, they say, is the result that follows when these preconditions are met.

Failure in this life: Several passages speak clearly of the possibility of failing to survive in this life. 16:9.3 uses the conditional "⟦Q 16:9.3⟧If mortal man fails to survive natural death⟦/Q⟧," which presupposes that there actually are cases of failing to survive at the point of natural death. Again, 113:2.4 states that "⟦Q 113:2.4⟧when human beings fail to survive⟦/Q⟧," their guardian seraphim "⟦Q 113:2.4⟧may repeatedly serve in similar capacities⟦/Q⟧." This suggests that failures to survive are not one-time occurrences.

The sentence of spiritual death: One of the strongest grounds for minority salvation is 112:3.2. This passage explains that when the mortal "⟦Q 112:3.2⟧has finally rejected survival⟦/Q⟧," he may be pronounced "⟦Q 112:3.2⟧spiritually insolvent, morontially bankrupt, in the conjoint opinion of the Adjuster and the surviving seraphim⟦/Q⟧." And such a death is declared to be "⟦Q 112:3.2⟧final in its significance irrespective of the temporary continuation of the living energies of the physical⟦/Q⟧..." This suggests that an individual's spiritual destiny can be finally decided even before physical death, and it becomes the central argument of this-life determinism.

Spiritual birth as a prerequisite: In 193:2.2 the risen Jesus says, "⟦Q 193:2.2⟧such survival is dependent on your having been previously born of the spirit of truth-seeking and God-finding⟦/Q⟧." Here the word "previously" strongly implies that spiritual birth must take place in this life as a prerequisite of survival, that is, of resurrection.

2.2 The Limitations of Minority Salvation

Despite these grounds, minority salvation faces serious philosophical and interpretive difficulties.

Philosophical limitation: The greatest limitation is the problem of proportionality mentioned above. If the divine plan includes an ascension journey to Paradise lasting hundreds of millions of years, how can the most confused, ignorant, and brief first stage of that plan, a fleeting hundred years, be the final test that justifies eternal extinction? This raises serious questions about the balance of divine justice and mercy.

Interpretive limitation: Minority salvation tends to inflate extreme cases such as 112:3.2 into a possibility of universal failure. The text seems clearly to distinguish passive failure due to ignorance or weakness from active rejection such as the final rejection of 112:3.2. 40:5.19 shows that the threshold of survival is very low ("⟦Q 40:5.19⟧the first faint flickers⟦/Q⟧"). If even faint flickers guarantee survival, the final rejection of 112:3.2 must mean not mere failure or a state of sin but an extreme spiritual state of consciously and stubbornly rejecting the light itself. Therefore 112:3.2 can apply not to a minority but only to a very small number of exceptional beings. Minority salvation can commit the logical error of inflating this possibility for a tiny few into a danger for the many.

3. The Grounds and Defects of Majority (Universal) Salvation (the Universal Resurrection View)

3.1 Textual Grounds

Majority salvation, which holds that nearly everyone is automatically resurrected into the morontia world, relies mainly on two passages and on particular interpretations of them.

Lucifer's claim: The key passage cited by supporters of this view is 53:3.5. There it is recorded that Lucifer "⟦Q 53:3.5⟧maintained that immortality was inherent in the system personalities, that resurrection was natural and automatic⟦/Q⟧." But this is only Lucifer's claim, and whether it is true or not cannot be known. The very fact that one must rely on a rebel's declaration to support a central doctrine of the revelation shows that there is no reliable textual evidence for this claim.

The new and living way: Another ground, 52:5.5, says that since Pentecost "⟦Q 52:5.5⟧Urantia mortals again may proceed directly to the morontia spheres⟦/Q⟧." Majority salvation reads this to mean that all mortals proceed directly to morontia. But this is a logical leap. The passage does not mean that the mortals who proceed to the morontia spheres are all mortals. That a way has been opened does not mean that everyone is automatically carried along it; it means only that qualified mortals have become able to ascend directly without waiting for the dispensational resurrection.

3.2 The Defect of Majority Salvation: Moral Laxity

The most serious problem with majority salvation is not only that its textual basis is weak but that it breeds spiritual laziness and moral laxity.

A personal testimony: "Even in my own case, after only a brief exposure to it, I actually found myself thinking, 'So all my hard work up to now was for nothing.'" This shows that majority salvation can immediately undermine the believer's sense of spiritual urgency and motivation for dedication.

The corruption of the attitude of faith: This doctrine carries a great risk of turning the active and dedicated attitude of "seek first the righteousness of God" into a passive and procrastinating attitude of "if I just live a good and diligent life, I suppose I will get the righteousness of God later on, in morontia." The rhetorical question "If we are told we get there for free, who would sacrifice and strive to seek?" pinpoints the fatal moral weakness of this doctrine.

Acts of faith made unnecessary: If everyone automatically enters the next stage, acts of faith in this life such as evangelism and repentance lose their importance. This can weaken the significance of death as a boundary and make the struggle of this life for spiritual growth meaningless.

3.3 Conclusion

Majority salvation, first, has so weak a textual basis that it must take a rebel's claim as its central ground; second, misreads 52:5.5; and third, has the moral weakness of undermining the central motivation of faith. It is therefore difficult to support either textually or morally.

4. Synthesis: Resurrection Is Opportunity, and Extinction Is a Final Choice

Between the harsh proportionality problem of minority salvation and the textual and moral weaknesses of majority salvation, the text provides reasoning that supports a third way. This perspective understands resurrection as the beginning of a process and extinction as the final result of conscious rejection.

4.1 The Nature of Resurrection: A Starting Line, Not a Finish Line

The key to resolving the contradictions of the two views lies in 47:3.7. This passage says that on the first mansion world "⟦Q 47:3.7⟧you will resume your intellectual training and spiritual development at the exact level whereon they were interrupted by death⟦/Q⟧," and that "⟦Q 47:3.7⟧Vous commencez là-bas exactement au point où vous en étiez en partant d’ici.⟦/Q⟧."

This passage shows that resurrection is not a reward or an amnesty but a continuation.

If resurrection were final salvation or amnesty, as majority salvation claims, there would be no need to resume training.

If resurrection were a reward only for those who had already obtained salvation in this life, as minority salvation claims, the idea of beginning again right where one left off would be unnecessary, since they would already be in a state of spiritual attainment.

The text shows that resurrection continues the mortal's testing process in a new environment (morontia) in which material limitations have been removed. This agrees with the hypothesis that "resurrection is the beginning of opportunity" and solves the hundred-year proportionality problem that minority salvation could not solve. The hundred years of this life are only the first stage of testing, and through resurrection the second stage of testing (the morontia life) begins again at the very level at which it stopped in this life.

4.2 The Nature of Extinction: Conscious Rejection, Not Failure

The second pillar supporting the third way is a redefinition of the cause of extinction. The text consistently shows that extinction results not from the failure of an ignorant hundred years but from a conscious rejection confirmed over time.

146:2.5: "⟦Q 146:2.5⟧Even the infinite love of God cannot force the salvation of eternal survival upon any mortal creature who does not choose to survive⟦/Q⟧." This suggests that extinction is not a capricious punishment by God but the result of respecting the creature's free will to the very end.

196:3.35: "⟦Q 196:3.35⟧If man does not choose to survive⟦/Q⟧..." This passage too defines extinction not as passive failure but as a matter of active choice.

112:3.2: Although it is also used as a ground for minority salvation, it identifies the cause as when the mortal "⟦Q 112:3.2⟧has finally rejected survival⟦/Q⟧," emphasizing a final and conscious rejection rather than temporary wandering or ignorance.

47:2.7: Even in the case of children, regarding the ascendant career, it says that if they "⟦Q 47:2.7⟧definitely decide against the Havona adventure ... there is no resurrection from such a second death⟦/Q⟧."

What appears consistently in these four passages is not passive failure or ignorance but an active, volitional, and final rejection ("does not choose to survive," "does not choose," "finally rejected," "definitely decide"). The Urantia Book's View of Reincarnation: A Critique of Reincarnation and a Reinterpretation of "Past-Life Memories"

1. Introduction: The Uniqueness of Personality and Eternal One-Directional Ascent

The two most fundamental pillars of the cosmology of The Urantia Book are the uniqueness of personality and an ascent that moves in one direction. 2. The Rejection of Reincarnation in the Text of The Urantia Book

The Urantia Book regards reincarnation not as a mere philosophical error but as a serious obstacle blocking the spiritual progress of mortals. This is clearly seen in the records concerning Melchizedek, Jesus, and Gautama Siddhartha.

2.1 The "Stultifying" Doctrine (94:2.3)

The Melchizedek author sharply criticizes the harm of the idea of reincarnation. He declares that nothing was so stultifying as the doctrine of the reincarnation of souls that came from the Dravidian Deccan.

This doctrine is stultifying because it robbed mortals of the hope of finding deliverance and spiritual advancement through death. In The Urantia Book, death is a deliverance and a graduation into the next stage of the eternal ascension career. But the doctrine of transmigration turned this hope into a fear of a weary and monotonous round of repeated rebirths.

2.2 Jesus and Gautama (130:2.8, 94:7.3)

Jesus clearly denied the form of reincarnation in which a human soul is reborn in the body of an animal. From that day on Ganid never again held the belief that the souls of men transmigrate into the bodies of animals.

The case of Gautama Siddhartha is even more interesting. The Urantia Book criticizes Gautama for failing to perceive the reality of the individual soul, yet it speaks favorably of his valiant fight against the time-honored belief in the transmigration of the soul. Gautama diagnosed the disease of transmigration correctly, but he prescribed the extreme remedy of denying the individual soul itself. The Urantia Book supports his conclusion that "transmigration is wrong" but rejects his premise that "there is no individual soul," and it offers instead the alternative of the eternal ascent of the individual soul.

In the universe of The Urantia Book, personality is so sacred and unique that it can never be recycled, and experience is so precious that it is never thrown away.

Q: Please give me the textbook explanations of the electron, the quantum, the neutron, the atom, and the element, with their characteristics and relationships.
A:
1. Definitions and Characteristics of Each Concept
Element
Definition: A kind (constituent) of matter that cannot be broken down further by chemical means (for example, hydrogen, oxygen, carbon, iron).
Characteristics: Its kind is determined by the number of protons in the atomic nucleus (the atomic number), and its properties recur with a regular periodicity in the periodic table.
Atom
Definition: The smallest physical and chemical unit of particle that makes up an element.
Characteristics: It consists of a dense atomic nucleus at the center and electrons circling around it, a structure resembling a solar system.
Proton vs. Quantum
Proton: A positively (+) charged particle that makes up the atomic nucleus. It is about 2,000 times heavier than the negatively (-) charged electron, and the number of protons is the atomic number of the atom and determines the kind of element.
Quantum: Not the name of a particle but the basic physical unit of energy, meaning that energy is absorbed or emitted in discontinuous minimal packets. (In Korean textbooks and everyday usage the word for "proton" is sometimes shortened to the word for "quantum," but as a physical concept the quantum is the minimal unit of energy.)
Neutron
Definition: A particle carrying no electric charge (neutral) that exists in the atomic nucleus together with the protons.
Characteristics: Among the protons, whose positive (+) charges repel one another, it binds the nucleus together so that it does not break apart, maintaining the stability of the atomic nucleus.
Electron
Definition: A minute, negatively (-) charged particle that revolves at high speed around the atomic nucleus.
Characteristics: It is extremely light, about 1/2,000 of the weight of the hydrogen atom, the lightest atom, and the movement and revolution of the outer electrons of the atom give rise to the chemical reactions and electrical phenomena of matter.

2. Their Organic Relationship
Macroscopic kind and microscopic particle: An element is the name denoting a chemical kind of matter, and the actual particle unit that makes up this element is the atom.
The internal structure of the atom: At the center of the atom lies the atomic nucleus, which is made up of protons and neutrons strongly bound together.
The motion of the electrons: In the outer orbits around the atomic nucleus, the electrons revolve.
The transfer of energy (the quantum): When an electron moves between orbits or absorbs or emits energy, the energy is transferred not as a continuous flow but in specific unit packets called quanta
