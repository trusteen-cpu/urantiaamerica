ch30.t | The Kingdom of Heaven
ch30.sub | The Inner Experience Ruled by the Father's Will
ch30.sum | The center of the gospel Jesus preached was the kingdom of heaven. Yet that kingdom is not a visible nation or institution but a spiritual relationship found wherever the spirit of God teaches and leads the soul of man. In it there is no high and mighty king, but a divine family of the Father and his sons. One enters the kingdom by faith, recognizing God's sovereignty, believing in sonship, and making the Father's will one's own. The religion of the kingdom is personal, but its fruits spread through society as brotherly love and unselfish service. This chapter considers in turn the nature of the kingdom, the way of entrance, its personal and social dimensions and growth, and the relation between the kingdom and the church. The kingdom is an experience that begins small as a mustard seed and grows all the way to Paradise, and its first home is always a single human heart.
ch30.s1 | The Nature of the Kingdom
ch30.s1.u1.k | The kingdom of heaven is not visible; it is wherever God's spirit leads the soul of man.
ch30.s1.u1.p | The Jews of Jesus' day awaited the kingdom of God as a political event, the restoration of David's realm. Jesus turned that expectation around. The kingdom is not a material thing to be found here or there; it is already among us, and its power lies not in armies or riches but in the spirit that teaches minds and rules hearts. Its marks are righteousness, peace, and joy.
ch30.s1.u2.k | The kingdom consists in recognizing God's sovereignty, believing in sonship, and desiring to do God's will.
ch30.s1.u2.p | Jesus summed up the kingdom in three essentials: recognizing that God is sovereign, believing that we are his sons, and having faith in the power of the supreme desire to do his will. Since God is spirit, this kingdom is spiritual. It is neither a material thing nor a system of knowledge; it is a living relationship between God and man.
ch30.s1.u3.k | The kingdom Jesus proclaimed has no mighty king; it is a divine family of the Father and his sons.
ch30.s1.u3.p | "Kingdom" was a word Jesus inherited, but what he put into it was not the relation of king and subject. It is not a realm ruled by a high and mighty king but a divine family of a Father and his children. So he asks us to stop fearing God as a king or serving him as a master, and instead to reverence him as Creator and love him as Father. As faith matures, this love deepens into worship.
ch30.s2 | The Way into the Kingdom
ch30.s2.u1.k | When the Father's will becomes truly one's own will, one is truly in the kingdom.
ch30.s2.u1.p | One who keeps God's will as an outward law differs from one who has made that will his own. Jesus says that while the Father's will remains a law, one is hardly in the kingdom. When that will truly becomes one's own, one is in very truth in the kingdom. The kingdom becomes an established experience within a person when that will becomes the desire of the heart.
ch30.s2.u2.k | Unless one is born of the spirit, one cannot enter the kingdom of God.
ch30.s2.u2.p | In his conversation with Nicodemus, Jesus called the door into the kingdom being born from above. This is not reforming oneself by moral effort but the spirit raising new life within a person. As we know the wind by the rustle of the leaves though we cannot see it, so it is with everyone born of the spirit. The kingdom begins, before any human resolve, with the birth of the spirit.
ch30.s2.u3.k | Faith is the price of entering God's family, and the forgiven one loves and forgives his neighbor.
ch30.s2.u3.p | Even after entering the door by faith, the righteousness of the kingdom must grow. Jesus explained the way through the experience of forgiveness. God's forgiveness is actually experienced in the measure that one forgives one's fellows, and one cannot truly forgive without loving one's neighbor as oneself. From that love moral conduct flows naturally. Righteousness is not strained effort but the fruit of love.
ch30.s3 | Person, Society, and the Growth of the Kingdom
ch30.s3.u1.k | The religion of the kingdom is personal, but its fruits are familial and social.
ch30.s3.u1.p | Because the kingdom begins in the individual heart, Jesus always exalted the sacredness of the person above the community. Yet the experience does not stay shut up within the individual. A person grows in character through unselfish service and unfolds his moral nature in loving relations with others. So the love call of the kingdom breaks down the world's hate, and the believer's unselfish service carries light to those in darkness.
ch30.s3.u2.k | The kingdom is an evolutionary experience that begins on earth and grows all the way to Paradise.
ch30.s3.u2.p | The kingdom does not drop from heaven complete in a single moment. Jesus explained that it is an experience beginning on this earth and advancing through successive life stations to Paradise. The parable of the mustard seed pictures this. As the least of seeds becomes a tree where birds can rest, the small faith within one person at last becomes shade where many find rest.
ch30.s3.u3.k | The world has never seriously tried the ideals of the kingdom, but each must first establish it in his own soul.
ch30.s3.u3.p | Looking at history, the progress of the kingdom seems slow. Yet the text says the world has never seriously tried out Jesus' ideals of the kingdom. Slow progress means not that the ideals have failed but that they have not yet been tried. The more important warning is not to make the mistake of waiting for the kingdom to appear in the world while failing to establish it in our own souls. The kingdom advances first within each person.
ch30.s4 | The Kingdom and the Church
ch30.s4.u1.k | The church could have been a natural social fruit of the kingdom, but it nearly replaced the kingdom concept.
ch30.s4.u1.p | It is natural and good for believers to gather in community. The problem was not the church's existence but its taking the place of the kingdom. To the Jews the kingdom was the community of Israel; to the gentiles it became the Christian church. But to Jesus the kingdom was the spiritual brotherhood of individuals who believed in the fatherhood of God and dedicated themselves to doing his will.
ch30.s4.u2.k | The inner kingdom Jesus taught will yet be proclaimed again to every church, religion, nation, and person.
ch30.s4.u2.p | That the story about Jesus displaced Jesus' gospel of the kingdom is a painful chapter of history. Yet the text does not give up hope. The kingdom that exists in the believer's heart will yet be proclaimed to the Christian church and to every other religion, nation, and individual. That proclamation will be a return not to a visible church or the expectation of a second coming, but to the Father's will ruling in the believer's heart.
ch30.qs1 | In what areas does God's will still remain an outward law for me, and what would it take for it to become my own will?
ch30.qs2 | How are the inner fruits of the kingdom I have experienced flowing out as service to my family and neighbors?
ch30.qs3 | For me, how are belonging to a church or faith community and belonging to the kingdom of heaven alike, and how are they different?
