ch29.t | Christology
ch29.sub | The Creator Son Made Flesh
ch29.sum | The Christology of the Urantia Book sees Jesus as Michael, the Creator Son of our local universe. Through seven bestowals he became a wise and sympathetic sovereign, and in the seventh and final bestowal he took human form on Urantia. He was not God in association with man but God incarnate in man, God and man from first to last. He was a true man who labored, grew weary, was tested, and died; and at the same time he was the revelation of the Father, who could say that he who had seen him had seen the Father. This chapter considers in turn Michael's bestowals, the oneness of divinity and humanity, the life and character that revealed the Father, and the religion of Jesus as distinct from a religion about Jesus. The aim of Christology is not to leave Jesus only as an object of worship but to lead us to believe as he believed and to follow him.
ch29.s1 | The Bestowals of Michael
ch29.s1.u1.k | A Creator Son's bestowals make him a wise, sympathetic, and understanding sovereign.
ch29.s1.u1.p | The life of Jesus is not a sudden exception but the final stage of a path all Creator Sons walk. A Creator Son is endowed with justice and righteousness, yet the Ancients of Days will not certify him as universe sovereign until he has lived as a creature in the creature's own environment; the ruler stands where the ruled stand. Christology begins here: our sovereign is one who knows our life from within.
ch29.s1.u2.k | Through seven bestowals Michael qualified to rule as supreme representative of the Trinity.
ch29.s1.u2.p | A Creator Son may rule his universe in his own right from the start. But Michael was not content with that; he sought to complete the experience of creature understanding by living seven times as various orders of creatures. His life on Urantia was the last of these bestowals. The meaning of this bestowal career is that sovereignty earned by experience inspires a deeper trust than sovereignty held by right.
ch29.s1.u3.k | In the Urantia bestowal Michael laid aside all superhuman help and depended wholly on the Father.
ch29.s1.u3.p | This passage is part of the counsel Michael received before his bestowal. Just as his creatures depend on him, he now had to depend wholly on his Paradise Father. The Creator of a universe chose to live as a man trusting the Father. So the faith Jesus asks of us is not a command handed down from above; it is a road he walked first.
ch29.s2 | God and Man
ch29.s2.u1.k | In Jesus, God and man were joined in one unified personality.
ch29.s2.u1.p | The long debate of Christology has been how divinity and humanity relate in Jesus. The Jews of his day pictured the Messiah as perfected human, as superhuman, even as divine, but never conceived of the two natures joined in one personality. That was revealed only in Jesus. The Urantia Book teaches that God and man can coexist in a unified personality, and that the present status of Christ Michael demonstrates it.
ch29.s2.u2.k | Jesus was a true man of flesh and blood who labored, grew weary, was tested, and died.
ch29.s2.u2.p | Emphasis on his divinity can make Jesus' humanity seem like an outer garment. The text corrects that tendency. He hungered, thirsted, and slept, and he passed through the full range of human feelings. That the Creator Son was literally made flesh and dwelt as a man of the realm tells us his human experience was no appearance but reality.
ch29.s2.u3.k | Consciousness of divinity grew gradually in Jesus' mind until his baptism.
ch29.s2.u3.p | Jesus did not grow up as a child who knew everything from the start. His awareness of his divine nature and prehuman existence grew gradually and was completed at his baptism. After that he seems to have had the power to limit his human consciousness of his divinity. His life can be an example to us precisely because he truly bore the limits of a man.
ch29.s3 | A Life That Revealed the Father
ch29.s3.u1.k | To see Jesus, more than to hear his words, is the way to know the Father.
ch29.s3.u1.p | Jesus taught little doctrine about the Father; that God is spirit and is a Father to his creatures was almost all. Instead he showed the Father by his life. So he never said that whoever heard him had heard God, but that whoever had seen him had seen the Father. The center of revelation is not a sentence but a person.
ch29.s3.u2.k | Jesus' character, sincere and original, showed love and mercy in place of fear.
ch29.s3.u2.p | What revealed the Father was not so much Jesus' miracles as his character. He was without affectation, lived as he taught, and was the truth itself. Unbound by tradition, he still did not discard the gems of older truth. The newest thing in his teaching was its emphasis on love and mercy in place of fear and sacrifice. In that character we see what God is like.
ch29.s3.u3.k | Jesus' faith was not traditional or intellectual but a personal trust pure as a child's.
ch29.s3.u3.p | Before he was an object of faith, Jesus was a man of faith. He went through all the ups and downs of life, yet never doubted God's watchcare. His faith was not an inherited tradition or a conclusion of the intellect but an insight born of communion with his indwelling Adjuster. In a mature intellect the trust of a child reigned. It was childlike, but never childish.
ch29.s4 | The Religion of Jesus
ch29.s4.u1.k | Jesus asks us not to believe in him but to believe with him, and to follow him.
ch29.s4.u1.p | The final question of Christology shifts from "Who is Jesus?" to "How shall we follow him?" Rather than demanding belief in himself, Jesus invites us to believe what he believed, and as he believed. To follow him is to share his faith and enter into his spirit of unselfish service for others. The most valuable knowledge is to know how Jesus lived.
ch29.s4.u2.k | Jesus founded a religion of personal experience; later followers founded a religion of worshiping Jesus.
ch29.s4.u2.p | The religion of Jesus and the religion about Jesus are not the same. One is the personal experience of doing God's will and serving one's brothers; the other is a community of faith that worships the glorified Christ. Both were latent in his divine-human life. Rather than setting them against each other, the text regrets that no unified religion arose honoring both his human and his divine natures.
ch29.s4.u3.k | The cross is not an offering to appease wrath but a symbol of the good giving themselves for the evil.
ch29.s4.u3.p | Jesus' death must be read in the same light as his life. The cross is not a sign of an innocent Son offered to an angry God in place of sinners. It is the symbol of holy service that gives one's life to the very end for the welfare and salvation of others. So the cross is not a sign of fear but the everlasting symbol of the good saving the evil by the devotion of love.
ch29.qs1 | What comfort does it give my own growth in faith that Jesus' awareness of his divinity grew gradually and that he depended wholly on the Father?
ch29.qs2 | How does the difference between believing in Jesus and believing with Jesus show up in my religious life?
ch29.qs3 | Which trait of Jesus' character do I most want to share today, and what can I do toward it?
