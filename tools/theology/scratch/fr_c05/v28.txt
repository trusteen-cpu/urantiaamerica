ch28.t | The Plan of Salvation
ch28.sub | The Father's Eternal Purpose and Salvation by Faith
ch28.sum | In the Urantia Book salvation is not a bargain for appeasing an angry God; it is a gift set within the eternal plan of the Deities to raise the creatures of time to the perfection of eternity. The Father's plan of attainment, the Son's bestowal plan, and the Infinite Spirit's plan of mercy ministry together achieve this purpose. The bestowals of the Sons are not meant to change the Father's attitude, and the death on the cross was no offering to appease wrath. Salvation is the Father's gift received by faith, and the righteous life is not its condition but its fruit. This chapter considers in turn the structure of the divine plan, the error of atonement ideas, sonship by faith, and the salvation of forgiveness and love. God is a Father before he is a judge, and his love does not merely set sin aside but renews a person and leads him to eternal survival.
ch28.s1 | The Divine Plan of Perfection Attainment
ch28.s1.u1.k | Salvation begins in the joint plan of Father and Son to lead the beings of time to eternal perfection.
ch28.s1.u1.p | To see salvation only as the solution to the problem of sin is to see just one corner of the picture. The Urantia Book sets salvation within a far larger frame. Father and Son are one in a vast plan to raise material beings to eternal perfection. Its starting point is the plan of progressive attainment, which includes the bestowal of Thought Adjusters and the gift of personality. Salvation is not damage control; it is a purpose intended from the beginning.
ch28.s1.u2.k | The Deities together foster the plan of ascension and, if required, co-operate in restoration and rehabilitation.
ch28.s1.u2.p | Having proclaimed the universal mandate to be perfect, the Father intrusted the execution of that tremendous undertaking to the Eternal Son, who fosters it together with the Infinite Spirit. Where rebellion mars the plan, the bestowals of the Sons recover what was lost, and to this is added the mercy ministry of the Infinite Spirit. Because the plans of the three Deities interlock, no world lies outside the reach of restoration.
ch28.s2 | No Wrath to Appease
ch28.s2.u1.k | The idea of appeasing an angry God with blood sacrifice is an affront to God.
ch28.s2.u1.p | For ages religion offered sacrifice, penance, and blood to buy the favor of God. The Urantia Book calls this idea a puerile and primitive philosophy and firmly rejects it. God is not divided into a personality of justice and another of mercy, so no mediator is needed between the two. The single sentence that God as a father transcends God as a judge sets the direction of the whole doctrine of salvation.
ch28.s2.u2.k | The bestowals of the Sons do not change the Father's attitude; they reveal the Father's nature.
ch28.s2.u2.p | The notion that the Son came to turn the Father's heart is widespread. But the Father's love has been unchanging from the first, so there is nothing to change. A Creator Son condescends to take upon himself the form and experiences of his creatures, and in so doing he reveals the Father's loving nature and beneficent authority. A bestowal does not persuade the Father; it reveals him.
ch28.s2.u3.k | Jesus did not die to atone for mankind's guilt or to appease the wrath of God.
ch28.s2.u3.p | The tradition of reading the cross as an offering of atonement and appeasement is deep and old. The Urantia Book plainly says that Jesus did not die to ransom man from apostate rulers, nor as a sacrifice to pay a debt mankind owed to God. Yet it does not erase the meaning of the cross; it notes that Urantia has become known among neighboring worlds as the World of the Cross. The cross did not change God's heart; it disclosed most deeply the love that was already there.
ch28.s3 | Salvation by Faith
ch28.s3.u1.k | Salvation is the Father's gift; accepted by faith, it makes one a son of God.
ch28.s3.u1.p | Jesus taught that salvation is a gift given by the Father and revealed by his Sons. What man must do is accept that gift by faith. By faith one is justified, by faith saved, and by the same faith one walks the endless path of perfection. Salvation is not an achievement of man but a gift freely given by the Father.
ch28.s3.u2.k | A righteous life is not the condition of salvation but the natural fruit of sonship already received.
ch28.s3.u2.p | Salvation is the gift of God, and righteousness is the natural fruit of the life that has received it. This does not make morality light; it puts things in the right order. We do not live righteously in order to be saved; we live righteously because we have been saved. One who has realized that he is a child of God finds it hard to live with the desire to sin. So kingdom believers do good not by compulsion but out of the joy of sonship.
ch28.s3.u3.k | Jesus abolished sacrifice and atonement and set the God-man relation on a parent-child basis.
ch28.s3.u3.p | Beneath the system of sacrifice lay a fictitious guilt, a sense of being abandoned alone in the universe. Jesus tore that foundation away by declaring that man is a child of God. Just as a child in a family pays no price to be loved, so no such ceremonies are needed in our relation with God. The Father looks not at a child's actual merit but at its motive and intent.
ch28.s3.u4.k | Mortals become faith sons of God by accepting sonship through faith.
ch28.s3.u4.p | Sonship with God begins not as something inborn by nature but as a status received by faith. When a person accepts that status in faith, he is already a son of grace and mercy. To this are added the facts that a Creator Son has become our elder brother and that the spirit of the Son has been poured out on all. Salvation is not a distant reward but a place in the family enjoyed now.
ch28.s4 | The Salvation of Forgiveness and Love
ch28.s4.u1.k | God's forgiveness need not be sought, only received as restored loyalty.
ch28.s4.u1.p | Ancient man could feel God's favor only by offering sacrifice. Modern man needs a new way of becoming conscious of salvation. The Urantia Book explains forgiveness as the renewal of loyalty relations with God. Because God is always ready to forgive, our part is not to extract forgiveness by effort but to realize and receive the restoration.
ch28.s4.u2.k | The cross places God and man on a family basis and shows a love that destroys sin.
ch28.s4.u2.p | The central truth of the universe is not the justice of a king who seeks satisfaction in the sufferings of an offending subject, but the love of a father for his son. The cross shows this forever. Mercy sets guilt to one side, but love destroys the sin and its weakness altogether. Jesus' forgiveness is not condonation but salvation from condemnation, and salvation does not slight wrongs; it makes them right.

ch28.qs2 | How does the order 'we live righteously because we are saved' change my moral striving?
ch28.qs3 | How can I practice in my relationships the difference between forgiveness that covers sin and love that makes things right?
