ch24.t = Sin
ch24.sub = Evil, Error, Sin, Iniquity, and the Price of Freedom
ch24.sum = The Urantia Book defines sin not as a vague state of fallenness but as a personal choice knowingly made against the will of God. The Gods do not create evil. Potential evil exists in a universe of differential levels of perfection, and sin is potential wherever imperfect beings can choose between good and evil. Freedom cannot escape the possibility of error, and in that very risk grows the goodness won through experience. Man is by nature imperfect but not necessarily a sinner, and he is not condemned for being born a descendant of Adam. This chapter considers the distinctions among evil, sin, and iniquity, the relation of freedom to evil, the nature of man, and a new understanding of sin together with God's attitude toward it.
ch24.s1 = Evil, Error, Sin, and Iniquity Distinguished
ch24.s1.u1.k = The Gods do not create evil; the deliberate choice of evil constitutes sin.
ch24.s1.u1.p = The first step in understanding sin is to distinguish the words. Potential evil exists in time within a universe of differing levels of perfection, and error arises where truth and falsehood coexist. But sin is not a circumstance; it is a choice. The deliberate choice of evil is sin, and the persistent pursuit of sin and error is iniquity. This distinction corrects both the idea that God is the cause of evil and the idea that every imperfection is sin.
ch24.s1.u2.k = Evil is unconscious transgression, sin is knowing transgression, iniquity is persistent and determined transgression.
ch24.s1.u2.p = When Thomas asked what evil is, Jesus answered in three steps. The standard throughout is the Father's will, and the difference lies in the degree of awareness and intent. Transgression without knowing is evil but not yet sin. Knowing and deliberate transgression is sin, and when that refusal hardens into a final rejection of the Father's loving plan, it becomes iniquity. Sin is less a list of deeds than the direction of the heart toward God.
ch24.s1.u3.k = Sin is purposeful resistance to divine reality, a conscious choice to oppose spiritual progress.
ch24.s1.u3.p = Defined from the viewpoint of cosmic philosophy, sin concerns one's relation to reality. Error is a misunderstanding of reality, and evil a partial maladjustment to it, but sin is resistance to reality one knows. When that resistance becomes open and persistent, personality itself disintegrates toward cosmic insanity. Sin is finally a path of self-destruction, a cutting of oneself off from reality.
ch24.s2 = Freedom and the Possibility of Evil
ch24.s2.u1.k = To be free, evolving man must be able to err.
ch24.s2.u1.p = Why did God not make a world without the possibility of mistakes? The text replies that such a world would be one without free intelligence. Perfect beings dwell in Havona, but evolving man must gain wisdom through experience, and a free but inexperienced intellect cannot be uniformly wise from the start. The possibility of mistaken judgment is the price of freedom; in itself it is not sin.
ch24.s2.u2.k = The power to choose good or evil is a universe reality, and God takes no one's freedom away.
ch24.s2.u2.p = Why evil and sin are permitted is a question that troubles every age. The text finds the answer in the reality of freedom. If freedom of choice is real, the possibility of wrong choice must be real too. Had God instantly prevented every rebellion, freedom would be no more than a symbolic ideal. The rulers of the universe deprive no one of this divine gift. The possibility of sin shows how seriously God takes freedom.
ch24.s2.u3.k = True liberty is the reward of progress; false liberty is a subtle deception of the error of time.
ch24.s2.u3.p = The greatest confusion left by the Lucifer rebellion concerned liberty. The rebellion promised freedom without any restraint, but such freedom is a self-destructive technique of cosmic existence. True liberty presupposes intelligence, maturity, fraternity, and fairness, and only a freedom that regards one's duties to neighbor and universe endures. Liberty is not the power to do as I please; it is the capacity to be ever more deeply related to reality.
ch24.s3 = Man Is Evil but Not Necessarily a Sinner
ch24.s3.u1.k = Imperfection of natural endowment is not sin; sin is a conscious attitude of rebellion.
ch24.s3.u1.p = Man is born with imperfect tendencies inherited from his animal origins, yet these are only the starting point of a long ascent, not grounds for condemnation. Jesus clearly distinguished between being exposed to evil and being a sinner. Evil is inherent in the natural order of this world, but sin is an attitude brought in by those who knew the spiritual light and chose darkness. This distinction gives man hope rather than shame.
ch24.s3.u2.k = Man is not a being fallen from perfection but one ascending toward divine perfection.
ch24.s3.u2.p = The idea that mankind began with perfect ancestors and fell through sin long dominated theology. Jesus reverses this picture: man begins low and rises toward God and divine perfection. Seen this way, salvation is not recovering a lost status but advancing toward a destiny not yet attained.
ch24.s3.u3.k = Sin is wholly personal, and no one suffers spiritual loss because of another's sin.
ch24.s3.u3.p = Caligastia's rebellion and Adam's default left deep wounds on this world: intellectual, social, and spiritual progress was retarded, and everyone born since has suffered temporal handicaps. Yet no one's eternal destiny has been jeopardized by them. The moral guilt and spiritual consequences of sin are wholly personal. This corrects the doctrine of original sin and at the same time reminds each person of responsibility for his own choices.
ch24.s4 = Sin Redefined and the Attitude of God
ch24.s4.u1.k = Sin must be redefined as deliberate disloyalty to Deity.
ch24.s4.u1.p = The ancients could be sure of God's favor only through sacrifice, but modern man needs a new understanding. The text redefines sin not as the breaking of a taboo but as a matter of loyalty to God. Undecided partial loyalty, divided loyalty, loyalty dying through indifference, and loyalty given to godless ideals are its stages. A sense of guilt is not itself sin; without conscious disloyalty to Deity there is no real sin.
ch24.s4.u2.k = The capacity to recognize guilt is a mark not of man's baseness but of his potential greatness.
ch24.s4.u2.p = Guilt is often regarded only as a crushing burden. The text reads it differently: the ability to recognize wrong as wrong is a transcendent excellence given to man and not to animals. The feeling of unworthiness is meant not to cast us down but to be the first stimulus toward the conquests of faith. Honest confession does not erase consequences, but it opens the door to spiritual growth.
ch24.s4.u3.k = The love of God saves the sinner; the law of God destroys the sin.
ch24.s4.u3.p = God is a person, and so he loves persons. Sin is not a person, nor is it a spiritual reality; God therefore takes no personal attitude toward it but deals with its existence through justice alone. Thus divine love and divine justice do not collide: love holds fast to the sinner, who has eternal possibilities, while law does away with sin, which is unreal. Unless a person finally identifies himself with sin, love has the last word.

ch25.t = Adam and Eve
ch25.sub = The Mission and Default of the Biologic Uplifters
ch25.sum = The Adam and Eve of the Urantia Book are not the first human beings of myth but a Material Son and Daughter sent from Jerusem, the system capital, to uplift the evolutionary races biologically and to quicken their culture. They undertook an almost hopeless task on a world wounded and isolated by rebellion, and in loneliness and impatience they fell into default by trying to outrun the divine plan through a short cut. Yet there was no "fall of man." Mankind was in fact uplifted by their contribution, and their transgression was an error of judgment, not deliberate rebellion. This chapter considers the mission of the biologic uplifters, the course of the default, a reinterpretation of the doctrine of the fall, and the story of repentance and restoration.
ch25.s1 = The Biologic Uplifters
ch25.s1.u1.k = Adam and Eve are a Material Son and Daughter sent to uplift the evolutionary races, the material gift of the Creator Son.
ch25.s1.u1.p = The Urantia Book presents Adam and Eve not as the first ancestors of mankind but as an order of sonship that comes according to a set universe plan. When primitive man reaches the limit of natural evolutionary development, the System Sovereign sends a pair of biologic uplifters. With the Planetary Prince they remain on that world throughout its evolution, upstepping the races and quickening culture. They are the material gift of the Creator Son to the inhabited worlds.
ch25.s1.u2.k = On normal worlds the Garden of Eden functions for ages as the pattern and center of world culture.
ch25.s1.u2.p = On worlds where the mission of Adam and Eve succeeds, the Garden of Eden remains for ages a cultural center and the social pattern of planetary life. Even in early times, while the violet people live relatively apart, their schools receive suitable students from the world races, and the industries of the Garden open new channels of commerce. This is the original shape of the divine plan. Only by keeping this normal picture in mind can we see, in reading Urantia's story, what was lost and what remained.
ch25.s1.u3.k = Adam and Eve took up an almost hopeless task on a planet isolated by rebellion.
ch25.s1.u3.p = Urantia was a world placed in spiritual isolation by the rebellion of its Planetary Prince, Caligastia. Adam and Eve arrived where nothing was ready for the proclamation of the brotherhood of man, a world groping in spiritual darkness, its confusion worse confounded. The text says no Adam was ever assigned to a more difficult world. In judging their failure we must remember these conditions first.
ch25.s2 = The Default
ch25.s2.u1.k = The default began not in evil intent but in a wish to hasten the good, born of loneliness and impatience.
ch25.s2.u1.p = Isolated, with no way to consult their superiors, Adam and Eve gradually lost heart. Then Serapatatia, a Nodite leader, urged a plan: if a leader of part violet descent could be born to the Nodites, those peoples would be bound more closely to the Garden. The idea was soberly and honestly considered to be for the good of the world. The tragedy came not from malice but from good intentions, from choosing a short cut instead of waiting.
ch25.s2.u2.k = The warning against mixing good and evil meant not to mingle the divine plan with human devising.
ch25.s2.u2.p = The story of the tree of knowledge of good and evil gains new meaning here. Good is carrying out the divine plan, sin is deliberate transgression of the divine will, and evil is the misapplication of plans that produces disharmony. Adam and Eve were warned again and again: on the day they mingled good and evil, they would become as the mortals of the realm. Even a good purpose, mixed with wrong methods, breeds confusion.
ch25.s2.u3.k = On the way to Paradise nothing is gained by trying to circumvent the divine plan through short cuts.
ch25.s2.u3.p = The text says that had Adam and Eve been a little more patient, they would eventually have succeeded. But Eve listened to the subtle propaganda of personal liberty. The lesson applies to every ascender: the divine plan may seem slow, yet it is the surest way, and impatient devising ends in a longer detour. Patience is a central virtue of spiritual maturity.
ch25.s3 = There Was No Fall of Man
ch25.s3.u1.k = There was no fall of man; mankind was in fact uplifted by Adam's bestowal.
ch25.s3.u1.p = The doctrine that mankind fell through Adam's transgression and inherited a sinful nature has been a great pillar of Christian theology. The Urantia Book plainly denies it. Adam and Eve fell from the high estate of Material Sonship to the status of mortals, but that was not the fall of man. Human history is a story of progressive evolution, and the Adamic bestowal left the peoples of the world greatly improved over their former biologic condition.
ch25.s3.u2.k = Adam should not be regarded as the cause of a curse on the human race.
ch25.s3.u2.p = Adam and Eve have long been blamed as the authors of every human misfortune. The text assesses them more fairly. They broke their covenant and lost their status, yet their contribution did much to advance the civilization of Urantia, and the high councils do not regard their mission as a total loss. Heaven's way of seeing is to recognize the good that the failed have left behind rather than to condemn them.
ch25.s3.u3.k = Perfection is not our origin but our eternal goal, and a personal, progressive universe is glorious.
ch25.s3.u3.p = The revelator who recounts the tragedy of Eden does not see the very possibility of mistakes as a flaw in the universe. The universe was not created perfect; perfection is not the starting point but the goal to be reached. That disagreement and misunderstanding are possible is evidence that personality is alive in the universe. This confession, that a personal and progressive universe is more glorious than a mechanically perfect one, places failure within the context of growth.
ch25.s4 = Repentance and Restoration
ch25.s4.u1.k = The transgression of Adam and Eve was an error of judgment, and their repentance was accepted.
ch25.s4.u1.p = Adam and Eve did not stage a conscious and deliberate rebellion. Their wrong was an error of judgment, and they sincerely repented. Remarkably, after being reduced to mortal status they experienced the indwelling of a Thought Adjuster, which they had never had before. Human status coupled with sincere repentance had made it possible for an Adjuster to indwell them. A new path began at the very place of failure.
ch25.s4.u2.k = Michael remembered the loyal hearts of those who defaulted and promised to call them forth.
ch25.s4.u2.p = The only personal message Adam and Eve ever received from Michael is the warmest passage in this story. The sovereign of the universe remembered not only their failure but the desire of their hearts to be loyal to the Father's will. God does not judge people by outcomes alone; he looks at motives and longings. This promise tells everyone who has failed that hope remains so long as the heart is turned toward God.
ch25.s4.u3.k = Raised in a special resurrection, Adam and Eve became ascending citizens and still serve Urantia.
ch25.s4.u3.p = The story of Adam and Eve does not end in failure. They fell asleep trusting the promise of resurrection and awoke on the mansion worlds in a special resurrection. They passed quickly through the worlds of progressive ascension, attained citizenship on Jerusem, and returned to their home planet as members of a different order of universe personalities. Today they are among the twenty-four counselors who serve Urantia. This story of restoration is a vivid testimony that salvation is open to all.
