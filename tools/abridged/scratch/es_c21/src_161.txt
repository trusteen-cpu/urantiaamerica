## 161.title   HINT: Las Conversaciones Ulteriores con Rodán
"Further Discussions with Rodan"
## 161.b0.ask
"“How can the infinite God be a person, and how can divinity dwell together with humanity in the man Jesus?”"
## 161.b1.h
"Overview"
## 161.b2.p
"In Paper 160 Rodan, the philosopher from Alexandria, set out his philosophy of living. Paper 161 tells what happened after that conversation. Late in September A.D. 29, Jesus left with ten of the apostles to attend the feast of tabernacles in Jerusalem, while Nathaniel and Thomas, with the Master’s permission, stayed on at Magadan for a few days to continue their earnest debate with Rodan."
## 161.b3.p
"Rodan had already been well instructed in Jesus’ teachings by one of the former apostles of John the Baptist, his teacher at Alexandria. So he and the two apostles did not differ over the gospel as a whole. They differed on just two matters: the personality of God and the divine nature of Jesus."
## 161.b4.p
"Can the infinite and eternal God really be called a person? And how could anyone be sure that a man who ate and walked with them was both the Son of Man and the Son of God?"
## 161.b6.h
"The Paper"
## 161.b7.h   HINT: 1. La Personalidad de Dios
"1. The Personality of God"
## 161.b8.p
"Rodan readily accepted all that was presented to him about the attributes of God, but he contended that the Father in heaven cannot be a person as man conceives personality. To him, personality meant full and mutual communication between beings of equality who are capable of sympathetic understanding. Since God is the infinite and eternal Creator, he has no equals, and so, said Rodan, he is transcendent to personality."
## 161.b9.p
"Thomas argued that God does communicate with man, and Nathaniel appealed to his own personal experience with God. Rodan allowed that he had recently had similar experiences himself, but he held that such experiences proved only the reality of God, not his personality. The two apostles asked Jesus to come to their rescue, but the Master refused to enter into the discussion. He only told Thomas that it mattered little what idea of the Father he held, as long as he was spiritually acquainted with the ideal of the Father’s infinite and eternal nature."

## 161.b13.p
"The third step is the heart of it. Jesus and the Father were one, and Jesus also communicated perfectly with men. Since both God and man understood the meaning of the symbols of Jesus’ communication, both possessed the attributes of personality needed to communicate with each other. Two things which are related to the same thing are related to each other. Rodan then confessed God as a person, on the condition that he might attach to the meaning of personality such extended values as superhuman, infinite, eternal, and universal. And he accepted Jesus as the personal revelation of the Father."
## 161.b15.h   HINT: 2. La Naturaleza Divina de Jesús
"2. The Divine Nature of Jesus"
## 161.b16.p
"One point remained: the divine nature of Jesus, a doctrine only recently announced in public. Nathaniel and Thomas presented their views together, and the book gives their teaching in a condensed and restated form under nine headings. It is less a theological theory than the testimony of men who had watched the Master day after day."

## 161.b18.p
"The apostles held that no mere man would sanely profess to forgive sin. Jesus was either just what he claimed to be, or else the greatest hypocrite and fraud the world had ever known — and they were persuaded that he was what he claimed. Their testimony ends this way: they knew he was human, and they were almost equally sure that he was also divine."
## 161.b19.p
"Strikingly, the first thing they spoke of after his own words was friendship. He made no use of flattery, yet treated them all with equal kindness; he did not hesitate to reprove them, yet it was plain that he truly loved them. The better you know him, they said, the more you will love him. His compassion was moved alike by physical suffering, mental anguish, or spiritual sorrow, and he asked questions to draw people out, not to gain information."
## 161.b21.p
"When their talks were over, the two apostles hurried on to Jerusalem and joined their fellows on Friday, and the other apostles learned much from hearing their experiences. Rodan returned to Alexandria, where he long taught his philosophy in the school of Meganta. He remained a faithful believer to the end of his days and gave up his life in Greece when the persecutions were at their height."
## 161.b22.h   HINT: 3. Las Mentes Humana y Divina de Jesús
"3. Jesus’ Human and Divine Minds"
## 161.b23.p
"Consciousness of divinity grew gradually in the mind of Jesus up to his baptism. Once he was fully self-conscious of his divine nature and prehuman existence, he seems to have had the power to limit his human consciousness of his divinity. From his baptism until the crucifixion it was entirely optional with him whether to depend only on the human mind or to use the knowledge of both the human and the divine minds."
## 161.b24.p
"He often withheld from his associates his foreknowledge of events and his awareness of their thoughts. He did not want to go too far beyond the idea of the human that his apostles and disciples held in their minds. The recorders say they saw three ways in which he worked, yet they could not always tell which method he used at a given moment, and they frankly admit that they do not know the full truth about this mystery."

## 161.b26.h
"Conclusion"
## 161.b27.p
"Paper 161 takes up the two hardest questions about God and Jesus through an honest conversation between a philosopher and two apostles. Rodan believed that the infinite God transcends personality, but Nathaniel pointed to the living bridge, Jesus. Because Jesus communicated fully with both God and man, God is shown to be more than a person but never less than a person. The apostles’ faith in Jesus’ divinity also rested not on doctrine but on what they had seen at his side: his friendship, his blameless life, and his intimate union with the Father. And how the human mind and the divine mind worked together in that one person, the book honestly leaves a mystery."
