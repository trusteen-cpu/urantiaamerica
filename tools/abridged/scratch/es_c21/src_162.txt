## 162.title   HINT: En la Fiesta de los Tabernáculos
"At the Feast of Tabernacles"
## 162.b0.ask
"“Why did Jesus, saying that the hour had now come, boldly enter Jerusalem, where those who sought to kill him were waiting, and openly reveal himself?”"
## 162.b1.h
"Overview"
## 162.b2.p
"While Nathaniel and Thomas stayed at Magadan to talk with Rodan, as told in Paper 161, Jesus and the ten apostles set out for Jerusalem to attend the feast of tabernacles. They took the shorter route through Samaria. A village on the eastern slopes of Mount Gilboa refused them lodging because they were Jews, and chased Philip and Matthew out with sticks and stones."
## 162.b3.p
"James and John asked permission to call fire down from heaven to devour the villagers, but Jesus severely rebuked them: vengeance savors not of the outlook of the kingdom of heaven. The company spent the night at the village by the Jordan ford and went on to Bethany. Jesus and the twelve then stayed near Jerusalem until the end of October, about four and one-half weeks."
## 162.b4.p
"Everyone knew that the Sanhedrin wanted him dead. Why, then, did Jesus choose this moment to stand in the temple courts at Jerusalem and declare openly who he was?"

## 162.b11.h   HINT: 2. El Primer Discurso en el Templo
"2. The First Temple Talk"
## 162.b12.p
"Asked how he could teach so fluently without having studied in the schools of the rabbis, Jesus answered that this teaching was not his own but His who sent him. Anyone who really desires to do the Father’s will shall know whether the teaching comes from God. The rulers sought to kill him, he said, because his gospel set men free from the burdensome traditions of a formal religion of ceremonies. And he appealed to the people: “⟦Q 162:2.2⟧Judge not according to outward appearances but rather judge by the true spirit of these teachings.⟦/Q⟧”"

## 162.b14.h   HINT: 3. La Mujer Adúltera
"3. The Woman Taken in Adultery"
## 162.b15.p
"Early on the third morning of the feast, hired agents of the Sanhedrin dragged a woman before Jesus, saying she had been taken in adultery, and asked whether she should be stoned according to the law of Moses. The book explains that the record we have of this episode is distorted: it was in fact a plot to trap Jesus. The woman’s husband, a man of Nazareth who had shamefully forced her to earn their living with her body, had made a bargain with the rulers’ hirelings to betray his own wife."

## 162.b18.h   HINT: 4. La Fiesta de los Tabernáculos
"4. The Feast of Tabernacles"
## 162.b19.p
"People came to this feast from all the known world, from Spain to India, which made it an ideal occasion for Jesus to proclaim his full gospel in Jerusalem for the first time. It was the feast of the harvest ingathering, held in the cool of autumn, and more Jews attended it than either Passover or Pentecost. At night great candelabras and scores of torches lit the temple, while the Roman castle of Antonia looked down in grim contrast upon the festive scene."

## 162.b21.h   HINT: 5. El Sermón Sobre la Luz del Mundo
"5. Sermon on the Light of the World"
## 162.b22.p
"On the evening before the last day, while the candelabras and torches lit up the temple, Jesus stood up and declared that he was the light of the world. When they charged that a man’s witness about himself cannot be true, he replied that the creature can never sit in judgment on the Creator. His witness was true, for he knew where he came from and where he was going, and he did not bear witness alone but together with his Father."

## 162.b29.p
"On the afternoon of the last day, in Solomon’s Porch, Jesus spoke to the believers about true freedom. When some objected that as children of Abraham they were in bondage to none, he explained that he was not speaking of outward subjection but of the liberties of the soul. Whoever commits sin is the bond servant of sin; but if the Son makes you free and makes you sons, you are free indeed."

## 162.b32.h   HINT: 8. La Visita con Marta y María
"8. The Visit with Martha and Mary"
## 162.b33.p
"While Lazarus and Mary sat at Jesus’ feet taking in his teaching, Martha busied herself with the evening meal. The book explains that she was unnecessarily distracted by numerous needless tasks; that was her disposition. When she asked Jesus to send her sister to help, he told her that only one thing is really worth while, and Mary had chosen it. Yet he wanted the two sisters to learn to serve together in co-operation and to refresh their souls together as well."
## 162.b35.h   HINT: 9. En Belén con Abner
"9. At Bethlehem with Abner"
## 162.b36.p
"After the feast Jesus did not teach again in the temple. He divided his time about equally between Bethany and Bethlehem. At Bethlehem he taught Abner and the other former apostles of John the Baptist, and both the courage he showed in Jerusalem and the sympathetic understanding of his private teaching won them over fully."
## 162.b37.p
"So, early in November, Abner and his eleven fellows cast their lot with Jesus and the twelve, and worked with them as one organization right down to the crucifixion. Jesus and his company rested at Ephraim and reached Magadan Park on November 2. The apostles, greatly relieved, no longer urged him to go up to Jerusalem."
## 162.b38.h
"Conclusion"
## 162.b39.p
"In Paper 162 Jesus no longer waits for his hour. He takes his frightened apostles into the midst of his enemies and openly reveals himself as the light of the world, the giver of the water of life, and the Son who brings true freedom. Yet his boldness never turns toward vengeance or condemnation. He rebukes the disciples who want fire from heaven, speaks without ill will to the officer sent to arrest him, and sends away uncondemned the woman used as bait in a trap. The outpouring of the spirit that the feast symbolized with water and light, he now truly promises. And as he told busy Martha, the lesser matters of life must give way before the greater things of the kingdom."
