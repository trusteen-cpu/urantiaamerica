## 167.title   HINT: La Visita a Filadelfia
"The Visit to Philadelphia"
## 167.b0.ask
"“In the days in Perea when there were almost no miracles, why did Jesus, on hearing about Lazarus, wait two more days and then set out for dangerous Judea?”"
## 167.b1.h
"Overview"
## 167.b2.p
"In Paper 166 we followed the last visits of the Perean ministry. Paper 167 now takes up the days when Jesus went to Philadelphia with ten apostles. At least two of the apostles always stayed behind at the Pella camp to teach the crowds, so as a rule only ten were with him at this time. When he reached Philadelphia, more than six hundred followers were with him."
## 167.b3.p
"These were days almost without miracles. Except for the cleansing of the ten lepers, there had been none on the Perean mission, and the gospel was proclaimed with power, most of the time without the personal presence of Jesus or his apostles. Good news came of the kingdom’s progress at Philadelphia and the nearby villages, and also from Alexandria and Damascus."
## 167.b4.p
"Then, just at this time, word came from Bethany that Lazarus was very sick. Why did Jesus not hurry to him at once, but wait two more days and then, despite his apostles’ protests, head into dangerous Judea?"

"The Paper"
## 167.b7.h   HINT: 1. El Desayuno con los Fariseos
"1. Breakfast with the Pharisees"
## 167.b8.p
"A wealthy Pharisee who had accepted Abner’s teachings invited Jesus to breakfast on the Sabbath, along with about forty leading men and a few lawyers, many of them from Jerusalem. A Pharisee of the Sanhedrin made straight for the seat of honor as was his habit, was moved four seats down, and was much offended. Only Jesus’ enemies noticed that he did not observe the ceremonial washing of hands."
## 167.b9.p
"Near the end of the meal a believer suffering from dropsy came in. He did not ask to be healed, but Jesus asked the company whether it was lawful to heal on the Sabbath, and then told the man to arise and go his way. Such works, he said, the Father does not to tempt people into the kingdom but to reveal himself to those already in it. Then he taught about places at the feast."

## 167.b15.h   HINT: 3. La Mujer de Debilidad de Espíritu
"3. The Woman with the Spirit of Infirmity"
## 167.b16.p
"For the first time since the Sanhedrin had closed the synagogues to him, Jesus taught in a synagogue. After the service he went to an elderly woman, bent over and bound by the depressions of fear for more than eighteen years, and told her that if she would only believe, she could be wholly loosed from her spirit of infirmity. She believed and straightened up at once. Her affliction was wholly mental; her bowed-over form was the result of a depressed mind."
## 167.b17.p
"The ruler of the synagogue was indignant that he had healed on the Sabbath, but Jesus asked whether each of them did not loose his ox on the Sabbath to lead it to water; should not this daughter of Abraham be loosed from her bondage? The ruler was later deposed and a follower of Jesus put in his place. Jesus often freed such victims from their bondage of fear, but people thought all such afflictions were either physical disorders or possession by evil spirits."

## 167.b19.h   HINT: 4. El Mensaje de Betania
"4. The Message from Bethany"
## 167.b20.p
"Late on the night of February 26 a message came from Martha and Mary: “⟦Q 167:4.1⟧Lord, he whom you love is very sick.⟦/Q⟧” Jesus seemed for a moment to be in communication with something beyond himself, and then said that this sickness was not to the death, but might be used to glorify God. He loved this family with a fervent affection, but a plan had come to him to give the leaders at Jerusalem one more chance, and he asked the Father’s consent to show his power over life and death."
   INL 167:4.1 | Lord, he whom you love is very sick. | AUTO: 
      V 167:4.1: Muy tarde el domingo por la noche del 26 de febrero, un mensajero de Betania llegó a Filadelfia, trayendo un mensaje de Marta y María que decía: «Señor, aquél que amas está muy enfermo». El mensaje le llegó a Jesús al final de la conferencia de la tarde y en el momento en que estaba dándoles las buenas noches a los apóstoles. Al principio Jesús nada respondió. Entonces se produjo uno de aquellos extraños interludios, un período en el cual parecía estar en comunicación con algo que estaba fuera de él y por encima de él. Luego, levantando la vista, se dirigió al mensajero, al alcance del oído de los apóstoles, diciendo: «Esta enfermedad no es en realidad para muerte. No dudéis que será para glorificar a Dios y exaltar al Hijo».

## 167.b23.h   HINT: 5. En el Camino a Betania
"5. On the Way to Bethany"
## 167.b24.p
"On the road to Judea Jesus taught about the “⟦Q 167:5.1⟧Terms of Salvation⟦/Q⟧” and told the parable of the Pharisee and the publican. Salvation is a free gift to all who have the faith to receive sonship; there is nothing man can do to earn it. The Pharisee boasted of his righteousness, while the publican beat his breast and asked for mercy. It was the publican who went home with God’s approval."
   INL 167:5.1 | Terms of Salvation | AUTO: 
      V 167:5.1: Camino a Judea, Jesús iba seguido de un grupo de casi cincuenta amigos y enemigos. Durante el almuerzo del mediodía, el día miércoles, habló a sus apóstoles y a este grupo de seguidores sobre los «Términos de la salvación»; y al fin de esta lección relató la parábola del fariseo y el publicano (un recolector de impuestos). Dijo Jesús: «Veis pues que el Padre otorga salvación a los hijos de los hombres, y esta salvación es un don para todos los que tienen la fe necesaria para recibir la filiación en la familia divina. No hay nada que el hombre pueda hacer para merecer esta salvación. Las obras de mojigatería no compran el favor de Dios, las oraciones públicas no expían la falta de fe viviente en el corazón. Podréis engañar a los hombres con vuestro servicio exterior, pero Dios mira dentro de vuestra alma. Lo que yo os digo está bien ilustrado por dos hombres que fueron a orar al templo, el uno un fariseo, y el otro un publicano. El fariseo estuvo de pie y oró para sí mismo: ‘Dios, doy gracias porque no soy como los demás hombres, que son extorsionadores, ignorantes, injustos, adúlteros o aun como este publicano. Yo ayuno dos veces por semana; doy diezmos de todo lo que gano’. Pero el publicano, parado a la distancia, ni siquiera se atrevió a levantar los ojos al cielo, sino que golpeándose el pecho dijo, ‘Dios, ten compasión de este pecador’. Yo os digo que el publicano se fue a casa con la aprobación de Dios, más bien que el fariseo, pues el que se exalta a sí mismo será humillado, pero el que se humilla será exaltado».

## 167.b27.p
"That night at Jericho the Pharisees tried to trap Jesus over divorce. He avoided the dispute, but he exalted marriage as the most ideal and highest of all human relationships, and bitterly denounced the lax practices that let a man put away his wife for trifling reasons, pointing out their injustice to women and children. He never sanctioned any divorce practice that gave man an advantage over woman. To the apostles he taught that marriage is honorable, and that parents raising children become copartners with the Makers of heaven and earth."

## 167.b36.h
"Conclusion"
## 167.b37.p
"Paper 167 follows days almost without miracles. Jesus teaches those who compete for seats of honor to take the lowest place, and through the parable of the great supper lets each person find in his own soul what it means to refuse an invitation. He frees a woman bent over by fear, and teaches that the prayer asking for mercy reaches God, not the prayer boasting of righteousness. He exalts marriage, honors women and children, and takes the little ones in his arms. And at the end of all this teaching, knowing his beloved friend has died, he waits two days and then sets out for dangerous Judea to give Jerusalem one more chance to believe."
