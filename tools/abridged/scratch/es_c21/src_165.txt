## 165.title   HINT: Comienza la Misión de Perea
"The Perean Mission Begins"
## 165.b0.ask
"“What do those who hear the voice of the true shepherd no longer fear, and how should they live between earthly riches and heavenly treasure?”"
## 165.b1.h
"Overview"
## 165.b2.p
"Back at Pella after the visit to the feast of dedication in Paper 164, Jesus now entered the last phase of his ministry on earth. On January 3, A.D. 30, Abner, chief of the seventy, gave his associates final instructions and sent them out to all the cities and villages of Perea. This Perean mission went on for almost three months and was the last ministry of the Master."
## 165.b3.p
"During this tour the women’s corps, now sixty-two strong, took over most of the ministry to the sick, and no miracles were worked. No other part of Palestine was so thoroughly worked, and in no other region did the better classes of citizens so generally accept the Master’s teaching. Perea, about equally gentile and Jewish, was the most beautiful and picturesque province of all Palestine."
## 165.b4.p
"In this season, when people gathered for the teaching alone, without miracles, what did Jesus say about the shepherd and his sheep, about fear and trust, and about wealth and treasure?"
## 165.b6.h
"The Paper"
## 165.b7.h   HINT: 1. En el Campamento de Pella
"1. At the Pella Camp"
## 165.b8.p
"By the middle of January more than twelve hundred people were gathered at Pella. Jesus usually taught at nine in the morning, Peter and the apostles taught in the afternoon, and in the evening the Master held question-and-answer sessions with about fifty advanced disciples. By the middle of March, when he set out for Jerusalem, over four thousand people were coming each morning."
## 165.b9.p
"The Master chose to end his work on earth when interest in his message had reached its highest point in this second, nonmiraculous phase of the kingdom. Three quarters of the crowd were truth seekers, but there were also many Pharisees, doubters, and cavilers. Abner knew Perea well, for it was where John the Baptist had done most of his work, and once the mission began, Abner and the seventy never returned to the Pella camp."
## 165.b10.h   HINT: 2. El Sermón del Buen Pastor
"2. Sermon on the Good Shepherd"
## 165.b11.p
"After the feast of dedication more than three hundred Pharisees and others from Jerusalem had followed Jesus to Pella. With disciples and bitter enemies both present, he spoke in a parable so that each could take what found a reception in his heart. The true shepherd enters the fold by the door; his sheep know his voice and follow him, but they flee from a stranger."

## 165.b13.p
"Jesus said he was both the door to the Father’s sheepfold and the true shepherd. He had many other sheep not of this fold, and these words were true not only of this world; all would be brought into one brotherhood of the sons of God. No man could take away his life: he had the power to lay it down and the power to take it up again, an authority received from his Father. The next day about half of the Jerusalem teachers professed belief in Jesus."
## 165.b15.h   HINT: 3. El Sermón del Sábado en Pella
"3. Sabbath Sermon at Pella"
## 165.b16.p
"On Sabbath, January 28, before almost three thousand people, Jesus preached on “⟦Q 165:3.1⟧Trust and Spiritual Preparedness.⟦/Q⟧” He warned them against the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy, born of prejudice and nurtured in traditional bondage. They were not to fear those who can kill the body but after that have no more power. Not one sparrow, sold five for two pennies, exists without the Father’s knowledge, and they were of much more value than many sparrows."

## 165.b19.h   HINT: 4. La División de la Herencia
"4. Dividing the Inheritance"
## 165.b20.p
"A young man asked Jesus to make his brother divide their inheritance with him. Jesus answered, “⟦Q 165:4.1⟧Man, who made me a divider over you?⟦/Q⟧” Then he warned everyone to keep free from covetousness, for a man’s life consists not in the abundance of what he possesses. He told of a rich man who planned to pull down his barns and build greater ones, but that very night brigands killed him, and his heirs fought over what was left. He had laid up treasures on earth, but he was not rich toward God."

## 165.b21.p
"Asked whether it is a sin to possess honest wealth, Jesus said it is not, but it becomes sin when wealth turns into treasures that absorb one’s interests and draw the affections away from the spiritual pursuits of the kingdom. He never approved of improvidence, idleness, or neglect of one’s family; he taught that the material must be subordinated to the welfare of the soul."

## 165.b25.h   HINT: 5. Las Conversaciones con los Apóstoles Sobre la Riqueza
"5. Talks to the Apostles on Wealth"
## 165.b26.p
"That evening, at Andrew’s request, Jesus spoke to the apostles alone. Since they had forsaken everything to become ambassadors of the kingdom, his words to them had to be somewhat different. They were not to be anxious about food or clothing, but to trust the Father, who feeds the ravens and clothes the lilies more gloriously than Solomon."

## 165.b28.p
"Then, with severe trials ahead, he told them to gird up the loins of their minds and keep their lamps burning, like servants watching for their master to return from the marriage feast. For the Son of Man would depart in an hour and a manner they did not expect."
## 165.b30.h   HINT: 6. La Respuesta a la Pregunta de Pedro
"6. Answer to Peter’s Question"
## 165.b31.p
"When Peter asked whether the parable was meant for the apostles, Jesus answered that in the time of testing a man’s soul is revealed. The tried and faithful steward is set over the household, but the servant who thinks his master is delayed and mistreats his fellows will be cast out in disgrace. Much has been given to them, so much will be required."
## 165.b32.p
"Jesus said his mission would not bring peace in the material affairs of men, not for a time at least; the gospel would divide families and friends. Each believer would have great and lasting peace in his own heart, but peace on earth would come only when all were willing to enter their inheritance of sonship with God. Even so, they were to go into all the world and proclaim the gospel to every man, woman, and child."
## 165.b33.h
"Conclusion"
## 165.b34.p
"Paper 165 shows how the teaching grew deepest just where the miracles stopped. Jesus stands as the true shepherd who lays down his own life, waiting for his sheep to know his voice and follow. Those who hear that voice do not fear those who can only kill the body; they trust the Father who forgets not even one sparrow. Wealth in itself is not sin, but when it becomes master and swallows the soul, a man grows poor toward God. The Father compels no one, and to those who seek the greater thing, the lesser follows like a shadow."
