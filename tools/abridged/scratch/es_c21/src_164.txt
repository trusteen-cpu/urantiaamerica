## 164.title   HINT: En la Fiesta de la Consagración del Templo
"At the Feast of Dedication"
## 164.b0.ask
"“Why did Jesus give the leaders who rejected the light one more chance, and in whom did that light first bear fruit?”"
## 164.b1.h
"Overview"
## 164.b2.p
"While the seventy were finishing their first mission and the camp at Pella was being set up, as told in Paper 163, Jesus went up secretly to Jerusalem for the feast of the dedication, taking only Nathaniel and Thomas. Not until they crossed the Jordan did the two apostles realize where the Master was going, and they used every sort of argument to dissuade him."
## 164.b3.p
"But Jesus gave only one reply: “⟦Q 164:0.1⟧I would give these teachers in Israel another opportunity to see the light, before my hour comes.⟦/Q⟧” They lodged that evening at Jericho. At the feast of tabernacles he had sought to reach pilgrims from all over the empire; this time he went for just one purpose — to give the Sanhedrin and the Jewish leaders another chance."

## 164.b13.h   HINT: 2. En Jerusalén
"2. At Jerusalem"
## 164.b14.p
"The main event of the visit was a meeting on Friday night at the home of Nicodemus. Some twenty-five Jewish leaders who believed Jesus’ teaching were there, fourteen of them present or recent members of the Sanhedrin, including Eber, Matadormus, and Joseph of Arimathea. Both the hearers and the two apostles were amazed at the breadth and depth of the Master’s learning and insight."
## 164.b15.p
"They proposed ways to win the remaining members of the Sanhedrin and offered to go with him before the seventy. Jesus listened silently, and then told them it would only multiply the wrath upon their own heads without easing the hatred toward him; each should go about the Father’s business as the spirit led. He surmised that most of the Jewish leaders would never accept the gospel, yet he gave them all this one more chance to choose."

## 164.b31.h
"Conclusion"
## 164.b32.p
"In Paper 164 Jesus gives the teachers of Israel one more chance to see the light, though he knows they will refuse it. Yet those who first recognized that light were not the experts in the law. It was the mercy of a Samaritan who drew no line around his neighbor, and a blind beggar who had not even asked to be healed. Under the pressure of the court, Josiah’s testimony grew, and he boldly said that one thing he knew. The leaders cast him out of the synagogue, but the Master sought him out and placed him among his spiritual brethren. Light is never forced; it bears fruit in those who answer it honestly."
