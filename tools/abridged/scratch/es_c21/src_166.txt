## 166.title   HINT: La Última Visita al Norte de Perea
"Last Visit to Northern Perea"
## 166.b0.ask
"“If God’s favor is not found in outward forms and prosperity, who will enter the narrow door with thanksgiving, and how are we to understand the accidents that befall us?”"
## 166.b1.h
"Overview"
## 166.b2.p
"The Perean mission that began in Paper 165 continues. From February 11 to 20, Jesus and the twelve toured all the cities and villages of northern Perea where Abner’s associates and the women’s corps were working, and found them meeting with success. Jesus repeatedly pointed out to his apostles that the gospel could spread without the accompaniment of miracles and wonders."
## 166.b3.p
"The whole three-month mission in Perea was carried on with little help from the twelve, and from this time the gospel reflected not so much Jesus’ personality as his teachings. Yet the book explains that soon after his death and resurrection his followers departed from these instructions and began to build the early church around miraculous concepts and the glorified memories of his personality."
## 166.b4.p
"What did Jesus teach about outer and inner cleanness, about those who give thanks and those who take blessings for granted, about the narrow door and the broad way, and about accidents that strike for no apparent reason?"

"The Paper"
## 166.b7.h   HINT: 1. Los Fariseos en Ragaba
"1. The Pharisees at Ragaba"
## 166.b8.p
"On Sabbath, February 18, a wealthy Pharisee of Ragaba named Nathaniel gave a breakfast for about twenty Pharisees and invited Jesus as the guest of honor. Jesus sat down at once without going to wash his hands in the purely ceremonial way, and the host and several others whispered and sneered."
## 166.b9.p
"Jesus told them he perceived they had brought him there not to ask about the gospel but to witness an exhibition of ceremonial devotion to their own self-righteousness. They carefully cleansed the outside of the cups and platters while the inside stayed filthy; they were meticulous in tithing and ostentatious in almsgiving, yet shunned justice, spurned mercy, and rejected truth. To the lawyers he said that they laid heavy burdens on men and took away the key of knowledge — but they could not shut the doors of the kingdom of heaven."

## 166.b11.p

## 166.b12.h   HINT: 2. Los Diez Leprosos
"2. The Ten Lepers"
## 166.b13.p
"The next day, near Amathus, ten lepers called out to him from a distance for healing. Nine were Jews and one a Samaritan; their common affliction had overcome all religious prejudice. When Simon Zelotes urged the Master to pass on because of the Samaritan, Jesus said that perhaps the Samaritan would prove more grateful than the Jews."
## 166.b14.p
"All ten were healed as they went to show themselves to the priests, but only the Samaritan turned back, fell at Jesus’ feet, and gave thanks. “⟦Q 166:2.5⟧Arise and go your way; your faith has made you whole.⟦/Q⟧” The apostles all looked at Jesus — all except Simon Zelotes, whose eyes were downcast. The book adds that of the ten only four really had leprosy, and the Samaritan was one of them."

## 166.b17.h   HINT: 3. El Sermón en Gérasa
"3. The Sermon at Gerasa"
## 166.b18.p
"At Gerasa a believing Pharisee asked whether few or many would really be saved. People had been taught that only the children of Abraham would be saved, or that only a few would enter, just as only Caleb and Joshua lived to enter the promised land. Jesus granted that the old proverb about the narrow door held much truth, but he gave it a new meaning."

## 166.b21.p
"To those who refused every invitation while the door was open and then sought entrance only for selfish glory, he would say, “⟦Q 166:3.4⟧I know not whence you are.⟦/Q⟧” But every one who sincerely desires eternal life shall certainly find it, and people will come from north and south, east and west, to take the kingdom by living faith. He still stands at the doors of men’s hearts and knocks."

## 166.b22.h   HINT: 4. La Enseñanza Sobre los Accidentes
"4. Teaching about Accidents"
## 166.b23.p
"On the way to Philadelphia, Thomas asked whether spirit beings could prevent accidents. Jesus reminded him that the Son of Man had consistently refused to use the forces of heaven for his own sustenance. The old belief that prosperity was the token of divine approval and adversity the proof of God’s displeasure, he declared, was superstition."
## 166.b24.p
"The Galileans whose blood Pilate mingled with the sacrifices, and the eighteen killed when the tower of Siloam fell, were not greater sinners than others; they were simply innocent victims of one of the accidents of time. Jesus then sorted the events of life into three groups."
## 166.b25.t
[["Events of life", "Their nature"], ["1. Normal happenings", "Part of the life we live with our fellows on earth"], ["2. Accidents of nature and mischances of men", "In no way prearranged or produced by spiritual forces"], ["3. Results of natural law", "The harvest of our efforts to comply with the laws governing the world"]]
## 166.b26.p
"A fig tree that bore no fruit for three seasons yielded an abundant crop once the gardener dug around it and fertilized it, complying with the laws of fruitfulness. The Father bestows material blessings without discrimination, but in giving spiritual gifts he is limited by man’s faith and by his willingness to abide by the Father’s will. One hour of teaching will not wholly change the beliefs of a lifetime, and the apostles did not grasp this until after the resurrection."
## 166.b27.h   HINT: 5. La Congregación en Filadelfia
"5. The Congregation at Philadelphia"
## 166.b28.p
"Of all the cities of Perea, Philadelphia had the largest group who accepted the teaching of the seventy. Its synagogue had never been under the supervision of the Sanhedrin and so had never been closed to Jesus’ teaching; Abner was teaching there three times a day. This synagogue later became a Christian church, the missionary headquarters for the east, and for centuries a center of Christian learning."
## 166.b29.p
"The book also tells of Abner’s later life. He became head of the Philadelphia church, but he fell out with James and Peter over administration and the jurisdiction of the Jerusalem church, and parted with Paul over philosophy and theology. This estrangement from Jerusalem explains why nothing is heard of Abner and his work in the Gospel records."
## 166.b30.t
[["Abner and…", "What the book says"], ["James and Peter", "He broke with them over administration and the Jerusalem church’s jurisdiction"], ["Paul", "He supported Paul’s mission to the gentiles but opposed his version of Jesus’ teachings"], ["The Philadelphia believers", "They held more strictly to the religion of Jesus, as he lived and taught, than any other group"]]
## 166.b31.p
"Though he lived a life of isolation, Abner lived to be 89 and died at Philadelphia on November 21, A.D. 74. To the very end he was a faithful believer in, and teacher of, the gospel of the heavenly kingdom."
## 166.b32.h
"Conclusion"
## 166.b33.p
"As the gospel spreads without miracles, Paper 166 clears away one old misunderstanding after another about God’s favor. Washing rituals do not cleanse the inner life; prosperity is not a sign of divine approval; accidents and sickness are not God’s punishments. Thanksgiving came first from a stranger who did not take his blessing for granted. The door of life is narrow, yet wide enough for all who sincerely seek to enter, and whether few or many are saved depends only on how many answer the invitation. And there were those, like Abner, who stayed faithful to that teaching to the end, even in isolation."
