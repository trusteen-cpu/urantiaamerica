### 187.title
The Crucifixion
### 187.b0.ask
“Facing the most shameful of deaths, how could Jesus go on forgiving and caring to the very end, and say, ‘It is finished’?”
### 187.b1.h
Overview
### 187.b2.p
Handed over by Pilate, Jesus now set out for Golgotha with two brigands. Twelve soldiers went with them under a centurion, the same captain who had led the soldiers to arrest Jesus in Gethsemane the night before. The two thieves were associates of Barabbas, so Jesus was in effect crucified in the place of Barabbas.
### 187.b3.p
The paper first makes clear that Jesus accepted this death of his own free will. He had said that no one takes his life from him: he lays it down of himself, and he has authority to take it up again. Just before nine in the morning a crowd of two hundred or more followed, but most of them were either his enemies or curious onlookers.
### 187.b4.p
How, then, facing the most shameful of deaths, could Jesus go on forgiving and caring to the end, and truly say, “⟦Q 187:5.5|187:5.6|21:4.5⟧It is finished⟦/Q⟧”?
### 187.b6.h
The Paper
### 187.b7.h
1. On the Way to Golgotha
### 187.b8.p
As was the custom, Jesus carried only the crossbeam on his shoulders. The captain led the procession bearing the notice Pilate himself had written in Latin, Greek, and Aramaic: “⟦Q 187:1.2|187:2.5|172:5.13⟧Jesus of Nazareth—the King of the Jews.⟦/Q⟧” The Jewish authorities pleaded for the wording to be changed, but Pilate refused: “⟦Q 187:1.3⟧What I have written, I have written.⟦/Q⟧” Crucifixion was not a Jewish punishment; it was a dishonorable death the Romans inflicted only on slaves and subject peoples.
### 187.b9.p
To the women of Jerusalem who followed him weeping, Jesus said, “⟦Q 187:1.6⟧Daughters of Jerusalem, weep not for me,⟦/Q⟧” but for the terrible troubles that were about to come upon the city. It was against the law to show sympathy for a condemned man, so these women were truly courageous. Jesus appreciated their compassion, yet he did not want them to suffer for it.
### 187.b10.p
Jesus had had neither food nor water nor a moment of sleep since the Last Supper, and he had been scourged besides. Shortly after passing through the city gate, he fell beneath the weight of the crossbeam. The captain ordered a passerby, Simon of Cyrene, to carry it the rest of the way. Simon later became a valiant believer in the gospel, and his two sons, Alexander and Rufus, became very effective teachers of the new gospel in Africa.
### 187.b11.h
2. The Crucifixion
### 187.b12.p
The soldiers nailed Jesus’ hands and feet to the wood. The cross was not high, so he could hear every word of derision and see the faces of all who mocked him. A society of Jewish women offered drugged wine to lessen the suffering, but Jesus only tasted it and refused to drink. He chose to keep his human consciousness to the very end and to conquer death by voluntary submission to the full human experience.
### 187.b14.p
These were Jesus’ only words as they nailed him to the crossbeam. The love that had been the mainspring of his whole life of unselfish service was revealed just as it was in the hour of crisis. The soldiers divided his clothes and cast lots for his seamless tunic. The paper says it was well that this happened, for the Master wanted his followers to have nothing material to tempt them into relic worship, only the memory of a life dedicated to doing the Father’s will.

3. Those Who Saw the Crucifixion
### 187.b17.p
At about half past nine Jesus was hung upon the cross, and before eleven more than a thousand people had gathered. The unseen hosts of a universe stood in silence, gazing at the Creator dying the death of the creature. Near the cross stood believers who held their peace: his mother Mary, Ruth, Jude, John, Salome, Mary Magdalene, and others.

As noon drew near, most of the crowd went away. When Jesus said, “I thirst,” the captain of the guard raised a sponge soaked in wine to moisten his lips. Just as Jesus had lived without resort to his supernatural power, he chose to die as an ordinary mortal upon the cross.
### 187.b20.h
4. The Thief on the Cross
### 187.b21.p
When one brigand railed at Jesus, the other thief, who had many times heard the Master teach, rebuked him and said, “⟦Q 187:4.1⟧Lord, remember me when you come into your kingdom.⟦/Q⟧” Jesus answered, “⟦Q 187:4.1⟧You shall sometime be with me in Paradise.⟦/Q⟧” Watching how Jesus faced death, this thief could no longer resist the conviction that the Son of Man was indeed the Son of God.
### 187.b22.p
This young man had been drawn into robbery by those who praised it as patriotic protest, and he had looked on Barabbas as a hero. Now, on the cross beside him, he saw a true hero. And if anyone else in the jeering crowd had experienced the birth of faith and appealed to Jesus’ mercy, he would have been received with the same loving consideration.
### 187.b23.p
At noon John returned with Mary the mother of Jesus. Jesus said to her, “⟦Q 187:4.7⟧Woman, behold your son!⟦/Q⟧” and to John, “⟦Q 187:4.7⟧My son, behold your mother!⟦/Q⟧” and then asked them to leave that place. Mary spent the rest of her life at John’s home in Bethsaida, and she did not live quite one year after the death of Jesus.
### 187.b24.h
5. Last Hour on the Cross
### 187.b25.p
Shortly after noon the sky darkened with a desert sandstorm, and fewer than thirty people remained. After one o’clock, as his human consciousness began to fail, Jesus repeated in his mind the twentieth, twenty-first, and twenty-second Psalms. “⟦Q 187:5.2⟧My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?⟦/Q⟧” was only one of the passages he was reciting. He did not feel that the Father had forsaken him.

### 187.b27.p
When the Roman centurion saw how Jesus died, he smote his breast and said, “⟦Q 187:5.5⟧Truly he must have been a Son of God,⟦/Q⟧” and from that hour he began to believe in Jesus. Jesus died royally, as he had lived. ### 187.b29.h
6. After the Crucifixion
### 187.b30.p
At about half past three David Zebedee sent out the last of his messengers with news of the Master’s death. John sent the women to the home of Elijah Mark and stayed at Golgotha until Joseph and Nicodemus came with Pilate’s permission to take the body. The intelligences of a vast universe were stunned by the mortal callousness that crucified their beloved Sovereign, and so ended a day of tragedy and sorrow.

Conclusion
### 187.b32.p
Paper 187 describes the suffering of the cross in detail, but at its center is not the pain but the person. Jesus refused the wine that would have dulled his pain and remained fully human to the end, and even as he was nailed to the cross he asked forgiveness for his executioners. He received the thief who confessed his faith, entrusted his mother to John, and recited the Psalms without ever feeling forsaken by the Father. Because the love he had carried all his life came out just as it was in the hour of crisis, he died royally, as he had lived, and could truly say, “⟦Q 187:5.5|187:5.6|21:4.5⟧It is finished.⟦/Q⟧”

### 188.title
The Time of the Tomb
### 188.b0.ask
“If the cross of Jesus was not the price of atonement, what does his death show us?”
### 188.b1.h
Overview
### 188.b2.p
The body of Jesus, who died on the cross, is now carried to the tomb. The roughly thirty-six hours from three o’clock Friday afternoon to three o’clock Sunday morning are, as the paper itself says, a chapter in the earth career of Michael which is little known to us.
### 188.b3.p
The rulers of the Jews planned, as was the custom, to have the body thrown into the open burial pits of Gehenna. But Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus went to Pilate and asked for the body. Pilate would not even take the money Joseph had brought; he quickly signed the order releasing the body to them.
### 188.b4.p
This paper tells of the burial, the guarding of the tomb, and the quiet Sabbath, and then faces the meaning of the cross directly. If the death of Jesus was not the price of atonement, what does the cross show us?
### 188.b6.h
The Paper
### 188.b7.h
1. The Burial of Jesus
### 188.b8.p
When Joseph presented Pilate’s order, the representatives of the Sanhedrin raised a tumult and tried to seize the body. The centurion had four soldiers stand over it with drawn swords, drove back the mob, and then said to Joseph, “⟦Q 188:1.1⟧This body is yours to do with as you see fit.⟦/Q⟧” Since a crucified person could not be buried in a Jewish cemetery, the two men decided to lay Jesus in Joseph’s new family tomb, where no one had ever lain.
### 188.b9.p
At about half past four, Joseph, Nicodemus, John, and the Roman centurion carried the body to the tomb. They wrapped it in bandages soaked with myrrh and aloes, laid it on a shelf, and rolled the doorstone across the entrance. The women, watching from hiding, did not think the body had been properly prepared, and they agreed to return with spices on Sunday morning after the Sabbath. Aside from David Zebedee and Joseph of Arimathea, very few disciples really believed that he would rise on the third day.
### 188.b10.h
2. Safeguarding the Tomb
### 188.b11.p
If Jesus’ followers had forgotten his promise to rise on the third day, his enemies had not. Fearing that the disciples would steal the body by night and proclaim a resurrection, the Jewish leaders asked Pilate to secure the tomb.

### 188.b18.h
4. Meaning of the Death on the Cross
### 188.b19.p
Jesus did not die to atone for the racial guilt of mankind or to appease the wrath of an offended God. Even so, we must not miss the true significance of the cross. Death is the last act in the mortal drama, and Jesus desired to live a full mortal life.

### 188.b21.p
The whole idea of ransom and atonement cannot be reconciled with the loving Father Jesus taught. Moses taught the justice of a Creator God; Jesus portrayed the love and mercy of a heavenly Father. What matters is not the fact of his death but the superb manner and the matchless spirit in which he met it. Salvation rests on the realities of the fatherhood of God and the brotherhood of man.
### 188.b22.p
The paper says that the concept of atonement and sacrificial salvation is rooted in selfishness. The believer’s chief concern should not be the selfish desire for personal salvation but the unselfish urge to love and serve others as Jesus loved and served. A true believer worries less about future punishment than about present separation from God. A wise father may discipline his son, but he does so in love and for correction, never in anger or retribution.
### 188.b24.h
5. Lessons from the Cross
### 188.b25.p
The cross forever places all relations between God and man on the family basis: God is the Father, and man is his son. Jesus’ love awakens the response of love in the human heart. Mercy sets the guilt of wrongdoing to one side, but love destroys sin and all its weakness forever.

His prayer, “⟦Q 188:5.6|187:2.4⟧Father, forgive them,⟦/Q⟧” made the cross an eternal symbol of the triumph of love over hate. That love passed on to the first martyr, who said as he was stoned, “⟦Q 188:5.6⟧Lay not this sin to their charge.⟦/Q⟧” Greater than a love that lays down its life for friends was the love that laid down its life for enemies.
### 188.b28.p
So we must not view the cross with the eyes of primitive or barbarian man, who saw God as a relentless enforcer of law. No one need ever fear that the Creator does not know the nature of his afflictions. The cross was not meant to reconcile man to God, but to awaken man to the Father’s eternal love and the Son’s unending mercy.

Conclusion

Paper 188 passes through the quiet hours of the tomb and sets the meaning of the cross on new ground. Joseph and Nicodemus overcame their fear to lay the Master’s body to rest, while his enemies sealed the tomb to prevent a resurrection. The revealers honestly say what they do not know about Jesus’ state in the tomb, yet they speak plainly about the meaning of the cross. It was not a ransom paid to an angry God, but the devotion freely given by a Son who loved even his enemies. That love does not merely set sin aside; it destroys it, and it awakens in those who behold it the desire to go and do likewise.

### 189.title
The Resurrection
### 189.b0.ask

### 189.b1.h
Overview
### 189.b2.p
The quiet Sabbath in the tomb has passed, and the story now moves to the resurrection at dawn on Sunday. Soon after Jesus was buried, the archangels who have charge of the resurrection met in council on their own initiative to consider how the Master might be restored. But by midnight they had concluded that the creature could do nothing to facilitate the resurrection of the Creator.
### 189.b3.p
Jesus’ Personalized Adjuster then told the waiting celestial hosts that their Creator-father had chosen to pass through the whole experience of his mortal creatures, from birth through natural death and the morontia resurrection on into spirit existence. They would see a phase of it, he said, but they could not take part in it. From Gabriel down to the most humble cherubim, all waited in anxious expectancy.
### 189.b4.p
What, then, happened in the sealed tomb? Was the resurrection of Jesus the revival of his body, or a rising of an altogether different order?

### 196.b13.h
2. The Religion of Jesus
### 196.b14.p
You may preach a religion about Jesus, but you must live the religion of Jesus. The paper explains that at Pentecost Peter unintentionally inaugurated a new religion of the risen Christ, and that Paul later transformed it into Christianity, based on his own experience. So the New Testament is a superb Christian document, but it contains very little about Jesus’ own religious life.

### 196.b16.p
The Gospels, too, show different sides of Jesus. Mark, Matthew, and Luke retain something of the human Jesus struggling nobly to know and do the divine will; John presents the triumphant Jesus walking the earth in the full consciousness of divinity. It was a great mistake to see him as only human or only divine. He was truly both human and divine, even as he yet is. The New Testament writers’ strong belief in the Lord’s immediate return also had much to do with leaving out the purely human side of the Master.
### 196.b17.p
In one short life Jesus traversed the whole religious progression that men ordinarily complete only at the end of the long pre-Paradise career. He grew from the humble dependence that made him say, “⟦Q 196:0.9|196:2.2⟧Why do you call me good?⟦/Q⟧” to the sublime consciousness of achieved divinity. This ascent was an exclusively mortal achievement, and afterward he was still the same human Jesus. The paper says the greatest mistake was that, while the human Jesus was recognized as having a religion, the divine Christ almost overnight became a religion.
### 196.b18.p
Jesus led men to feel at home in the world and taught them that the world was not fundamentally evil. He did not share Paul’s pessimistic view of humankind; he saw most men as weak rather than wicked, more distraught than depraved, and all of them as God’s children. Many of his seemingly hard sayings were more a pledge of his own devotion than commands to others.
### 196.b19.h
3. The Supremacy of Religion
### 196.b20.p
Personal, spiritual religious experience is an efficient solvent for most human difficulties. Religion does not remove human troubles, but it does dissolve, illuminate, and transcend them. Man is educated by fact, ennobled by wisdom, and saved by religious faith.

### 196.b22.p
The progressive comprehension of reality is the equivalent of approaching God, and finding God is the experience of self-completion; to experience total reality is the full realization of God. Worship is not a futile monologue of self-deception but personal communion with the source of reality. God is not the invention of human idealism but the personality of love from whom all truth, beauty, and goodness are derived.

### 196.b25.p
The human mind does not create real values; it can only discover, recognize, interpret, and choose them. The moral values of the universe become ours through three choices: self-judgment, or moral choice; social-judgment, or ethical choice; and God-judgment, or religious choice. Morality is the essential soil of God-consciousness but not the source of religious experience. Religion enhances everything that morality recognizes and assures its survival.

### 196.b27.p
Love is the highest motivation man can use in his universe ascent, but love divested of truth, beauty, and goodness is only a sentiment. The great challenge to modern man is to achieve better communication with the divine Monitor that dwells within his mind. The paper urges us not to be discouraged: the revelation of God in and through Jesus shall not fail.
### 196.b29.h
Conclusion
### 196.b30.p
At the close of this long book, Paper 196 turns our eyes back to the faith of the human Jesus. Jesus trusted God like a child yet met every trial with the courage of a grown man, and by that faith he ascended from the human to the divine, becoming a new and living way that every mortal may walk. The paper urges us to go beyond a religion about Jesus back to the religion of Jesus. That does not mean copying his outward life, but trusting God as he trusted God and believing in men as he believed in men. When we do, religion becomes a living experience of security in the Father’s love and joy in serving our brothers.
