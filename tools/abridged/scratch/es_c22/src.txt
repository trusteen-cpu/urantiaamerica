## 168.title
The Resurrection of Lazarus
HINT: La Resurrección de Lázaro
## 168.b0.ask
“Why did the greatest work of power, raising Lazarus four days dead, both give birth to faith and hasten the death of Jesus?”
## 168.b1.h
Overview
## 168.b2.p
In Paper 167 we saw Jesus wait two more days after hearing about Lazarus and then set out for Bethany. Paper 168 begins shortly after noon on Thursday, when he reached the village. Lazarus had been dead four days and lay in the family tomb at the far end of the garden; that very morning the stone had been rolled across the entrance. The sisters had believed Jesus need only speak the curative words for their brother to recover, and they were greatly puzzled by his message that the sickness was not to the death.
## 168.b3.p
Martha went out to meet him and fell at his feet: “⟦Q 168:0.6⟧Master, if you had been here, my brother would not have died!⟦/Q⟧” Jesus answered that he was the resurrection and the life, and Martha confessed that she had long believed he was the Son of the living God. Mary too fell weeping at his feet with the same words, and his soul was moved with compassion. Yet among the mourners were some who grieved only outwardly while harboring bitter enmity toward him.

    V 168:0.6: Cuando Marta se encontró con Jesús, cayó a sus pies, exclamando: «Maestro, ¡si hubieras estado tú aquí, mi hermano no hubiera muerto!» Muchos temores pasaron por la mente de Marta, pero no expresó ninguna duda, ni tampoco se atrevió a criticar ni a poner en tela de juicio la conducta del Maestro en relación con la muerte de Lázaro. Cuando ella hubo hablado, Jesús se inclinó y, haciendo que se levantara, dijo: «Solamente ten fe, Marta, y tu hermano se levantará nuevamente». Entonces respondió Marta: «Sé que se levantará en la resurrección del último día; y aun ahora yo creo que lo que tú le pidas a Dios, nuestro Padre te lo otorgará».
## 168.b4.p
What, then, did the greatest manifestation of divine power during his life in the flesh accomplish, and why did it both bring forth faith and hasten his death?

## 169.title
Last Teaching at Pella
HINT: La Última Enseñanza en Pella
## 169.b0.ask
“If God is seeking us even while we are seeking him, how does the Father become visible in the life of Jesus?”
## 169.b1.h
Overview
## 169.b2.p
In Paper 168 we saw the raising of Lazarus and the Sanhedrin’s decision, soon afterward, to put Jesus out of the way. Paper 169 now follows Jesus through his last week at the Pella camp. Every afternoon he preached to the crowds, and every night he answered questions for the apostles and the more advanced disciples. News of the resurrection of Lazarus had already reached the camp, and nothing since the feeding of the five thousand had stirred the people so much.
## 169.b3.p
Meanwhile the Pharisees and the chief priests had begun to draw up their charges against him.
## 169.b4.p
So in this last week, with hostility closing in, what did Jesus most want to convey? And how does the heavenly Father show himself in his teaching and in his life?

## 169.b25.h
4. The Father and His Kingdom
HINT: 4. El Padre y Su Reino
## 169.b26.p
Jesus proclaimed a kingdom, yet he never once called the Father a king. He named the spiritual brotherhood of man the kingdom of heaven and its spirit head the Father in heaven, and he spoke of himself as the Son of Man and the apostles’ elder brother. Nor did he ever offer arguments to prove the Father. Everything he taught about the Father centered on this: he and the Father are one, and he who has seen the Son has seen the Father.
## 169.b27.p
So we learn about God not by leaning on Jesus’ teachings but by watching the divinity of his life. God can never be understood through teaching the mind alone; he can be known only through the realities of experience. In fact, Jesus taught only two things about God: that God in himself is spirit, and that in all his dealings with his creatures he is a Father.

Conclusion
## 169.b33.p
Paper 169 shows what Jesus most wanted to give in his last week at Pella. The stories of the lost sheep, the lost coin, and the lost son all point the same way: God seeks us before we seek him, and he embraces a returning child before the confession is even finished. ## 170.title
The Kingdom of Heaven
HINT: El Reino del Cielo
## 170.b0.ask
“Where is the kingdom of heaven that Jesus proclaimed, and why does it begin within us rather than in a distant future or a visible church?”
## 170.b1.h
Overview
## 170.b2.p
In Paper 169 we heard the parables Jesus told during his last week at Pella and his teaching about the Father. Paper 170 turns to his final sermon there, given on Saturday afternoon, March 11. It was one of the notable addresses of his public ministry, a full and complete discussion of the kingdom of heaven.
## 170.b3.p

Conclusion
## 170.b35.p
Paper 170 unfolds the kingdom of heaven layer by layer. ## 172.b1.h
Overview
## 172.b2.p
In Paper 171 we followed Jesus through Jericho on his last journey toward Jerusalem. Paper 172 begins with his arrival at Bethany on Friday afternoon, March 31, A.D. 30. So many people came every day to hear Lazarus tell of his resurrection that Jesus stayed at the home of a neighboring believer named Simon.
## 172.b3.p
Many thought Jesus would defy the Sanhedrin’s death decree, go into Jerusalem, and proclaim himself king of the Jews. But Lazarus, Martha, and Mary understood more fully that he was not that kind of king, and they dimly sensed that this might be his last visit. The chief priests chose not to seize him among his friends and waited for him to come into the city. Jesus knew all this, yet he remained majestically calm.
## 172.b4.p
Why, then, did Jesus this time allow the people to acclaim him and ride into Jerusalem on a donkey? And what did each of the twelve, who watched that day at his side, see and feel?

## 173.b7.h
1. Cleansing the Temple
HINT: 1. La Limpieza del Templo
## 173.b8.p
A huge commercial traffic had grown up around the temple worship. Worshipers could bring their own animals, but these were often rejected by the temple inspectors as having a “blemish,” so people bought their sacrifices from the temple pens, even at high prices. Most of the profit went to the ruling high-priestly families. Once the priests even demanded a week’s wages from the poor for a pair of doves worth a few pennies.
## 173.b9.p
Money-changing was a great business too. The temple tax of a half shekel had to be paid in Jewish silver, so the changers exchanged more than twenty kinds of foreign money and took a commission. The temple treasury often held upwards of ten million dollars, while the common people lived in poverty and went on paying these unjust levies.

## 173.b11.p
As Jesus was about to teach, several things struck him at once: a heated quarrel over an Alexandrian Jew who had been overcharged, the bellowing of a drove of about a hundred bullocks, and a simple Galilean being ridiculed and jostled. ## 175.title

## 175.b27.h
Conclusion
## 175.b28.p
The last temple discourse was not an outburst of anger. Jesus held out the hand of mercy to the very end, and he honestly exposed the structure of hypocrisy that pushed that hand away. What he condemned was not people but a religion that says what is good and does not do it. The Sanhedrin answered his last invitation with a sentence of death, and Judas, having heard only half the discourse, settled on leaving. But the paper speaks plainly to us who read this history: this story can never be grounds for hatred of any people, and God never withdraws his love, from Jew or from gentile. In the end Jesus’ words are addressed to religious people of every age — that is, to us.

Key Points of Paper 175
## 176.title
Tuesday Evening on Mount Olivet
HINT: El Anochecer del Martes en el Monte de los Olivos
## 176.b0.ask
“If Jerusalem is to fall and the Master is to come again, what should believers hold on to in the meantime?”
## 176.b1.h
Overview
## 176.b2.p
In Paper 175 Jesus finished his last discourse in the temple and left. Paper 176 begins on the road outside. When Matthew pointed to the massive temple buildings, Jesus said that before long not one stone would be left upon another.
## 176.b3.p
The apostles could not imagine how the temple could be destroyed unless the world itself was ending. When they had settled on the slope of Olivet under the full moon, Nathaniel asked how they would know when these things were about to happen.
## 176.b4.p
That evening Jesus spoke of two subjects, the destruction of Jerusalem and his own second coming — and it became the teaching that most confused the apostles. What was Jesus trying to keep apart, and what did he tell them to do while they waited?
## 176.b6.h
The Paper
## 176.b7.h
1. The Destruction of Jerusalem
HINT: 1. La Destrucción de Jerusalén
## 176.b8.p
Jesus warned that after he left, many would come claiming to be deliverers, and they must not be deceived. They should not be troubled by wars, famines, or earthquakes, nor by being thrown out of the synagogue and brought before judges. The spirit would teach them in that very hour what to say, and even in persecution his spirit would not desert them.
## 176.b9.p
Jesus knew that his people’s rejection of a spiritual Messiah, and their stubborn clinging to the hope of a material deliverer, would bring them into conflict with Rome and lead to the nation’s overthrow. Since Jerusalem was to be the cradle of the early gospel movement, he gave specific instructions so that its teachers would not perish in the coming revolt.

## 176.b11.p
In fact, practically all the believers fled north to Pella when the Roman troops first appeared, and so they were saved. Yet many took these predictions to be about a New Jerusalem and the end of the world. Jesus told them that they always went wrong because they tried to attach the new teaching to the old.
## 176.b12.h
2. The Master’s Second Coming
HINT: 2. El Segundo Advenimiento del Maestro
## 176.b13.p
Jesus said that his kingdom is not of this world; what was now ending would become a new beginning, from which the gospel would go out to all peoples. After he left, he would guide the kingdom through his spirit poured out upon all flesh, and he promised that he would sometime return to this world. But the time of his return is known only in the councils of Paradise; not even the angels of heaven know it.
## 176.b14.t
[["Two events", "What Jesus said"], ["The travail of Jerusalem", "It would be fulfilled before this generation passed away"], ["The coming again of the Son of Man", "No one in heaven or on earth may presume to name the time"]]
## 176.b15.p
Just as the fig tree’s new leaves tell us summer is near, Jesus said, we should discern when the long winter of material-mindedness is passing and the spiritual springtime of a new dispensation is coming. The point is not to calculate dates, but to read wisely the signs of a ripening age.
## 176.b16.p
Jesus then brought the meaning of his return down to each individual life. What the whole world must face at the end of an age, each of us will face personally when our natural life ends and we pass through the portal of death. The paper notes that this evening’s words were recorded with more confusion than any other teaching: in the second century a Jewish apocalyptic writing by one Selta was copied into the Gospel of Matthew, and the parable of the ten virgins came from it.
## 176.b18.h
3. Later Discussion at the Camp
HINT: 3. La Conversación Posterior en el Campamento
## 176.b19.p
Around the campfire Thomas asked what their attitude should be while the Master was away. Jesus answered that their connection with the kingdom is spiritual and personal — the experience of knowing by faith that one is a son of God. Nations may fall and an age may end, but nothing can be of serious concern to one who knows that his life is eternally secure in the Father.
## 176.b20.p
But this survival faith must be a living faith. Jesus warned that having once accepted sonship will not save a person who knowingly and persistently rejects the truth that he must bear the fruits of the spirit. Then he told the parable of the talents.

## 176.b22.p
In the affairs of the eternal kingdom, no one can stand still. The Father requires all his children to grow in grace and in the knowledge of truth, and whatever we do for one of the least of his brethren, we do for him. The paper says that merely preserving truth in fixed forms — “⟦Q 176:3.7⟧we have allowed no changes⟦/Q⟧” — is spiritual indolence; truth must grow by passing through personal experience. And how like every selfish mortal the one-talent servant was, blaming his own failure on someone else, often the one who least deserved it.

In the next world we will be asked to account for the gifts and stewardships of this one. Whether our talents are few or many, the reckoning will be just and merciful, but those who used their gifts only for selfish ends must accept the consequences of their choice. That night, as they went to rest, Jesus said that what they had freely received they should freely give, and that in the giving the truth would multiply.
## 176.b25.h
4. The Return of Michael
HINT: 4. El Retorno de Micael
## 176.b26.p
Of all Jesus’ teachings, none has been so misunderstood as his promise to return. Urantia is the world where Michael experienced his seventh and last bestowal, so it is natural that he would want to come back. His followers first tied his return to the fall of Jerusalem, and when he did not appear then, they tied it to the end of the age, even to the end of the world.
## 176.b27.t
[["Jesus’ two promises", "What became of them"], ["To send the Spirit of Truth", "Fulfilled on the day of Pentecost"], ["To return in person", "He did not say how, where, or when"]]
## 176.b28.p
The authors say they lean toward believing that Michael will return many times, but they confess they have not the slightest idea when or how. It is best, therefore, not to link his return to any set event or epoch. For mortals, his second coming has no more practical importance than natural death, which brings us directly into the presence of the sovereign of our universe.
## 176.b30.h
Conclusion
## 176.b31.p
The evening on Olivet was a time when two subjects became tangled and gave rise to misunderstanding. Jesus clearly separated the fall of Jerusalem, a historical event close at hand, from his own return, a promise whose time no one knows. And in both cases he turned his disciples’ eyes away from dates of the end and toward the life of today. For one whose life is hidden in the Father by faith, the upheavals of the world are no cause for fear; what remains is faithful service that multiplies the talents entrusted to us. The best way to wait for his return is to live each day ready to welcome him, whenever death may come.
