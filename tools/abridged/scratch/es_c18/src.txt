135.b3.p "John grew up as an ordinary child in a small village west of Jerusalem. The great event of his early childhood was a visit, at about six years of age, to Jesus and the family at Nazareth. His father Zacharias was a priest, his mother Elizabeth was far better educated than most women of Judea, and since John was their only child, they gave much time to his mental and spiritual training."
135.b4.p "How did this young shepherd of the wilderness become the preacher who stirred all Palestine — and what did he hold on to through the long waiting of his imprisonment?"

135.b7.h "1. John Becomes a Nazarite"
   HINT 1. Juan Se Hace Nazareo
135.b8.p "When John was fourteen, his parents took him to Engedi by the Dead Sea, the southern headquarters of the Nazarite brotherhood, and there he was solemnly inducted into the order for life. He took the same life vows as Samson and the prophet Samuel. Life Nazarites were the only persons besides the high priest ever permitted to enter the holy of holies, and the Jews regarded them with almost the same respect."

135.b10.p "At sixteen John read about Elijah and was so impressed that from that day on he always wore a hairy garment with a leather girdle. Already more than six feet tall, with his flowing hair and peculiar dress, he was indeed a picturesque youth."
135.b11.h "2. The Death of Zacharias"
   HINT 2. La Muerte de Zacarías
135.b12.p "In A.D. 12, when John was eighteen, his father died. Because the Nazarite vow forbade contact with the dead, even one’s own family, John was deeply troubled, and after the burial he went to Jerusalem to offer the sacrifices for his cleansing. That September he visited Nazareth, ready to launch out on his lifework. But Jesus’ words and example persuaded him to go home, take care of his mother, and await “⟦Q 135:2.2⟧the coming of the Father’s hour.⟦/Q⟧” The two did not meet again until the baptism in the Jordan."

135.b13.p "Because John refused the priest’s allowance due him from the temple funds, the family nearly lost its home, and mother and son moved south to Hebron. John tended sheep in the wilderness of Judea and often visited the Nazarite brotherhood at Engedi. He was so different from most of the Nazarites that he found it hard to fit in, but he was very fond of Abner, the head of the colony."
135.b14.h "3. The Life of a Shepherd"
   HINT 3. La Vida de un Pastor
135.b15.p "Along a little brook John built more than a dozen stone shelters and night corrals. With Ezda, an orphan boy he had in a way adopted, he lived very simply on mutton, goat’s milk, wild honey, and locusts. The shepherd’s life gave him plenty of time to think. He loved the writings of the prophet Daniel and read a thousand times Daniel’s description of the great image that stood for the great kingdoms of the world."

135.b18.h "4. The Death of Elizabeth"
   HINT 4. La Muerte de Elisabet
135.b19.p "In A.D. 22, when John was twenty-eight, his mother died suddenly. After the funeral he gave his flocks to the brotherhood and withdrew for a season to fast and pray. He knew only the old ways of approaching God — the ways of Elijah, Samuel, and Daniel. For two and a half years he lived at Engedi, reading over and over the last five chapters of Isaiah and the prophet Malachi."
135.b20.p "Malachi’s promise that Elijah would come before the day of the Lord held John back for more than two years. He knew he was not Elijah, and he never called himself by that name. Yet Elijah’s influence led him to attack the sins of his day directly and bluntly. He was a clear thinker, a powerful speaker, and a fiery denunciator. At last, in March of A.D. 25, he swept aside all doubts and left Engedi to begin his public preaching."
135.b21.h "5. The Kingdom of God"
   HINT 5. El Reino de Dios
135.b22.p "To understand John’s message we must know the condition of the Jewish people. For almost a hundred years Israel had been unable to explain why God’s chosen people remained subject to gentile rulers. A new school of teachers, the apocalyptists, taught that the Jews were paying the penalty for the nation’s sins and that this discipline was nearly over. To the Jews of Palestine, the “⟦Q 135:3.2|135:3.4|135:4.3⟧kingdom of heaven⟦/Q⟧” meant an absolutely righteous state in which God would rule the nations of earth as he rules in heaven."

In the fifteen months before his arrest he baptized well over one hundred thousand penitents."

135.b39.p "Among his disciples a debate arose: would the Messiah be a military leader who would strike Rome, or one who had come to set up a spiritual kingdom? 139.b2.p "In Paper 138 we saw how the twelve apostles were gathered and organized. Now Paper 139 brings each of the twelve into close view, one by one. That fact is an eloquent testimony to the charm and righteousness of his life on earth."
139.b3.p "The apostles learned about the kingdom of heaven from Jesus, and Jesus learned a great deal about human nature from them. They should not be thought of as ignorant and unlearned men. 139.b4.p "What, then, drew these twelve men, so different in temperament and background, to Jesus? And how did knowing him change them?"

139.b20.h "5. Philip the Curious"
   HINT 5. Felipe el Curioso
139.b21.p "Philip, the twenty-seven-year-old steward, always wanted to be “shown.” He was an ordinary man, lacking imagination, but methodical and thorough; for four years he provided food for everyone with hardly a lapse. Jesus never once rebuked him for the foolish questions he asked in the middle of a profound discourse, for he knew that a rebuke would keep this honest soul from ever asking freely again."
139.b22.p "Philip had the rare gift of saying not “Go” but “Come.” That is always the effective technique in all forms and phases of teaching, and even parents may learn from it. He revered Jesus’ unfailing generosity. He became the first to win souls outside the Jewish fold, and at last he was crucified and buried at Hierapolis."
139.b23.h "6. Honest Nathaniel"
   HINT 6. El Honesto Natanael
139.b24.p "Nathaniel, twenty-five, was the youngest child of a family and supported his aging parents; with Judas Iscariot he was the best educated of the twelve. His great virtue was an honesty without guile; his weakness was a pride and prejudice that judged people in advance. Yet he was not stubborn — one look at Jesus’ face, and he changed his mind."
139.b25.p "He was the philosopher and storyteller of the group, and when tension rose he eased it with flashes of humor. When Judas complained about him, Jesus answered that it is not the Father’s will that his children should partake only of the serious things of life. Nathaniel revered Jesus’ tolerance above all. He went to Mesopotamia and India to preach the gospel and died in India."
139.b26.h "7. Matthew Levi"
   HINT 7. Mateo Leví
139.b27.p "Matthew, a thirty-one-year-old customs collector of Capernaum, was the only man of means in the group. That he, a publican, had been accepted was a source of overwhelming gratitude to him, and what he valued most in Jesus was his forgiving disposition. His notes later became the basis of the Gospel of Matthew, written by Isador."
139.b28.p "He gave practically all his wealth to the work, but he gave it in the names of other believers for fear his fellows would consider the money tainted. When he was looked down upon, he bore it quietly and never revealed his generosity; the apostles died without ever learning that he had been their benefactor. Because he was among the twelve, the doors of the kingdom stayed open to multitudes of souls who had thought themselves outcasts."
139.b29.h "8. Thomas Didymus"
   HINT 8. Tomás el Dídimo
139.b30.p "Thomas, twenty-nine, a former carpenter and stone mason, was the scientist of the group — the only one of the twelve with a truly analytical mind. An unhappy childhood had left him with a suspicious and pessimistic temperament, yet the better his fellows knew him, the more they liked him, and they found him supremely honest and loyal. His presence among the twelve was a standing declaration that Jesus loved even honest doubters."
139.b31.p "In council he argued for caution, but once a decision was made, he was the first to cry, “Let’s go!” — a good loser. He revered Jesus’ superbly balanced character, at once merciful and just, strong and gentle. Thomas is the great example of a man who doubts, faces his doubts, and wins; a man who would have left at the first sign of fraud believed in Jesus to the end. He was put to death in Malta."

139.b34.h "9 and 10. James and Judas Alpheus"
139.b35.p "The Alpheus twins were ordinary fishermen of twenty-six who managed the crowds; they were the odd-job men and errand boys of the apostolic group. They hardly understood the philosophical discussions, but they rejoiced to know themselves the least of all the apostles. Seeing two men like themselves in places of honor, countless timid believers were encouraged to enter the kingdom."
139.b36.p "James loved Jesus’ simplicity, and Judas his unostentatious humility. They were loyal to the end and, after John, were among the first to believe in the resurrection, but after the crucifixion they went back to their families and their nets. Jesus welcomed these young men of one talent to positions of honor on his personal staff."
139.b37.h "11. Simon the Zealot"
   HINT 11. Simón el Zelote
139.b38.p "Simon Zelotes, twenty-eight, was a former merchant and a fiery agitator devoted to the Zealots; he took charge of the group’s recreation and play. His strength was an inspiring loyalty that could lead a hesitant person into the joy of faith in about fifteen minutes. But four years was too short a time to turn a Jewish nationalist into a spiritual internationalist."
139.b39.p "He marveled at Jesus’ calmness and his unexplainable poise. Jesus often told him that it was proper to want to see the social order improved, but that this was not the business of the kingdom of heaven; their business was to be ambassadors of a spiritual government. After the persecutions Simon collapsed for a while, but he rose again and carried the gospel into the heart of Africa, where he was buried."
139.b40.h "12. Judas Iscariot"
   HINT 12. Judas Iscariote
139.b41.p "Judas, born at Kerioth, was thirty and unmarried. His Sadducean parents had disowned him, and he was the only Judean among the twelve. He was an able treasurer and kept his trust honestly until his betrayal; money was never the motive for it. But he never truly understood himself, and there was no trait of Jesus that he especially admired."
139.b42.p "For Jesus, Judas was an adventure in faith, for it is the nature of the Sons of God to give every creature an equal chance of salvation. But when light is not honestly received and lived up to, it tends to become darkness within the soul. Judas brooded over his personal disappointments and resentments, and after being publicly rebuked over the ointment, he decided on revenge. Jesus did everything possible short of interfering with his moral freedom, and he had only pity for his betrayer."

139.b44.h "Conclusion"
139.b45.p "Paper 139 shows that Jesus did not choose perfect men. He took them just as they were — the impulsive and the doubting, a publican and a Zealot, plain fishermen. Each of the twelve was drawn to a different side of Jesus, and each was changed, little by little, by living with him. The son of thunder became the apostle of love; the doubter overcame his doubts; the least of them rejoiced in their place. Even the tragedy of Judas reminds us that the door of eternal life is open to all, but that the light must be received and lived."

141.b40.h "Conclusion"
141.b41.p "Paper 141 shows how quietly and patiently the first steps of the public work were taken. Jesus welcomed John’s disciples without criticism, took no sides in the apostles’ quarrels, and taught that one should not take anything away from a person of another faith but add to what he has. The God he presented was not a judge who records sins but a Father who loves each person; when we take the Father’s will not as our law but as our own will, we become his free children. And those children, even without thinking alike, can be one in their purpose toward the same Father."
