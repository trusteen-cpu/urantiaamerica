123.title "The Early Childhood of Jesus"
   HINT: La prime enfance de Jésus
123.b0.ask "“How did the Son of God grow up as an ordinary boy — asking, learning, and playing?”"
123.b1.h "Overview"
123.b2.p "In Paper 122 we saw Jesus born in Bethlehem and carried off to Alexandria to escape Herod. Now Paper 123 follows his early childhood, from his infancy in Alexandria to about his eighth year in Nazareth."
123.b3.p "In Alexandria Joseph worked as a foreman of laborers and began to dream of becoming a builder. Mary was the most devoted of mothers, yet she came to see that overprotection could make a child self-centered, and so she let Jesus grow up like other children. The believers of Memphis and Alexandria gave the family a copy of the Greek scriptures and urged them to stay in Egypt, but in 4 B.C. they returned to Palestine. Mary wanted to live in Bethlehem; Joseph chose Galilee for the child’s safety and education."
123.b4.p "What kind of home and school, then, did the boy Jesus grow up in at Nazareth? And what questions did he ask along the way?"
123.b6.h "The Paper"
123.b7.h "1. Back in Nazareth"
   HINT: 1. De retour à Nazareth
123.b8.p "The family quietly returned to Nazareth and, for the first time since Jesus’ birth, settled in a home of their own. Jesus was three years and two months old. He missed his friends in Alexandria, yet he was delighted to have a home of his own. Joseph and Mary agreed not to tell the people of Galilee that Jesus was a child of promise, and they faithfully kept that agreement."
123.b9.p "In his fourth year Jesus became a lifelong friend of Jacob, a neighbor boy, and his brother James was born. Joseph built a workshop near the village spring, beside the caravan resting place, and Jesus grew up there hearing the stories of caravan travelers from every corner of the earth."
123.b10.h "2. The Fifth Year (2 B.C.)"
   HINT: 2. La cinquième année (an 2 av. J.-C.)
123.b11.p "On February 11, 2 B.C., Jesus reached the age of his first wholehearted moral decision, and a Thought Adjuster came to dwell with him. This Adjuster had served before with Machiventa Melchizedek. Like countless other children, Jesus did not know that the divine Monitor had come. From this day his guardianship was entrusted to the Adjuster and to the seraphic guardians."
123.b12.p "His sister Miriam was born. From five to ten years of age Jesus was one continuous question mark. The most valuable thing in his early training was that his parents answered his questions honestly and with care. The Galilean Jews divided a child’s life into seven stages and taught their children systematically."

123.b15.h "3. Events of the Sixth Year (1 B.C.)"
   HINT: 3. Les évènements de la sixième année (l’an 1 av. J.-C.)
123.b16.p "Joseph taught Jesus Greek from the copy of the Greek scriptures given them in Alexandria. Since there were only two such copies in all Nazareth, seekers after truth were always coming to the house. Before he was six, Jesus asked his father what caused an earthquake, and Joseph answered, “⟦Q 123:3.2⟧I really do not know.⟦/Q⟧” It was the first great shock of his life. Very early, too, Jesus began to doubt explanations that blamed natural happenings on invisible spirits."
123.b17.p "After a visit from the family of his cousin John, Jesus became interested in the history and feasts of Israel, and Joseph explained the meaning of each one."

123.b19.p "That year Jesus’ prayers puzzled his parents a little. After saying the prayers he had been taught, he always insisted on having “⟦Q 123:3.6⟧just a little talk with my Father in heaven.⟦/Q⟧” Joseph widened his work as a builder, and his income tripled. Jesus went with his father to neighboring towns and watched how people lived. Joseph patiently explained the reasons behind the family’s discipline, and then Jesus gladly cooperated."
123.b20.h "4. The Seventh Year (A.D. 1)"
   HINT: 4. La septième année (l’an 1 de l’ère chrétienne)
123.b21.p "In January of this year Nazareth had its heaviest snowfall in a hundred years. Children of that time played much at weddings and funerals, but there were few organized games. Jesus had a gift for humor and play, but little chance to show it; even so, he was cheerful on the whole until he was fourteen. Mary sold pigeons, and Jesus managed the charity fund made up from what she earned."
123.b22.p "During an unusual sandstorm in July, Jesus fell down the stone stairs. The paper explains that this was no neglect on the part of midwayers or seraphim; it was an accident that could not have been avoided. Celestial personalities do not arbitrarily step into ordinary physical events. Even in special cases, midway creatures may intervene only at the command of their superiors."
123.b24.h "5. School Days in Nazareth"
   HINT: 5. Les années d’école à Nazareth
123.b25.p "At seven Jesus entered the synagogue school. He already spoke Aramaic and Greek well, and now he learned Hebrew. The pupils had no textbooks; they learned by repeating together, in unison, the sentences spoken by their teacher, the chazan."
123.b26.t [["Course", "Age", "What was studied"], ["Elementary school", "Seven to ten", "The basics of the Hebrew law"], ["Advanced school", "Ten to thirteen", "Memorizing the deeper teachings of the law"], ["Graduation", "Thirteen", "Returned to the parents as a “⟦Q 123:5.2|124:5.4⟧son of the commandment⟦/Q⟧”"]]
123.b27.p "The paper explains that Jesus was educated in three places. Nazareth was a caravan crossroads with many gentiles, and a place where the traditional law was interpreted freely. Jesus spoke Greek with caravan travelers of many peoples. The chazan said he seemed to learn more from Jesus’ questions than he had ever taught him."
123.b28.t [["Place of learning", "What he gained"], ["His home", "Moral training and spiritual culture"], ["The chazan of the synagogue", "Intellectual and theological education"], ["Mingling with his fellow men", "Equipment of mind and heart for grappling with the problems of life"]]
123.b29.p "The “birthday text” Jesus chose was a passage from Isaiah: to preach good tidings to the meek, and to proclaim liberty to the captives. On Sabbath afternoons he climbed the high hill with his father, looked out over Mount Carmel and Mount Hermon, the Jordan valley and the Mediterranean, and listened to the story of Elijah. Before he was eight he talked with the village mothers at the spring, and he learned to milk, to make cheese, and to weave."
123.b30.h "6. His Eighth Year (A.D. 2)"
   HINT: 6. Sa huitième année (an 2)
123.b31.p "Jesus was not a brilliant pupil, but he was diligent, and he stood in the upper third of his class. Each month he spent a week with an uncle who was a fisherman or one who was a farmer. He learned mathematics from a teacher from Damascus, and he traded dairy products for harp lessons, so that by eleven he was a skillful harpist. Nor did he ever stop asking awkward questions — about the dry and rainy seasons, or why the heat and cold changed as they did."
123.b32.p "That year Nahor, a rabbi teacher from Jerusalem, came and urged that Jesus be educated in Jerusalem. Mary half agreed; Joseph hesitated. The decision was left to Jesus. Two days later, after talking with his Father in heaven, he answered that he would stay at home."
123.b34.h "Conclusion"
123.b35.p "The young Jesus of Paper 123 is no wonder-working prodigy. He is a boy who asks endless questions, who falls, plays, and learns. He was surprised that his father did not know everything, he asked the meaning of every feast, and he insisted on talking with his heavenly Father as with a friend. His true education came from the love of his home, the teaching of the synagogue, and his dealings with all kinds of people at the spring and in the workshop. A Thought Adjuster quietly came to dwell in his mind, and the boy chose to grow up beside the parents who loved him. In this way the Son of God skipped not a single step of ordinary human growth."
