127.title "The Adolescent Years"
   HINT: Les années d’adolescence
127.b0.ask "“From fifteen to twenty, how did the young head of a family grow, through trial after trial, into a full-grown man?”"
127.b1.h "Overview"
127.b2.p "In Paper 126 Jesus lost his father and, at fifteen, became the head of a large family. Paper 127 follows his adolescent years from sixteen to twenty. Within a few years of his father’s death all the family property was gone, and Jesus came more and more fully to understand his prehuman existence and his purpose of revealing the Paradise Father."
127.b3.p "The paper says that no young person will ever be called to pass through harder tests than Jesus endured in those years from fifteen to twenty. Because he truly lived through adolescence, the Son of Man became a refuge that understands the troubled and perplexed young people of all worlds. Slowly but surely, through real experience, he was earning the right to be the sovereign of his universe."
127.b4.p "What, then, did this young man choose in the face of poverty, the turmoil of nationalism, a death in the family, and the love of a woman? And how did he become a man?"
127.b6.h "The Paper"
127.b7.h "1. The Sixteenth Year (A.D. 10)"
   HINT: 1. La seizième année (an 10)
127.b8.p "This year Jesus reached his full physical growth. He was calm and serious, yet kind and sympathetic, and even in the most ordinary meetings his twofold nature, human and divine, showed through. He always displayed the combination of a sympathetic friend and an authoritative teacher."
127.b9.p "His mother had told the family that Jesus was to be the deliverer of the Jewish people, so they were confused when he frankly denied such ideas. When the synagogue school would not take girls, Jesus ran a home school, holding that girls should learn just as boys did. By the end of the year he had nearly decided to raise his family and see his brothers and sisters married before beginning any public work. His mission was so unusual that no one could advise him about it, and so he spoke of it less and less."
127.b10.h "2. The Seventeenth Year (A.D. 11)"
   HINT: 2. La dix-septième année (an 11)
127.b11.p "Opposition to paying taxes to Rome stirred up unrest, and a nationalist party arose that would soon be called the Zealots. Jesus listened carefully to its organizers and asked many questions, but he refused to join. His mother, his uncle, and even his brother James urged him to enlist, and Isaac, a wealthy moneylender, offered to support the family if he would become a leader."
127.b12.p "Unable to reveal that he was more than a man, he turned for the first time to a conscious public strategy. Bravely and diplomatically, he explained that his first duty was to his family — that his widowed mother and his eight brothers and sisters needed a father’s care and guidance. A speech by James helped him through the crisis, but the division of feeling in Nazareth never healed. This became one of the chief reasons for the later move to Capernaum."

127.b15.p "During this year, by the power of his own decisions and the help of his indwelling Monitor alone, he steadily brought together his divine and human natures."
127.b16.h "3. The Eighteenth Year (A.D. 12)"
   HINT: 3. La dix-huitième année (an 12)
127.b17.p "Everything the family owned was sold except the house and the garden. Jesus took James up to the Passover and told him the history of the places along the way, just as their father had once done for him. In the temple discussions he asked no questions at all, and to the disappointed James he said only, “⟦Q 127:3.5|100:7.14|128:1.15⟧My hour has not yet come.⟦/Q⟧”"
127.b18.p "Back home he worked in the old repair shop near the caravan lot and greatly enjoyed meeting ordinary people from all over the country. In September Elizabeth and John came to visit, and the two young men agreed not to meet again until the heavenly Father called them. In December the youngest, Amos, died of a high fever, and Mary at last accepted Jesus as the true head of the family."
127.b19.p "When poverty deepened and funeral costs were added, Jesus encouraged his mother: grief did not help, but her smile could inspire the children. His steady, practical optimism was contagious, and the children looked forward to better days and grew in noble character. He could live “⟦Q 102:2.8⟧as seeing Him who is invisible.⟦/Q⟧”"
127.b20.h "4. The Nineteenth Year (A.D. 13)"
   HINT: 4. La dix-neuvième année (an 13)
127.b21.p "Jesus persuaded his mother to give up the old way of forbidding evil and instead to teach the children with positive commands to do good. All his life he gave counsel in the form “⟦Q 127:4.2⟧you shall do this,⟦/Q⟧” and he never stressed evil by forbidding it. Punishment was hardly ever needed. On the rare occasions when Jude was punished, it was only with the unanimous agreement of the older children and Jude’s own consent. His fairness and personal consideration won the love of the whole family."

127.b23.p "He liberalized the family’s customs, including the keeping of the Sabbath, and Mary agreed wholeheartedly. To pay for Jude’s schooling, he even sold his beloved harp."
127.b24.h "5. Rebecca, the Daughter of Ezra"
   HINT: 5. Rébecca, la fille d’Ezra
127.b25.p "Poor as he was, Jesus was one of the finest young men in Nazareth, and Rebecca, the eldest daughter of Ezra, a wealthy merchant, fell in love with him. Mary and Miriam tried to discourage her, but this only strengthened her resolve, and with her father’s help she invited Jesus to her seventeenth birthday party."
127.b26.p "Jesus kindly explained that no amount of money could take the place of his personal duty to raise his father’s family. Sincerely grateful for Rebecca’s admiration, he told her that he could not think of marriage until he had fulfilled his first duty to his family, and that he should not take on lifelong obligations before his destiny had been made clear. The paper says that he was truly “⟦Q 119:6.6|128:1.5⟧in all points tested.⟦/Q⟧” Rebecca moved away to Sepphoris, but she waited for him all her life. During the years of his public work she followed him devotedly, and she stood beside the cross."
127.b27.h "6. His Twentieth Year (A.D. 14)"
   HINT: 6. Sa vingtième année (an 14)
127.b28.p "From this time on, people’s affection for him took on the quality of worshipful, admiring respect. Jesus kept the Passover at Bethany with Lazarus, Martha, and Mary — without the paschal lamb. He shared unleavened bread and wine, calling them “⟦Q 127:6.7|121:5.12|130:8.3⟧the bread of life⟦/Q⟧” and “⟦Q 127:6.7|34:6.8|143:5.10⟧the water of life,⟦/Q⟧” and afterward he celebrated this sacrament every time he visited Bethany. At home, however, he went on keeping the Passover with his family “⟦Q 127:6.7⟧according to the law of Moses.⟦/Q⟧”"
127.b29.p "When his mother asked him about marriage, he answered that everything must wait for “my hour.” At twenty, Jesus was fast becoming a grown man. The paper lists, one by one, what he learned in these years."
127.b30.t [["What he learned", "In what way"], ["Responsibility", "He learned well how to bear it"], ["Disappointment", "He kept on bravely when his plans were thwarted"], ["Injustice", "He stayed fair and just even in the face of injustice"], ["Ideals and reality", "He adjusted spiritual ideals to the practical demands of earthly life"], ["Two goals", "He worked toward the near need and the distant ideal together"], ["Heaven and earth", "He lived the heavenly life within the earthly one"], ["Defeat and victory", "He turned the difficulties of time into the triumphs of eternity"]]
127.b32.p "Having put in order in his youth the knowledge he gathered as a child, he now stood on the threshold of full manhood, filled with sympathy for the weaknesses of human nature. He was ready to carry on his supreme mission: to reveal God to man and to lead man to God."
127.b33.h "Conclusion"
127.b34.p "Paper 127 shows us not grand miracles but the heavy days of an ordinary young man. Jesus turned down the fervor of nationalism and a generous offer of support. He bore the death of his youngest brother and deepening poverty. He even declined, with courtesy, the sincere love of a young woman, for the sake of his duty to his family. He passed through all these trials with practical optimism and positive teaching, learning to fit ideals to reality and to turn the difficulties of time into the triumphs of eternity. Because he lived his adolescence to the full, he became a brother who understands every troubled young person."
