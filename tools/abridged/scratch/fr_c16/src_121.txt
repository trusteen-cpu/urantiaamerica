121.title "The Times of Michael’s Bestowal"
   HINT: L’époque de l’effusion de Micaël
121.b0.ask "“Why did the Creator Son choose that very age, and that very land, in which to be born as a man?”"
121.b1.h "Overview"
121.b2.p "In Paper 120 we watched Michael lay aside all his powers and set out for Urantia. Now Paper 121 takes a wide look at the stage on which he would be born: the world of the first century. From this paper on, the life of Jesus is told by a secondary midwayer who was once attached to the apostle Andrew."
121.b3.p "The narrator tells us that Andrew refused to let copies of his own record be multiplied. He knew how careful the Master had been to leave no written record behind him, and a similar attitude among the other apostles greatly delayed the writing of the Gospels."
121.b4.p "What kind of world, then, was Jesus born into? And how did the politics, philosophy, and religion of that age both prepare the way for a new gospel and stand in its way?"
121.b6.h "The Paper"
121.b7.h "1. The Occident of the First Century after Christ"
   HINT: 1. L’Occident au premier siècle après le Christ
121.b8.p "Jesus did not come in an age of spiritual decadence. He came at a time when spiritual thinking and religious living were reviving. Greek culture and the Greek language had spread far and wide. The Jews, part Occidental and part Oriental in nature, were well fitted to carry a new religion both east and west, and the tolerant rule of Rome made this easier still. Paul — a Hebrew of the Hebrews, a Roman citizen, who preached in Greek — shows how all of these came together in one man."
121.b9.t [["Influences that bound European civilization together", "What each contributed"], ["1. Roman", "Political and social institutions"], ["2. Greek", "Language and culture, and some philosophy"], ["3. Jewish", "The rapid spread of religious and moral teaching"]]
121.b10.p "The Mediterranean world was one empire. Good roads linked its centers, and pirates had been swept from the seas. Yet under this outward peace and prosperity most people lived in poverty, and a happy middle class hardly existed yet."
121.b11.h "2. The Jewish People"
   HINT: 2. Le peuple juif
121.b12.p "Palestine was the crossroads where three continents met. Most of the caravan routes from the East passed through it on their way to the Mediterranean ports, and more than half of that traffic went right by the little town of Nazareth. Over two hundred synagogues scattered across the Roman world became the cultural centers where the new gospel would first be received."
121.b13.t [["People", "What they prepared for the new gospel"], ["The Greeks", "Language and culture"], ["The Romans", "Roads and a unified empire"], ["The scattered Jews", "Synagogues and organized religious communities"]]
121.b14.p "Even under Roman overlordship, the Jews enjoyed a good deal of self-rule and waited for the Messiah, a great deliverer. The paper explains that they credited the independence they had gained by political luck to their being a “chosen people.” This made Roman rule all the harder to bear, and it kept them from learning that their mission was spiritual, not political. In those days Galilee was more gentile than Jewish."

   HINT: 3. Parmi les Gentils
121.b16.p "Roman society was made up of five well-defined strata. The paper explains that half the population of Rome were slaves; the ablest of them could buy their freedom and rise to high places, and the early church was made up largely of the lower classes and the slaves."

121.b21.h "4. Gentile Philosophy"
   HINT: 4. La philosophie des Gentils
121.b22.p "In the minds of the nobler gentiles there was a soil of goodness in which the seed of Christianity could take root. The gentile world of that day was ruled by four philosophies, all growing out of Platonism."
121.b23.t [["School", "Teaching and influence"], ["1. The Epicurean", "The pursuit of happiness; it fought fatalism and superstition"], ["2. The Stoic", "A controlling Reason-Fate; virtue is its own reward"], ["3. The Cynic", "Man can save himself; simplicity and virtue"], ["4. The Skeptic", "Certainty is impossible — a negative outlook"]]
121.b24.p "Stoicism rose to a sublime morality that no purely human philosophy has ever surpassed. Yet it never became a religion, because its followers failed to see themselves as the children of a loving Father. The Cynics drew much from what remained of the teachings of Machiventa Melchizedek, and they prepared the common people for the later missionaries. But on the whole these philosophies belonged to the strong and the wise. They were not a religion of salvation for the poor and the weak."
121.b25.h "5. The Gentile Religions"
   HINT: 5. Les religions des Gentils
121.b26.p "The religions of earlier ages had been mainly tribal or national affairs. They did little to satisfy the personal longings of the ordinary man. In the Occident of Jesus’ day there were four kinds of religion."
121.b27.t [["Religion", "Character"], ["1. The pagan cults", "Greek and Latin mythology joined with patriotism and tradition"], ["2. Emperor worship", "Men deified and worshiped; it led to persecution"], ["3. Astrology", "A Babylonian pseudoscience turned into a religion"], ["4. The mystery religions", "New cults from the Levant that promised personal salvation"]]
121.b28.p "The mystery religions shared a myth about a god who died and returned to life, a brotherhood that crossed racial lines, elaborate ceremonies of initiation, and a promise of survival after death. The paper warns us not to confuse Jesus’ teaching with them. Still, by revealing a hunger for personal religion, these cults helped prepare the way for Jesus."
121.b29.t [["Aspect", "The mystery cults", "Paul’s Christianity"], ["Salvation", "Magical rituals and formulas", "Moral redemption and a new life"], ["Deliverance", "From sorrow and death", "From sin, and toward righteous character"], ["Foundation", "Myth", "The historic fact of Michael’s bestowal"]]
121.b30.p "To such a generation, perplexed by incomplete philosophies and complex cults, Jesus gave his gospel of personal religion."
121.b32.h "6. The Hebrew Religion"
   HINT: 6. La religion hébraïque
121.b33.p "Religious thought in Jerusalem in Jesus’ time had been strongly influenced by Greek culture and philosophy. Three languages were spoken in Palestine, and the Greek translation of the Hebrew scriptures was the decisive influence that later sent Paul’s Christianity flowing west instead of east."
121.b34.t [["Language", "Who spoke it"], ["An Aramaic dialect", "The common people"], ["Hebrew", "The priests and rabbis"], ["Greek", "The educated classes and the better strata of Jews"]]
121.b35.p "Philo of Alexandria was a great teacher who sought to harmonize Greek philosophy with Hebrew theology. The paper names him as one of seven human teachers who combined the better elements of earlier teachings: Sethard, Moses, Zoroaster, Lao-tse, Buddha, Philo, and Paul. Philo had left behind the doctrine that sin can be forgiven only by the shedding of blood. Paul’s doctrine of original sin, by contrast, came partly from Mithraism and had little in common with what Jesus taught."
121.b36.p "The scattered Jews all looked to the temple at Jerusalem, and as many as two and a half million of them came to the city for the great feasts. Eastern and Western Jews were of one mind in making Jerusalem the center of their worship and in looking for the Messiah."
121.b37.h "7. Jews and Gentiles"
   HINT: 7. Juifs et Gentils
121.b38.p "The paper explains that the Jews of that day had built a solid wall between themselves and the gentile world. They had fallen into a self-righteousness that leaned on the letter of the law and on false pride of descent. That is why they found it so hard to accept Jesus’ teaching of the spiritual brotherhood of all men. What bound them was a bondage far more real than Rome’s — the bondage of ritual and tradition."
121.b39.p "Jeremiah had spoken of a law written in men’s hearts, and Ezekiel of a new spirit living in man’s soul. But when Jewish religion became trapped in the inertia of tradition, the stream of religious evolution passed westward to the peoples of Europe. In Paul’s Antioch cult, the gospel of Jesus was mixed with other teachings."
121.b40.t [["What was blended into Paul’s Christianity", "What it brought"], ["The philosophy of the Greeks", "Ideas about eternal life"], ["The mystery cults", "Redemption and atonement through the sacrifice of a god (Mithraism)"], ["The Jewish religion", "A sturdy morality"]]
121.b41.p "The people of that time believed that spirits dwelt even in rocks and trees, and everyone took miracles as commonplace. Small wonder they were astonished at the new proclamations of the carpenter of Nazareth."
121.b42.h "8. Previous Written Records"
   HINT: 8. Écrits antérieurs
121.b43.p "The midwayer narrator had the help of the lost record of the apostle Andrew and of celestial beings, yet he chose to use the Gospels of Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John as well. The paper explains how the four Gospels came to be written."
121.b44.t [["Gospel", "Real author and background", "The Jesus it portrays"], ["Mark", "John Mark, from Peter’s memories; finished in A.D. 68", "A minister among men"], ["Matthew", "Isador, a disciple of Matthew; Pella, A.D. 71", "The son of David, the fulfillment of prophecy"], ["Luke", "Luke the physician, from Paul’s story; A.D. 82", "The friend of publicans and sinners"], ["John", "Written by Nathan in A.D. 101, at John’s urging", "The ministry in Judea and around Jerusalem"]]
121.b45.p "These writers portrayed Jesus honestly, as they had seen, remembered, and learned. Their records are imperfect, yet they were enough to change the course of Urantia history for almost two thousand years. The narrator explains that wherever possible he used human records and human concepts first, and turned to superhuman sources only when these fell short."
121.b47.h "Conclusion"
121.b48.p "Paper 121 shows that the life of Jesus did not begin in a vacuum. Rome’s roads and peace, Greece’s language and philosophy, and the synagogues of the scattered Jews had paved the road on which the new gospel would run. People looked for morality in philosophy and for a promise of salvation in the mystery cults, yet they never reached a personal religion in which they were children of a loving Father. Jesus came when that hunger was deepest. And the midwayer who tells his story humbly admits that his account gathers and weaves together the thoughts of many human minds — and so he opens the door to the life story that follows."
