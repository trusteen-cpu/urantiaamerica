126.title "The Two Crucial Years"
   HINT: Les deux années cruciales
126.b0.ask "“How did a fourteen-year-old who had lost his father carry both the burden of his family and the question of his mission?”"
126.b1.h "Overview"
126.b2.p "In Paper 125 Jesus came home to Nazareth from the temple in Jerusalem, burning with the awareness that he must begin his “Father’s business.” Paper 126 covers what followed: his fourteenth and fifteenth years, the most crucial and difficult two years of his earth life. He had begun to be self-conscious of his divinity and destiny, but he had not yet achieved much communication with his Thought Adjuster. These years were therefore a great test and a real trial."
126.b3.p "His parents did not understand their son, but they truly loved him. Jesus respected the sincere Pharisee and the honest scribe, but he despised hypocritical religious leaders. He was tempted to become the Messiah the Jews expected, yet he did not give in. The people of Nazareth expected him to become a great teacher in Israel."
126.b4.p "But just when the outlook seemed brightest, his father Joseph died. With all his plans in ruins, what did the boy hold on to? And how did he shape his sense of mission?"
126.b6.h "The Paper"
126.b7.h "1. His Fourteenth Year (A.D. 8)"
   HINT: 1. Sa quatorzième année (an 8)
126.b8.p "At fourteen Jesus made good yokes, worked skillfully with canvas and leather, and was fast becoming a carpenter and cabinetmaker. In summer he often climbed the hill northwest of Nazareth to pray and meditate, and he grew more and more aware of the nature of his bestowal. From there he looked out on Megiddo, Taanach, Dothan, Ebal and Gerizim, and he recalled the history and traditions of his father Joseph’s people."
126.b9.p "Joseph had put money aside so that Jesus could begin a long course of study in Jerusalem at fifteen. But his parents often doubted their son’s destiny. Dozens of times his mother waited for him to work a miracle, but nothing extraordinary ever happened. In those days people believed that a man of promise would prove his calling by wonders. Still, through this year it could truly be said that Jesus “⟦Q 126:1.7⟧grew in favor with man and with God,⟦/Q⟧” and the family’s future looked bright."
126.b10.h "2. The Death of Joseph"
   HINT: 2. La mort de Joseph
126.b11.p "On September 25, while working on the governor’s residence at Sepphoris, Joseph was badly hurt when a derrick fell, and he died before Mary could reach him. The carpenter boy, just past fourteen, now took on, along with his heavenly Father’s mission, the care of his widowed mother, seven brothers and sisters, and one more child yet unborn. The paper explains that this heavy but highly educational responsibility was permitted."
126.b12.p "Jesus accepted this responsibility willingly and carried it out faithfully to the end. He would not now study under the rabbis in Jerusalem, and he never received from any human source his authority to teach truth. He measured every institution and custom by the same test, and he devoted his life to two purposes: caring for his family and preparing to do his Father’s will. He was generous yet frugal, saving yet liberal."

126.b14.h "3. The Fifteenth Year (A.D. 9)"
   HINT: 3. La quinzième année (an 9)
126.b15.p "This year Jesus took firm charge of the family’s affairs, but their savings were nearly gone. In April the youngest child, Ruth, was born, and for almost twenty years Jesus cared for her more tenderly than anyone. He tried to teach the younger children to pray in their own words, and when this did not go as he hoped, one October evening he wrote out a standard family prayer in charcoal on a smooth cedar board. This was the first form of the prayer later known as “⟦Q 126:3.3⟧the Lord’s Prayer.⟦/Q⟧”"
126.b16.p "He rightly reasoned that supporting his family was his first duty. And after thoroughly studying the Jewish ideas about the Messiah, he became sure that he was not that Messiah and would never sit on David’s throne. Reading “⟦Q 126:3.8|136:1.6⟧The Book of Enoch⟦/Q⟧” in the synagogue library, he was deeply moved by the term “⟦Q 126:3.6|126:3.8|0:5.3⟧Son of Man,⟦/Q⟧” and he decided then and there to take it as his title when he began his work."
126.b17.t [["Question", "Jesus’ conclusion"], ["The throne of David", "He would never sit on it"], ["A deliverer leading Jewish armies", "Never to be expected of him"], ["A teacher for the Jews alone", "Not his mission"], ["The “⟦Q 126:3.6|126:3.8|0:5.3⟧Son of Man⟦/Q⟧” in the Book of Enoch", "Nearest the truth — adopted as his title"]]
126.b19.p "Yet confusion remained. Was he a Jew? Was he of the house of David? Could his mother be right? What should be his relation to his family, to the Jewish religion, to Rome, and to the gentiles? He turned these questions over as he worked at the carpenter’s bench. Little by little he learned not to reveal all his thoughts, and though he longed for a friend who would understand him, he carried his burdens alone."
126.b20.h "4. First Sermon in the Synagogue"
   HINT: 4. Premier sermon dans la synagogue
126.b21.p "On the first Sabbath after his fifteenth birthday, Jesus conducted the morning service of the synagogue for the first time. He read passages of Scripture that he had chosen himself. The villagers had never seen him so solemn, so earnest, so manly and authoritative."

126.b23.p "That afternoon he climbed the hill with James. When they came back, he wrote out the Ten Commandments in Greek on two boards. Martha painted them, and for a long time they hung on the wall above James’s workbench."
126.b24.h "5. The Financial Struggle"
   HINT: 5. La lutte financière
126.b25.p "The family slipped back more and more into simple living, and a carpenter’s wages fell to about twenty-five cents a day. When the tax collector threatened to take his harp, Jesus gave his copy of the Greek scriptures to the synagogue library as his coming-of-age offering. The hardest blow came at Sepphoris, when Herod ruled that nothing was owed to Joseph. After this unjust decision Jesus never again trusted Herod Antipas."
126.b26.p "All his plans seemed thwarted, yet he did not falter or lose heart. Day by day he faithfully met the duties in front of him. The hard experience of supporting his family protected him from drifting into idle meditation or mysticism. He rented land and gave each of his brothers and sisters a garden plot, adapting himself to reality and doing his best to meet his family’s hopes. With the end of his fifteenth year, his growth in body and mind came to a close, and the real career of this young man of Nazareth began."

126.b28.h "Conclusion"
126.b29.p "Paper 126 shows how a young man passed through the two hardest years of his life. His father’s death closed the road to study in Jerusalem and placed the family’s living on his shoulders, but Jesus accepted that burden willingly and faithfully met each day’s duties. At the same time, beside his workbench, he thought deeply about his mission, turned away from the path of a political Messiah, and chose the name “⟦Q 126:3.6|126:3.8|0:5.3⟧Son of Man.⟦/Q⟧” With no one to understand him, he carried his burdens alone — yet he did not waver. That is why his life is a comfort to every disappointed idealist."
