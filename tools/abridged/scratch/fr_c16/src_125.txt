125.title "Jesus at Jerusalem"
   HINT: Jésus à Jérusalem
125.b0.ask "“What disappointed the twelve-year-old Jesus when he first saw the temple at Jerusalem, and what did he resolve?”"
125.b1.h "Overview"
125.b2.p "In the previous paper we saw the later childhood of the boy Jesus as he grew, learned, and worked in Nazareth. Now Paper 125 follows the first visit to Jerusalem that he could remember. No event in all his earth life was more fascinating or more humanly thrilling, and the days of the week after the Passover were his first taste of complete freedom from responsibility."
125.b3.p "Women were not required to go up to the Passover, but Jesus said he would not go unless his mother went too. Yet as soon as he passed through the temple gates, his disillusionment began. His mother had to go off to the women’s gallery, the ceremony of consecration was a mere formality, and the explanations of the rituals spoke of the wrath of God. The boy told Joseph that the heavenly Father could not love his children less than his earthly father loved him — and his parents never again tried to change his mind."
125.b4.p "What, then, troubled this twelve-year-old in the temple? What did he ask the teachers over four days? And with what resolve did he go home?"
125.b6.h "The Paper"
125.b7.h "1. Jesus Views the Temple"
   HINT: 1. Jésus visite le temple
125.b8.p "Jesus was shocked by the spirit of irreverence he saw in the temple courts. In the court of the gentiles the money-changers and the sellers of sacrificial animals, the shouting and cursing, and the bleating of sheep were all mixed together, and painted courtesans walked about openly. He admired the feeling and ceremony of the temple, but in the faces of thoughtless worshipers he saw spiritual ugliness."
125.b9.p "In the court of the priests, the slaughter of droves of animals and the washing away of their blood were more than this nature-loving boy could bear. He clutched his father’s arm and begged to be taken away. Joseph wisely led him to the Gate Beautiful and explained the feasts at which Galileans attended temple worship three times a year."

125.b11.h "2. Jesus and the Passover"
   HINT: 2. Jésus et la Pâque
125.b12.p "Five families from Nazareth kept the Passover at the home of Simon in Bethany. As a new son of the covenant, Jesus was asked to tell the origin of the Passover. He did it well, yet he quietly worked in his impressions of what he had lately seen and heard, which embarrassed his parents. He was sure that his heavenly Father took no pleasure in the sight of sacrifices, and he became more and more determined someday to establish a bloodless celebration of the Passover."
125.b13.p "That night the boy hardly slept, troubled by dreams of slaughter. For the rest of the week he went about Jerusalem with Lazarus and spent most of his time at the teaching conferences near the temple. After a night spent in Bethany, Lazarus, Martha, and Mary came to love him like a brother. He also talked with more than one hundred and fifty boys of his age, learned how people lived in faraway lands, and began to long to travel all over the world."
125.b14.p "Several times his parents found their son alone, his head in his hands, deep in thought, and they did not know what to do. Before they left, they arranged for Jesus to come back at fifteen to study at the most famous rabbinical academy, but Jesus seemed indifferent."
125.b15.h "3. Departure of Joseph and Mary"
   HINT: 3. Le départ de Joseph et de Marie
125.b16.p "On the first day of the week after the Passover, the Nazareth company set out, the men and the women traveling in separate groups, as was the custom. Jesus was so absorbed in a temple discussion about angels that he did not notice the time to leave had passed. Each parent supposed he was with the other, and they did not discover he was missing until they reached Jericho."

125.b18.h "4. First and Second Days in the Temple"
   HINT: 4. Premier et deuxième jours dans le temple
125.b19.p "On the first day Jesus only listened to the discussions in the quieted temple. Then he went back to Bethany and meditated alone in the garden. The next morning, from the brow of Olivet, he looked out over a people spiritually poor, bound by tradition, and watched over by Roman legions — and he wept."
125.b20.p "On the second day he joined boldly in the discussions with questions of his own, but always in a manner fitting his age. He asked whether it was just to put to death a drunken gentile who had wandered by mistake into the forbidden area. A narrow-minded teacher questioned his age and sneered that he was from Nazareth. But the leader insisted that the boy was not to blame, and Jesus was allowed to stay on as a student in the discussions."
125.b21.h "5. The Third Day in the Temple"
   HINT: 5. Le troisième jour dans le temple
125.b22.p "On the third day a large crowd gathered, drawn by reports of a boy who was confounding the wise men of the law. Joseph and Mary went into the temple several times, but it never occurred to them to look among the discussion groups. Before the day was over, the attention of all the main groups had turned to Jesus’ questions."

125.b24.p "For more than four hours he taught by asking questions. He blended wisdom with humor, he was always fair, and he never took unfair advantage of anyone. He was free of any wish to win an argument for the sake of logical triumph. He cared only to proclaim truth and so reveal God more fully."
125.b26.p "That night he stayed late in the garden at Bethany, wrestling with how he might reveal a more beautiful idea of the heavenly Father to his spiritually blind countrymen and set them free from the bondage of tradition. But no clear light came."
125.b27.h "6. The Fourth Day in the Temple"
   HINT: 6. Le quatrième jour dans le temple
125.b28.p "On the afternoon of the fourth day, the leader seated the boy beside him and asked him to give his views on prayer and worship. Just then his parents, passing through the temple court, recognized their son’s voice. Mary scolded him in front of everyone, and her rebuke brought one of the greatest opportunities of his young life to an awkward end."
125.b29.p "After a moment’s thought, Jesus answered: since it was time for him to be about his Father’s business, should they not have expected to find him in his Father’s house? Then, when only his parents were left, he eased their distress by saying that no one had done anything but what he thought best. On the brow of Olivet he cried out, trembling, that he would return to cleanse the temple and deliver his people from their bondage."
125.b31.p "He tried to conform even when he could not agree, and he was an artist at fitting his devotion to duty to the obligations of family loyalty and social service. Mary, for her part, took his words on Olivet as a prophecy of a Messianic mission as Israel’s deliverer, and she began to try to steer her son’s thinking in patriotic and nationalistic directions."
125.b32.h "Conclusion"
125.b33.p "Paper 125 shows the disillusionment and the resolve of a boy facing the realities of religion for the first time. Jesus was deeply troubled by the trading and the blood sacrifices in the temple and by the separation his mother had to endure. Yet he did not try to win by criticizing; he taught by asking questions. At the root of all his questions lay one conviction: the heavenly Father could not love less than an earthly father. And though he burned with longing for his mission, he chose to go home, obey his earthly father, and wait for his hour."
