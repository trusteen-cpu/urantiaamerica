177.title "Wednesday, the Rest Day"
   HINT El Miércoles, Día de Descanso
177.b0.ask "“On a quiet Wednesday before the storm, while Jesus spent the day in the hills with a boy, why did Judas go to the house of the high priest?”"
177.b1.h "Overview"
177.b2.p "In Paper 176 Jesus spoke on the Mount of Olives about the destruction of Jerusalem and his second coming. The Wednesday of Paper 177 is a day of rest that follows all that tension. Jesus asked his apostles to rest, to think over everything that had happened since they came to Jerusalem, and to meditate on what lay just ahead."
177.b3.p "He told them not to go within the gates of Jerusalem, and then he made ready to go into the hills alone. When David Zebedee wanted to send three armed men with him, Jesus declined. No man, he said, would lay hands on him until the hour when he was ready to lay down his life in conformity to his Father’s will."
177.b4.p "Yet one boy, John Mark, holding a lunch basket, followed him all the same. On the same day, below the same hill, two lives moved in completely different directions. One walked with God; the other walked to the house of the high priest. What was it that set the two apart?"
177.b6.h "The Paper"
177.b7.h "1. One Day Alone with God"
   HINT 1. Un Día a Solas con Dios
177.b8.p "John Mark would not let go of the basket. The Master might set it down while he turned aside to pray and go on without it, he said, so it would be better if he came along to carry it. Jesus did not refuse a boy who longed with all his heart to go with him. He invited him to come and to ask any question that arose in his heart."
177.b9.p "Jesus spent his last day of quiet on earth with this truth-hungry youth and in talk with his Paradise Father. On high this event became known as “⟦Q 177:1.3⟧the day which a young man spent with God in the hills.⟦/Q⟧” It shows forever that the Creator is willing to fellowship the creature. Jesus assured John that he would live to become a mighty messenger of the kingdom, and John never revealed to anyone what passed between them that day."

177.b10.p "From then on, through the few remaining hours of Jesus’ earth life, John Mark never let the Master out of his sight for long. He was always hiding somewhere near, and he slept only when Jesus slept. The same heart that would not let go of a basket held on to the very end."
177.b11.h "2. Early Home Life"
   HINT 2. La Vida Hogareña de los Primeros Años
177.b12.p "When John asked how he could be sure he would become a messenger of the kingdom, Jesus answered that he could depend on John’s faith and love because they were grounded in his early training at home. John’s parents bore each other a sincere affection, and they had wisdom as well as love. By contrast, the parents of John’s friend Amos, who had also wanted to come along, loved their son so much that they denied him that same experience."

177.b15.p "A child draws all his early ideas — intellectual, social, moral, and even spiritual — from his parents and his home. His first impressions of the universe come from his mother’s care, and his first ideas of the heavenly Father come from his earthly father. The book says that a gospel founded on the father-child relationship can hardly win worldwide acceptance until home life embraces more of love and more of wisdom. Modern homes have more freedom, but that freedom is not restrained by love, motivated by loyalty, or directed by wisdom."
177.b16.h "3. The Day at Camp"
   HINT 3. El Día en el Campamento
177.b17.p "The apostles spent the day walking about the mountain and visiting the disciples, but by afternoon they missed the Master badly. Except for Judas, every one of them secretly wished he had been in John Mark’s place. Nathaniel said that their trouble was being only halfhearted: if they had all wanted to go with Jesus as much as the boy did, the Master would surely have taken them all."
177.b18.p "Meanwhile David Zebedee, convinced that the rulers would kill Jesus, had quietly sent for Mary the mother of Jesus and all his family to come to Jerusalem. More than twenty Greeks also came to the camp, and had it not greatly disturbed his apostles, Jesus would have ordained them as he had the seventy. In the city, the rulers were puzzled that Jesus did not come back to the temple, and they were busy perfecting their plot."
177.b19.h "4. Judas and the Chief Priests"
   HINT 4. Judas y los Altos Sacerdotes
177.b20.p "Soon after Jesus left for the hills, Judas disobeyed the Master’s request and hurried to the house of Caiaphas the high priest. The day before, he had told some Sadducean relatives and friends that Jesus was a well-meaning dreamer but not the deliverer of Israel, and that he wanted a way to withdraw gracefully. They flattered him with promises of high honors from the Sanhedrin."
177.b21.p "It was not money that moved Judas. The book describes what had piled up in his subconscious mind: a pride that could not endure being identified with a movement of defeat, jealousy of Peter, James, and John, and an old resentment that Jesus had not saved John the Baptist. Presented to Caiaphas by his cousin, Judas asked what they would give him for his service. Caiaphas, barely hiding his disdain, told him to arrange the arrest with the captain of the guard, and they settled on Thursday night."

177.b23.p "The other apostles had craved the same worldly honor in their minds, but with their hearts they loved Jesus and the truth. Judas was different: he grew to love that desire with his whole heart. Jesus loved and trusted Judas just as he loved and trusted the others. But through disappointment, jealousy, and long-continued resentment, even a love that was once genuine can turn into actual hate."
177.b24.p "The book judges that, in its last conscious motive, Judas’s betrayal was the cowardly act of a selfish deserter who thought only of his own safety and glory. He considered himself clever for trading his lost hopes in a new kingdom for honor in the old order. And the chief priests now had an ally who would make sure Jesus could not slip from their grasp, with no need to arrest him in public."
177.b26.h "5. The Last Social Hour"
   HINT 5. La Última Hora Social
177.b27.p "At the Wednesday evening social hour Jesus tried to cheer his apostles, but they could not lift their spirits. He warned them not to trust the support of the crowds. Those who know the gospel only in the mind and have not experienced it in the heart cannot be depended upon when real trouble comes, and some who had been very close had already made up their minds to desert."

177.b29.p "This Wednesday evening was the low-tide mark of the apostles’ spiritual life up to the hour of the Master’s death. Just before midnight, knowing this was the last night he would sleep through with his chosen family, Jesus sent them to rest. Tomorrow, he said, would be one more day to do the Father’s will and to know the joy of being his sons."
177.b30.h "Conclusion"
177.b31.p "The Wednesday of rest sets two roads side by side. A boy who would not let go of a basket spent a whole day alone with God because of the wholehearted longing of his heart, and Jesus reminded him that his steady faith had grown in a home where love prevailed and wisdom reigned. At the same hour Judas, cut off from his brethren, was led by long-stored resentment, jealousy, and the craving for honor to the house of the high priest. What separated the two was not talent or opportunity but what each heart chose to love. And even on the night the betrayal was settled, Jesus told his apostles to meet the next day as a day of joy in doing the Father’s will."

178.title "Last Day at the Camp"
   HINT El Último Día en el Campamento
178.b0.ask "“How should a son of the heavenly kingdom live as a citizen of the nations of this world?”"
178.b1.h "Overview"
178.b2.p "On the Wednesday night of Paper 177, Jesus sent his apostles to sleep, telling them that the next day would be one more day of joy in doing the Father’s will. Paper 178 is that Thursday — his last free day on earth as a divine Son incarnated in the flesh."
178.b3.p "After breakfast Jesus led his apostles and his chosen disciples, Jews and gentiles alike, to a secluded spot above the camp and gave them his farewell address. Judas was not there; he did not return until midafternoon. The subject of the talk was a surprising one: the relation of sonship with God to citizenship in earthly governments."
178.b4.p "Why would a man facing death the next day speak about politics, religion, and the duties of a citizen? And how, that same afternoon, did Jesus make sure his last supper would not be disturbed?"
178.b6.h "The Paper"
178.b7.h "1. Discourse on Sonship and Citizenship"
   HINT 1. La Disertación Sobre la Filiación y la Ciudadanía
178.b8.p "For almost two hours Jesus talked with about fifty of his followers and answered a score of questions. The kingdoms of this world are material, and they may need physical force to keep order. But the kingdom of heaven is a spiritual brotherhood of the spirit-born sons of God, and it may be spread only by the power of the spirit. There is no conflict between sonship and citizenship. Only when a Caesar tries to take God’s place and demands worship must the believer worship God alone."
178.b9.t [["The duties of a son of the kingdom", "What they mean"], ["Duty to man", "Being a faithful citizen of the earthly kingdom"], ["Duty to God", "Spiritual loyalty and worship"], ["Service to the brotherhood of believers", "A third and sacred obligation, freely taken on"]]
178.b10.p "Sonship in the kingdom should make us better citizens, because brotherhood and service are the cornerstones of the gospel. Loving service is the social lever that lifts the races of darkness, and the Spirit of Truth is its fulcrum. If an unbeliever can be a superior civil servant, the believer should ask himself whether the roots of truth in his heart have not died."
178.b11.t [["What not to do", "What to do"], ["Give spiritual worship to earthly rulers", "Worship God alone, and seek to enlighten the rulers"], ["Use government power to advance the kingdom", "Spread it by spirit power and loving service"], ["Impose truth and righteousness by law", "Persuade men’s minds, but never compel them"], ["Let humanitarian work replace the gospel", "Let service overflow as a by-product of the gospel"], ["Attack the old ways", "Put the leaven of new truth into the old beliefs"]]
178.b12.p "Jesus foresaw that as long as rulers acted like religious dictators, believers would face persecution. Yet that very suffering would in time bring the gradual divorce of politics and religion, and religious liberty for all nations. The greater danger, he warned, would come later, in times of prosperity, when many in high places would accept the gospel in name only."
178.b13.p "Believers are not to be passive mystics, colorless ascetics, or dreamers who trust a fictitious Providence for everything. They should be gentle and patient with people, yet valiant and mighty in proclaiming the truth. The gospel must grow like a living seed, taking new forms in each generation; it must never become merely a sacred memory or tradition."
178.b15.p "Jesus reminded them that they had never directly attacked the persons or authority of those who sit in Moses’ seat. They had only offered them new light, and they clashed with them only when those leaders blocked the preaching of the gospel. So the disciples were to preach only the good news, let controversy come only when others forced it on them, and stand firmly in defense of the truth when a willful unbeliever attacked. Beneath all this lies the great law of human fairness: whatever you would that men should do to you, do even so to them."
178.b16.p "Finally Jesus urged them to love one another, not to strive with men, even with unbelievers, and to be loyal citizens, upright artisans, praiseworthy neighbors, and understanding parents."
178.b17.h "2. After the Noontime Meal"
   HINT 2. Después del Almuerzo
178.b18.p "The Greeks understood this address best. Even the apostles could not reconcile the coming end of his earthly ministry with his talk of gospel work stretching far into the future. David Zebedee, who knew all about Judas’s plot through his secret agents, began to ask the Master about it, but Jesus raised his hand and stopped him. He knew all about it, he said; David was to tell no one, and he was not to doubt that the will of God would prevail in the end. To Abner in Philadelphia, Jesus sent word to go on with his work."
178.b19.p "By noon everyone had heard that Lazarus had fled in haste from Bethany. Only then did the apostles and disciples begin to sense how grimly determined the rulers were to destroy Jesus and his teachings."
178.b20.p "When Philip asked about preparing for the Passover, Jesus called Peter and John as well and gave them private directions. At the city gate they would meet a man carrying a water pitcher; they were to follow him, and they would be shown an upper room made ready. The house was John Mark’s, and this had been arranged between Jesus and the boy in the hills the day before."

178.b23.h "3. On the Way to the Supper"
   HINT 3. Camino a la Cena
178.b24.p "Jesus did not want to pass through the city until after sunset, so he had the apostles sit down at the place where he had spoken the evening before about the destruction of Jerusalem. As the Father had sent him, he said, so he would send them out to finish the work he had begun. He warned them not to throw away their lives in some foolish attempt to defend the Son of Man. He needed no defense by the hand of man, for he was determined to do the will of his Father."
178.b25.p "Even when they saw the city destroyed, they were to remember that they had already entered upon the eternal life of endless service in the ever-advancing kingdom of heaven. In the Father’s universe there are many abodes, and those who enter by faith and remain by living service will surely ascend to the worlds on high. Then Jesus set out on the last journey he would ever make with all twelve. No one in the crowds recognized them, and the apostles did not know that one of their own had already plotted to betray him."
178.b27.h "Conclusion"
178.b28.p "On his last free day on earth, Jesus left his followers a way to live in the world after he was gone. A son of the heavenly kingdom does not turn his back on the world; he becomes a better citizen, and he spreads truth not by power or force but by loving service and the power of the spirit. The gospel is not a fixed tradition but a living seed that grows anew in every generation. And on that afternoon, as the shadow of betrayal deepened, Jesus quietly protected his last supper and gave himself to the Father’s will. He was now ready to send his disciples into the world, just as the Father had sent him."
