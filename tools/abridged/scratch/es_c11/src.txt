## 78.title [HINT: La Raza Violeta Después de los Días de Adán]
"The Violet Race after the Days of Adam"
## 78.b0.ask
"“How did the second garden, the cradle of civilization for thirty thousand years, enrich the whole world while itself disappearing?”"

"Overview"

"In Paper 77 we heard about the origin of the midwayers, along with the stories of the Nodites and the Adamsonites. Paper 78 now follows the long history of Adam’s descendants who stayed in the second garden — the violet race. The second garden was the cradle of civilization for almost thirty thousand years, and as the Adamites blended with the Nodite and Sangik tribes, they came to be known as the Andites."

"The paper covers the time from just after Adam’s default, about 35,000 B.C., through the forming of the Andites about 15,000 B.C., to their final disappearance from their Mesopotamian homeland about 2000 B.C."

## 78.b12.p
"For thousands of years Adam’s descendants worked at irrigation and flood control in the south, strengthened their defenses in the north, and tried to keep the traditions of the first Eden alive. Adam left behind a great intellectual and spiritual culture but no advance in mechanical devices, for every civilization is limited by natural resources, inborn genius, and enough leisure for invention. By about 19,000 B.C. the Adamites numbered four and a half million."

One year, unusually heavy rains swept away the whole village; only Noah and his family were saved in their houseboat. With these floods the second garden was no more."

## 79.title [HINT: La Expansión Andita en el Oriente]
"Andite Expansion in the Orient"
## 79.b0.ask
"“What did the Andites who went east leave to India and China, and why did those two civilizations fall short of the heights they once promised?”"
## 79.b1.h
"Overview"
## 79.b2.p
"In Paper 78 we saw how the culture of the second garden spread across the world through the Andites while their Mesopotamian homeland disappeared. Paper 79 turns our gaze eastward. Asia is the homeland of the human race. Andon and Fonta were born on this continent, the Sangik peoples arose here, and southwestern Asia saw the successive civilizations of the Dalamatians, Nodites, Adamites, and Andites."
## 79.b3.p
"This paper begins with the Andites of Turkestan and then follows the two Andite waves into India, the long struggle between the red and yellow races in eastern Asia, and the dawn and growth of Chinese civilization."
## 79.b4.p
"Why, then, did India and China, which received the same Andite inheritance, go such different ways? And what made the difference?"
## 79.b6.h
"The Paper"
## 79.b7.h [HINT: 1. Los Anditas del Turquestán]
"1. The Andites of Turkestan"
## 79.b8.p
"For more than twenty-five thousand years, down to about 2000 B.C., the heart of Eurasia was mainly Andite. From Turkestan they moved into Europe, into China by way of Sinkiang and Tibet, and into India through the Punjab and Baluchistan. These early migrations were not conquests but a continual drifting. The Tarim valley in Sinkiang was the easternmost outpost of true Andite culture; in its day it rivaled Mesopotamia itself, but it perished when the rain winds shifted."
## 79.b9.p
"From about 8000 B.C., as central Asia grew drier, the Andites crowded into the river valleys and onto the seashores, and a new class of men — the traders — appeared in large numbers. Instead of going back to herding, they gathered in cities of manufacture and trade, and Adonia, near present-day Ashkhabad, became the commercial metropolis of central Asia. The last great display of the Andites’ military genius came in A.D. 1200 with the Mongols under Genghis Khan, who also proclaimed “⟦Q 79:1.9⟧one God in heaven.⟦/Q⟧”"
## 79.b10.h [HINT: 2. La Conquista Andita de la India]
"2. The Andite Conquest of India"
## 79.b11.p
"India is the only place where all the Urantia races were blended. Lying close to where the Sangik races arose, it drew many peoples from early times, and by 20,000 B.C. western India already carried Adamic blood. About 15,000 B.C. population pressure in Turkestan and Iran set off the first large Andite movement into India; for over fifteen centuries they poured in through Baluchistan and spread over the Indus and Ganges valleys."
## 79.b12.p
"The book finds the main reason India failed to lead Eurasia in its geography: pressure from the north only crowded people southward into the Deccan, surrounded on all sides by the sea. The Andite conquerors tried to keep their identity through strict marriage rules, but by 10,000 B.C. they had been absorbed — though the whole population was markedly improved by this blending. The book explains that race mixture favors a versatile culture, yet depending on the makeup of the stock, its gains may not last."
## 79.b13.h [HINT: 3. La India Dravidiana]
"3. Dravidian India"
## 79.b14.p
"The Andite conquerors mixed with the native peoples to become the Dravidians. Even a small amount of Adam’s blood greatly sped up social development and at once produced the most versatile civilization then on earth. The thread of monotheism in India’s religious history comes from the teachings of the second garden. As early as 16,000 B.C. a hundred Sethite priests entered India, but within five thousand years their doctrine of the Paradise Trinity had degenerated into the triune symbol of the fire god."
## 79.b15.p
"The Dravidians were among the earliest city builders. By 7000 B.C. camel trains traveled regularly to Mesopotamia, ships sailed to the Sumerian cities of the Persian Gulf and as far as the East Indies, and an alphabet and the art of writing were brought in from Sumeria. The civilization they built spread over all India and survives to this day in the Deccan."

"Overview"

"According to the book, the modern white peoples carry surviving strains of the Adamic stock mixed with the Sangik races, especially the blue, along with a considerable share of the original Andonite stock and still more of the early Nodite strains. "1. The Adamites Enter Europe"
## 80.b8.p
"Even before the last Andites were driven out of the Euphrates valley, many of their kin had entered Europe as adventurers, teachers, traders, and warriors. "Paper 85 lays out the long list of what man has worshiped: first stones, then hills, trees, and animals, water and fire, the sun, moon, and stars, and finally himself. At the root lay fear and illusion before powers he did not understand, and traces of it remain in our tombstones, Christmas trees, and candles. Yet the book says that all this primitive worship was not mere accident. From the beginning the spirit of worship was awakening the urge to worship within the human mind, and when the spirit of wisdom guides that urge, man at last begins to turn from the objects of nature to the God who made nature."
