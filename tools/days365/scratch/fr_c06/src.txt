=== d123 61:6.1
REF: Le grand évènement de cette période glaciaire fut l’apparition évolutive de l’homme primitif. Légèrement à l’Ouest de l’Inde, sur une terre maintenant immergée et parmi les descendants des anciens lémurs d’Amérique du Nord émigrés en Asie, les mammifères précurseurs de l’homme apparurent soudainement. Ces petits animaux marchaient principalement sur leurs pattes de derrière ; ils possédaient un gros cerveau proportionnellement à leur taille et comparativement au cerveau des autres animaux. Dans la soixante-dixième génération de cet ordre de vie, un groupe nouveau et supérieur d’animaux se différencia soudain. Ces nouveaux mammifères intermédiaires — qui avaient presque deux fois la taille de leurs ancêtres et possédaient des facultés cérébrales accrues en proportion — venaient à peine de bien s’établir quand les primates, représentant leur troisième mutation vitale, apparurent soudain. (Au même moment, un développement rétrograde, survenu au cœur de la souche des mammifères intermédiaires, donna naissance aux ancêtres de la race simienne ; depuis ce jour et jusqu’aux temps présents, la branche humaine a progressé selon une évolution graduelle, tandis que les tribus simiennes sont restées stationnaires ou ont même réellement rétrogradé.)
T: When Humankind First Budded
[0] Following the mammalian era, Paper 61 reaches the glacial period and records its most important event. Today's passage declares that the great event of this glacial period was the evolution of primitive man. Slightly to the west of India, on land now under water, among the offspring of Asiatic migrants of the older North American lemur types, the dawn mammals suddenly appeared. These small animals walked mostly on their hind legs, and they possessed large brains in proportion to their size. Life's long preparation had at last begun to take its decisive steps toward man.
[1] The passage goes on to describe three sudden leaps. In the seventieth generation of the dawn mammals a new and higher group, the mid-mammals, suddenly differentiated, almost twice the size of their ancestors and possessing proportionately increased brain power. They had only well established themselves when the Primates, the third vital mutation, suddenly appeared. And from these Primates the first human beings were at last born (see Paper 62). The appearance of man was not an accumulation of slow change but a series of leaps occurring within a plan long prepared by the Life Carriers.
[2] The sentence in parentheses in the passage also carries deep meaning. At this same time, a retrograde development within the mid-mammal stock gave origin to the simian ancestry. From that day to this the human branch has gone forward by progressive evolution, while the simian tribes have remained stationary or have actually retrogressed. Though they came from the same root, one branch moved forward while the other stayed behind. This contrast is a biological fact, yet it also leads us to think about the crossroads between growth and stagnation. The Life Carriers must have watched this parting of the ways with both hope and regret. Not all life advances alike, but to life that seeks to advance, the universe always opens a way.
[3] The fact that human beings evolved from animals does not lower our dignity. Quite the opposite. The Urantia Book tells us that the Life Carriers prepared for this moment over hundreds of millions of years, and that when man appeared as a will creature, the whole universe rejoiced and acknowledged it (see 62:7.1). We are not beings molded at a stroke from clay but the fruit brought forth by the long history of life. And to that fruit God sends the Thought Adjuster, opening the possibility of eternal life. Animal origin and divine destiny meeting in one being: that is what a human being is.
[4] This passage leads us to reflect on our daily choices. Just as two branches from one root walked entirely different paths, within us too there are forces that would press forward and forces that would stay put or slide back. Our animal nature wants to remain in comfort and familiarity, but the spirit calls us higher. The Urantia Book teaches that man is a being moving from animal nature to spiritual nature. That journey is made not by a single decision but by small choices every day.
[5] Today, look honestly at the part of you that would stagnate and the part that would grow. If you find a desire to stay in familiar habits, take even one small step forward. Born human after hundreds of millions of years of preparation, we are beings called to grow higher every day.
PR: Find one comfortable habit you would rather stay in, and take one small step of growth beyond it today.

T: Survival by a Hairbreadth
[0] Paper 62 records, from the Life Carrier's point of view, the dawn races of early man, the course that led from the lemurs through the dawn mammals, the mid-mammals, and the Primates to the first human beings. Today's passage vividly shows how precarious that course was. The Life Carrier says that we can hardly realize by what narrow margins our prehuman ancestors missed extinction from time to time. It is astonishing to read that had the ancestral frog of all humanity jumped two inches less on a certain occasion, the whole course of evolution would have been markedly changed.
[1] The passage lists the moments of crisis concretely. The immediate lemurlike mother of the dawn-mammal species escaped death no less than five times by mere hairbreadth margins before she gave birth to the father of the new and higher order. But the closest call of all was when lightning struck the tree in which the prospective mother of the Primates twins was sleeping. Both parents were severely shocked and badly burned, and three of their seven children were killed by this bolt from the skies. The destiny of humankind trembled atop a single tree, before a single stroke of lightning. That the Life Carrier recorded this scene in such detail is surely meant to show us both the fragility and the preciousness of life. Humankind was not strong and secure from the beginning. A fragile and imperiled life has passed through countless crises to reach today.
[2] Reading this record, we realize afresh how wondrous it is that we are alive here now. Through countless crises that look like chance, the stem of life continued unbroken. The Urantia Book tells us that the Life Carriers watched over the stream of life and tended it within permitted limits. Yet even they did not control everything. Life grew in freedom and danger, and through it all the great current moved toward its purpose. Providence does not manipulate every event; it works through every event (see Paper 118).
[3] The latter part of the passage relates another fascinating scene. The couple whose treetop home had been struck were really the leaders of the more progressive group of the mid-mammal species, and following their example, more than half the tribe moved to a new place and built new abodes and shelters. The crisis became not merely a threat but an occasion for change. Wounded leaders opened a new way, and from among those who followed it came the ancestors of humankind. Hardship, in effect, led the community to a better place.
[4] This story gives deep comfort to our personal lives as well. We too have passed through many narrow escapes. Looking back, there are moments when, had things been only slightly different, the person I am now would not exist. That I stand here today after all those moments may be not mere luck but evidence of unseen care. Our guardian angels and Thought Adjusters have been beside us even when we did not know it. And the wounds and crises we have suffered can also become occasions that open new ways.
[5] Today, recall one moment in your life when you came through by a hairbreadth. Give thanks for the hand that protected you then, and receive the very fact that you are alive now as a gift. Then resolve not to waste that gift, but to add your part to carrying the stem of life on to the next generation.

T: Prayer, the Desire of the Soul
[0] The seventh section of Paper 74 describes life in the Garden. Adam's children learned in the schools, and the residents of the Garden lived together keeping the moral law. Today's passage shows the worship life of that community. The public worship hour of Eden was noon; sunset was the hour of family worship. At the middle and at the close of each day there was a time set aside for remembering God. The worship of the community and the worship of the family were harmoniously set within the rhythm of the day.
[1] But the passage records one particular effort that Adam made. Adam did his best to discourage the use of set prayers, teaching that effective prayer must be wholly individual, that it must be the “⟦Q 74:7.21⟧desire of the soul.⟦/Q⟧” But the Edenites continued to use the prayers and forms handed down from the times of Dalamatia. Adam also endeavored to substitute the offerings of the fruit of the land for the blood sacrifices in the religious ceremonies, but had made little progress before the disruption of the Garden. That his teaching failed to take root was less a failure on Adam's part than a sign that people's hearts had not yet grown that far.
[2] Adam's teaching goes to the heart of prayer. Prayer is not reciting set sentences but the sincere desire of the soul offered to God. Memorized prayers may help at first, but when repeated without heart they become empty forms. God listens not to beautiful sentences but to a sincere heart. Even clumsy, short words reach heaven when they come from the depths of the soul. The Urantia Book teaches in many places that prayer is personal communion with God.
[3] It is interesting that people did not readily accept Adam's teaching. Old forms are familiar and comfortable. New ways feel strange and difficult. People probably felt it was safer to recite set prayers, for opening one's heart and speaking directly to God demands more courage and honesty. Today, tens of thousands of years later, we feel the same temptation. Forms are easy to hold on to, but giving our hearts always calls for a fresh decision.
[4] Jesus taught the same thing. He warned against those who prayed at length to be seen by others, and told us to go into our closet and pray to the Father who is in secret. When he taught his disciples to pray, he gave it not as a formula to be memorized and repeated but as an example showing the spirit of prayer. The Father knows what we need before we ask. What he desires is our heart itself. Prayer does not change God; it changes the one who prays. In the moment we sincerely open our hearts, we come a little nearer to the Father's will.
[5] Today we can look again at our own prayers. Are we not repeating the same words out of habit? Do my prayers truly carry the desire of my soul? The habit of praying at set times is precious, but a sincere heart must dwell within those times. At the middle and at the close of this day, like the people of Eden, it would be good to pause for a moment and tell the Father, just as it is, the desire in the depths of our hearts.
PR: At noon and at sunset today, pause for a moment and offer the Father the desire of your soul in your own words, not memorized ones.
