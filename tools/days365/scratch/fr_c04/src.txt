== d074 37:6.4 | Learning While Working
REF_FR: Fondamentalement, le système éducatif de Nébadon s’occupe de vous affecter à une tâche, et ensuite il vous procure les occasions de vous instruire sur la méthode idéale et divine d’accomplir au mieux cette tâche. Vous recevez une mission bien définie, et en même temps on vous fournit les maitres qualifiés pour vous enseigner la meilleure méthode de l’accomplir. Le plan divin d’éducation associe intimement le travail et l’instruction. Nous vous enseignons la meilleure façon d’exécuter les choses que nous vous commandons de faire.
[0] Paper 37, in introducing the various personalities of the local universe, describes the educational system of Nebadon. Today’s passage sets out the core principle of that system concisely. Fundamentally, the Nebadon educational system provides for your assignment to a task and then affords you opportunity to receive instruction as to the ideal and divine method of best performing that task. You are given a definite task to perform, and at the same time you are provided with teachers who are qualified to instruct you in the best method of executing your assignment. The divine plan of education provides for the intimate association of work and instruction.
[1] This principle differs somewhat from what we usually think of as education. In this world we usually study for a long time first and only later begin to work. But in the education of the universe, work and learning go together. We learn by actually doing, and we put what we learn into practice at once. Knowledge becomes living wisdom when it is used, and it becomes true understanding when it is confirmed through experience. This principle runs through the whole long ascending career, from the mansion worlds to Paradise.
[2] We are already tasting this principle here on earth. God has placed us in concrete settings, in family, workplace, and community, and has given us tasks to carry out there. What we are entrusted with as parents, colleagues, and neighbors are all first courses in the education of the universe. And when we do not know how to do that work, the Thought Adjuster and the Spirit of Truth within us, and the unseen angels, become our teachers. We are already attending a divine school. There is a reason God has placed us where we are now. There are things that can be learned only in that place, and experiences that can be gained only with those people. So when we see our present place not as somewhere we want to escape but as a classroom where learning awaits, our day takes on an entirely different meaning.
[3] This realization transforms the meaning of daily life. There are times when repetitive housework or exhausting labor seems to have nothing to do with spiritual growth. But according to the passage, the task we are given is itself the place of learning. The question is not the kind of work but our attitude toward it. When we do even the same work with a desire to learn the best way of doing it according to God’s will, it becomes an education of eternal value (see Paper 39, section 4).
[4] This principle also reminds us that spiritual growth does not come through head knowledge alone. Knowing the truth and living the truth are different things. However much we learn about love, we cannot say we know love unless we have actually loved someone. God teaches us love by giving us people to love and work to serve. In this divine way of education, in which work and instruction are woven together, we gradually grow to resemble God. The harder the work feels on a given day, the more we are learning. Difficulty may be not a sign of failure but a signal that the divine teacher has begun the next lesson.
[5] Today, look upon one task you have been given as an assignment in a divine school. Ask the teacher within how you might do it best and most lovingly. In that very place where we learn as we work and work as we learn, the education of the universe has already begun.
PR Choose one task today and carry it out while asking what the most loving way to do it would be.

== d075 37:10.5 | A Hand to Those Behind
REF_FR: Un corps de mortels ascendants comprenant les créatures médianes glorifiées est toujours attaché au service de l’univers. Après avoir atteint Salvington, ces ascendeurs sont utilisés dans une variété à peu près infinie d’activités touchant la conduite des affaires de l’univers. À partir de chaque niveau d’aboutissement, ces mortels en progrès reviennent en arrière et descendent tendre une main secourable aux compagnons qui les suivent dans la montée. Les mortels de cet ordre qui séjournent temporairement sur Salvington sont affectés sur demande à presque tous les corps de personnalités célestes comme aides, étudiants, observateurs et instructeurs.
[0] Today’s passage tells us that ascending mortals are always attached to the service of the local universe. Ascenders who have attained Salvington are used in an almost endless variety of activities, and on requisition they are assigned to practically all corps of celestial personalities as helpers, students, observers, and teachers. And the passage leaves us one beautiful sentence: from each level of achievement these advancing mortals reach back and down to extend a helping hand to their fellows who follow them in the upward climb.
[1] This picture shows that the ascending career of the universe is never merely an individual race. Those who have gone ahead help those who come behind, and those who have been helped in turn help the next. Many of those who will welcome and teach us on the mansion worlds were once mortals who lived on evolutionary worlds, just as we do. Because they remember the help they received, they now gladly reach out their hands to us. The universe is bound together in this way by a vast chain of beings lifting one another up. So the path of ascent is not a lonely climb but more like an expedition roped together. Those in front break the trail, those behind follow it, and when someone slips, another holds him fast. In this cosmic friendship no one is left alone, and every ascender grows into one who passes on the help he has received. It is precisely because such beings await us that our first awakening on the mansion worlds will not feel strange.
[2] It is also worth noting that the passage assigns ascenders as students and teachers at the same time. In the universe, learning and teaching are not separated. Everyone learns from those ahead and teaches those behind. This connects with the spirit of the Nebadon educational system we saw in earlier sections. The moment we share what we have received, our learning is completed, and in the process of teaching we learn more deeply.
[3] This principle applies just as much on earth. All of us have come this far with someone’s help. Parents, teachers, and friends, and those who walked the path of faith before us, held out their hands to us. Now it is our turn. If there is someone just beginning the life of faith, someone facing life’s hardships for the first time, someone wandering on a road we have already traveled, we must reach back and down to them.
[4] What matters here is the phrase reach back and down. It does not mean looking down in pity, but bending low and lowering our hand to a height the other can grasp. The essence of that helping hand is not the superiority of one who has gone ahead but the solidarity of a fellow climber. Jesus too set the example of service when he washed the feet of his apostles (see Paper 179). True achievement is revealed not by how high we have climbed but by how many we climb with. The hand we hold out to someone will one day pass on to yet another. In this way love is handed on from hand to hand, binding the whole universe into one.
[5] Today, think of someone standing on a road you have already traveled. Share your experience with them, offer a word of encouragement, and hold out your hand. The hand we extend becomes one link in the chain of love that runs through the entire universe.
PR Today, offer a word of encouragement to someone who is now going through a difficulty you have already been through.

== d076 38:2.1 | The Angels Who Love Us
REF_FR: Les anges n’ont pas de corps matériels, mais sont des êtres bien précis et distincts les uns des autres. Ils sont de nature et d’origine spirituelles. Bien qu’invisibles aux mortels, ils vous perçoivent tels que vous existez dans la chair, sans l’aide de transformateurs ni de traducteurs. Ils comprennent intellectuellement le mode de vie des mortels et partagent tous les sentiments et les émotions non sensuels des hommes. Ils apprécient vos efforts en musique, en art et en humour véritable, et y prennent grand plaisir. Ils se rendent pleinement compte de vos luttes morales et de vos difficultés spirituelles. Ils aiment beaucoup les êtres humains, et il ne peut résulter que du bien de vos efforts pour les comprendre et les aimer.
[0] Paper 38 speaks of the ministering spirits of the local universe, the angels. Today’s passage paints a warm picture of what angels are. Angels do not have material bodies, but they are definite and discrete beings; they are of spirit nature and origin. Though invisible to us, they perceive us as we are in the flesh. They understand the mode of mortal life, they share human emotions and sentiments, and they greatly enjoy our efforts in music, art, and real humor.
[1] That angels enjoy our humor may sound surprising. We easily imagine angels as solemn and distant beings. But the angels The Urantia Book portrays are companions who deeply sympathize with our lives. They rejoice with us when we sing, they value our efforts when we strive to create something beautiful, and they know the worth of our laughter when we laugh together. Angels are beings who love the bright side of human life. This means that angels are not strangers living in a world utterly different from ours. They are children of God as we are, and though different in nature, they are brothers and sisters who serve the same Father and move toward the same goal.
[2] The passage also says that angels are fully cognizant of our moral struggles and spiritual difficulties. When we wrestle with temptation, when we suffer because we know the right way yet fail to follow it, when we lose our way in seeking God, they understand it all. And the passage adds one decisive sentence: they love human beings. Guardian seraphim are assigned according to our spiritual growth and work beside us, and they keep the record of our survival until we awaken on the mansion worlds after death (see Paper 113).
[3] This truth gives us deep comfort. We are not fighting alone. Even in the loneliest moments, when we feel most alone, unseen companions who understand and love us are at our side. They are not watchers waiting to condemn our failures but friends who long for our success and strive along with us. When we fall they grieve, and when we rise again they rejoice. This unseen friendship makes our daily lives far less lonely.
[4] The last line of the passage extends an invitation to us: only good can result from our efforts to understand and love them. Yet this does not mean we should worship angels. The Urantia Book teaches plainly that worship is to be offered to God alone. To understand and love the angels is to be grateful for their ministry and to follow willingly in the direction they seek to lead us, the direction toward God. The best way to respond to their love is to live a better and more beautiful life. Their love is close to an unconditional friendship.
[5] Today, try going through the day conscious of the unseen companionship of angels. When you hear beautiful music, laugh with someone, or overcome a small inner struggle for the sake of what is right, remember that an angel who loves you is rejoicing with you there. That thought alone will make your day a little warmer and a little braver.
PR Today, in any moment when you feel alone, remember that an angel who understands and loves you is by your side.

== d077 38:9.13 | The Reward of Faithful Service
REF_FR: Les Médians restent pendant de longues périodes sur un monde habité, mais, s’ils sont fidèles à leur mission, leurs services au long des âges pour maintenir la souveraineté du Fils Créateur finiront très certainement par être pris en considération. Ils recevront la récompense qui leur est due pour leur patient ministère auprès des mortels matériels sur leur monde du temps et de l’espace. Tôt ou tard, toutes les créatures médianes accréditées seront enrôlées dans les rangs des Fils ascendants de Dieu, et dument engagées dans la longue aventure d’ascension au Paradis en compagnie de ces mêmes mortels d’origine animale, leurs frères terrestres, qu’ils ont si soigneusement protégés et si efficacement servis pendant leur long séjour planétaire.
[0] The final section of Paper 38 speaks of the midway creatures, the midwayers. Midwayers are beings midway between humans and angels, who remain for long periods on an inhabited world and help with planetary affairs. Today’s passage tells of their future. Midwayers remain for long periods on an inhabited world, but if faithful to their trust, they will eventually and most certainly be recognized for their agelong service in maintaining the sovereignty of the Creator Son, and they will be duly rewarded for their patient ministry to the material mortals on their world.
[1] What is the reward the passage speaks of? It is to enter the ranks of the ascending Sons of God. The midwayers will be duly initiated into the long adventure of the Paradise ascent in company with those very mortals of animal origin, their earth brethren, whom they so jealously guarded and so effectively served during the long planetary sojourn. To walk the same road together with those they served: this is their reward. The end of service is not a high position but companionship with those one loves. This shows how warm the justice of the universe is. Those who have served long are not exalted alone; they begin a new adventure together with those they served. Their friendship does not end on the planet but continues along the road of eternal ascent.
[2] The midwayers of our world, Urantia, have a special history. In the Caligastia rebellion some midwayers joined the rebellion, but the loyal midwayers worked quietly for long ages to uphold the spiritual order of this world (see Paper 77). They assisted Jesus during his earthly life, and they still help with planetary affairs today. Their service is a record of patience spanning hundreds of thousands of years, most of it carried out unknown to and unrecognized by human beings.
[3] This passage awakens us to the value of unrecognized service. Around us too there are people who work quietly in places no one notices: parents who have sacrificed for their families through long years, those who hold up a community from out of sight, those who faithfully carry out work that earns no praise. The passage says that such faithfulness is never forgotten. In God’s universe faithful service is certainly recognized and duly rewarded. In God’s record every labor that people overlooked is written down without omission.
[4] Another lesson from the midwayers’ story is patience. They serve in the same place not for a short time but for long ages. In our lives too, true achievement usually comes through long patience. A day or two of zeal is easy, but to remain faithful year after year, decade after decade, is hard. Yet that very constancy builds trust and forms character. Faithfulness is the strength to keep doing one’s appointed work even when no results can be seen, and it is one of the virtues God prizes most. So even today’s small faithfulness is never in vain; one day its meaning will shine out clearly in the light of the universe.
[5] Today, try to work faithfully in your appointed place even if no one is watching. And seek out those around you who serve quietly, and acknowledge their labors. When we recognize one another here on earth, we give a foretaste of the truth that God remembers all faithful service.
PR Today, find someone who works quietly in a place no one notices and acknowledge their labors.

== d078 39:4.13–39:4.14 | The Key of Sincerity
REF_FR: Les stimulateurs de moralité dépeignent aux mondes habités la vie mortelle comme une chaine ininterrompue de beaucoup de chainons. Votre court séjour sur Urantia, sphère d’enfance de mortel, n’est qu’un seul chainon, le tout premier dans la longue chaine qui s’étend à travers les univers et au long des âges éternels. Il ne s’agit pas tant de ce que vous apprenez dans cette première vie ; c’est l’expérience de vivre cette vie qui est importante. Même le travail dans ce monde, si important qu’il soit, n’est pas de loin aussi important que la manière dont vous l’effectuez. Une vie de droiture n’apporte pas de récompense matérielle, mais une satisfaction profonde — une conscience de réussite — qui transcende toute rémunération matérielle concevable. / Les clefs du royaume des cieux sont la sincérité, plus de sincérité et encore plus de sincérité. Tous les hommes possèdent ces clefs. Les hommes s’en servent — élèvent leur statut spirituel — par des décisions, plus de décisions et encore plus de décisions. Le choix moral le plus élevé est celui de la plus haute valeur possible, et toujours — dans chaque sphère et dans toutes les sphères — c’est le choix de faire la volonté de Dieu. Si un homme effectue ce choix, il est grand, même s’il n’est que le plus humble citoyen de Jérusem ou même le plus insignifiant mortel d’Urantia.
[0] Paper 39 introduces the various orders of the seraphic hosts and their ministries. Today’s passage conveys what is taught by those seraphim called the quickeners of morality. They portray mortal life as an unbroken chain of many links. Our short sojourn on Urantia is only a single link, the very first in the long chain that is to stretch across universes and through the eternal ages. And they say that it is not so much what you learn in this first life; it is the experience of living this life that is important.
[1] These words turn our standards of value upside down. We usually judge a life by what it has accomplished or how much it knows. But the passage says that even the work of this world, paramount though it is, is not nearly so important as the way in which you do this work. In God’s eyes the process matters more than the result, and character more than achievement. If we have done something honestly, lovingly, and faithfully, that experience remains as a strong link in the eternal chain. Jesus too spent most of his life doing ordinary work as a carpenter, as a father figure, and as head of a family. His greatness lay in the way he did that ordinary work in keeping with the Father’s will. Our daily lives, then, can likewise be filled with holy meaning.
[2] The passage goes on to give a famous saying: The keys of the kingdom of heaven are: sincerity, more sincerity, and more sincerity. And all men have these keys. To enter the kingdom of heaven requires no special talent, knowledge, or position. What is needed is sincerity, an honest and constant heart toward God. No one can take these keys from us, and they are given equally to all.
[3] The way to use these keys is by decision. The passage says that men use them, and advance in spirit status, by decisions, by more decisions, and by more decisions. The highest moral choice is the choice of the highest possible value, and always this is to choose to do the will of God. Sincerity is proved not in a single moment of inspiration but in decisions repeated day after day. As the habit of choosing God’s will in small things accumulates, our souls grow. Each time we choose honesty after hesitation, patience instead of anger, service instead of comfort, the key quietly turns.
[4] The last sentence of the passage gives great comfort. If man thus chooses, he is great, though he be the humblest citizen of Jerusem or even the least of mortals on Urantia. In God’s universe greatness is determined not by position or ability but by choice. So none of us has any reason to think ourselves insignificant. However ordinary a life may be, true greatness is achieved in the very place where we sincerely choose God’s will. This is a mysterious key that fits better the more it is used.
[5] Today, give your attention less to the results of your work than to the way you do it. At each moment of small decision, ask what God’s will might be and sincerely choose it. The key of sincerity is already in our hands. Today, use it to open the door of the kingdom of heaven once more.
PR Today, take one task and carry it through to the end with sincerity, focusing on the way you do it rather than on the result.

== d079 39:5.7 | Spirits Who Plant Trust
REF_FR: 4. Les esprits de confiance. La suspicion est la réaction innée des hommes primitifs. Les luttes pour se maintenir en vie au cours des premiers âges n’engendrent pas naturellement la confiance. La confiance est une nouvelle acquisition humaine due au ministère de ces séraphins planétaires du régime adamique. Ils ont pour mission d’inculquer la confiance dans le mental des hommes évoluants. Les Dieux sont très confiants ; le Père Universel désire se fier libéralement — en tant qu’Ajusteur — à une association avec l’homme.
[0] Today’s passage introduces the angels among the planetary seraphim called the Spirits of Trust. The passage says that suspicion is the inherent reaction of primitive men. The survival struggles of the early ages did not naturally breed trust. The unfamiliar was dangerous, other tribes were enemies, and nature was unpredictable. In such surroundings suspicion was an instinct for survival. That is why trust is a new human acquisition, brought about by the ministry of these planetary seraphim.
[1] These words help us understand why we find it so hard to believe. Within us remain habits of wariness and suspicion formed over hundreds of thousands of years. That we cannot easily trust people, that we are anxious about the future, that we doubt even the goodness of God, is not because we are especially wicked but because of our evolutionary heritage. Yet the passage tells us that trust can be learned and acquired. The Spirits of Trust have the mission to inculcate trust into the minds of evolving men. Rather than blaming ourselves for our habits of suspicion, it is important to remember that a path lies open before us to slowly transform them into trust. Heavenly helpers are assisting in that change.
[2] The most beautiful sentence in the passage is this: The Gods are very trustful. God does not suspect; God trusts us. The proof of it is the Thought Adjuster. The passage says that the Universal Father is willing freely to trust himself, the Adjuster, to man’s association. That the Infinite One should entrust a fragment of himself to the mind of a frail and imperfect mortal is a trust greater than we can imagine. God trusts us even though he knows we may fail.
[3] This truth casts new light on our faith. Faith, in the end, is trusting God. But God trusted us first. Long before we believed in God, he believed in us and entrusted himself to us. Our trust is therefore a response to God’s trust. Just as a person who is trusted learns how to trust, so when we realize that we are trusted by God, trust begins to grow within us as well.
[4] Trust matters not only in our relationship with God but in our relationships with people. A heart full of suspicion can never enjoy peace, and a society full of suspicion can never achieve brotherhood. Jesus taught a childlike trust in the Father and said that such trust sets us free from fear (see Paper 140). Of course, this does not mean a blind trust without wisdom. But the attitude of opening one’s heart first and believing in another’s good will becomes a power that heals relationships and builds community. Trust is the courage to risk being hurt, and that courage grows from the assurance that God trusted us first. As those trusted by God, we too become people who know how to trust.
[5] Today, meditate on how much God trusts you. Even at this moment he is entrusting himself to you, within you. In response to that trust, hand over one worry to God today, and open your heart once more to someone you have doubted. This is how trust takes root within us, little by little.
PR Today, confess that you are entrusting to God one worry that makes you anxious, and let your heart rest.

This status is our deepest identity, one that no failure or circumstance can take away.
[5] Today, begin your day by calling God “our Father.” Put trust and joy into that call rather than fear. And look upon the people you meet today as children of the same Father. The love that called us, lowly as we are, to be his children will make our day new.
PR When you pray today, call God “our Father,” and speak with the trust and joy of a child.

== d082 41:2.5 | Turning Light into Life

So the small faithfulness we keep today becomes preparation for the greater service we will be given.

Today’s passage says that this world is indeed a foretaste of paradisiacal glory and grandeur. Our present difficulties are brief moments when seen from the eternal career. And beyond them await beauty and opportunities for growth that our language cannot fully express. This hope does not make us turn away from reality; rather, it becomes a power that helps us live today more meaningfully. Today’s labors are small steps on the road toward eternal joy.
[4] At the same time, this passage hints that we can taste Paradise in advance even now, on this earth. If Jerusem is a foretaste of Paradise, then the true love, beauty, and truth we experience on earth can be called a foretaste of Jerusem. When we love someone sincerely, when we feel awe before the beauty of nature, when we enjoy peace in deep communion with God, we are tasting little by little the glory of the world to come. The kingdom of heaven has already begun within us.
[5] Today, look for the moments in your life when you taste heavenly beauty in advance. You will find that taste in a beautiful landscape, a warm conversation, or a time of quiet prayer. And give thanks, remembering that such moments are small previews of the endless glory yet to unfold. The best is yet to come.

Rien n’a priorité sur le travail concernant votre sphère statutaire — le présent monde ou les suivants. Mais, bien que le travail soit important, le moi ne l’est pas. Quand vous vous sentez important, vous perdez tant d’énergie à justifier la fierté de votre égo qu’il reste peu d’énergie pour faire le travail. C’est l’importance attachée au moi et non l’importance attachée au travail qui épuise les créatures immatures. C’est l’élément moi qui épuise, et non l’effort pour aboutir. Vous pouvez faire une œuvre importante si vous ne vous attribuez pas d’importance. Vous pouvez faire plusieurs choses aussi facilement qu’une seule si vous laissez votre moi de côté. Paper 48 tells how they teach newly arrived ascenders to keep their poise and cheerfulness under pressure, and then gives us the wisdom of today's passage. Its heart is contained in a single sentence: though the work is important, the self is not. This simple distinction shows us exactly where our energy leaks away.

Worries about how we look to others, whether we are losing face, whether we are being recognized, rob us of most of our strength. The wear and tear of ego dignity leaves little energy for the work that actually needs doing. That is why the words, you can do several things as easily as one if you leave yourself out, come as such profound news of liberation.

Very important is the work of preparation for the next higher sphere, but nothing equals the importance of the work of the world in which you are actually living. This is counsel not to treat life on earth as a waiting room for heaven. Those who do not take themselves too seriously are not easily crushed by failure or easily wounded by criticism. Humility is not belittling oneself; it is being freed from worry about oneself so as to give one's whole heart to the work and to people. When the passage adds that variety is restful and monotony is what wears and exhausts, it is in the same spirit, showing us how to work with vitality.
[4] This teaching is revealed most vividly in the life of Jesus. He made no effort to guard his dignity, and even in the face of misunderstanding and accusation he wasted no strength defending himself. == d098 49:4.8–49:4.9 | Only the Spirit Is Immortal
REF_FR: Le mental est le don de l’Esprit Infini ; il fonctionne de la même manière dans des milieux divers. Les mortels sont apparentés en mental, indépendamment de certaines différences structurelles et chimiques caractérisant la nature physique des créatures volitives des systèmes locaux. Quelles que soient les variantes planétaires personnelles ou physiques, la vie mentale des divers ordres de mortels est très similaire et leurs carrières immédiates après la mort se ressemblent beaucoup. / Mais le mental mortel sans l’esprit immortel ne peut survivre. Le mental de l’homme est mortel ; seul l’esprit effusé est immortel. La survie dépend de la spiritualisation par le ministère de l’Ajusteur — de la naissance et de l’évolution de l’âme immortelle ; il faut tout au moins qu’aucun antagonisme ne se soit développé envers la mission de l’Ajusteur qui cherche à effectuer la transformation spirituelle du mental matériel.
[0] Paper 49 unfolds the astonishing variety of the inhabited worlds scattered across the universe. There are worlds of nonbreathing beings, mortals who differ completely in stature, constitution, and modes of nourishment, and races possessing one brain or three. Yet while outward appearances vary without end, today's passage points to one thing held in common beneath all these differences: mind is the bestowal of the Infinite Spirit and functions quite the same in diverse environments.
[1] This teaching gives us the ground for a profound brotherhood. However great the differences in physical structure or chemistry, the mental life of mortal will creatures is very similar. Their immediate careers after death are very much alike. It enlarges the heart to realize that brothers and sisters living on countless worlds of the universe think, choose, and seek God just as we do. If we remember this truth when we meet neighbors whose skin, speech, or culture differs from ours, we will see our common source before we see our differences.
[2] But the passage goes on at once to add a solemn warning: mortal mind without immortal spirit cannot survive. The mind of man is mortal; only the bestowed spirit, the Thought Adjuster, is immortal. Survival is dependent on spiritualization by the ministry of the Adjuster, on the birth and evolution of the immortal soul. Brilliant intellect or great learning does not of itself guarantee eternal life. Only when the mind cooperates with the spirit to nurture a new reality, the morontia soul, does the way of survival open.
[3] What is especially comforting here is the mention of the minimum condition for survival. At least, the passage says, there must not have developed an antagonism towards the Adjuster's mission of effecting the spiritual transformation of the material mind. We need not become perfect saints to survive; the starting point is not to refuse the indwelling spirit of God but to open our minds to its leading. A sincere longing to do God's will, honest prayer, and obedience in each small choice make the soul grow (see Paper 110).
[4] This teaching tells us plainly where to invest our time and strength. Bodily health and intellectual ability are precious gifts and should be well tended. But they are only tools for the earthly journey; they do not themselves endure forever. What endures forever is the soul, grown out of the decisions the mind has made together with the spirit, out of moments of loving truth, choosing goodness, and delighting in beauty. That is why The Urantia Book explains survival not as a matter of merit or creed but as a matter of spiritual cooperation. However simple a person may be, if he sincerely seeks God his mind is joined hand in hand with the spirit; however gifted a person may be, if he finally refuses the spirit's leading his talents will pass away with time. This humbles us, yet at the same time gives us great hope.
[5] Today, quietly examine where your mind is turned. Ask whether your thoughts through the day dwell only on what is mortal, on gains and worries that will pass away. And, even briefly, open your heart to the Thought Adjuster within. A mind that cooperates with the spirit is already building something eternal in the days of this earthly life. That is the most precious possession we have.
PR Check today whether your thoughts dwell only on what will pass away, and offer a prayer that opens your heart to the indwelling spirit.
