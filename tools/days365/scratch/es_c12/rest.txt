== d284 144:2.3 Prayer, the Breath of the Soul
REF: «La oración es el aliento del alma y debe conduciros a persistir en vuestro intento de conocer la voluntad del Padre. Si alguno de vosotros tiene un vecino, y vas a verle a la media noche para decirle: ‘amigo mío, préstame tres panes, porque acaba de llegar un viajero amigo mío, y nada tengo para darle’; y tu vecino responde, ‘ya no me molestes; mi puerta ya está cerrada y mis hijos y yo ya estamos acostados; por eso no puedo levantarme a darte pan’, pero perseverarás y explicarás que tu amigo tiene hambre, y que no tienes comida para darle. Y yo te digo que si tu vecino no quiere levantarse para darte pan por amistad, se levantará y te dará tantos panes como necesites simplemente para que no lo importunes más. Así pues, si la perseverancia gana el favor de un simple mortal, imaginaos cuanto más ganará vuestra perseverancia en el espíritu, el pan de la vida de las manos generosas del Padre en el cielo. Nuevamente os digo, pedid y se os dará; buscad y encontraréis, golpead la puerta y se os abrirá. Porque el que pide recibe; el que busca encuentra; y el que golpea la puerta de la salvación, la puerta se le abrirá.
0 While they were resting at the encampment near Mount Gilboa, the apostles asked Jesus to teach them how to pray. Just as John the Baptist had taught his disciples to pray, they too wished to learn prayer from their Master. This passage is the heart of Jesus’ discourse on prayer given in answer to that request. Jesus calls prayer “⟦Q 144:2.3⟧the breath of the soul.⟦/Q⟧” As the body cannot live without breathing, so the soul cannot stay alive without prayer. Prayer is not a religious act reserved for special occasions; it is the natural breathing that sustains spiritual life.
1 Jesus says that prayer “⟦Q 144:2.3⟧should lead you to be persistent in your attempt to ascertain the Father’s will.⟦/Q⟧” This makes the purpose of prayer clear. Prayer is not a means of changing God’s mind so as to get our own way; it is a channel through which our minds are changed so that we may know and do the Father’s will. In the verse just before, Jesus taught that prayer is the personal and spontaneous expression of the soul’s attitude toward spirit, the communion of sonship and the expression of fellowship. Prayer is a living conversation between the Father and his child.
2 Jesus tells the parable of the man who went at midnight to borrow bread from a friend. The friend refuses at first, saying the door is shut and he and his children are in bed, but because of the man’s importunity he finally gets up and gives him as many loaves as he needs. With this parable Jesus teaches the value of persistence. “⟦Q 144:2.3⟧If, then, persistence will win favors even from mortal man, how much more will your persistence in the spirit win the bread of life for you from the willing hands of the Father in heaven.⟦/Q⟧” This does not mean that God answers reluctantly. Rather, our persistent prayer enlarges our souls into vessels able to receive the answer.
3 It matters, too, that what Jesus sets before us to ask for here is the bread of life. Jesus did not teach prayer chiefly as a means of obtaining material wishes. The most precious answer to prayer is spiritual food: the wisdom to discern the Father’s will and the strength to do it. It is worth noticing as well that the man in the parable asked for bread not for himself but for a friend who had come to him. Jesus himself usually prayed not for himself but for his associates, praying for himself only in the great crises of his earth life (Paper 144, section 4).
4 Finally, Jesus promises: “⟦Q 144:2.3⟧Ask and it shall be given you; seek and you shall find; knock and it shall be opened to you. For every one who asks receives; he who seeks finds; and to him who knocks the door of salvation will be opened.⟦/Q⟧” This promise lifts us out of discouragement. Even when the answer seems slow in coming, prayer is never scattered into empty air. The Father always hears, and he gives us what is best at the best time. Our part is to come to the Father steadily, as naturally as breathing.
5 Fill this day with the breath of prayer. Long prayers at set times are good, but also call on the Father briefly while you work and as you walk, seeking his will. And ask for the bread of life not only for your own needs but for someone beside you. The soul of one who prays as naturally as breathing gains new strength each day and moves a little deeper, day by day, toward the Father’s will.
PR Call on the Father in short prayers throughout the day, and ask the bread of life for one person beside you.
== d285 144:4.3 Sonship, a Gift Received
REF: En toda oración, recordad que la filiación es un don. Ningún niño tiene que hacer nada para ganar el estado de hijo o hija. El hijo terrestre adquiere el ser por voluntad de sus padres. De la misma manera llega el hijo de Dios a la gracia y a la nueva vida del espíritu por voluntad del Padre en el cielo. Por consiguiente, el reino del cielo —la filiación divina— debe ser recibida por el hijo como si fuese un niño pequeño. La rectitud —el desarrollo progresivo del carácter— se gana, pero la filiación se recibe mediante la gracia y por la fe.
0 Continuing the teaching on prayer, this passage reminds us of a fundamental truth to remember in all praying: “⟦Q 144:4.3⟧sonship is a gift.⟦/Q⟧” “⟦Q 144:4.3⟧No child has aught to do with earning the status of son or daughter. The earth child comes into being by the will of its parents.⟦/Q⟧” No one did anything to qualify for being born. “⟦Q 144:4.3⟧Even so, the child of God comes into grace and the new life of the spirit by the will of the Father in heaven.⟦/Q⟧” That we are children of God springs not from our merit but from the Father’s love.
1 This truth changes the very posture of prayer. If sonship had to be earned, we would wonder anxiously each time we prayed whether we were worthy enough. But if sonship is a gift already given, we can come boldly to the Father as his children. A little child does not stop to weigh whether he deserves something before asking his parents for it. He simply comes to them in trust. Prayer should begin from exactly this kind of trust.
2 That is why Jesus taught that “⟦Q 144:4.3⟧the kingdom of heaven — divine sonship — be received as by a little child.⟦/Q⟧” To receive it as a little child does not mean becoming childish; it means having the simple trust that knows how to receive a gift as a gift. The older we grow, the more awkward we feel about receiving anything without paying for it. The habit of calculating everything and weighing our deserving seeps into our relationship with God as well. But the Father’s love is not something to be calculated. It is a gift to be received with gratitude. To receive a gift gladly is the surest way to delight the Giver.
3 Yet the passage adds an important distinction: “⟦Q 144:4.3⟧You earn righteousness — progressive character development — but you receive sonship by grace and through faith.⟦/Q⟧” Becoming a child of God is a gift, but growing up as a child of God is a lifelong process that requires our effort and choice. Confusing the two leads to trouble. Trying to earn sonship by effort leads to legalism, while assuming that character growth also comes automatically leads to spiritual sloth.
4 The Urantia Book shows the beautiful relationship between the two. Because we are already beloved children, we strive to grow. We do not grow in order to become children; we grow because we are children. Just as a child who is safely loved in the Father’s house grows most healthily, so the assurance of sonship becomes the firmest foundation for the development of character. When Jesus earlier called prayer the breath of the soul and taught us to ask with persistence (Paper 144, section 2), it was so that we, already his children, might grow day by day in fellowship with the Father.
5 When you pray today, begin with gratitude, receiving as a gift the fact that you are a child of God. Lay aside the mind that weighs its own deserving, and come to the Father as a little child. Then, within that love, tell the Father of one trait of character you need to grow in today, and resolve to work toward it. To grow joyfully upon the foundation of sonship received as a gift: this is the path a child of God walks, and the direction toward which our prayers should turn each day.
PR Begin today’s prayer with thanks that you are a child of God, and tell the Father of one trait of character you need to grow in.
== d286 145:2.4 The Father Who Knows Me
REF: «Bien sabéis que aunque un padre de corazón tierno ama a su familia en su totalidad, los considera así en grupo, debido a su poderoso afecto por cada uno de los individuos que forman esa familia. Ya no tienes que acercarte al Padre en el cielo como hijo de Israel, sino como hijo de Dios. Como grupo, sois efectivamente los hijos de Israel, pero como individuos, cada uno de vosotros es hijo de Dios. Yo he venido, no para revelar el Padre a los hijos de Israel, sino más bien para traer este conocimiento de Dios y la revelación de su amor y misericordia al creyente individual, como una genuina experiencia personal. Todos los profetas os han enseñado que Yahvé ama a su pueblo, que Dios ama a Israel. Pero yo he venido entre vosotros para proclamar una verdad aun más grande, una verdad que muchos de los últimos profetas también alcanzaron a comprender, o sea, que Dios os ama —a cada uno de vosotros— como individuos. Durante todas estas generaciones habéis vosotros tenido una religión nacional o racial; yo he venido ahora para daros una religión personal.
0 In the synagogue at Capernaum, Jesus proclaims a decisive turning point in Israel’s long religious tradition. God is not only one who cares for a nation as a group; he is a Father who loves each individual within that nation. Jesus explains this through the figure of a kindhearted father. A father cherishes his family as a whole not because the family as a group comes first, but “⟦Q 145:2.4⟧because of his strong affection for each individual member of that family.⟦/Q⟧” And so Jesus says: “⟦Q 145:2.4⟧No longer must you approach the Father in heaven as a child of Israel but as a child of God.⟦/Q⟧”
1 This teaching shifts the center of gravity of religion. In a national religion, the individual relates to God because he is a member of the community. Ritual and law, lineage and belonging become the tokens of faith. But in the personal religion Jesus brought, the knowledge of God and “⟦Q 145:2.4⟧the revelation of his love and mercy⟦/Q⟧” become, for each believer, “⟦Q 145:2.4⟧a genuine personal experience.⟦/Q⟧” It is in the same spirit that Jesus, in the paragraph that follows, quotes Jeremiah and Ezekiel: the day has already come when the law is written not on tablets of stone but on the heart, and when all, from the least to the greatest, shall know God personally.
2 This does not mean we may neglect the community. Jesus acknowledged that members of a family sometimes suffer together the consequences of group errors, and he added that the hope of a better nation and a better world depends in the end on the progress and enlightenment of the individual (145:2.8). The spiritual renewal of the individual is the starting point for renewing the community. As The Urantia Book stresses again and again, within each person dwells a Thought Adjuster sent by the Universal Father, and it is that indwelling spirit that calls us to the eternal ascent toward Paradise (145:2.9).
3 This personal religion turns the relationship between God and me from a transaction into a fellowship. I do not stand before God leaning on the merits of the whole nation or the righteousness of my ancestors; I seek the Father’s face myself and ask his will. Personal religion is therefore both freer and more demanding, for no one can believe for me, and no one can know God in my place.
4 Before these words today, we must ask ourselves: is my faith something I inherited, or something I have experienced for myself? Belonging to a church or a fellowship, or taking part in set ceremonies, does not by itself mean knowing God. God does not see me as one face in a crowd; he deals with me as a Father who knows my name and searches my heart. So we too should be able to speak to the Father in our own words, without borrowing the words of others.
5 At the same time, this truth changes the way we see others. If God loves me as an individual, then everyone I meet is likewise a child receiving his own unique love. When we lay aside the habit of judging people by groups and labels and try to see each person through the Father’s eyes, personal religion at last begins to bear the fruit of brotherhood in our lives. Today, remember that you are loved by the Father as an individual, and treat others as one who has received that love.
PR When you pray today, set aside fixed phrases and tell the Father about your day directly, in your own name and your own words.
== d287 145:5.6 Truth Rather Than Signs
REF: Cuando Jesús escuchó esto, contestó: «Andrés, ¿acaso no te enseñé a ti y a estos otros que mi misión en la tierra es la revelación del Padre, y mi mensaje, la proclamación del reino del cielo? ¿Cómo puede ser pues que quieras que yo me desvíe de mi misión para gratificar a los curiosos y para satisfacer a los que buscan signos y portentos? ¿Acaso no hemos estado entre estas gentes durante todos estos meses? ¿Acaso han venido ellos antes en multitudes para escuchar la buena nueva del reino? ¿Por qué vienen ahora a importunarnos? ¿No buscan acaso curar su cuerpo físico en vez de regocijarse porque han recibido la verdad espiritual para la salvación de su alma? Cuando los hombres son atraídos a nosotros por manifestaciones extraordinarias, muchos de ellos vienen buscando, no la verdad y la salvación, sino más bien la curación de sus dolencias físicas y la redención de sus dificultades materiales.
0 At sundown in Capernaum, 683 people were healed all at once in an astonishing event. At dawn the next day the crowds thronged again before the house of Zebedee. Andrew and the apostles saw this as a great opportunity and went to find Jesus, who was praying in the hills, to urge him to come back. But Jesus’ answer was firm. He reminded them that his “⟦Q 145:5.6⟧mission on earth is the revelation of the Father,⟦/Q⟧” and his “⟦Q 145:5.6⟧message the proclamation of the kingdom of heaven.⟦/Q⟧”
1 Jesus saw clearly why the people had come. They were seeking not spiritual truth for the salvation of their souls but the healing of their bodies and deliverance from their material difficulties. Those drawn by extraordinary manifestations seek signs, not truth. Just before this scene, The Urantia Book records that this mass healing came as a surprise even to Jesus and that nothing like it happened again in his earth life (145:3.11). Jesus healed out of compassion, but he would never allow healing to crowd out teaching.
2 This scene reveals a principle that runs through the whole of Jesus’ public ministry. He did not turn away from the sick, and he charged his apostles to minister to them. But the aim of his ministry was always to lead people to the Father, to truth and spiritual liberty. It was for the same reason that later, after the feeding of the five thousand, Jesus withdrew when the people sought to make him king (see Paper 152). Material wonders may make people marvel, but they cannot transform the soul.
3 Jesus did not leave the crowd because he was indifferent to them. On the contrary, he loved them deeply, and so he did not want them to be content with a passing restoration of the body and miss the eternal life of the soul. In the other cities of Galilee there were people waiting for the good news of the kingdom, and he said that the way was already prepared. True love gives others not what they want at the moment but what they truly need, just as wise parents do not give their child everything he asks for.
4 The same temptation comes into our own life of faith. When our prayers are answered and things go well, we feel close to God; when the hoped-for results do not come, we are disappointed and drift away. At such times we must ask what we were seeking God for. It is not wrong to ask for the restoration of health or circumstances, but when that becomes the whole of our faith, we are no different from the crowd seeking signs. A faith that uses God as a means is in the end a faith that makes ourselves the goal. God knows our needs and cares for them, but what he most longs to give us is fellowship with himself, and the spiritual life that grows within that fellowship.
5 Jesus told the apostles to go back and bid the people rejoice in the liberty of the sons of God (145:5.7). True joy comes not from the disappearance of our problems but from realizing that we are the Father’s children. Today, let us look honestly at what we are seeking, and become people who seek the Giver before the gifts, and truth before signs. Then, even on days when the answer is slow in coming, we will not be shaken, and we will rest in peace within the Father’s will.
PR Write down the things you are asking of God today, and see how many of them are for truth and spiritual growth.
== d288 146:2.4 When Forgiveness Becomes Real
REF: 3. Al abrir los mortales el terminal humano del canal de comunicación entre Dios y el hombre, la corriente constante del ministerio divino a las criaturas de los mundos, se hace inmediatamente disponible. Cuando el hombre escucha las palabras del espíritu de Dios dentro del corazón humano, existe inherente a esta experiencia el hecho de que Dios escucha simultáneamente la súplica del hombre. Aun el perdón de los pecados también opera en esta misma forma infalible. El Padre en el cielo te ha perdonado aun antes de que hayas pensado en pedírselo, pero dicho perdón no es asequible en tu experiencia religiosa personal hasta tanto no perdones tú a tus semejantes. El perdón de Dios —como hecho — no depende de que perdones a tus semejantes, pero en experiencia depende en forma precisa de este factor. Y este hecho de la sincronía del perdón divino y humano fue así reconocido y vinculado en la oración que Jesús enseñó a los apóstoles.
0 At Jotapata Jesus gave his apostles a number of teachings on prayer, and the third of them likens communication between God and man to a channel. God’s end is always open; the ever-flowing stream of divine ministry pours out continually to the creatures of the worlds. The question is the human end. When a man opens that end, when he seeks to hear God’s spirit speak within his heart, he immediately touches that stream, and “⟦Q 146:2.4⟧inherent in such an experience is the fact that God simultaneously hears that man’s prayer.⟦/Q⟧”
1 Jesus applies this principle to forgiveness. “⟦Q 146:2.4⟧The Father in heaven has forgiven you even before you have thought to ask him.⟦/Q⟧” God’s forgiveness is not a conditional bargain. Yet that forgiveness becomes real in our personal religious experience only when we forgive our fellow men. This teaching, which distinguishes forgiveness as fact from forgiveness as experience, is very delicate: “⟦Q 146:2.4⟧God’s forgiveness in fact is not conditioned upon your forgiving your fellows, but in experience it is exactly so conditioned.⟦/Q⟧” Here lies the reason why, in the prayer Jesus taught, the petition for the forgiveness of our sins stands side by side with our forgiving of others.
2 Here we see how far ahead of us God’s forgiveness goes. Even before we recognize our wrong and repent, the Father’s heart is already open toward us. Just as the father in the parable of the prodigal son saw his son from afar and ran to him first, God is waiting for us to return. But for that love to become a living experience within us, we must open the door of our hearts from our side as well, and that door is opened through forgiving others.
3 Why should this be so? Because an unforgiving heart closes the human end of the channel by its own act. While we nurse a grudge, we cannot feel the Father’s mercy flowing in. It is not that God withdraws his forgiveness, but that we leave no room in which to receive it. In the teaching that follows, Jesus says that those who would receive mercy must show mercy, and that with the same spirit in which we judge others we shall ourselves be judged (146:2.6). He also makes clear that mercy cannot circumvent the basic laws of justice in the universe (146:2.5).
4 When we test this truth in daily life, we discover its depth. When there is someone in our heart we have not forgiven, prayer feels heavy and dry. But when we let that person go, however hard it is, something long blocked within us is strangely released, and the Father’s love becomes real to us anew. Before it is a gift to the other person, forgiveness is the key that opens the door through which we experience God’s forgiveness. What must come before any answer to prayer is a heart opened in this way.
5 Forgiveness does not deny the wound or justify the wrong. It is the decision to release the other person from the prison of my verdict and to leave judgment to the Father. Before you pray today, search your heart, and if there is resentment you are holding, lay it down first. Then we will taste the joy of the forgiveness already given at last becoming our own. Forgiveness may not be finished with a single decision. As we practice laying it down again each time the same hurt resurfaces, our hearts grow ever more like the Father’s heart.
PR Call to mind one person you have not forgiven, and offer even a single sentence of prayer to bless them.
== d289 146:3.6 Love, the Measure of Spirit Guidance
REF: «Aunque no podéis observar la obra del espíritu divino en vuestra mente, existe un método práctico que os permite determinar hasta qué punto habéis puesto el control de los poderes de vuestra alma al servicio de las enseñanzas y dirección de este espíritu residente del Padre celestial, y ése es: la magnitud de vuestro amor al prójimo. Este espíritu del Padre comparte el amor del Padre, y a medida que va dominando al hombre, lo conduce infaliblemente en la dirección de la adoración divina y del amor y respeto por los semejantes. Al principio, creéis que sois hijos de Dios porque mis enseñanzas os permiten apercibiros de la presencia guiadora de nuestro Padre en vuestro corazón; pero pronto se derramará sobre toda la carne el Espíritu de la Verdad, y vivirá entre los hombres y les enseñará, así como yo ahora vivo entre vosotros y os hablo las palabras de la verdad. Y este Espíritu de la Verdad, que habla por las dotes espirituales de vuestra alma, os ayudará a conocer que sois los hijos de Dios. Prestará testimonio infalible mediante la presencia residente del Padre, vuestro espíritu, que para entonces residirá en todos los hombres así como ahora reside en algunos, diciéndoos que sois en realidad hijos de Dios.
0 At Ramah Jesus taught his apostles about the indwelling spirit. “⟦Q 146:3.6⟧You cannot observe the divine spirit at work in your minds.⟦/Q⟧” The activity of the Thought Adjuster takes place largely beyond our consciousness. How, then, can I know whether I am truly being led by that spirit? Jesus offers one clear and practical standard: “⟦Q 146:3.6⟧the degree of your love for your fellow men.⟦/Q⟧” This standard is truly practical, for anyone can examine himself by it every day.
1 This standard holds because “⟦Q 146:3.6⟧this spirit of the Father partakes of the love of the Father.⟦/Q⟧” As it dominates man, “⟦Q 146:3.6⟧it unfailingly leads in the directions of divine worship and loving regard for one’s fellows.⟦/Q⟧” One direction is worship of God; the other is love for our fellows. So if we claim that our spiritual experience is deepening while we grow cold toward people, that experience ought to be questioned. A change in how we treat our neighbors testifies to the presence of the spirit more honestly than any mystical feeling or special insight. Jesus’ own life was exactly so. Because he communed most deeply with the Father, he loved people most deeply.
2 Jesus goes on to foretell the day when “⟦Q 146:3.6⟧the Spirit of Truth shall be poured out upon all flesh.⟦/Q⟧” That Spirit will live among men and teach all men; “⟦Q 146:3.6⟧speaking for the spiritual endowments of your souls,⟦/Q⟧” it will help us to know that we are the sons of God. Since Pentecost we have been living in the age in which this promise is fulfilled (see Paper 194). The indwelling spirit of the Father and the Spirit of Truth bear witness together, letting us know in the depths of our souls that “⟦Q 146:3.6⟧you are in reality the sons of God.⟦/Q⟧”
3 The Urantia Book teaches that the Thought Adjuster works quietly in the depths of our minds, preserving in the soul the spiritual values we choose. We often cannot feel this activity directly, but its fruits show plainly. We begin to treat a little more generously someone we once could not bear; we come to rejoice in another’s joy; we begin to think of others’ needs before our own interests. Such small changes are the very signs that the spirit has begun to govern us.
4 This teaching makes spiritual self-examination very concrete. How patient was I today with the person who irritates me? How much was my heart moved when I saw someone in need? How hard did I try to understand someone who thinks differently from me? Honest answers to such questions show how far I have yielded the control of my soul powers to the Father’s spirit. Love is the surest measure of spiritual growth. And these questions are not for judging others but solely for examining myself.
5 There is no need to be discouraged when we find our love lacking. It is only a sign that some area remains not yet surrendered. The more we commune with the Father in worship, the more his love overflows within us, and that love flows naturally on to our neighbors. Loving God and loving people are not two tasks but one fruit borne by one spirit. Today let us pray that our love may grow a little wider.
PR Deliberately show one kindness today to the person you find hardest to deal with.
== d290 147:5.8 A Small but Living Faith
REF: «Esta mujer transformada que algunos de vosotros visteis en la casa de Simón hoy, está, en este momento, viviendo en un nivel vastamente inferior al de Simón y al de sus asociados bien intencionados; pero mientras estos fariseos están ocupados con el falso progreso en la ilusión de traspasar los círculos engañosos de los servicios ceremoniales sin sentido, esta mujer ha comenzado, con gran honestidad, la larga y pletórica búsqueda de Dios, y su camino hacia el cielo no está obstruido por el orgullo espiritual ni por la autosatisfacción moral. Esta mujer está, hablando desde un punto de vista humano, mucho más lejos de Dios que Simón, pero su alma está en movimiento progresivo; está encaminada hacia un objetivo eterno. Hay en esta mujer extraordinarias posibilidades espirituales para el futuro. Algunos entre vosotros podréis no estar en niveles reales de alma y espíritu particularmente elevados, pero estáis progresando diariamente en el camino vivo que se os ha abierto, a través de la fe, en dirección a Dios. Existen enormes posibilidades en cada uno de vosotros para el futuro. Es mucho mejor tener una fe pequeña pero viva y en crecimiento que poseer un gran intelecto con sus ramas muertas de sabiduría mundana y de descreimiento espiritual».
0 In Jerusalem Jesus was invited to the house of Simon the Pharisee. During the meal a woman who had once lived a sinful life came in, poured ointment on Jesus’ feet, and wet them with her tears. Simon judged Jesus in his heart, but Jesus awakened him with a parable: the one who is forgiven much loves much. That evening Jesus explained the meaning of the event more deeply to his apostles, and today’s passage is that teaching. The event vividly shows how God looks upon people.
1 Jesus contrasts two standards for judging a spiritual condition: the level at which one now stands, and the direction in which one is moving. Humanly speaking, the woman stood far below Simon. But Simon and his associates were “⟦Q 147:5.8⟧occupied with the false progress of the illusion of traversing deceptive circles of meaningless ceremonial services,⟦/Q⟧” and their path was blocked by spiritual pride and moral self-satisfaction. The woman, by contrast, had “⟦Q 147:5.8⟧in dead earnest, started out on the long and eventful search for God,⟦/Q⟧” and her soul was “⟦Q 147:5.8⟧in progressive motion.⟦/Q⟧” Jesus saw that difference clearly and judged people not by appearance but by the direction of the soul.
2 This agrees with the perspective of growth that runs through the whole of The Urantia Book. God looks less at where we stand now than at where we are walking. The Thought Adjuster does not come to the finished person; it indwells one who has the potential to grow and leads him day by day. That is why Jesus encourages his apostles too: “⟦Q 147:5.8⟧There are tremendous possibilities in each of you for the future.⟦/Q⟧” His repeated exhortation to grow in grace carries the same meaning (147:8.5). What matters to him is not how high or low the starting point is, but the sincerity of each step.
3 Jesus’ last words linger especially in the heart: “⟦Q 147:5.8⟧Better by far to have a small but living and growing faith than to be possessed of a great intellect with its dead stores of worldly wisdom and spiritual unbelief.⟦/Q⟧” Knowing much does not mean being spiritually advanced. Indeed, the more religious knowledge piles up, the easier it is to sit, like Simon, in the seat of judgment over others. What matters is whether our faith is alive and moving, growing another step today.
4 The living faith spoken of here is not information about God but trust in God and response to him. The woman may have known no theology, but she knew she had been forgiven, and she answered that love with her whole being. Simon, on the other hand, knew the law well, yet that knowledge did not open his heart. Faith is like a seed: life matters more than size. A faith as small as a mustard seed, if it is alive, grows into a great tree, while even the largest seed bears nothing if it is dead.
5 These words bring both comfort and warning to two kinds of people. To those who think little of themselves because of past wrongs, they bring the comfort that the step they have now taken toward God is precious above all else. To those who have long lived the life of faith and consider themselves to have climbed rather high, they bring the warning that a faith standing still is a faith already beginning to harden. Today let us quietly consider where we stand, and in which direction we are walking.
PR Write down one way your faith has grown over the past year, and one place where it has stood still.
== d291 147:8.5 Faith Rather Than Fasting
REF: Luego, hasta muy entrada la noche, expuso Jesús a sus apóstoles la verdad de que era la fe de ellos la que les aseguraba el reino del presente y del futuro, y no la aflicción de su alma ni el ayuno de su cuerpo. Exhortó a los apóstoles a que por lo menos estuvieran a la altura de las ideas del antiguo profeta y expresó la esperanza de que también progresarían mucho más allá de los ideales de Isaías y de los antiguos profetas. Sus últimas palabras esa noche fueron: «Creced en la gracia por medio de esa fe viviente que es capaz de comprender el hecho de que sois hijos de Dios y a la vez de reconocer a todos los hombres como hermanos».
0 In Capernaum the disciples of John and the Pharisees asked why Jesus’ disciples did not fast. Jesus answered that the children of the bridechamber cannot fast while the bridegroom is with them, and that new wine must be put into new wineskins (147:7.2). That night he read with his apostles from the book of Isaiah: the fast God delights in is not bowing the head and sitting in ashes, but loosing the bands of wickedness and sharing bread with the hungry (147:8.3). Since fasting and prayer were then the marks of a pious man, it seemed strange to many that his disciples did not fast.
1 Today’s passage is the conclusion of that night. Long into the night Jesus set forth to his apostles the truth “⟦Q 147:8.5⟧that it was their faith that made them secure in the kingdom of the present and the future, and not their affliction of soul nor fasting of body.⟦/Q⟧” Self-tormenting austerities do not bring us closer to God. For God is not one who must be appeased by our suffering, but a Father who already receives us the moment we approach him in faith.
2 The Urantia Book corrects in many places the age-old notion of God as an angry deity who must be appeased. The idea of buying divine favor through sacrifice and austerity arose in primitive religion, and Jesus replaced it with the revelation of a loving Father (see Paper 89). The Father takes no pleasure in his children tormenting themselves. What pleases him is that his children come to him in trust and share with others the love they have received.
3 This does not mean Jesus made light of the ideals of the prophets of old. He exhorted the apostles at least to live up to the ideals of Isaiah and the older prophets, and he hoped they would grow far beyond even those ideals. To lay aside formal religious practices is not to take an easier road but a deeper one. Choosing inward faith in place of outward duty, and service to one’s neighbor in place of self-affliction, actually asks more of us. The way of Jesus is not a way of lessening duty but of changing motive.
4 The last words Jesus spoke that night hold two things together: grasping the fact that I am a son of God, and at the same time recognizing every man as a brother. His charge was to “⟦Q 147:8.5⟧grow in grace by means of that living faith⟦/Q⟧” which holds both together. The fatherhood of God and the brotherhood of man are the two pillars of Jesus’ gospel, and neither alone can make a whole faith. We cannot claim to love the Father while turning away from our brother, nor should we lose fellowship with the Father while claiming to serve our brother.
5 We too often try to measure faith by the quantity of religious effort. We count how long we prayed, how much we endured, what we gave up and how much. But what the Father sees is not the weight of that effort but the depth of our trust and the breadth of our love. Today, instead of adding or subtracting something, quietly hold fast to the fact that you are the Father’s child, and treat those you meet along the way as brothers and sisters. That is the true fast that God has chosen.
PR Call to mind three times today that you are a child of God, and each time treat someone beside you as a brother or sister.
== d292 148:4.10 Children Who Bear the Father’s Spirit
REF: «Acaso no leíste en las Escrituras, Tomás, donde está escrito: ‘Vosotros sois los hijos del Señor vuestro Dios’. ‘Yo seré su Padre y él será mi hijo’. ‘Lo he elegido para que sea mi hijo —yo seré su Padre’. ‘Trae de lejos mis hijos, y mis hijas de los confines de la tierra; trae todos los llamados de mi nombre, pora gloria mía los he creado’. ‘Sois los hijos del Dios viviente’. ‘Los que tienen el espíritu de Dios son en verdad hijos de Dios’. Mientras hay una parte material del padre humano en el hijo natural, hay también una parte espiritual del Padre celestial en cada hijo de la fe del reino.»
0 In the days of the new school of the prophets at Bethsaida, Thomas asked Jesus the difference between evil, sin, and iniquity. In answering, Jesus corrected a misunderstanding widespread at the time: the idea that man is by nature estranged from God, an alien or stepchild, who can become part of God’s family only by going through a legal process of adoption. Jesus said that such a concept arose from a misunderstanding of the Father and from ignorance of the origin, nature, and destiny of man (148:4.8).
1 As evidence to the contrary, Jesus reminds Thomas of a number of passages in the Hebrew scriptures. Words such as “⟦Q 148:4.10⟧You are the children of the Lord your God⟦/Q⟧” and “⟦Q 148:4.10⟧They who have the spirit of God are indeed the sons of God⟦/Q⟧” had been written long before. People had simply forgotten that truth beneath the weight of law and ritual. Jesus did not invent a new doctrine; he drew out the most precious truth buried in the scriptures and set it once more in the light.
2 The last sentence of the passage sums up this truth in a beautiful figure: “⟦Q 148:4.10⟧While there is a material part of the human father in the natural child, there is a spiritual part of the heavenly Father in every faith son of the kingdom.⟦/Q⟧” The Urantia Book calls this spiritual part the Thought Adjuster. It is a fragment of God that comes directly from the Universal Father, arriving to indwell the normal human mind at about the time moral decision begins (see Paper 107). That we are children of God is not merely a legal title but a real spiritual lineage. Between God and us, therefore, there is from the very beginning a spiritual bond that cannot be broken.
3 This does not mean Jesus took human imperfection lightly. He acknowledged that man is by nature evil, that is, imperfect and immature, and he said that rebirth of the spirit is essential to entering the kingdom of heaven. Evil is a deviation arising from ignorance or immaturity regarding God’s will; sin is the deliberate choice to go against it knowingly; and iniquity is the persistent repetition of that choice (148:4.2). We are born with imperfection, but whether we become sinners depends on our choices. Imperfection is not a wall that separates us from the Father, but a starting point to be overcome together with the Father’s spirit.
4 This truth transforms our self-understanding from the root. I am not an outsider who must strive to win God’s favor, but a child who already bears the Father’s spirit within. There is no need to begin our faith in guilt and fear. Rather, the way of faith is to trust the Father’s spirit within us and to respond to its guidance a little more each day. The way to God is not a journey in search of a distant palace, but the act of listening to the One who has already come to dwell within us.
5 And this truth applies equally to others. The Father’s spirit dwells within the people I meet as well. Even if they do not yet know it, they are sons and daughters of God in the making. Therefore we can treat no one as a person with whom God has nothing to do. May this be a day of remembering the Father’s spirit within, both before the mirror and before the faces of our neighbors.
PR As you begin the day, quietly confess that the Father’s spirit dwells within you, and ask him for guidance for the day.
== d293 148:5.3 He Does Not Send Affliction
REF: «Pero, hijo mío, debes saber que el Padre no aflige a sus hijos deliberadamente. El hombre desencadena sobre sí mismo aflicción innecesaria como resultado de su negación persistente a marchar en los buenos caminos de la voluntad divina. La aflicción está en potencia en el mal, pero buena parte de ella se produce por el pecado y la iniquidad. Muchos acontecimientos inusitados han acaecido en este mundo, y no es raro que todos los hombres pensadores se preocupen por el espectáculo que presencian de sufrimiento y aflicción. Pero puedes estar seguro de una cosa: el Padre no envía aflicción como castigo arbitrario de la fechoría. Las imperfecciones y desgracias del mal son inherentes; los castigos del pecado son inevitables; las consecuencias destructoras de la iniquidad son inexorables. El hombre no debe culpa a Dios por las aflicciones que son el resultado natural de la vida que él elige vivir; tampoco debe el hombre quejarse de esas experiencias que son parte de la vida tal como se la vive en este mundo. Es la voluntad del Padre que el hombre mortal trabaje con perseverancia y firmemente hacia el mejoramiento de su condición en la tierra. La aplicación inteligente permitirá al hombre sobreponerse a buena parte de su miseria en la tierra.
0 Nathaniel had long wrestled with the purpose of affliction. It was hard for him to understand why good people suffer and why God permits it. Before anything else, Jesus makes one thing clear to him: “⟦Q 148:5.3⟧the Father does not purposely afflict his children.⟦/Q⟧” This simple declaration shakes to the roots many misconceptions about suffering, above all the old notion that behind every misfortune lies God’s punishment. The problem of suffering is one of the hardest questions believers of every age have had to face.
1 Jesus carefully distinguishes the causes of affliction. “⟦Q 148:5.3⟧The imperfections and handicaps of evil are inherent⟦/Q⟧” in an evolving world; “⟦Q 148:5.3⟧the penalties of sin are inevitable; the destroying consequences of iniquity are inexorable.⟦/Q⟧” And “⟦Q 148:5.3⟧man brings down upon himself unnecessary affliction as a result of his persistent refusal to walk in the better ways of the divine will.⟦/Q⟧” Yet in none of these cases does God “⟦Q 148:5.3⟧send affliction as an arbitrary punishment for wrongdoing.⟦/Q⟧” Affliction is for the most part the natural result of universe laws interlocking with human choices.
2 In the section that follows, Jesus interprets the book of Job and stresses the same lesson (148:6). Job’s friends insisted that suffering was proof of sin, but that was a misunderstanding of God. Jesus also warned against the tendency to lay at God’s door everything people do not understand (148:5.4). Making God the direct cause of every event may seem to honor him, but in truth it is a road to doubting his love.
3 What, then, should we do in the face of suffering? Jesus’ answer is remarkably practical. We should neither blame God nor complain of life, but “⟦Q 148:5.3⟧work persistently and consistently toward the betterment of [our] estate on earth.⟦/Q⟧” “⟦Q 148:5.3⟧Intelligent application would enable man to overcome much of his earthly misery.⟦/Q⟧” Disease can be reduced by medicine, poverty by wise cooperation, conflict by understanding and dialogue. It is not resigning ourselves to suffering as God’s will, but striving to overcome it, that is the Father’s will.
4 This teaching also changes our attitude toward those who suffer. To probe for the cause when someone meets with hardship and to search out their fault is the mistake Job’s friends made. Our task is not to judge but to stand beside them, and to look together for practical ways to lighten their load. The Urantia Book says that difficulties can become part of the experience that grows the soul (154:2.5), but this does not mean glorifying suffering; it means drawing meaning even out of it.
5 If there is something painful in your life today, first lay down the thought that God is punishing you. The Father is beside you even in that pain, and he longs to give you strength to overcome it wisely. When we choose trust instead of resentment and effort instead of resignation, affliction can become, instead of something that breaks us, a tool that shapes us into deeper and more mature souls. He is not the one who inflicts pain, but the one who walks with us through it and holds us up.
PR Write down one difficulty you are facing now, and carry out one small thing you can do today to improve it.
