T: Melchizedek Prepared the Way
0 Paper 93 tells the story of Machiventa Melchizedek, who appeared in the flesh on Urantia about two thousand years before Christ. He came as an emergency bestowal to guard the light of truth that was dying out after the default of Adam and Eve. Dwelling at Salem, he taught the one God and proclaimed the good news that salvation is obtained through faith. Today's passage sums up his mission in a word: Melchizedek prepared the way and set the monotheistic stage of world tendency for the bestowal of an actual Paradise Son of the one God.
1 Let us notice the phrase prepared the way. Melchizedek's mission was not complete in itself but was preparation for a greater work. He laid the groundwork in human minds so that when Michael, that is, Jesus, came, people would be able to understand him. Had Jesus come to a world with no concept of the one God, his teachings would hardly have been received. The seed Melchizedek sowed grew through Abraham, Moses, and the prophets, and at last prepared the stage for the coming of Jesus. His sojourn in the flesh lasted less than a hundred years, but the way he prepared endured for thousands.
2 The passage says that Melchizedek vividly portrayed this God as the Father of all, and represented him to Abraham as a God who would accept man on the simple terms of personal faith. This simple gospel was so far ahead of its time that many could not understand it, yet the truth did not vanish but carried on. Because Abraham held fast to this truth, he is remembered by many today as the father of faith.
3 The passage's final sentence is moving. Michael, when he appeared on earth, confirmed all that Melchizedek had taught concerning the Paradise Father. Across a span of two thousand years the teachings of the two joined into one. Truth does not change with the passing of time. The God of love whom Melchizedek taught, the God who receives us by faith, was the same as the Father Jesus revealed. Though ages change and expressions differ, the heart of truth is always the same.

The stories of God told to our children, the kindness shown to a friend, the service quietly rendered in our community accumulate unseen and will one day help someone's spiritual awakening. Even if we never see the fruit ourselves, sowing seed is never in vain. God's work is always like a relay race carried on across many generations.
5 Today, live with the awareness that your life is preparing the way for someone. Do not be disappointed even if there is no visible result. Just as the seed Melchizedek sowed blossomed two thousand years later, everything done in love and truth will surely bear fruit in God's time.
PR: Today, sow a small seed of God's love in someone's life, and leave its fruit to God.

T: God Agrees to Do Everything
0 At the heart of Paper 93 stands the covenant between Melchizedek and Abraham. Melchizedek told Abraham to count the stars of heaven and promised that his descendants would be as many; Abraham believed, and it was counted to him for righteousness. Today's passage reveals the meaning of this covenant. This covenant of Melchizedek with Abraham represents the great Urantian agreement between divinity and humanity whereby God agrees to do everything; man only agrees to believe God's promises and follow his instructions. Within this short covenant lies the whole secret of living by faith.
1 The structure of this covenant is truly astonishing. In an ordinary contract, both parties exchange things of equal value. But in this covenant God does everything, and man has only to believe and follow. It is a promise so favorable to man that it seems almost unfair. Yet that is exactly where the essence of God's love lies. God is not one who seeks to extract something from us but one who seeks to give us everything. Our part is only to trust and accept that love.
2 The passage says that heretofore it had been believed that salvation could be secured only by works, sacrifices and offerings. As we saw in Paper 89, humanity long bargained with the gods, trying to buy their favor with offerings. But Melchizedek again brought to Urantia the good news that salvation, favor with God, is to be had by faith. This is also the heart of the gospel Jesus later taught. The kingdom of God is not something bought by effort but a gift received by faith.
3 But the passage adds a sad fact as well. This gospel of simple faith in God was too advanced; the Semitic tribesmen subsequently preferred to go back to the older sacrifices and atonement for sin by the shedding of blood. Why did people abandon such good news? Perhaps because they felt secure only when they had offered something and made an effort. Paying the price themselves felt more certain than believing in a love freely given. This attitude lingers in us today too. Rather than receive grace, we want to feel that we have earned our worthiness.
4 A life of faith is not a life of doing nothing. In the covenant, man agrees to follow God's instructions. But that obedience is not a price paid to gain salvation; it is a response to love already received. A child who trusts the father finds joy in doing the father's will. Acts done grudgingly out of fear and acts done gladly out of love may look alike outwardly, but their roots are entirely different. That is why the life of faith is not a burden but freedom, not duty but joy.
5 Today, consider what you are striving to do in order to earn God's love. Then lay that burden down and believe that God has already promised to do everything. When we gladly follow his will on the foundation of that trust, we, like Abraham, enjoy the peace of being counted righteous by faith. Faith does not remove our share of effort, but it lifts fear and anxiety from that effort.
PR: Set aside the striving to earn God's love, and gladly do one good deed in gratitude for the love already received.

T: Victorious Without Striving
0 Paper 94 shows how the teachings of Melchizedek spread eastward and left their mark on the religious thought of India and China. In sixth-century B.C. China a great teacher named Lao-tse appeared. Building on the concepts of the Salem tradition, he taught that Tao was the One First Cause of all creation. Today's passage says that his understanding of the eternal purpose of God was clear, and it presents his teaching: The Absolute Deity does not strive but is always victorious; he does not coerce mankind but always stands ready to respond to their true desires.
1 The words does not strive but is always victorious beautifully express the way God rules. Worldly powers win by pushing with force and crushing their rivals. But God does not rule that way. He respects our free will and never drags us along against it. Just as water does not fight the rock but flows gently around it and in the end wears it away, God's will works quietly and patiently until it is at last fulfilled. The Urantia Book says that the will of God is eternal in patience and eternal in the inevitability of its expression.
2 The words that he does not coerce mankind but always stands ready to respond to their true desires are also a deep comfort. God waits for us to open our hearts. When we truly seek him and long for the good, he always responds. The Thought Adjuster, too, never oppresses us from within but gently leads us to choose the higher thing for ourselves. Because coerced love is not love, God waits for our free response. The patience of the God who waits shows the depth of his love.
3 The passage then gives Lao-tse's teaching about the true religionist. Expressing the truth that it is more blessed to give than to receive, he said that the good man seeks not to retain truth for himself but rather attempts to bestow these riches upon his fellows, for that is the realization of truth. Truth is not a treasure to hoard but a spring to let flow. When we share the truth we have received, it does not diminish; rather, it grows deeper within us. Jesus too taught: freely you have received, freely give.
4 Finally the passage says that the will of the Absolute God always benefits, never destroys; the purpose of the true believer is always to act but never to coerce. This shows how believers should live in the world. We must be active in doing good, but we must never force our convictions on others. The many historical tragedies in which people were oppressed and coerced in the name of religion were the result of forgetting this teaching. Just as God does not coerce us, we must not coerce our neighbors.
5 Today, look for the places where you are trying to gain something by striving and pushing. In your family, at work, or in your faith community, rather than forcing your will through, try doing good gently and patiently, as God does. And share without reserve the truth and love you have received. Love that does not strive wins in the end, in the deepest place.
PR: Today, when you want to insist on your own way, step back and do good gently and patiently instead.

T: Proclaiming the Gospel of Jesus

All Urantia is waiting for the proclamation of the ennobling message of Michael, unencumbered by the accumulated doctrines and dogmas of nineteen centuries of contact with the religions of evolutionary origin. Paper 196 distinguishes between the religion of Jesus and the religion about Jesus, saying that people became so absorbed in worshiping Jesus that they lost the religion Jesus himself had (see Paper 196). Then the gospel of Jesus can cross the walls of religion and reach every human heart.

T: Man Proposes, God Disposes
0 Paper 95 introduces Amenemope, a great teacher of Egypt. Today's passage summarizes his central teachings. This wise man of the Nile taught that riches take themselves wings and fly away, that all things earthly are evanescent. When we look to them, we gain a center that does not waver. The saying that man proposes but God disposes humbles our plans and releases us from clinging to outcomes. The faith that planning is our part while the outcome is in God's hands keeps us from collapsing even in the face of failure.
4 The passage says that Amenemope's teachings, translated into Hebrew, determined the philosophy of the Old Testament Book of Proverbs, and translated into Greek, they gave color to all subsequent Hellenic religious philosophy. The wisdom of one man flowed across many peoples and ages. Truth knows no borders, and God has taught humanity through teachers of many peoples. That the voice of an Egyptian sage is contained in the wisdom of the Proverbs we read reminds us once again that God's truth is not the exclusive property of any one people.

T: A Growing Concept of God
0 Paper 96 tells how the Hebrews came to understand their God, Yahweh. At first Yahweh was a rough nature god associated with the volcano of Mount Sinai. Moses labored heroically to lift this tribal deity toward the Most High God whom Melchizedek had taught. Today’s passage points to the most unique and amazing feature of Hebrew religious history: the continuous evolution of the concept of Deity from the primitive god of Mount Horeb, up through the teachings of their successive spiritual leaders, to the Deity doctrines of the Isaiahs, who proclaimed that magnificent concept of the loving and merciful Creator Father.
1 These words teach us far more than a fact of religious history; they reveal something about the very nature of faith. God himself does not change. But man’s understanding of God grows. The Yahweh the Hebrews first knew was a jealous and wrathful god of war who struck down his enemies. Yet through Samuel and Elijah, Amos and Hosea, Micah and Jeremiah, and finally the Isaiahs, that understanding widened and deepened into a God of justice, a God of love, the Father who is Creator of the whole universe (see Paper 97).
2 This evolution was possible because every age had its courageous spiritual leaders. They were not content with the common understanding of their day; they laid hold of a higher truth. At times they met the opposition of priests and kings and were ignored by the people. After Moses died, the lofty concept he had taught quickly declined, and the people went back to the golden calf. But the spark of truth never went out. When one man fell, another rose up and carried the flame forward. Truth reaches beyond any single lifetime, passing from generation to generation and growing a little brighter each time.
3 The Urantia Book teaches that we should read the Hebrew scriptures with this evolutionary perspective in mind. The portraits of God in the Bible are not all on the same level. Some passages show a primitive tribal deity; others come close to the loving Father whom Jesus revealed. If we fail to see this and accept every verse as equally true, we fall into confusion. But once we understand the flow of this evolution, we can see in the scriptures a majestic journey in which the understanding of God grows ever brighter.
4 The same principle applies to our personal faith. Each of us must grow in our understanding of God. If we are still holding on to the picture of God we had as children, our faith has stalled. As we pray, worship, study truth, and experience God in daily life, our concept of God grows broader and deeper. We walk within our own lifetime the road the Hebrew people traveled over many centuries. At the end of that road stands the loving Father whom Jesus showed us. Living faith refuses to stay with yesterday’s understanding and moves on today toward a greater light.
5 Today, quietly write down what kind of God you understand God to be right now. Then hold that understanding up against the Father whom Jesus revealed, and see how close it comes. If fear and severity still linger there, take time to picture him anew as the Father of love and mercy.
PR: Write down three words that describe God as you see him, and compare them with the Father whom Jesus revealed.

0 The last section of Paper 96 deals with two books of Hebrew scripture, the Psalms and Job. Of the Psalms today’s passage says that no collection of religious writings gives expression to such a wealth of devotion and inspirational ideas of God as the Book of Psalms. We often think of the Psalms as the work of one man, David, but in fact they are a collection of praises and prayers left by countless believers from many eras and many lands. Some came from the wisdom of Egypt, some from the Salem missionaries of Mesopotamia, and some were written by the prophets of the exile. God receives not only our joy but our sorrow and confusion as well. And in such honest prayer our concept of God steadily grows.
