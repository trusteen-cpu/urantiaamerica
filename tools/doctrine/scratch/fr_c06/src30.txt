ch30:0 "The three preceding chapters, on religion, worship, and prayer, spoke of the way by which man comes to God. But the one who walks that way is a being with a body, and the body falls ill and the mind is wounded. What, then, is the relation between sickness and suffering and God? Is illness the punishment for sin? Does faith cure disease? Why did Jesus at times heal the sick and at other times decline to heal? The Urantia Book gives consistent answers to these questions. This chapter begins with mankind's age-old theories of disease and then examines in turn the true causes of suffering, the healing ministry of Jesus and its limits, and the relation between faith and material means, before asking what the full restoration of body, mind, and spirit means."
ch30:1 "1. Mankind's Age-Old Theories of Disease"
ch30:2 "Before speaking of healing, The Urantia Book first shows how human beings have understood disease. To primitive man sickness and death were inexplicable, and whatever could not be explained was laid to the spirits."
ch30:4 "Because disease was regarded as a spirit phenomenon, the shaman was medicine man and priest and doctor all at once (90:3.2). Paper 90, “Shamanism—Medicine Men and Priests”, groups into five the theories people put forward to explain the cause of suffering. The primitive mind lacked facts, but it was logical in its own way."

ch30:6 "The fourth theory, the idea that sickness is the punishment for sin, the revelator says, still survives widely today (90:3.8). Only with the fifth theory did man begin to look at disease without fear."
ch30:8 "Yet this ancient history holds a fact that today's reader should not overlook. The theories of the ancients were wrong, but their treatments were not always in vain. They had unbounded faith in their methods of treatment, and the revelator says, “and that, in itself, is a powerful remedy” (90:4.1). Nor is this fact true only of ancient times."
ch30:10 "The power of faith over body and mind operates ahead of the rightness or wrongness of any theory. This fact will prove an important clue to understanding the healings of Jesus later on."
ch30:11 "2. Suffering Is Not God's Punishment"
ch30:12 "The idea that sickness is the punishment for sin still prevailed in Jesus' day. The rabbis taught that all cases of blindness from birth were caused by sin (164:3.3). When Nathaniel asked whose sin had caused the blindness of a certain beggar, Jesus answered, “Neither did this man sin nor his parents” (164:3.7). That blindness had come in the natural course of events. When John asked at Bethsaida about the sufferings of the innocent, Jesus set out three causes of suffering."
ch30:14 "The third cause is the harvest of persistence in rebellion against the righteous rule of heaven. To this belongs the fact that the natural order of this planet has been many times upset by the sinful adventures of rebels (148:5.2). Yet none of the three causes is a punishment sent directly by God. Jesus made this point clear again and again."

ch30:17 "This teaching frees the sufferer from a double burden. On top of the pain of illness, he need not also bear the fear of having been forsaken by God. At the same time, the teaching returns responsibility to man. Man should not blame God for the natural results of the life he chooses to live, and “Intelligent application would enable man to overcome much of his earthly misery” (148:5.3)."
ch30:18 "3. The Healings of Jesus and Their Limits"
ch30:19 "Jesus healed many of the sick. Yet The Urantia Book does not portray those healings as the center of his proclamation of the gospel. When nearly a thousand sick people thronged to him at sundown in Capernaum, Jesus was deeply moved, but he knew a principle."
ch30:21 "That day Jesus passed the decision about healing to the will of his Father, and since the Father's will interposed no objection, 683 persons were made whole in a moment of time (145:3.10). But the outcome showed that Jesus' principle was right."
ch30:23 "Those who had been healed waited late into the evening to thank him, but Jesus, much perturbed by what had happened, withdrew. When he returned late, he refused their congratulations and adoration, only saying:"
ch30:25 "At dawn the next day, when the apostles told him that the sick were thronging again, Jesus still departed for other towns. He said that his mission was the revelation of the Father and the proclamation of the kingdom, and that many of those who flocked to him for the healing of their bodies were not coming to seek truth and salvation (145:5.6). The healing wonders were not a part of his plan of proclaiming the kingdom; they came incidentally, because a being with creator prerogatives was on earth in association with divine mercy and human sympathy (145:3.15). The healings of Jesus recorded in The Urantia Book are not all of one kind."

ch30:27 "4. Faith and Healing"
ch30:28 "As the table shows, many of Jesus' healings were not miracles he consciously willed. The key lay in the faith of the one healed. When Veronica, long afflicted with a hemorrhage, touched the hem of his garment in the crowd, Jesus called her before him and corrected her misunderstanding."
ch30:30 "The revelator honestly states that it was never revealed just what occurred in such cases of spontaneous healing (149:1.3). He offers only the opinion that three factors worked together."

ch30:33 "The expression 'suffered men to heal themselves' is important. The initiative in healing lies with the one who believes. That faith sought spiritual benefit rather than bodily restoration. By contrast, some sought healing for wholly selfish purposes. A rich widow of Tyre acted as if the power of God could be bought with money; she sought only the cure of her physical ailments and never became interested in the gospel of the kingdom (149:1.9)."
ch30:34 "The recoveries at the Bethsaida hospital are explained by the same principle. During the time a thousand patients recovered there, no supernatural miracle ever occurred, yet the patients kept saying that Jesus had healed them (148:2.1). The revelator explains the nature of those recoveries in this way."
ch30:36 "A man at the pool of Bethesda illustrates this principle most simply. For many years he had been afflicted by the infirmities of his troubled mind and had waited for somebody to help him. Rejoicing at Jesus' words, he picked up his bed and went home, even though it was the Sabbath day."
ch30:38 "Faith is not magic; it is a power that raises up a person bound by fear and helplessness."
ch30:39 "5. Material Means and Prayer"
ch30:40 "If faith is so important to healing, is medicine unnecessary? The Urantia Book's answer is decisive. When Jesus healed the blind Josiah, he deliberately used spittle, clay, and the pool of Siloam, and he had a clear teaching purpose in doing so."
ch30:42 "The Bethsaida hospital was run in the same spirit. Elman, the Syrian physician, and his associates used all known material methods of treatment together with the spiritual practices of prayer and faith encouragement (148:2.1). Medicine and faith do not compete; they co-operate. The place of prayer is set within that co-operation."
ch30:44 "Even in actual bacterial disease, prayer has added to the efficacy of other remedies, and it has turned many a complaining invalid into a paragon of patience (91:6.2). But prayer must not take the place of care. Jesus taught this at Jotapata."
ch30:46 "6. The Full Restoration of Body, Mind, and Spirit"
ch30:47 "What, then, is the ultimate goal of healing? To the sick gathered at the pool of Bethesda Jesus gave not a miracle but a message. That message reverses the priorities of healing."
ch30:49 "These priorities agree with the definition of his mission that Jesus gave to Nathaniel. When the problems of the spirit are solved, the mind is quickened, and a quickened mind deals better with material problems."
ch30:51 "That Jesus first said to the paralytic, “Son, fear not; your sins are forgiven” (148:9.2), and that the healing of the epileptic boy is recorded as “a true healing of a double affliction, a physical ailment and a spirit malady” (158:5.5), point in the same direction. True healing is the restoration of the whole person."

ch30:53 "Such healing is possible even when the body is never cured. Job never learned the reason for his suffering, yet he attained a vision of God. Jesus summed up that victory in these words."
ch30:55 "The healing of the body ends someday, but the peace of soul that comes from the vision of God continues beyond death. It is worth recalling that one of the marks of genuine faith seen in Chapter 27 was the maintaining of poise in the midst of disease and acute suffering (101:3.8)."
ch30:56 "In Closing: Beyond the Body to the Whole Person"
ch30:57 "The Urantia Book's teaching on healing unfolds in this way. For long ages mankind blamed disease on spirits, magic, and sin, but Jesus made clear that suffering is not an arbitrary punishment from God. Suffering comes from the accidents of time and the imperfections of evil, from the consequences of sin, and from the harvest of rebellion. Jesus healed many of the sick, yet he did not build a spiritual movement on material wonders, and many of his healings were wrought by people's own living faith. Faith does not replace medicine, and prayer does not replace loving care. And the ultimate goal of healing goes beyond the restoration of the body to the restoration of the whole person, cleansed of spiritual disease and moral infirmity. With this, the doctrines of salvation and the religious life in Part Six are complete. Part Seven, building on these teachings, examines the relationship between the religion of Jesus and Christianity."
