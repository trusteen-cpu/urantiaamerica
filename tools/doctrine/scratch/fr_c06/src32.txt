ch32:title "Christianity and The Urantia Book"
ch32:sub "Continuity and Expansion"
ch32:0 "The previous chapter examined how The Urantia Book views the origin, history, and future of Christianity. This chapter goes a step further and asks how The Urantia Book itself stands in relation to Christianity. Does it present itself as a new religion that replaces Christianity, or does it seek to carry forward something within Christianity and to enlarge something else? To answer, we must first know how The Urantia Book understands revelation. When revelation is seen not as a series of disconnected events but as a continuing process, the relation between Christianity and The Urantia Book appears not as opposition but as continuity and expansion. This chapter examines that relation in four layers: the principle of continuing revelation, what The Urantia Book carries forward, what it seeks to enlarge, and the relation between the church and the kingdom."
ch32:1 "1. Revelation Continues and Grows"
ch32:2 "The first principle The Urantia Book lays down about revelation is that revelation is progressive. Not all truth is given in a single revelation; rather, light increases step by step through the history of a world."
ch32:4 "The same paragraph also gives the reason. If revelation is to uplift evolutionary religion, it must not stray too far from the thought of its age, and so “Always must the religion of revelation be limited by man's capacity of receptivity” (92:4.1). In keeping with this principle, The Urantia Book names five revelations of epochal significance in the history of Urantia. The fourth is Jesus of Nazareth, and the fifth is the Urantia Papers."

ch32:6 "Of the five revelations, the one directly connected with Christianity is the fourth. The Urantia Book summarizes the revelation of Jesus in these words."
ch32:8 "The phrase “has generally persisted” pinpoints the role of Christianity. That the teachings of Jesus have survived for nearly two thousand years is owing to Christianity, which has passed them on and guarded them. Christianity was the vessel that carried the fourth revelation from generation to generation. The fifth revelation did not come to break that vessel. It came to bring out once again what the vessel holds."
ch32:9 "In this list The Urantia Book places the revelation of Jesus before itself. The fifth revelation does not seek to surpass the fourth but to shed fresh light on it. At the same time, The Urantia Book frankly states the limits of its own revelation."
ch32:11 "This refusal to exalt itself as the final revelation is important for understanding its relation to Christianity. The Urantia Book does not seek to tear down the truths of Christianity and sit in their place. Rather, as one link in a continuing revelation, it seeks to make brighter the light held by the link before it."
ch32:12 "2. What It Carries Forward: Human Records and Concepts"
ch32:13 "The way The Urantia Book carries Christianity forward appears most clearly in the principles by which it narrates the life of Jesus. The midwayer narrator states that the aim was not to discard the Gospels but to make use of them (121:8.1). After explaining one by one the circumstances in which the four Gospels were written, he assesses their authors this way."
ch32:15 "The narrator further says that he drew on concepts of Jesus' teachings gathered from more than two thousand human beings, from the time of Jesus down to his own day, and turned to revelation only when human expression was lacking (121:8.13). The Urantia Book thus stands upon the insights accumulated by the God-knowing men and women of Christian history, and it honors them."
ch32:16 "Its view of Scripture follows the same principle. Jesus taught Nathaniel that the Scriptures are the work of men and reflect the degree of enlightenment of their times, yet he did not belittle their worth."
ch32:18 "These words distinguish between honoring the Scriptures and making an idol of them. Jesus said that the Scriptures also contain much that misrepresents the Father in heaven, but he did not consider that this erased the value of the Scriptures as a whole. The Scriptures are sacred because they are the record of men who were searching for God (159:4.4). The Urantia Book's attitude toward the Scriptures is the same. It does not discard them; it seeks to reveal more clearly the very God whom their writers were seeking."
ch32:19 "What Jesus took issue with was not the Scriptures themselves but the doctrine that asserts their infallibility. He made clear where the authority of truth resides."

ch32:22 "3. What It Enlarges: The Religion of Jesus"
ch32:23 "The greatest thing The Urantia Book seeks to add to Christianity is not a new doctrine but the religion of Jesus himself. The revelators set the religion Jesus founded side by side with the religion Paul founded."
ch32:25 "The same paragraph says that both concepts were potential in Jesus' divine-human life. What The Urantia Book hopes for, then, is not to cast off the religion of Paul but to recover the other half it missed and bind the two concepts into one. That missing half is the struggling human Jesus."
ch32:27 "Recovering the human Jesus is not merely a matter of historical reconstruction. In this way he became “the new and living way whereby all mortals may so ascend from humanity to divinity” (196:2.4). When faith that worships Jesus is joined by faith that trusts the Father together with Jesus, Christianity regains its original breadth. This is also why The Urantia Book narrates the life of Jesus at such length and in such detail. If Christianity is to regain its power, it must above all meet Jesus himself once more."

ch32:30 "4. Doctrines Carried Forward and Corrected"
ch32:31 "The Urantia Book sheds new light, one by one, on the core doctrines of Christianity. Its method is consistent: it preserves the spiritual truth contained in a doctrine and removes the mistaken ideas that have wrapped around that truth. The Trinity is a good example. After explaining that the early Christian concept of the Trinity was comprised of the Universal Father, the Creator Son of Nebadon, and the Mother Spirit of the local universe (104:1.12), the revelators render this judgment on its value."
ch32:33 "The doctrine of the atonement follows the same path. The Urantia Book finds the purpose of the incarnation not in appeasing an angry God but in revealing the Father's love. Yet it adds that even the one who framed the atonement doctrine realized something of this truth."
ch32:35 "This correction does not diminish Jesus. Paper 188 says that though it may hardly be proper to call Jesus a sacrificer or a ransomer, “it is wholly correct to refer to him as a savior” (188:4.7). Jesus showed the way of salvation more clearly and certainly. The standard of correction is the God whom Jesus revealed. Once the Father is grasped as true and loving, the primitive notion of God as an offended monarch has no place left to stand."
ch32:37 "So it is with the doctrine of original sin. The Urantia Book says that though the animal nature may be hereditary, “sin is not transmitted from parent to child” (188:4.5). Sin is an act by which the individual will rebels against the Father's will, and so salvation too is a gift that the individual receives by faith. Concerning the kingdom of God as well, The Urantia Book looks ahead to the day of return to Jesus' own concept."

ch32:40 "5. The Church and the Kingdom: Cocoon and Butterfly"
ch32:41 "The relation between Christianity and The Urantia Book becomes most delicate in its view of the church. The Urantia Book acknowledges that the church was an inevitable and useful social result of Jesus' life and teachings (170:5.10). The trouble lay in the church's displacing the kingdom. Yet the revelators see the kingdom within this church not as dead but as sleeping."
ch32:43 "The same paper also likens the church to a shadow. A shadow is not the substance, but it tells us that the substance exists."
ch32:45 "A cocoon is not a prison that confines the butterfly but a house that protects it. This image shows how The Urantia Book regards the church. Jesus did not found the church, but neither did he abandon it. As long as the church proclaims the name of Jesus and leads people to read his words, the kingdom can grow within it. What The Urantia Book says to the church is not that it should close its doors but that it should dare to espouse the Master's program (195:10.10)."
ch32:47 "The true church that The Urantia Book portrays, then, is not a visible organization. Nor does the path to reuniting a Christianity divided into sects lie in organizational merger."
ch32:49 "Uniformity puts everyone into the same mold; unity is the oneness of different people through faith union with the same Jesus. This invisible brotherhood may embrace many different churches and groups, but within it there is no place for sectarian rivalry or claims of spiritual infallibility (195:10.14)."

ch32:51 "In Closing: The Same Jesus, a Brighter Light"
ch32:52 "The relation between The Urantia Book and Christianity can be summed up this way. Revelation continues and grows, and The Urantia Book, as a link that comes after the revelation of Jesus, casts its light anew. It carries forward the Gospels, the Scriptures, and the human insights of Christian history; in each doctrine it preserves the spiritual truth while correcting the mistaken ideas; and it restores the religion of Jesus to the religion about Jesus. The Spirit of Truth given at Pentecost prepared for this expansion from the very beginning."
ch32:54 "The anchor holds fast, and the light grows. The life of Jesus is fixed as a fact of history that cannot be rewritten. But the meaning of that life unfolds ever more deeply in each generation under the guidance of the Spirit of Truth. The understanding Christianity grasped in its first centuries cannot be the whole of that meaning, nor can the understanding The Urantia Book now presents be the last. What The Urantia Book offers Christianity is not a different Jesus but a broader understanding of the same Jesus. And that hope reaches even to the uniting of divided believers."
ch32:56 "With this, the seven parts of the book come to an end. The Afterword looks back on how these doctrines, which began with God and passed through man, history, Jesus, and salvation to arrive at Christianity, converge into one truth and one way."

ch33:label "Afterword"
ch33:title "One Truth, One Way"
ch33:0 "Over thirty-two chapters this book has set out the doctrines of The Urantia Book one by one. It began with God and passed through the universe and the celestial beings, man, the history of mankind, Jesus, and salvation and the religious life, arriving at last at Christianity. In each chapter it asked for the definition, the basis, the structure, and the consequences of a doctrine, and at every link it set down the text. Looking back over that road now, we find that doctrines which seemed to be separate subjects are joined in a single stream. Every doctrine comes forth from the Universal Father and returns to the Universal Father."
ch33:1 "At the very opening of its first paper, The Urantia Book already declared the direction of the whole book. All the will creatures of the universe are on the same journey, and at the end of that journey is the same One."
ch33:3 "Within this passage the doctrines find their places. The doctrines of the I AM, the Trinity, and the Supreme Being tell from whom this journey begins and toward whom it moves. The doctrines of the organization of the universe, the Sons of God, and the angels tell in what order, and with whose help, the journey is made. The doctrines of personality, mind, the Thought Adjuster, and the soul tell what the traveler who sets out on this journey is made of. The doctrines of human history tell what setbacks the journey has met on this planet and how it has been restored. And the doctrine of Jesus shows the example of one who walked the journey from beginning to end."
ch33:4 "The doctrines of salvation and the religious life speak of the door by which we enter the journey and of the way we walk. Salvation is a gift received by faith; faith is the way to know God; and the kingdom of heaven is the brotherhood of those who, by that faith, have God as their Father. Worship and prayer are the communion we share with the Father along the way, and eternal life is the promise that the journey does not end at death. Finally, the doctrines of Christianity and The Urantia Book tell how this truth has been passed down, obscured, and brought to light again in the history of mankind. Seen in this way, the seven parts are not seven subjects but seven facets of a single journey."
ch33:5 "It is no accident that the doctrines are joined in this way. Because truth comes from the one God, the doctrines that reflect that truth do not collide with one another but illuminate one another. Readers of this book have likely had the experience, more than once, of finding that a deep understanding of one doctrine opens another of its own accord. Know personality, and survival comes into view; know the Thought Adjuster, and prayer comes into view; know the life of Jesus, and the future of Christianity comes into view. This is why this book has tried to read the doctrines not as items to memorize one by one but as a chain of logic."

ch33:7 "One truth gives rise to one way. In The Urantia Book that way is not a system of doctrine but a person. Spending the last night with his apostles, Jesus answered Thomas, who asked about the way, in these words."
ch33:9 "Read within the whole logic of The Urantia Book, these words take on a broader meaning. Jesus is the way because he revealed the Father most fully and walked in person the way by which man ascends to the Father. That way is not confined to any one people or any one church. Since the Spirit of Truth was poured out upon all flesh, every truth-loving soul can stand upon that way. And within the one who walks it, the Thought Adjuster sent by the Father walks along."

God is our Father, and therefore all men are brothers. The fatherhood of God and the brotherhood of man: this is the one truth of which The Urantia Book speaks, and to live out that truth in love and service is the one way."
