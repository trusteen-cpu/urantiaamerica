ch28:0 "In the previous chapter religion was defined as the experiencing of divinity in consciousness. When that experience seeks nothing and turns toward God alone, The Urantia Book calls it worship. Worship is not merely one part of religious experience; it is its summit. The Urantia Book portrays worship as an activity that runs through the whole career of an ascending creature, beginning in the brief life on earth and reaching all the way to Paradise. This chapter asks in turn what worship is, why it is for its own sake, to whom it is directed, how it takes place within a person, and what it means for life and destiny."
ch28:1 "1. The Definition of Worship: The Highest Privilege"
ch28:2 "The most majestic definition of worship in The Urantia Book appears in Section 7 of Paper 27, “Conductors of Worship”, which deals with the directors of worship on Paradise. Before describing worship on Paradise, the revelator first makes plain what worship is for every intelligent being."
ch28:4 "This definition speaks of worship in the language of relationship. Before it is a rite of offering something to God, worship is the recognition of, and delight in, the intimate relationship that already exists between Creator and creature. The quality of worship, then, is not determined by the splendor of a ceremony or the length of the words spoken. The same paragraph goes on to say how worship deepens."
ch28:6 "Worship grows along the same path as the knowledge of God. The more deeply God is known, the wider worship becomes, until at last it is the highest delight a creature knows. Worship is a joy that springs from knowing, and that joy in turn deepens the knowing. Jesus expressed the same fact in terms of the relation between part and whole."
ch28:8 "If worship is a relationship, it cannot be a solitary monologue. From the human side worship is a subjective experience, but what that experience reaches is the most real of realities. Paper 196, “The Faith of Jesus”, makes this plain."
ch28:10 "2. Worship Is for Its Own Sake"
ch28:11 "Nothing reveals the nature of worship more sharply than a comparison with prayer. The two are often offered together, but they part company in motive. Section 3 of Paper 5, “God’s Relation to the Individual”, entitled “True Worship”, states the difference this way."
ch28:13 "We do not worship God because of what he gives. “we simply worship God for what we comprehend him to be” (5:3.3). The same paragraph states the motive of worship still more clearly."
ch28:15 "Here worship leaves the logic of exchange entirely behind. Just as we love a person not for what he has but for who he is, worship delights in God for his own sake. That is why, the moment any self-interest enters worship, the character of the act changes."
ch28:17 "This does not make prayer inferior. The same paragraph adds that there is no reason why prayer should not be addressed to the Father as a part of true worship (5:3.4). Jesus taught the two functions as a pair: prayer sustains life, and worship creates it anew."

ch29:0 "In the previous chapter worship was defined as an act that asks for nothing. Is prayer, then, which does ask for something, inferior to worship? The Urantia Book does not say so. Prayer is the act of coming to God with the needs of oneself and one's neighbor, and through those very needs it becomes a way of growth. Jesus introduced prayer to his apostles in these words."
ch29:2 "As breath keeps the body alive, prayer keeps the soul alive. This chapter asks what prayer is, where it came from and how it has grown, what it changes and what it does not, and how it is answered. The clues to these questions lie in Paper 91, “The Evolution of Prayer”, and in the teaching Jesus gave his apostles at Gilboa, at Jotapata, and on the road from Bethany."
ch29:3 "1. The Definition of Prayer: The Attitude of the Soul Toward the Spirit"
ch29:4 "In his discourse at the Gilboa encampment, Jesus defined prayer not as a fixed formula but as an attitude of the soul. John the Baptist had taught his disciples a short prayer, but he did so to give them something to teach the multitude, not to provide a set form for the expression of their own souls (144:2.1)."
ch29:6 "Three things are contained in this definition. Prayer is a personal and spontaneous expression. Prayer is fellowship between the Father and his child. And prayer leads to a person's spiritual progress. Words, therefore, are not the essence of prayer. Paper 91 says, “God answers the soul's attitude, not the words” (91:8.12). The words of a prayer are merely the intellectual channel in which the river of spiritual supplication flows."
ch29:7 "Prayer is a subjective act, but it is not shut up within the subjective. When a person reaches out, God responds. Paper 91 makes known what form that response takes."
ch29:9 "What genuine prayer gives a person points in the same direction. The fruit of prayer is not outward things but inward growth."

ch29:12 "The Urantia Book does not portray prayer as complete from the beginning. Prayer grew as human self-consciousness grew. Its starting point was the capacity to be conscious of another being."
ch29:14 "Just as a child talking with an imaginary playmate turns monologue into dialogue, primitive man first learned to speak toward someone. That alter ego was at first a fiction, but over the generations it was progressively exalted until at last it reached the One God."

ch29:32 "The same principle settles the relation between prayer and effort. Prayer is not a means of exemption from toil but a source of strength for those who toil. Paper 91 states the balance this way."
