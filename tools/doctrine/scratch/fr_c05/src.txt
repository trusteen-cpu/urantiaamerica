ch23:6 = "This passage shows the three elements of salvation at once. The gift comes from the Father, the Sons reveal it, and man receives it by faith. Salvation is not a passive event of escaping punishment but an active event of entering the family of God. Yet even that faith is not a merit that man produces by himself: “Even this saving faith you have not of yourselves; it also is the gift of God”(143:2.7). ch23:14 = "If forgiveness is inherent in God, where does the sense of separation come from? ch25:1 = "1. Definition: A Kingdom Founded on Fatherhood and Brotherhood"
ch25:2 = "In explaining Jesus' concept of the kingdom, Paper 170 first sets out its foundation. The kingdom does not begin with an institution or a territory. It begins with two truths."
ch25:4 = "Why these two? If God is a Father, then all men are his children; and if all are children of one Father, then men are brothers to one another. Fatherhood is the cause, and brotherhood is its result. That is why Paper 52 says that the brotherhood of man “is, after all, predicated on the recognition of the fatherhood of God”(52:6.7). Yet these two are not simply placed one after the other; on the level of personality they interlock as part and whole."
ch25:6 = "God does not love mankind as a mass but loves each person as an individual child. Yet because that love reaches all people alike, love directed to the individual becomes in itself the brotherhood that binds the whole. The kingdom of heaven is the state in which this very paradox is realized among men. The kingdom begins in the experience of the individual but never stays with the individual. In this respect the kingdom of heaven is also connected with the uniqueness of personality seen in Chapter 9. Personalities can be associated, but they are not added together. The kingdom is not a collective that melts people into a single mass but a family formed under one Father by sons and daughters who are each unique. Where the whole embraces the individual without the individual vanishing into the whole, there is the kingdom Jesus spoke of."
ch25:7 = "2. Background: Ideas of the Kingdom in Jesus' Day"
ch25:8 = "When Jesus used the expression ‘kingdom of heaven,’ it already carried many meanings. Jesus did not discard the expression; he took up its highest heritage but filled it with new content. Using the word people were waiting for could open their ears, but the old expectations clinging to it constantly bred misunderstanding. The text says that even the gospel writers were colored by the belief that Jesus would soon return to establish the kingdom in power, and that his teachings were distorted for that reason (170:2.10). Jesus, however, did not connect the establishment of the kingdom with the idea of his return to this world."

ch27:34 = "5. Religion and Theology, Philosophy, and Science"
ch27:35 = "If religion is experience, what is theology? The Urantia Book does not discard theology, but it fixes its place clearly. Theology is an honest attempt to interpret experience, and it cannot take the place of experience. Hence, “No matter how illusory and erroneous one's theology, one's religion may be wholly genuine and everlastingly true” (103:9.2). Conversely, when theology takes the place of experience, religion loses its life."
ch27:37 = "This sentence candidly admits the many errors that have piled up in the history of religion, yet it safeguards religion itself. That religionists have believed much that was false does not invalidate religion, because religion rests not on interpretation but on the recognition and experience of values (103:1.5). For the same reason, religion cannot be judged from outside. The revelator says that religion “can never be observed, much less understood, from the outside” (101:2.16). Among those who have had a similar experience no argument about God is needed, and to one who lacks such assurance no argument is convincing."
ch27:39 = "Nor is religion at war with science or philosophy. The three are different ways of approaching reality, and revelation binds them together."

ch27:53 = "In Closing: Experience, the Way to Know God"
ch27:54 = "The Urantia Book's teaching on religion forms a single chain. Religion is the experiencing of divinity in consciousness. That experience springs from the Adjuster who indwells a person, and it first appears as a moral choice that rises above selfishness. According to the quality of the experience, religion divides into the religion of fear, the religion of authority, and the religion of the spirit, and Jesus called people to the religion of the spirit, which asks for the whole person. Theology, philosophy, and science interpret and assist this experience but cannot replace it, and true religion proves itself by its fruits in adversity. The act in which this experience turns most purely toward God is worship. The next chapter examines worship, which The Urantia Book calls 'the highest privilege and the first duty of all created intelligences.'"
