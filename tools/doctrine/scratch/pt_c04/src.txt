ch17:title "The World-Wide Spread of the Melchizedek Teachings"
ch17:0 "The earlier chapters of Part Four looked at plan and setback. The rebellion of Caligastia overthrew the spiritual order of the planet, and the default of Adam put the plan of biologic uplift awry. This chapter takes up the first link of recovery. Heaven did not abandon isolated Urantia. When the light of truth was going out, a Melchizedek Son came in human form and taught the one God and the favor received through faith, and that teaching permeated every religion of East and West. This chapter asks: Why did he come, and what did he teach? How did that teaching spread, and why did it grow dim in place after place? And what did all of this prepare?"
ch17:1 "1. A Fading Light and an Emergency Son"
ch17:2 "The Melchizedek Sons are known as emergency Sons, who take up their work when special problems arise (93:0.1). Twelve Melchizedeks became the receivers of Urantia after the secession of Caligastia, and after the default of Adam they returned and again took charge of the planet (93:0.2). But the world they faced was this."
ch17:4 "By about 3000 B.C. the concept of God had grown very hazy in the minds of men (93:1.1). The receivers knew that Michael's bestowal would take place on this planet, but they did not know when. They asked for help, but the only answer they received was to maintain truth by methods of their own choosing until the bestowal Son should come (93:1.2). As a result, one of the receivers volunteered."
ch17:6 "This bestowal was not part of the planned dispensations. By the order heaven had established, a Magisterial Son or a bestowal Son should have come after Adam. But on a world where that order had been disrupted by rebellion and default, a celestial being volunteered to fill the gap. Recovery began not with the mechanical progress of a plan but with a voluntary decision of love. The two setbacks seen in the previous chapters both arose from impatience and self-assertion. Lucifer asserted self-determination, and Eve wanted immediate results. The volunteering of Machiventa stands on the opposite side. He did not assert himself but came down to a lowly place, and he did not hurry for results but prepared the way for a Son who would come two thousand years later."
ch17:7 "2. The Sage of Salem"
ch17:9 "He first appeared to man in the tent of Amdon, a Chaldean herder, and within a few years he had gathered disciples who later formed the nucleus of the Salem community (93:2.1, 93:2.4). After his departure Salem became the city of the Jebusites, and later it was called Jerusalem. He lived as men live, but he left no offspring, and during his incarnation he received a Thought Adjuster (93:2.6–2.7)."

ch17:14 "The worship of Salem was very simple. Every person who entered the Melchizedek church committed to memory and signed three beliefs: to believe in the one Universal Father and Creator; to accept the covenant that God grants his favor to faith, not to sacrifices and burnt offerings; and to keep the seven commandments and tell the good news to all men (93:4.2–4.4). Even this short creed was far too advanced for the people of that day."

ch17:17 "No sacrifices of any kind were permitted at Salem. Knowing how difficult it is suddenly to uproot long-established customs, Melchizedek gave the sacrament of bread and wine in place of the blood sacrifice, but he never wholly eradicated the sacrificial tendency of his followers, Abraham included (93:4.14). The teaching of favor received through faith collided head-on with the old religious feeling that sought to buy the goodwill of the gods by sacrifice. He touched nothing else. He did not attempt to reform the moral code, to change the habits of the world, or to spread scientific truths (93:4.15). This restraint stands in sharp contrast to the error later committed by the missionaries in Mesopotamia. Melchizedek kept his mission narrow and clear."
ch17:19 "4. The Covenant with Abraham"
ch17:20 "To keep truth alive there had to be people to receive it and carry it on. The Melchizedek receivers had long been watching the ancestors of Abraham, and the children of Terah fulfilled their expectations (93:5.3). The revelator here makes one distinction clear."
ch17:22 "At first Abraham dreamed of conquering Canaan and of a temporal kingdom. Melchizedek persuaded him to accept the spiritual concept of the kingdom of heaven in place of material conquest, and at Salem he made a formal covenant with him (93:6.1, 93:6.3)."
ch17:24 "The structure of this covenant is the doctrinal center of this chapter. Until then people believed that salvation could be secured only by the works of sacrifices and offerings. Melchizedek again brought the good news that the favor of God is to be had by faith (93:6.4). The teaching that salvation is a gift received through faith becomes the theme of Chapter 23. And as whom did he present this God to Abraham?"
ch17:26 "Melchizedek decided to depart before Abraham's death. The reason is significant."
ch17:28 "When the bearer of truth becomes an object of worship, the truth is obscured. He wanted not himself but the one God to take root in people's minds. One night he retired to his tent, and in the morning he was not there (93:8.1). His disappearance left Abraham with a lifelong sorrow, and the organization at Salem nearly vanished, yet the traditions of that era became the foundation on which Moses later led the Hebrew slaves out of Egypt (93:9.1–9.2)."

ch17:30 "The missionaries of Salem went out even to the remotest tribes of Africa and Eurasia."
ch17:32 "They reached Europe and the British Isles, the Andonites of Iceland, and, passing through China, even Japan (93:7.2). But the results were vague. Except in Palestine, the concept of one God never won the lasting allegiance of an entire tribe or race (93:7.3). The missionaries chiefly trained native converts so that they would become teachers among their own people (94:0.1). The revelator honors them by saying that Urantia has never had more enthusiastic missionaries than these. But enthusiasm alone could not preserve truth. In an age without printing it was very difficult to preserve truth from generation to generation. The revelator states the general reason in these words."

ch18:sub "The Bestowal of Michael"
ch18:0 "The fourth part of The Urantia Book is devoted almost entirely to the life of one man. Yet the revelators do not write that life as the biography of a great religious teacher. Jesus of Nazareth is the seventh and final “bestowal” of Michael, the Creator Son of the local universe of Nebadon. To understand Jesus, then, we must first ask what a bestowal is. Why does the Creator come down in the likeness of his own creatures? What does that act accomplish for the Creator himself, and what does it accomplish for man? This chapter builds the chain of doctrine that answers these questions. The question of who Jesus is, that is, the relation between his divinity and his humanity, is left for the next chapter."
ch18:1 "1. Bestowal Is the Nature of the Paradise Sons"
ch18:2 "Bestowal is neither a chance event nor an emergency measure. Paper 119 teaches that bestowal springs from the very nature of the Paradise Sons. Sons who resemble the Father long to come close to the lives of the creatures they have made, and that longing began with the Eternal Son."
ch18:4 "When a Creator Son takes charge of a local universe, he pledges to the Trinity that he will not assume full sovereignty until his seven creature bestowals have been certified (119:0.3). Why is such a pledge needed? The revelators state its purpose plainly."
ch18:6 "Here is the first link in the doctrine of bestowal. The Creator is by nature just and merciful, but the mercy that has known the creature's lot from within can be gained only through experience. This is exactly the experience that the Ancients of Days of the superuniverse require."
ch18:8 "This does not make bestowal a technical requirement for governing a universe. The revelators grant that a local universe could be managed efficiently without bestowal. But when those who are governed are imperfect and erring creatures, the matter is different. Efficiency is one thing; understanding is another."
ch18:10 "The doctrine of bestowal thus follows from a more basic doctrine about the character of God. The Father is love, and love is not content to look down from afar; it seeks to come near and share the experience. The bestowal of a Paradise Son is the form that this love takes within the order of creation."
ch18:11 "2. Seven Bestowals and a Sovereignty Earned by Experience"
ch18:12 "Michael bestowed himself seven times over a span of nearly one billion years (119:0.7). He already held the rights of a Creator, and had he wished, he could at any moment have asserted personal sovereignty as a Creator Son (120:0.3). But he did not wish to rule by that isolated right alone."
ch18:14 "The right of creatorship is given by birth, but the qualification to rule is earned by experience. This distinction is the second link in the doctrine of bestowal."
ch18:16 "This experience has two layers. One is living directly as the various orders of creatures; the other is voluntarily subordinating himself, in each bestowal, to a different will of the Paradise Deities. So even for the same Creator, rule after the bestowals carries a different standing from rule before them."

ch18:22 "The seventh bestowal differed fundamentally from the six before it in one respect. Previously Michael had appeared as a fully grown being of the order he had chosen; this time he had to begin from the very beginning. Immanuel spoke these words to Michael as he set out on the bestowal."

ch18:48 "6. The Consequences of Bestowal"
ch18:49 "Michael's bestowal changed not only the administration of the universe but also the situation of this planet. ch19:0 "In the previous chapter we saw why, and under what conditions, Michael came to Urantia. Now a sharper question remains. Who is Jesus of Nazareth, who came in this way? Is he God, is he man, or is he some being between the two? This question troubled his disciples while he was still alive, and it has held the minds of his followers ever since. The Urantia Book answers it with a short and clear principle, and then shows how that principle appears in Jesus' consciousness, in his titles, and in his faith. This chapter lays out that answer step by step."
ch19:1 "1. Two Origins, One Personality"
ch19:2 "The starting point of Christology is Paper 120, section 4, “The Incarnation—Making Two One.” To leave no room for misunderstanding, the revelators set a denial and an affirmation side by side."
ch19:4 "The origins are two. He is a Creator Son who came from Paradise, and he is a man born of a woman. But the personality is one. It is not that someone who is God and someone else who is man live together in Jesus. Nor is there any particular moment at which this union began."
ch19:6 "The revelators use the Trinity as an analogy. Just as the Trinity is three and yet one Deity, so God and man in Jesus are two and yet one. What, then, grew during his life? The answer is consciousness. The only progressive factor in this relationship was the human mind's increasingly self-conscious realization and recognition of the fact that he was God and man (120:4.2). On this point the Jesus of The Urantia Book differed from every messianic concept of his day. The Jews had pictured the Messiah as a man, as a superhuman, and even as a divine being, “but they never entertained the concept of the union of the human and the divine”(136:1.6)."

ch21:26 "Among Jesus' discourses, the one that most concisely shows the center of his teaching is the sermon at the ordination of the apostles, commonly called the Sermon on the Mount. The heart of that sermon lies in distinguishing two dimensions of love."
ch21:28 "Brotherly love is love between equals. Fatherly love is a love that sees another as God sees him, a love that desires the other's growth even when he is not lovable. Jesus did not demand the impossible of his apostles. But he taught them what happens when they strive to be perfect even as the Father in heaven is perfect."
ch21:30 "Fatherly love is not an emotion squeezed out by the will; it is the result of a changed way of seeing. When we come to see people as God sees them, the way we love changes accordingly. The text of the ordination sermon presents this goal directly."
ch21:32 "This love also sets the direction of apostleship: “You are commissioned to save men, not to judge them.”(140:3.17) One who loves as a father would rather bring his brother home than sit in judgment on his faults. The eight beatitudes show the way to this love in two stages. The first four are attitudes of faith; the last four are the reactions of fatherly love that issue from those attitudes (140:5.4–5)."

ch21:38 "4. A Positive Religion"
ch21:39 "Another part of the framework of Jesus' teaching is its positive character. In place of negative compliance with ceremonial requirements, he enjoined the positive doing of what religion requires."
ch21:41 "This principle also changes the meaning of the teaching about turning the other cheek. Jesus did not teach passive nonresistance."
ch21:43 "Turning the other cheek is not a matter of standing idle under abuse but a positive act to save an erring brother, and going the second mile is a challenge to the unjust man (159:5.9, 159:5.15). Jesus distinguished three ways of meeting evil."

ch21:50 "5. The Manner of His Teaching: Simplicity and Symbol"

ch21:57 "In Closing: From the Father to the Brother"
ch21:58 "The framework of Jesus' teaching is a single chain. God is a loving Father who goes out first in search and accepts us even before we repent. ch22:label "Chapter 22"
ch22:title "The Final Week of Jesus"
ch22:0 "As we saw in Chapter 18, Michael's sovereignty had already been won before his baptism, and the life and death that followed were “a purely personal ministry”(186:5.3). What, then, was the final week in Jerusalem for? This chapter does not retell the events of that week one by one. Instead it asks what doctrine the week reveals. As what kind of king did Jesus enter Jerusalem? What does the Last Supper commemorate? Was the cross the will of the Father, or the work of men? What does the death on the cross mean, and what kind of event was the resurrection? On many points the answers of The Urantia Book to these questions differ from the commonly accepted interpretations."
ch22:1 "1. Entering as the King of Peace"
ch22:2 "Until then Jesus had always suppressed public acclaim that would exalt him as the Messiah. But now that the Sanhedrin had already resolved on his death, he decided to enter Jerusalem publicly (172:3.2). The revelators first make clear the motive for that decision."
ch22:4 "The manner he chose was itself the message. What he had not achieved by direct teaching, namely, that his kingdom was not of this world but purely spiritual, he sought to show by a symbol (172:3.6)."
ch22:6 "The crowd waved palm branches and shouted hosanna. But Jesus, looking down on the city from the brow of Olivet, wept. He knew how deep the acclaim really went."
ch22:8 "When the kingdom they expected did not come, that same crowd forsook him a few days later. Here lies the lesson of the entry. Emotional enthusiasm is not faith. Faith is tested when expectations collapse, and acclaim that rested on national hopes for a Messiah turned at once into rejection when those hopes were dashed. The next day Jesus drove the animals and the money-changers out of the temple courts. This act springs from the same principle. He did not use force to seize power, but he would not stay silent before the commercialization of religion that exploited the poor. Enormous profits piled up in the temple precincts from the sale of sacrificial animals and from money-changing, and poor pilgrims sometimes paid a week's wages for a pair of doves (173:1.2)."
ch22:10 "The entry and the cleansing explain each other. The entry on a donkey showed that his kingdom was not a kingdom of force; the cleansing of the temple showed that neither was it a kingdom indifferent to injustice. The King of peace was resolute before a religion that trampled on the weak. After these two events the religious leaders hardened their determination to do away with him (173:1.9)."

ch22:12 "2. The Last Supper: A New Passover Without Blood"
ch22:13 "On Thursday evening, at the Passover supper, Jesus washed the feet of his apostles (179:3) and instituted a new supper of remembrance. The revelators explain the relation between the old Passover and the new supper in these words."
ch22:15 "The liberation commemorated by the new supper is not the liberation of a nation but the liberation of the individual soul from the bondage of ceremonialism and selfishness. And the cup and the bread were not symbols of a sacrificial victim. The cup is the sign of the bestowal of the Spirit of Truth, and the bread is the word of the Father revealed in the Son, the bread of life (179:5.1, 179:5.3). Above all, this supper is not a rite in memory of someone who is absent."
ch22:17 "As he finished the supper, Jesus said, “This is the new Passover which I leave with you”(179:5.9). The revelators record that they ended this celebration as a bloodless Passover (179:5.10). ch22:19 "3. Gethsemane: The Will of the Father and the Work of Men"
ch22:20 "After the supper Jesus prayed three times in Gethsemane. In this scene his humanity is revealed most deeply. He grieved over parting from his apostles, he looked for a way of escape, and he was weary (182:3.7–9). Yet each time he prayed, something grew."
ch22:22 "What, then, was the cup he said he would drink, the will of the Father? Here The Urantia Book makes a decisive distinction. The Father's will was that the Son should finish his earth career naturally, as all mortals do. But the cruel manner of that death was not the Father's will."
ch22:25 "This distinction comes from the doctrine of the character of God. A loving Father does not plan the torture of his Son. Just as ordinary people cannot expect to be spared suffering in their last hours by a special dispensation, neither did he expect it (183:1.2). ch22:26 "The struggle of Gethsemane ended in victory. Before Judas and the soldiers arrived, he had regained his poise."
ch22:28 "This victory was not a victory of miracle but of faith. He went through the very fear that people go through, and his trust in the Father was greater than that fear. So Gethsemane becomes a path that people can walk after him."

ch22:30 "4. The Trials and the Cross: Dying Like a King"
ch22:31 "When he was arrested, Jesus knew that his work on earth was finished, and he took no interest in the details of the trials (186:2.1). ch22:37 "5. The Meaning of the Cross"
ch22:38 "The Urantia Book does not accept substitutionary and atonement interpretations of the cross. Jesus did not die to atone for the guilt of mankind, to appease the wrath of God, or to pay a ransom (188:4.1, 188:4.3). This subject is taken up further in Chapters 23 and 31. The question here is what meaning, then, the cross still holds. The revelators' answer lies not in the fact of the death but in its manner."
ch22:40 "What do that manner and that spirit reveal? First, that the relation between God and man is not a relation of the law court but of the family."
ch22:42 "Second, that this love is strong enough to overcome sin and evil. With the prayer “Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do.” he made the cross a symbol of the triumph of love over hate (188:5.6)."
ch22:44 "The gospel, therefore, does not stand leaning on the cross. That man may know by faith that he is a son of God would have been just as true had Jesus not died in this way (186:5.9). The cross did not create that truth; it illuminated it most brightly. The revelators close section 5 of Paper 188 in these words."
ch22:46 "What needed reconciling was not God but man's thinking. God was Father from the beginning, and the cross was a revelation that brings man to realize that fact. This is why the revelators say that, while it is not fitting to call Jesus a ransomer, it is entirely proper to call him a savior (188:4.7)."

ch22:49 "6. The Resurrection: A Morontia Transit"
ch22:50 "At dawn on Sunday, April 9, A.D. 30, Jesus came forth from the tomb in morontia form (189:1.1). To prevent misunderstandings about the resurrection, the revelators make three points clear. The first concerns the body."
ch22:52 "He did not come forth in the glorious form of Michael, but in the very form of morontia personality in which ascending mortals awaken on the first mansion world (189:1.8–9). This is the heart of the doctrine of the resurrection. Jesus' resurrection followed the same path as human survival. The path he walked is the path people walk. By dying as a mortal and living again as a morontia being, he showed that survival after death is not a theory but a path actually traveled. What, then, of the empty tomb? The celestial hosts had been permitted to dissolve his body without the delays of time (189:2.7). The empty tomb is a fact, but it is not in itself the proof of the resurrection. When the fact of the empty tomb was joined to the fact of the resurrection, the belief arose that the material body had been raised, but truth about spiritual reality is not established merely by combining apparent facts (189:2.6)."

ch22:55 "In Closing: The Week That Love Won"
ch22:56 "The doctrine of the final week runs like this. Jesus entered Jerusalem not as a warrior but as the King of peace; he opposed the exploitation of religion but did not seek to seize power. He left a new Passover without blood, so that his followers would commemorate the brotherhood of the faith sons set free from ceremonialism and selfishness. The cup he drank was the Father's will that he live mortal experience to the end, while the cruelty of the cross was the work of men. The cross was not a sacrifice to appease wrath but the final revelation of a love that places the relation between God and man upon the family basis, and the resurrection was the event in which he first walked the path of survival that people will walk. With this the study of Jesus in Part 5 comes to an end. The next part examines how man follows this path to reach God, beginning with the doctrine of salvation."
