### p160.001
Introduction
  HEAD: []

### p160.002
On September 18, when they were given a week of rest, most of the apostles went home to see their families. But Nathaniel and Thomas were very busy. The reason was Rodan, a Greek philosopher from Alexandria. Rodan had recently become a disciple of Jesus, and he had come to Magadan hoping to weave together the philosophy he had studied and the teachings of Jesus. Jesus welcomed Rodan warmly but did not take part in the discussions himself; he told Nathaniel and Thomas to listen carefully to him and share the gospel with him. This paper sums up the talks Rodan gave.

### p160.003
1. Rodan’s Greek Philosophy
  HEAD: ['1: La philosophie grecque de Rodan']

### p160.004
Rodan said that human life is moved by three forces: urges, desires, and lures that draw us toward something higher. A person of strong character is one who turns inborn urges into good social living and passing desires into higher, lasting dreams. Animals do not ask about the meaning of life, but human beings are able to see values and meanings. Rodan said that discouragement, worry, and laziness are signs of moral immaturity, and that a mature person looks on those who are less grown-up with a parent's patience.

### p160.005
To solve a problem well, we must first honestly admit what the difficulty is. People often turn away from their problems out of fear or pride, but only those with courage look the truth straight in the face. And to win the cooperation of others, tact and tolerance are a must.

### p160.006
Rodan said that the greatest way of solving problems he had learned from Jesus was the time of worship, withdrawing alone to commune with God. Jesus often prayed alone and gave his heart over to the Father, saying, “⟦Q 160:1.11⟧Not my will, but yours, be done.⟦/Q⟧” This kind of worship rests the mind, takes away fear, and gives courage. Rodan admitted that his own philosophy had lacked direction and power, and that Jesus' gospel filled in what was missing.
  Q#0 160:1.11 [None] EN: Not my will, but yours, be done.
  VERSE: Je suis profondément impressionné par l’habitude qu’a Jésus de s’en aller seul à l’écart pour une période d’examen solitaire des problèmes de la vie ; pour rechercher de nouvelles réserves de sagesse et d’énergie pour faire face aux multiples exigences du service social ; pour vivifier et rendre plus profond le but suprême de la vie en soumettant effectivement sa personnalité totale à la conscience du contact avec la divinité ; pour saisir et posséder des méthodes nouvelles et meilleures pour s’adapter aux situations toujours changeantes de l’existence vécue ; pour effectuer les reconstructions et réadaptations vitales de ses attitudes personnelles qui sont si essentielles pour apercevoir, avec une perspicacité accrue, tout ce qui est réel et valable ; et pour faire tout cela en ne visant que la gloire de Dieu — exprimer sincèrement la prière favorite de votre Maitre : « Que ta volonté soit faite, et non la mienne. »

### p160.007
2. The Art of Living
  HEAD: ['2: L’art de vivre']

### p160.008
Rodan said that what sets humans apart from animals is that they can share thoughts and ideals through words and symbols. Because of this ability to share meanings, culture and civilization come into being. The best social group is the family, and true friendship also helps a person grow a great deal.

### p160.009
Friendship and love bring four gifts. First, we can open our hearts to one another and be understood. Second, by pooling our wisdom we can avoid prejudice and narrow thinking. This is why Jesus always sent his disciples out two by two. Third, friends renew our zest and courage for living and add to our joy. Fourth, when we share hardships and sorrows, the pain becomes much smaller. Rodan said these relationships come about most beautifully in a marriage and a home where people truly care for each other.

### p160.010
3. The Lures of Maturity
  HEAD: ['3: Les attraits de la maturité']

### p160.011
Then where does the power we need to grow mature come from? Rodan said that emotional excitement actually drains our strength. The secret is worship, which is communion with God. Worship, through quiet thinking in meditation and the rest of letting go, turns weakness into strength, fear into courage, and our own will into God's will. When it becomes a habit, it is hard at first, but later it actually becomes a rest and even saves time.

### p160.012
Rodan said that there are times when we must fight for what is right, but Jesus wins the same victory more easily through tact and tolerance. He reported Jesus' saying that a “⟦Q 160:3.4⟧wise man, when seeking entrance through the locked door, would not destroy the door but rather would seek for the key wherewith to unlock it⟦/Q⟧”. A person who holds a high goal can be freed from small quarrels such as worry, jealousy, and revenge.
  Q#0 160:3.4 [None] EN: wise man, when seeking entrance through the locked door, would not destroy the door but rather would seek for the key wherewith to unlock it
  VERSE: Ma philosophie me dit qu’il y a des moments où je dois combattre, s’il en est besoin, pour défendre ma conception de la droiture ; mais je suis certain que le Maitre, d’un type de personnalité plus mature, gagnerait facilement et élégamment une victoire équivalente par sa technique supérieure et séduisante de tact et de tolérance. Bien trop souvent, quand nous luttons pour la bonne cause, cela se termine par une défaite à la fois pour le vainqueur et pour le vaincu. Hier encore, j’ai entendu le Maitre dire que « si un sage désire entrer par une porte verrouillée, il ne la fracture pas, mais cherche plutôt la clef pour la déverrouiller. » Nous engageons trop souvent une bataille simplement pour nous convaincre que nous n’avons pas peur.

### p160.013
4. The Balance of Maturity
  HEAD: ['4: L’équilibre de la maturité']

### p160.014
Even while looking toward eternal goals, we must faithfully take care of our life now. Rodan named health, clear thinking, ability and skill, the wealth needed for living, the strength to bear defeat, and education and wisdom as things life truly needs. He said that skill gives great satisfaction in life, so we should learn to do at least one thing well, and not be fooled by the sweet rewards of dishonest ways. He also told them to keep good memories, especially the precious moments of friendship, like a gallery in the mind.

### p160.015
Above all, we must learn to accept failure gracefully. If we hide failure behind a forced smile, we end up facing even greater disappointment. Success gives courage, but wisdom comes when we accept failure and learn from it. For someone who seeks God, failure is not the end; it is only one step in learning wisdom.

### p160.016
5. The Religion of the Ideal
  HEAD: ['5: La religion de l’idéal']

### p160.017
Rodan said that religion is a wholehearted response to the highest ideal that all people can honor. Religion is not just an idea believed in the head; it is action shown in one's life. And true religion seeks to change people and to change the world. Rodan said that without the supreme ideal of the eternal God, there can be no true spiritual religion.

### p160.018
Rodan said that Jesus' religion is the highest religious idea in the world. That is because Jesus did not speak of God only as a faraway ideal; he taught that God is a loving Father whom anyone can actually meet by entering the kingdom of heaven. Rodan declared, “⟦Q 160:5.8⟧I am a believer, I have embarked⟦/Q⟧”, and said he would set out on this eternal adventure.
  Q#0 160:5.8 [None] EN: I am a believer, I have embarked
  VERSE: Je ne suis pas seulement intrigué par les idéaux parfaits de la religion de votre Maitre, mais je me sens puissamment poussé à confesser ma croyance dans ses déclarations que ces idéaux de réalités d’esprit sont accessibles ; que vous et moi, nous pouvons entreprendre cette longue et éternelle aventure avec l’assurance de sa part qu’en fin de compte, nous arriverons certainement aux portes du Paradis. Mes frères, je suis un croyant, je me suis lancé, je suis en route avec vous dans cette aventure éternelle. Le Maitre dit qu’il est venu du Père et nous montrera le chemin. Je suis entièrement persuadé qu’il dit la vérité, et définitivement convaincu qu’en dehors du Père Universel, il n’y a ni idéaux de réalités ni valeurs de perfection qui soient accessibles.

### p160.019
True religion does not come from feeling or thinking that we want to be like God; it needs a living spiritual experience. Rodan's talks show the way of growing mature through friends and family, through failure and worship. If we too are good friends, are honest when we fail, and take quiet time to talk with God, we can grow more mature step by step.

### p161.001
Introduction

### p161.002
On September 25, Jesus surprised everyone by announcing that the next morning he would go with the twelve apostles to Jerusalem for the Feast of Tabernacles. But Nathaniel and Thomas were still in the middle of their discussions with Rodan, the Greek philosopher, so with Jesus' permission they stayed at Magadan a few more days. They also learned that in Alexandria Rodan had been well taught in Jesus' teachings by a former disciple of John the Baptist. During the past week Rodan had talked about his philosophy; this time Thomas and Nathaniel took turns sharing the gospel of the kingdom with Rodan, and they discussed things deeply together.

### p161.003
1. The Personality of God
  HEAD: ['1: La personnalité de Dieu']

### p161.004
The question on which Rodan and the two apostles disagreed was whether God is a personality. Put simply, a personality means a personal being who can know others and share hearts with them. Rodan argued that to be a personality, one must be able to communicate fully with an equal, and since God is infinite and has no equal, he must be beyond personality. When the two apostles asked Jesus for help, Jesus did not join the discussion; he only told Thomas that if he knew the Father spiritually, it did not matter very much what idea of him he held.

### p161.005
In the end Thomas gave up the argument, but Nathaniel won Rodan over. Nathaniel first pointed out that the Father communicates fully with the Eternal Son and the Infinite Spirit, who are his equals. He also said that the very way Jesus, the Son of God, lived sharing his heart with people was proof that God and man can communicate with each other. And he said that God must be a personality, because he is the one who made all personalities and the destination that all personalities will at last reach.

### p161.006
Rodan finally said he would confess God as a personality. But he added a condition: God must be infinitely greater than what people think of as personality. Rodan said that “⟦Q 161:1.11⟧while God must be infinitely more than a personality, he cannot be anything less⟦/Q⟧”, and he accepted Jesus as the one who shows us the Father.
  Q#0 161:1.11 [None] EN: while God must be infinitely more than a personality, he cannot be anything less
  VERSE: Après avoir entendu ces arguments, Rodan dit : « Je suis convaincu. Je professerai que Dieu est une personne si, en professant cette croyance, vous me permettez de nuancer ma profession d’une telle croyance en attachant au sens de personnalité un ensemble de valeurs plus larges, telles que suprahumaine, transcendante, suprême, infinie, éternelle, finale et universelle. Je suis désormais convaincu que Dieu doit être infiniment plus qu’une personnalité, mais qu’il ne peut rien être de moins. Je suis d’accord pour mettre fin à la discussion et pour accepter Jésus en tant que révélation personnelle du Père ; et en tant que celui qui comble toutes les lacunes de la logique, de la raison et de la philosophie. »

### p161.007
2. The Divine Nature of Jesus
  HEAD: ['2: La nature divine de Jésus']

### p161.008
The question that remained was the teaching that Jesus is divine. Nathaniel and Thomas told why they believed it. Jesus was the most unselfish friend in the world; he was the friend of sinners, and he loved even his enemies. He scolded the apostles at times, but anyone could see that he truly loved them. The more you knew him, the more you loved him.

### p161.009
The apostles believed that Jesus was always without sin. Jesus prayed for the apostles, but he never asked them to pray for him, and he never did anything he needed to repent of. The apostles' devotion came from repentance, but Jesus' devotion came from righteousness. Jesus was always moved when he saw people suffering; he was fair and yet merciful, kind and yet brave.

### p161.010
Jesus also knew about things that happened where he was not present, and he seemed to know the thoughts in people's hearts. When he asked questions, it was not to get information but to draw out what the other person was really thinking. Jesus said astonishing things like “⟦Q 161:2.9⟧Before Abraham was, I am.⟦/Q⟧” and said “⟦Q 161:2.9⟧that anyone who has seen him has seen the Father⟦/Q⟧”, and he said them as naturally as a child. The two apostles said they were convinced that Jesus was both the Son of Man and the Son of God.

### p161.011
When the discussions were over, Nathaniel and Thomas hurried to Jerusalem to join the other apostles. Rodan went back to Alexandria and taught philosophy for many years. He lived as a faithful believer all his life and gave his life in Greece during a time of persecution.

### p161.012
3. Jesus’ Human and Divine Minds
  HEAD: ['3: Le mental humain et le mental divin de Jésus']

### p161.013
The Urantia Book explains how Jesus' human mind and divine mind worked together. Jesus' awareness that he was God grew little by little until his baptism. After that, it seems, Jesus could choose for himself whether to use only his human mind or also the knowledge of his divine mind. Sometimes he knew and acted only as much as an ordinary person would; at other times he acted with wisdom beyond human ability.

### p161.014
Jesus often hid his knowledge of the future and of people's hearts, so that his disciples would not feel that all their thoughts were being read. The Urantia Book honestly says that how these two minds were joined and worked as one is a mystery we cannot fully understand. Like Rodan and the apostles, who kept talking all the way through hard questions until they found the truth, we too can honestly ask about what we wonder and think it through together.

Jesus and the ten apostles planned to travel to Jerusalem by the shorter road through Samaria. At sunset Philip and Matthew went into a village to find a place to stay, but the villagers, who strongly disliked Jews, drove them away with sticks and stones. Angry, James and John asked Jesus to call down fire from heaven to destroy the village. The group arrived at Bethany, and for about the next six weeks Jesus stayed near Jerusalem and in Bethlehem.

### p162.005
His enemies could not easily seize him. There were secret believers in Jesus even inside the Sanhedrin, and many of the people gathered for the feast were friendly toward him. His enemies also saw how bold he was and guessed that the Roman authorities might have promised to protect him. People's opinions were divided—some called him a good man, others a prophet, the Messiah, or a troublemaker—but even his enemies were amazed at the teaching of a man who had never studied under the rabbis.

### p162.006
2. The First Temple Talk
  HEAD: ['2: La première discussion au temple']

### p162.007
On the first day Jesus taught in the temple, someone asked how he could teach like that when he had never studied with the rabbis. Jesus answered that this teaching was not his own but belonged to the One who sent him, and that anyone who wanted to do the Father's will would know whether it came from God. He also said that the leaders wanted to kill him because he was teaching a gospel that set people free from heavy traditions.

### p162.008
The Sanhedrin sent an officer named Eber to arrest Jesus. Jesus told him not to be afraid but to come close and listen, and he said that he held no ill will toward anyone. After hearing Jesus, Eber went back without arresting him and reported, “⟦Q 162:2.9⟧we never heard a man speak like this man⟦/Q⟧”. Then he asked the leaders whether they could condemn a man without even hearing him.

The Urantia Book explains that this woman was being forced and used by a wicked husband, and that the husband had plotted this with Jesus' enemies.

### p162.012
4. The Feast of Tabernacles
  HEAD: ['4: La fête des Tabernacles']

### p162.013
The feast of tabernacles was a festival of thanks for the autumn harvest, and Jews came to it from all over the world, from Spain to India. People lived in booths covered with leaves, and at night they enjoyed the feast under the great candlesticks and torches that lit up the temple. The priests brought water from the pool of Siloam in a golden pitcher and poured it on the altar, a ceremony that stood for the pouring out of God's spirit. A feast that gathered so many people was the right time for Jesus to boldly proclaim his gospel before the whole world.

### p162.014

### p162.023

Jesus did not say that owning wealth was wrong in itself, but again and again he rebuked the strong for unfairly taking advantage of the weak.

It means that God treats each person with love, according to that person's needs.

### p165.002
On January 3, A.D. 30, Abner, the leader of the seventy messengers, gave his associates their final instructions and sent them out to the cities and villages of Perea. Perea was a beautiful region across the Jordan, where Jews and gentiles lived in about equal numbers. This Perean mission, which lasted almost three months, was Jesus' last ministry; after it he would go to Jerusalem to go through the final events of his life on earth.

### p165.003
There were no miracles during this time, and the women's corps, which had grown to sixty-two, took on most of the care of the sick. No part of Palestine was reached by the gospel as thoroughly as Perea. Jesus sometimes stayed at the Pella camp, and sometimes went with the twelve apostles to visit and help in the cities where the seventy were working.

### p165.004

### p165.005
By the middle of January more than twelve hundred people had gathered at Pella. When Jesus was at the camp, he usually taught the multitude at nine o'clock in the morning; Peter and the other apostles taught in the afternoon; and in the evening he held question-and-answer times with his close disciples. By the middle of March more than four thousand people were gathering every morning. Jesus decided to finish his work on earth when people's interest was at its highest, even without miracles. Most of those who gathered were seekers of truth, but there were also Pharisees among them, looking for something to criticize.

He also said that he had power to lay down his life and power to take it up again. ### p166.012
4. Teaching about Accidents
  HEAD: ['4: Une leçon sur les accidents']

### p166.013

### p166.015

### p166.016
Of all the cities of Perea, Philadelphia was the one where the most people accepted the gospel. This synagogue later became a church and a center for spreading the gospel to the lands of the east.

Abner remained a faithful teacher of the gospel until he died at the age of eighty-nine. A life that holds on to the truth even when no one notices, and a heart that knows how to be thankful for the grace received, are precious for us too.

### p170.003
1. Concepts of the Kingdom of Heaven
  HEAD: ['1: Concepts du royaume des cieux']

### p170.004
The old Hebrew scriptures held two ideas of the kingdom: a kingdom that already exists now, and a future kingdom to be completed when the Messiah came. Mixed with these were the later Jewish idea of a worldwide kingdom coming down miraculously from heaven, and the Persian teaching that good would defeat evil at the end of the world. Just before Jesus came, the Jews had combined all of these and were expecting the Messiah to come and begin an age of Jewish triumph.

### p170.005
But the kingdom Jesus always taught the apostles was a personal experience—the experience of a person in relationship with the Father in heaven and with neighbors. Jesus' last word was always “⟦Q 170:1.11⟧the kingdom of heaven within you.⟦/Q⟧” The Urantia Book names several reasons why the meaning of this saying became confused for centuries: the idea developed through many stages, the gospel moved from the Jewish land into the gentile world, and the gospel gradually became a religion about Jesus.

  HEAD: ['2: Le concept du royaume chez Jésus']

### p170.007
Jesus' kingdom stands on the truth that God is the Father of all people and that all people are brothers. Human life gains a new dignity and noble goals.

Instead of a picture of a king and his subjects, he wanted to give them a picture of the Father in heaven and his family, of liberated sons of God gladly serving one another. But he did not succeed. Jesus taught that the kingdom has a stage in this world, in which we do God's will and love our neighbors, and a stage in heaven, where that love is made complete.

Two things are needed to enter the kingdom: a childlike faith that opens the heart and trusts the Father, and a heart that hungers for righteousness and truth. ### p170.010
3. In Relation to Righteousness
  HEAD: ['3: En rapport avec la droiture']

### p170.011
Jesus taught that childlike faith is the key that opens the door of the kingdom, but that once inside, we must grow step by step. The process goes like this: when we forgive others, we experience God's forgiveness; only when we love our neighbor as ourselves can we truly forgive; this love is the highest ethics; and right conduct comes naturally from that love.

### p170.012

Since every act is judged by its motive, the highest goodness is the goodness that comes out naturally, without our even knowing it. The religion of the kingdom is personal, but its fruits spread out to families and to the whole of society.

### p170.013
4. Jesus’ Teaching about the Kingdom
  HEAD: ['4: L’enseignement de Jésus sur le royaume']

### p170.014
Jesus never defined the kingdom in a single sentence. On this day Jesus described the kingdom in five stages: the inner experience of a person with God; the brotherhood formed by believers; the superhuman kingdom of God, which includes even unseen spirit beings; a new social order in which the will of God is better done; and the age of light and life that will come in the far future. He also stressed, as the heart of the gospel, the worth of the individual, the importance of the will, fellowship with God, the joy of loving service, and the priority of spiritual things over material ones.

### p170.015
Jesus promised that he would come again someday, and he also spoke of a future kingdom, but he never said these were the same event. Yet the disciples tied the two together and expected Jesus to return soon and set up a glorious kingdom. The Urantia Book says that the world has never really tried these teachings, but that we should not be discouraged; above all, it urges us never to neglect building the kingdom within our own souls.

The Urantia Book also explains how the idea of the kingdom changed after Jesus left. According to The Urantia Book, the rise of the church itself was natural and helpful, but it was unfortunate that the church almost replaced the spiritual kingdom in the heart that Jesus had spoken of. Becoming a church member is not the same as entering the fellowship of the kingdom, because one is spiritual and the other mainly social.

### p170.018
But The Urantia Book speaks of hope. Jesus' teachings have an eternal power, and they will surely bear fruit in the minds of thinking people. The Urantia Book compares today's church to a cocoon in which the kingdom is sleeping, and says that someday it will awaken like a beautiful butterfly. It also says that there may be differences of opinion among believers, but that a lack of spiritual brotherhood cannot be excused. The kingdom of heaven is not a faraway land; it begins now, in our hearts, with the choice to follow God's will.

### p171.008
When they reached the ford of the Jordan and the people began to murmur on learning that Jesus was not going straight to Jerusalem, Jesus climbed onto a large rock and preached the sermon on “⟦Q 171:1.2⟧Counting the Cost.⟦/Q⟧” From now on, he said, anyone who wished to follow him must be ready to give everything to doing the Father's will. Someone who wants to build a tower first sits down and counts the cost, and a king about to go to war first considers whether he can win. In the same way, anyone who wants to be Jesus' disciple must first consider whether he is ready to endure persecution and disappointment.
  Q#0 171:1.2 [None] EN: Counting the Cost.
  VERSE: Quand le Maitre partit de Pella, les disciples, au nombre d’un millier environ, qui campaient avec les apôtres, le suivirent. Au gué du Jourdain sur la route de Jéricho, la moitié environ de ce groupe le quitta en apprenant qu’il allait à Hesbon, et après qu’il eut prêché son sermon sur « l’Évaluation du Prix ». Cette moitié poursuivit sa route et monta à Jérusalem, tandis que l’autre moitié du groupe suivit Jésus pendant deux semaines durant sa tournée dans les villes de la Pérée du sud.

### p171.009
Jesus warned that those who follow him hoping for honor or glory are like salt that has lost its flavor, and he told those who were not willing to drink the cup of suffering with him to go home in peace. After this about five hundred followed Jesus to Heshbon, and the rest went up to Jerusalem. Even so, the apostles still believed that after a short trial, the kingdom they were hoping for would be set up in the end.

### p171.010
3. The Perean Tour
  HEAD: ['3: La tournée en Pérée']

### p171.011
For more than two weeks Jesus and the apostles toured the villages of southern Perea where the seventy had been working. At Heshbon Jesus met Abner and urged him not to be shaken by whatever happened in Jerusalem, but to go on with his work. This was the last time Abner saw Jesus in the flesh. As he said good-bye, Jesus told Abner that he would pray that he might have the wisdom to love and understand his brethren.

### p171.012
The apostles knew that the Sanhedrin had sentenced Jesus to death and ordered that his whereabouts be reported, but this time they were not very afraid. Since they had seen Lazarus raised, they believed that in a crisis Jesus would drive back his enemies with divine power. Many of the apostles could not even imagine that Jesus, “⟦Q 171:3.5⟧the resurrection and the life⟦/Q⟧”, could die.
  Q#0 171:3.5 [None] EN: the resurrection and the life
  VERSE: La majorité des apôtres et beaucoup de ses proches disciples ne croyaient pas que Jésus puisse mourir. Croyant qu’il était « la résurrection et la vie », ils le considéraient comme immortel et déjà triomphant de la mort.

### p171.013
4. Teaching at Livias
  HEAD: ['4: Enseignement à Livias']

### p171.014
On the night they stayed at Livias, Simon Zelotes and Peter secretly got hold of more than a hundred swords and handed them out. Jesus knew about this but acted as if he did not. Early the next morning Jesus gathered the apostles and told them not to trust in human defenses such as swords. He told them plainly that the Son of Man would be handed over in Jerusalem, mocked, and killed, but that on the third day he would rise. But the apostles could not take these words as they were. In those days 'the third day' was often used to mean 'soon,' so they understood him to mean that after a short struggle the kingdom would rise.

### p171.015
Some friendly Pharisees warned Jesus to get away because Herod wanted to kill him. Jesus said that Herod would actually rather have him die in Jerusalem, and he told them to tell “⟦Q 171:4.6⟧that fox⟦/Q⟧” that he would finish his mission in a few days. Then he grieved over Jerusalem: many times he had wanted to gather her children together, “⟦Q 171:4.7⟧even as a hen gathers her own brood under her wings⟦/Q⟧”, but they would not accept him. What gave the apostles the courage to follow him to Jerusalem was the raising of Lazarus, and what held Jesus up in this hard time was the memory of the transfiguration.
  Q#0 171:4.6 [None] EN: that fox
  VERSE: Après avoir écouté ce que les pharisiens avaient à dire, Jésus répondit : « Je connais bien Hérode et sa peur de cet évangile du royaume. Mais, ne vous y trompez pas, il préfèrerait de beaucoup voir le Fils de l’Homme monter à Jérusalem et que ce soient les chefs religieux qui le fassent souffrir et mourir. Ayant souillé ses mains du sang de Jean, il n’est pas désireux de porter la responsabilité de la mort du Fils de l’Homme. Allez dire à ce renard que le Fils de l’Homme prêche aujourd’hui en Pérée, qu’il ira demain en Judée et qu’au bout de quelques jours, il aura parachevé sa mission sur terre et sera prêt pour son ascension vers le Père. »
  Q#1 171:4.7 [None] EN: even as a hen gathers her own brood under her wings
  VERSE: Puis Jésus se tourna vers ses apôtres et dit : « Depuis les temps anciens, les prophètes ont péri à Jérusalem, et il sied que le Fils de l’Homme aille dans la cité de la maison du Père pour être offert comme prix du sectarisme humain et comme conséquence des préjugés religieux et de l’aveuglement spirituel. Ô Jérusalem, Jérusalem, qui tues les prophètes et lapides les instructeurs de la vérité ! Que de fois j’aurais voulu rassembler tes enfants comme une poule rassemble ses poussins sous ses ailes, mais vous n’avez pas voulu me laisser faire ! Voici, votre maison va vous être abandonnée dans la désolation ! Vous désirerez maintes fois me voir, mais vous ne me verrez pas. Vous me chercherez alors, mais vous ne me trouverez pas. » Après avoir ainsi parlé, Jésus se tourna vers ceux qui l’entouraient et dit : « Quoi qu’il en soit, allons à Jérusalem pour assister à la Pâque et faire notre devoir en accomplissant la volonté du Père qui est aux cieux. »

### p171.016
5. The Blind Man at Jericho
  HEAD: ['5: L’aveugle de Jéricho']

### p171.017
Near the gate of Jericho there was a beggar named Bartimeus, who had been blind since his youth. He had made up his mind that if Jesus ever came again, he would ask him to open his eyes. When he heard that Jesus was passing by, he cried out, “⟦Q 171:5.2⟧Jesus, Jesus, have mercy upon me!⟦/Q⟧” and when people told him to be quiet, he cried out even louder. Jesus stopped and called him, and Bartimeus threw off his cloak and came running. “⟦Q 171:5.3⟧your faith has made you whole.⟦/Q⟧” At once he received his sight, and he glorified God and told people what had happened.
  Q#0 171:5.2 [None] EN: Jesus, Jesus, have mercy upon me!
  VERSE: La nouvelle de l’approche de Jésus avait été annoncée dans tout Jéricho, et des centaines d’habitants s’étaient rassemblés pour aller à sa rencontre. Quand cette foule revint en escortant le Maitre à son entrée dans la ville, Bartimée entendit le grand bruit du piétinement de la multitude et sut qu’il se passait quelque chose d’inhabituel. Il demanda donc à ses voisins ce qui arrivait, et l’un des mendiants répondit : « Jésus de Nazareth est en train de passer. » Quand Bartimée entendit que Jésus était à proximité, il éleva la voix et commença à crier : « Jésus, Jésus, aie pitié de moi ! » Et, tandis qu’il criait de plus en plus fort, certains accompagnateurs de Jésus allèrent vers lui et le réprimandèrent en le priant de se tenir tranquille ; mais cela ne servit à rien ; Bartimée n’en cria que plus fort.
  Q#1 171:5.3 [None] EN: your faith has made you whole.
  VERSE: Quand Jésus entendit l’aveugle crier, il s’arrêta et, quand il le vit, il dit à ses amis : « Amenez-moi cet homme. » Sur quoi, ils allèrent trouver Bartimée en disant : « Aie bon courage et viens avec nous, car le Maitre t’appelle. » À l’audition de ces paroles, Bartimée rejeta son manteau et sauta au milieu de la route, tandis que les personnes les plus proches le guidaient vers Jésus. S’adressant à Bartimée, Jésus dit : « Que veux-tu que je fasse pour toi ? » Et l’aveugle répondit : « Je voudrais que ma vue soit rétablie. » À l’audition de cette demande et devant cette foi, Jésus dit : « Tu recouvreras la vue ; va ton chemin ; ta foi t’a guéri. » Et Bartimée recouvra immédiatement la vue ; il resta près de Jésus, glorifiant Dieu, jusqu’au départ de Jésus le lendemain matin pour Jérusalem ; et, alors, il précéda la multitude en proclamant à tout le monde comment sa vue lui avait été rendue à Jéricho.

### p171.018
6. The Visit to Zaccheus
  HEAD: ['6: La visite à Zachée']

### p171.019
Zaccheus, the chief publican of Jericho, wanted very much to see Jesus, but he was short and could not see over the crowd. So he ran ahead and climbed a sycamore tree by the road. Jesus stopped under it and said, “⟦Q 171:6.1⟧Make haste, Zaccheus, and come down, for tonight I must abide at your house.⟦/Q⟧” Zaccheus was so happy that he almost fell out of the tree.
  Q#0 171:6.1 [None] EN: Make haste, Zaccheus, and come down, for tonight I must abide at your house.
  VERSE: Quand le cortège du Maitre entra dans Jéricho, le soleil était sur le point de se coucher, et Jésus était disposé à demeurer dans la ville pour la nuit. Au moment où il passa devant le bureau de douane, Zachée, le chef publicain ou percepteur des taxes, se trouvait là ; or, il désirait grandement voir Jésus. Ce chef publicain était fort riche et avait beaucoup entendu parler de ce prophète de Galilée. Il avait résolu de voir quelle sorte d’homme était Jésus la prochaine fois qu’il viendrait à Jéricho. En conséquence, Zachée chercha à se frayer un chemin à travers la foule, mais elle était trop dense, et Zachée était de petite taille, de sorte qu’il ne pouvait voir par-dessus les têtes. Alors, le chef publicain suivit la foule jusqu’au centre de la ville, non loin de l’endroit où il habitait. Voyant qu’il ne parviendrait pas à fendre la foule et imaginant que Jésus allait peut-être traverser la ville sans s’y arrêter, il courut en avant et grimpa dans un sycomore dont les branches étendues surplombaient la route. Il savait que, de cette manière, il pourrait bien voir le Maitre lors de son passage. Et il ne fut pas déçu, car, en passant par là, Jésus s’arrêta, leva les yeux vers Zachée et dit : « Dépêche-toi de descendre, Zachée, car ce soir il faudra que je demeure dans ta maison. » Quand Zachée entendit ces paroles surprenantes, il tomba presque de l’arbre dans sa hâte d’en descendre. Allant vers Jésus, il exprima sa grande joie de ce que le Maitre veuille bien s’arrêter chez lui.

### p171.020
People grumbled that Jesus was staying at the house of a sinner who took the people's money. Then Zaccheus stood up on a chair and declared that he would give half of his goods to the poor, and that whatever he had taken unjustly, he would pay back four times over. Jesus said, “⟦Q 171:6.3⟧Today has salvation come to this home⟦/Q⟧”, and said that the Son of Man had come to seek and to save the lost.
  Q#0 171:6.3 [None] EN: Today has salvation come to this home
  VERSE: Quand Zachée eut fini de parler, Jésus dit : « Aujourd’hui, le salut est venu dans cette maison, et tu es devenu, en vérité, un fils d’Abraham. » Puis, se tournant vers la foule assemblée autour d’eux, Jésus dit : « Ne vous étonnez pas de ce que je dis et ne vous offensez pas de ce que nous faisons, car j’ai constamment déclaré que le Fils de l’Homme est venu chercher et sauver ce qui était perdu. »

### p171.021
7. ‘As Jesus Passed By’
  HEAD: ['7: « Tandis que Jésus passait »']

### p171.022
Wherever Jesus went, he spread joy. The Urantia Book says that goodness wins respect, but that only when it goes together with graciousness is it loved by people. Because Jesus understood people deeply and loved them sincerely, he could be a true friend. He was never in a hurry, he did not pry into or meddle in other people's affairs, and he was a good listener. People who talked with him felt not as if they were confessing their faults but as if they were talking things over together.

### p171.023
Most of the important things Jesus did happened naturally, “⟦Q 171:7.9⟧as he passed by⟦/Q⟧”. Rather than following a plan made in advance, he always kept his heart open to meet the needs of the one person, the one child, he met along the way. Great things happened because not only did people believe in Jesus, but Jesus also deeply believed in people. The Urantia Book says that followers in every age must also learn to do good to others as they pass by, while carrying out their daily duties.
  Q#0 171:7.9 [None] EN: as he passed by
  VERSE: La plupart des choses réellement importantes que Jésus dit ou fit semblèrent se produire par hasard, « tandis qu’il passait ». Le ministère terrestre du Maitre présenta fort peu d’aspects professionnels, bien prévus, ou prémédités. Il dispensa la santé et répandit le bonheur avec naturel et grâce au cours de son voyage à travers la vie. Il était littéralement vrai qu’il « circulait en faisant du bien ».

### p171.024
8. Parable of the Pounds
  HEAD: ['8: La parabole des mines']

### p171.025
On the way up to Bethany, Jesus told the apostles the parable of the pounds. This parable was based on the true story of Archelaus, who tried to become king and was rejected by his people. Jesus told the apostles ahead of time that they were expecting to receive the kingdom in Jerusalem, but that they would be disappointed. A nobleman, leaving for a far country, gave each of his servants one pound. When he returned, one servant had made ten pounds and another five, and they received praise and reward, but the servant who, out of fear, had only wrapped the money in a napkin was rebuked.

### p171.026
Later Nathaniel summed up the parable like this: we are not responsible for what is beyond our ability; those who are faithful in small things are faithful in great things; and those who show the same faithfulness, even with fewer opportunities, receive the same reward. After lunch, Jesus pointed west with a bright face and said, “⟦Q 171:8.14⟧Come, my brethren, let us go on into Jerusalem, there to receive that which awaits us⟦/Q⟧”. Being faithful in the small things entrusted to us, without comparing ourselves with others—that is the faithfulness God wants from us.
  Q#0 171:8.14 [None] EN: Come, my brethren, let us go on into Jerusalem, there to receive that which awaits us
  VERSE: Quand ils eurent fini de déjeuner et que la multitude de ceux qui le suivaient eut continué sa route vers Jérusalem, Jésus se dressa devant les apôtres à l’ombre d’un rocher qui surplombait la route. Avec une dignité sereine et une majesté pleine de grâce, il montra l’occident du doigt et dit : « Venez, mes frères, entrons dans Jérusalem pour y recevoir ce qui nous attend. Nous accomplirons ainsi, en toutes choses, la volonté du Père céleste. »
