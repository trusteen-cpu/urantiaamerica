### p185.001
Introduction
  HEAD: []

### p185.002
A little after six o'clock on the morning of Friday, April 7, A.D. 30, Jesus was brought with his hands bound before Pilate, the Roman governor. About fifty accusers went along, including Caiaphas, members of the Sanhedrin, and Judas Iscariot, and so did the apostle John. The Jewish leaders believed that entering a gentile's building on the day of preparation for the Passover would make them unclean, so they would not go inside the governor's residence, and the trial was held on the steps outside the gate. The Urantia Book points out that while they were trying to kill an innocent man, they were carefully keeping their rules of purity, and it says they are not the only people who cling to small formalities and miss the duties that truly matter.

### p185.003
1. Pontius Pilate
  HEAD: ['1: Ponce Pilate']

### p185.004
Pilate was a capable administrator, but morally he was a coward. He did not understand the faith the Jews were willing to die for, so he clashed with them badly several times, as when he tried to bring military standards bearing Caesar's image into Jerusalem, or to build an aqueduct with money from the temple treasury. Each time he backed down in the end, so the Jewish leaders learned that they could shake him just by threatening a revolt or threatening to report him to the emperor.

### p185.005
Pilate was later removed as governor for massacring Samaritans, and he never escaped the guilt of having agreed to Jesus' crucifixion. His wife, Claudia, on the other hand, heard about Jesus and later devoted herself to spreading the gospel. The Urantia Book says that if Rome had sent a better governor, this tragedy would not have happened.

### p185.006
2. Jesus Appears Before Pilate
  HEAD: ['2: Jésus comparait devant Pilate']

### p185.007
When Pilate asked what crime they were charging him with, the leaders at first did not even name a specific charge, saying, “⟦Q 185:2.1⟧If this man were not an evildoer, we should not have delivered him up to you⟦/Q⟧”. When Pilate said he would not put anyone to death without a trial, only then did they hand him a written charge: that Jesus had corrupted the nation and stirred up rebellion, that he taught people not to pay taxes to Caesar, and that he called himself king of the Jews.
  Q#0 185:2.1 [sentence] EN: If this man were not an evildoer, we should not have delivered him up to you
  VERSE: Lorsque Jésus et ses accusateurs furent réunis devant la salle du tribunal de Pilate, le gouverneur romain sortit sur le perron et demanda en s’adressant à la compagnie assemblée : « Quelles accusations portez-vous contre cet homme ? » Les sadducéens et les conseillers qui avaient pris sur eux de se débarrasser de Jésus avaient décidé de se présenter devant Pilate pour demander confirmation de la sentence de mort prononcée contre Jésus, mais sans vouloir porter d’accusations précises. C’est pourquoi le porte-parole du tribunal du sanhédrin répondit à Pilate : « Si cet homme n’était pas un malfaiteur, nous ne te l’aurions pas livré. »
  AUTO: Si cet homme n’était pas un malfaiteur, nous ne te l’aurions pas livré

### p185.008
Jesus had never been tried on these charges, yet he gave no answer at all. He did not look at his accusers with scorn, but with deep pity and sad affection. Pilate was deeply impressed by his dignified, quiet manner, so he decided to take Jesus inside and question him privately.

### p185.009
3. The Private Examination by Pilate
  HEAD: ['3: L’interrogatoire en privé par Pilate']

### p185.010
Pilate had Jesus sit down and asked him about the taxes, and John testified that Jesus and the apostles had always paid their taxes to Caesar and to the temple. When asked whether he was a king, Jesus answered, “⟦Q 185:3.3⟧Do you not perceive that my kingdom is not of this world?⟦/Q⟧” Jesus' kingdom, he explained, is the brotherhood of people who have become sons and daughters of God through faith and by love, and it is open to both Jews and gentiles. Jesus said he had come into this world to show the Father to all people and to bear witness to the truth, and Pilate, half mocking, asked back, “⟦Q 185:3.5⟧what is truth⟦/Q⟧”.
  Q#0 185:3.3 [sentence] EN: Do you not perceive that my kingdom is not of this world?
  VERSE: Jésus dit alors à Pilate : « Ne perçois-tu pas que mon royaume n’est pas de ce monde ? S’il était de ce monde, mes disciples se battraient surement pour que je ne sois pas livré aux mains des Juifs. Ma présence ici, devant toi et dans ces liens, suffit pour montrer à tous les hommes que mon royaume est une domination spirituelle, la fraternité même des hommes qui sont devenus fils de Dieu par la foi et par amour. Ce salut est offert aussi bien aux Gentils qu’aux Juifs. »
  AUTO: Ne perçois-tu pas que mon royaume n’est pas de ce monde ?
  Q#1 185:3.5 [None] EN: what is truth
  VERSE: Alors, Pilate dit à moitié ironiquement et à moitié sincèrement : « La vérité, qu’est-ce que la vérité ? — qui la connait ? »

### p185.011
Pilate was convinced that Jesus was not a dangerous revolutionary, so he went outside and declared that he found no fault in him. But when the leaders protested fiercely, saying that Jesus had been stirring up the people ever since Galilee, Pilate tried to escape responsibility by sending Jesus to Herod, the ruler of Galilee.

### p185.012
4. Jesus Before Herod
  HEAD: ['4: Jésus devant Hérode']

### p185.013
Herod Antipas wanted to see Jesus perform a miracle with his own eyes. He did not remember that Jesus was the boy who, as a youth, had stood before him in Sepphoris to ask for the wages owed to his father, who had died there unjustly. Herod questioned him for about fifteen minutes and mocked him, telling him to show a miracle, but Jesus did not answer a single word. In the end Herod sneered at Jesus, put an old purple royal robe on him, and sent him back to Pilate. Herod, who had been tormented by fear ever since he killed John the Baptist, was freed from that fear only when he saw that Jesus was a completely different man from John.

### p185.014
5. Jesus Returns to Pilate
  HEAD: ['5: Jésus revient devant Pilate']

### p185.015
Pilate again said that Jesus was innocent and offered to have him whipped and then release him. Just then a crowd came streaming in, following the custom of releasing one prisoner at the Passover, so Pilate tried to use this chance to free Jesus and asked them whom he should release, the murderer and robber Barabbas or Jesus. But when the chief priests led the way shouting, “⟦Q 185:5.4⟧Barabbas, Barabbas!⟦/Q⟧” the crowd joined in. The people who had followed Jesus like a hero only a few days before no longer respected Jesus as he stood bound and unresisting.
  Q#0 185:5.4 [None] EN: Barabbas, Barabbas!
  VERSE: Pilate se leva et expliqua à la foule que Jésus lui avait été amené par les chefs des prêtres qui demandaient sa mise à mort en formulant certaines accusations, mais qu’il ne croyait pas que cet homme méritât la mort. Pilate dit : « Alors, qui préférez-vous que je vous relâche, ce Barabbas, l’assassin, ou ce Jésus de Galilée ? » Lorsque Pilate eut ainsi parlé, les chefs des prêtres et les conseillers du sanhédrin crièrent tous de leur voix la plus perçante : « Barabbas, Barabbas ! » Et, quand les gens assemblés virent que les chefs des prêtres voulaient que Jésus fût mis à mort, ils se joignirent rapidement aux clameurs réclamant son exécution tandis qu’ils vociféraient pour la libération de Barabbas.

### p185.016
At that moment his wife, Claudia, sent him a letter: “⟦Q 185:5.8⟧I pray you have nothing to do with this innocent and just man⟦/Q⟧”. While Pilate hesitated, the leaders stirred up the crowd even more, and no matter how many times Pilate asked, the crowd only shouted, “⟦Q 185:5.9⟧Crucify him! Crucify him!⟦/Q⟧” Pilate knew Jesus was innocent, but he did not have the courage to go against the angry people.
  Q#0 185:5.8 [None] EN: I pray you have nothing to do with this innocent and just man
  VERSE: Pilate signifia à son auditoire son désir de lire la communication qu’il venait de recevoir, avant de poursuivre l’examen de l’affaire en cours. Il ouvrit la lettre de sa femme et y lut : « Je te supplie de ne participer en rien à la condamnation de l’homme intègre et innocent que l’on appelle Jésus. J’ai beaucoup souffert en rêve cette nuit à cause de lui. » Cette note venant de Claudia eut pour effet non seulement de bouleverser Pilate et de retarder ainsi le jugement de l’affaire, mais aussi, malheureusement, de laisser aux dirigeants juifs un temps considérable pour circuler librement dans la foule. Ils en profitèrent pour inciter la populace à demander la libération de Barabbas et à réclamer à grands cris la crucifixion de Jésus.
  Q#1 185:5.9 [None] EN: Crucify him! Crucify him!
  VERSE: Finalement, Pilate s’attaqua une fois de plus à la solution du problème auquel il était confronté en demandant à l’assemblée mixte des dirigeants juifs et des gens demandant une amnistie : « Que ferai-je de celui que l’on appelle le roi des Juifs ? » Ils crièrent à l’unisson : « Crucifie-le ! Crucifie-le ! » L’unanimité de cette exigence de la part de cette foule bigarrée effraya et alarma Pilate, juge injuste et tenaillé par la peur.

### p185.017
6. Pilate’s Last Appeal
  HEAD: ['6: Le dernier appel de Pilate']

### p185.018
Jesus' friends either had not yet heard of his arrest or were hiding in fear, and the only people there were Jesus' enemies and a crowd that was easily swayed. To appeal to their pity, Pilate had Jesus whipped, which was against Roman law as well. The soldiers put a crown of thorns on him, put a reed in his hand, and mocked him, saying, “⟦Q 185:6.2⟧Hail, king of the Jews!⟦/Q⟧”
  Q#0 185:6.2 [None] EN: Hail, king of the Jews!
  VERSE: Pilate voulut faire un dernier appel à leur pitié. Ayant peur de défier les clameurs de cette foule égarée qui criait pour obtenir le sang de Jésus, il ordonna aux gardes juifs et aux soldats romains de prendre Jésus et de le flageller. C’était en soi une procédure injuste et illégale, car la loi romaine réservait uniquement la flagellation aux condamnés à mort par crucifixion. Les gardes emmenèrent Jésus pour ce supplice dans la cour ouverte du prétoire. Ses ennemis n’assistèrent pas à la flagellation, mais Pilate y assista. Avant que les flagellateurs en eussent fini avec ce flagrant abus, il leur ordonna de s’arrêter et fit signe qu’on lui amena Jésus. Avant d’attacher Jésus au poteau de flagellation et de le frapper de leurs fouets à nœuds, ses bourreaux l’avaient de nouveau vêtu de la robe pourpre et avaient tressé une couronne d’épines qu’ils posèrent sur son front. Après avoir placé un roseau dans sa main comme simulacre d’un sceptre, ils s’agenouillèrent devant lui et se moquèrent de lui en disant : « Salut, roi des Juifs ! » Puis ils crachèrent sur lui et le souffletèrent. Avant de le rendre à Pilate, l’un d’eux lui prit le roseau des mains et lui en frappa la tête.

### p185.019
Pilate brought out Jesus, covered in blood, and cried, “⟦Q 185:6.3⟧Behold the man!⟦/Q⟧” But the hearts of the people, gripped by hatred and prejudice, were not moved; instead they shouted even louder for crucifixion. When the high priest said Jesus must die because he had called himself the Son of God, Pilate began to tremble with fear that Jesus might really be a divine being.
  Q#0 185:6.3 [sentence] EN: Behold the man!
  VERSE: Ensuite, Pilate conduisit le prisonnier saignant et lacéré devant la foule bigarrée et le présenta en disant : « Voici l’homme ! De nouveau, je vous déclare que je ne le trouve coupable d’aucun crime et, après l’avoir flagellé, je voudrais le relâcher. »

### p185.022
8. Pilate’s Tragic Surrender
  HEAD: ['8: Le tragique abandon par Pilate']

### p185.023
Jesus was about to be handed over to death with no formal charge, no evidence, no witnesses, and no verdict. Afraid of a riot, Pilate released Barabbas, washed his hands with water, and said, “⟦Q 185:8.2⟧I am innocent of the blood of this man⟦/Q⟧”. But washing your hands does not make your responsibility disappear. Pilate, who knew what was right but backed away out of fear, reminds us how important it is to have the courage to stand on the side of what is right.
  Q#0 185:8.2 [sentence] EN: I am innocent of the blood of this man
  VERSE: Pilate craignait un tumulte ou une émeute. Il n’osa pas risquer de troubles de cet ordre au moment de la Pâque à Jérusalem. Il avait récemment reçu une réprimande de César et ne voulait pas risquer d’en recevoir une autre. La populace applaudit lorsqu’il ordonna de relâcher Barabbas. Il fit ensuite apporter une bassine et un peu d’eau, puis se lava les mains devant la foule en disant : « Je suis innocent du sang de cet homme. Vous êtes décidés à ce qu’il meure, mais je n’ai trouvé aucune culpabilité en lui. Occupez-vous-en. Les soldats le conduiront. » Alors, la populace applaudit et répondit : « Que son sang soit sur nous et sur nos enfants. »

John did not want to leave his Master alone, but he hurried straight to the home of Lazarus in Bethany, where Jesus' family had gathered. ### p186.003
1. The End of Judas Iscariot
  HEAD: ['1: La fin de Judas Iscariot']

### p186.004
Around half past eight in the morning, when Pilate's trial was over, Judas Iscariot appeared before the Sanhedrin and asked for his reward. He expected to be praised and honored for doing a great service for the nation, but the high priest's servant only called him out of the council chamber and handed him a bag with thirty pieces of silver. That was the price of a slave at the time. Judas felt deeply humiliated and disappointed.

### p186.005
When Judas saw from a distance the cross being raised with Jesus nailed to it, he rushed back to the Sanhedrin and cried out, “⟦Q 186:1.4⟧I have sinned in that I have betrayed innocent blood⟦/Q⟧”, but the leaders mocked him and told him that was his own problem. He threw the silver onto the temple floor and left. The Urantia Book says that at this moment Judas understood what sin really is. Sin is attractive while you are doing it, but afterward only its cold, naked results are left. In despair, Judas finally went alone to a ravine outside the city and took his own life.
  Q#0 186:1.4 [positional] EN: I have sinned in that I have betrayed innocent blood
  VERSE: Judas aperçut de loin que l’on dressait la croix où Jésus était cloué. À cette vue, il retourna précipitamment au temple, écarta de force l’huissier et se trouva en présence du sanhédrin, qui était encore en session. Le traitre était à peu près hors d’haleine et profondément bouleversé, mais il réussit à balbutier les paroles suivantes : « J’ai péché en ce sens que j’ai trahi un sang innocent. Vous m’avez insulté. Vous m’avez offert de l’argent pour ce service —le prix d’un esclave. Je me repens d’avoir fait cela ; voilà votre argent. Je veux échapper à la culpabilité de cet acte. »

### p186.006
2. The Master’s Attitude
  HEAD: ['2: L’attitude du Maitre']

### p186.007
When Jesus was arrested, he already knew that his work on earth was finished, and he did not care about the details of the trials. He did not answer the false testimony, and he kept silent before Herod, who was only curious. But he always answered clearly when asked whether he was the Son of God, and he spoke to Pilate only when it might help Pilate understand a little more of the truth. Jesus did not try to change the outcome by clever words; he believed it was the Father's will for him to go along with the natural course of human events, like everyone else.

### p186.008
Jesus did not lose his calm and dignity even in the face of insults and threats, and he did not grow angry even at the soldiers who mocked and beat him. Instead, he felt sorry for Pilate and tried to shed light into his darkened mind. The Urantia Book says that the trial was really a scene of “⟦Q 186:2.6⟧Pilate on trial before Jesus⟦/Q⟧”. When Pilate cried, “⟦Q 186:2.11⟧Behold the man!⟦/Q⟧” the cry “⟦Q 186:2.11⟧Behold God and man!⟦/Q⟧” echoed throughout the whole universe. All his life Jesus had shown God to people, and now he gave a new revelation of man to God.

### p186.009
3. The Dependable David Zebedee
  HEAD: ['3: David Zébédée, celui sur qui on pouvait compter']

### p186.010
After Jesus was handed over to the Roman soldiers, the temple guards went to Gethsemane to arrest his followers, but they had all scattered already. The guards burned the empty camp and went back, and the Sanhedrin relaxed, thinking that Jesus' followers were too frightened to do anything. David Zebedee had moved his tent ahead of time and, hidden away, kept up his headquarters for passing on news. Within five minutes he sent messengers off to many places, including Bethsaida, Alexandria, and Damascus, and all that day he sent news every half hour to the apostles and to Jesus' family.

### p186.011
David was almost the only follower who took Jesus literally when he said he would “⟦Q 186:3.4⟧rise again on the third day⟦/Q⟧”. When he found that the others did not expect a resurrection so soon, he kept quiet about it, but he arranged for his messengers to gather early Sunday morning at the home of Nicodemus. His simple but dependable preparations would play a big part two days later.
  Q#0 186:3.4 [None] EN: rise again on the third day
  VERSE: Ce David Zébédée, dont le mental était si particulier, était le seul des principaux disciples de Jésus à être enclin à prendre au pied de la lettre et comme un fait positif l’affirmation que le Maitre allait mourir et « ressusciter le troisième jour ». David avait une fois entendu Jésus faire cette prédiction. Ayant une tournure de mental pratique, il se proposait maintenant de rassembler ses messagers de bonne heure, le dimanche matin, chez Nicodème afin de les avoir à sa disposition pour répandre la nouvelle, au cas où Jésus ressusciterait d’entre les morts. David ne tarda pas à découvrir qu’aucun des disciples de Jésus ne s’attendait à le voir revenir si tôt de la tombe. Il parla donc peu de sa conviction et ne dit pas qu’il avait mobilisé tout son corps de messagers de bonne heure le dimanche matin, sauf aux coureurs qui avaient été dépêchés le vendredi matin vers les villes lointaines et les centres de croyants.

### p186.012
4. Preparation for the Crucifixion
  HEAD: ['4: Préparatifs pour la crucifixion']

### p186.013
After Pilate washed his hands, Jesus was handed over to the Roman soldiers. The soldiers took off the robe Herod had put on him and dressed him in his own clothes again.   HEAD: ['1: Jésus — l’Homme']

### p196.006

The Urantia Book says that Jesus, like us, climbed step by step from human consciousness to the realization of divinity, with the help of faith and the Thought Adjuster, and it describes these seven stages, from the arrival of the Adjuster, through his baptism, the Mount of Transfiguration, and the resurrection and ascension, to the final embrace of the Father.

### p196.008
2. The Religion of Jesus
  HEAD: ['2: La religion de Jésus']

### p196.009
The Urantia Book says, “⟦Q 196:2.1⟧You may preach a religion about Jesus, but, perforce, you must live the religion of Jesus⟦/Q⟧”. The gospel of the kingdom was founded on Jesus' own religious experience, while Christianity was founded mainly on Paul's experience. People came to worship the divine Christ, but the man Jesus, who bravely walked the path of faith until he became one with God, was largely forgotten. The Urantia Book says that Jesus was truly man and truly God, and that his life is a living way that everyone can walk.
  Q#0 196:2.1 [positional] EN: You may preach a religion about Jesus, but, perforce, you must live the religion of Jesus
  VERSE: Un jour, une réforme dans l’Église chrétienne pourrait avoir un impact assez profond pour revenir aux purs enseignements religieux de Jésus, source et aboutissement de notre foi. On peut prêcher une religion à propos de Jésus, mais obligatoirement, on doit vivre la religion de Jésus. Dans l’enthousiasme de la Pentecôte, Pierre inaugura involontairement une nouvelle religion, la religion du Christ ressuscité et glorifié. L’apôtre Paul transforma plus tard ce nouvel évangile en christianisme, religion où il incorpora ses propres vues théologiques et décrivit sa propre expérience personnelle avec le Jésus de la route de Damas. L’évangile du royaume est fondé sur l’expérience religieuse personnelle de Jésus de Galilée ; le christianisme est fondé presque exclusivement sur l’expérience religieuse personnelle de l’apôtre Paul. Presque tout le Nouveau Testament est consacré non à décrire la vie religieuse significative et inspirante de Jésus, mais à analyser l’expérience religieuse de Paul et à décrire ses convictions religieuses personnelles. ### p196.010
Jesus did not see the world as basically evil, and he helped people live comfortably in this world. ### p196.011
3. The Supremacy of Religion
  HEAD: ['3: La suprématie de la religion']

### p196.012
The Urantia Book says that a living religious experience is a power that solves many of life's difficulties. Religion does not make problems disappear, but it shines light on them and helps us rise above them. People are educated by facts, ennobled by wisdom, and saved by faith. There are three proofs that God's spirit lives within us: that we can love others without expecting anything back, that we can realize the universe is friendly to us, and that we can worship God.

### p196.013
Spiritual joy cannot be seen under a microscope, and love cannot be weighed on a scale, but they are real. The Urantia Book says that if the Hebrews had a religion of morality and the Greeks a religion of beauty, Jesus showed a religion of love: the security of living in the Father's love and the joy of sharing that love with our neighbors. The Urantia Book explains that morality is the soil in which religion grows, but morality alone cannot show the ultimate good; religion makes morality higher and more meaningful. It also says that while art seeks beauty, science seeks the mysteries of the universe, and philosophy seeks the meaning of life as a whole, religion is a person's highest reaching out to find God and to become like him. It adds that some people's lives are too great and noble to stop at merely being successful. And The Urantia Book tells us, “⟦Q 196:3.33⟧Be not discouraged⟦/Q⟧”. Humanity is still growing, and the revelation of God given through Jesus will not fail.

### p196.014
We have now come to the last paper of The Urantia Book.
