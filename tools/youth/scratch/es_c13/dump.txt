## p138.001
Introduction
   HEAD: []

## p138.002
On the afternoon of the day he preached about the kingdom, Jesus called his six apostles together and talked about his plan to visit the cities around the Sea of Galilee. He did not invite his brothers James and Jude to this meeting, because he had decided not to include his close family in the apostolic group. Because of this the distance between Jesus and his family slowly grew, and this gap was not fully closed until after his death and resurrection. His mother Mary swung back and forth between faith and disappointment, but Ruth, the youngest, never once lost her faith in her brother.

## p138.003
1. Final Instructions
   HEAD: ['1: Las Instrucciones Finales']

## p138.004
Jesus sent the six apostles out two by two. He told them not to baptize or preach in public, but to meet people one at a time and share the good news. He also told them that he would soon set up twelve apostles, so each of them should choose one person from among the believers. The apostles were a little disappointed and afraid, but Andrew encouraged them, saying that the Master “⟦Q 138:1.3⟧has manifested great confidence in us inasmuch as he has intrusted us with the choosing of these six new apostles⟦/Q⟧”.

     AUTO: None
     VERSE: Antes de salir a cumplir su misión los seis estuvieron juntos intercambiando muchas ideas sobre el mandato de Jesús de que cada uno de ellos debería elegir un nuevo apóstol. Finalmente prevaleció el consejo de Andrés, y se marcharon ellos a sus tareas. En esencia Andrés dijo: «El Maestro tiene razón; somos demasiado pocos para abarcar toda la tarea. Esa mañana, al separarse para cumplir su obra, había un dejo oculto de depresión en el corazón de cada uno de ellos. Sabían que extrañarían a Jesús, y además de su temor y apocamiento, éste no era el modo en que se habían imaginado que se inauguraría el reino del cielo.

## p138.005
Meanwhile Jesus visited his family in Nazareth and did everything he could to keep their love and trust. He also longed to rescue John, who was in prison, but once again he waited for the Father's will.

## p138.006
2. Choosing the Six
   HEAD: ['2: La Selección de los Seis']

## p138.007
The first two-week trip was a great success. The apostles learned how precious it is to meet people personally, and that religion is, in the end, a personal experience. When they came back, each recommended one person, and with everyone's approval six new men were chosen.

## p138.008
They were Matthew Levi, a tax collector; Thomas, a fisherman and carpenter; the twin brothers James and Judas Alpheus, who were fishermen; Simon, a leader among the Zealots who resisted Rome; and Judas Iscariot, who came from a wealthy family and had experience handling money. Judas Iscariot was the only one of the twelve apostles from Judea.

## p138.016
Jesus taught them about the work of the Holy Spirit and the angels, but because the apostles tried to fit the new teaching into the ideas they already had, they did not understand much of it. Jesus also had them spend a day on their own so they could get to know one another. And he told them not to clash with the government, and to leave the rebuking of rulers to him.

## p138.017
6. The Week of Intensive Training
   HEAD: ['6: La Semana de Capacitación Intensiva']

## p138.018
The next week was a time of intensive training. Each of the first six apostles taught the new apostle he had recommended everything he had learned so far, and in the evenings they all gathered to hear Jesus teach. Jesus set aside every Wednesday as a day off and would often say, “⟦Q 138:6.2⟧My children, go for a day of play⟦/Q⟧”. He knew that rest and play are truly necessary for people.

     EN: My children, go for a day of play
     AUTO: Hijitos míos, id por un día de esparcimiento
     VERSE: Por aquella época estableció Jesús un día de asueto en la mitad de la semana para descanso y recreación. Y se adhirieron ellos a este programa de descanso semanal por el resto de la vida material del Maestro. Como regla general, los miércoles no llevaban a cabo sus actividades regulares. En este día feriado semanal generalmente Jesús se apartaba de ellos, diciendo: «Hijitos míos, id por un día de esparcimiento. Descansad de las arduas labores del reino y disfrutad del refrescante efecto que proviene de volveros a vuestras antiguas vocaciones o de descubrir nuevas actividades recreativas». Aunque Jesús, en este período de su vida terrenal, no necesitaba realmente de este día de descanso, se adaptó a este plan porque sabía que era mejor para sus asociados humanos. Jesús era el instructor —el Maestro; sus asociados eran sus alumnos —sus discípulos.

## p138.019
Jesus told the apostles to proclaim not stories about himself, but the gospel of the kingdom of heaven and the heavenly Father. And he always urged them, “⟦Q 138:6.4⟧Go seek for the sinners; find the downhearted and comfort the anxious⟦/Q⟧”. Jesus had tremendous power, yet he wanted to do the Father's work in the quietest and least showy way.

Siempre, su exhortación a los apóstoles era: «Buscad a los pecadores; encontrad a los afligidos y consolad a los ansiosos».

## p138.023
8. First Work of the Twelve
   HEAD: ['8: La Primera Labor de los Doce']

## p138.024
The apostles went out two by two, and Jesus took turns going with each pair. Jesus taught that sins are forgiven through faith in God, without self-punishment or sacrifices, and that the heavenly Father loves all his children with the same eternal love. John had said to repent in order to escape the coming wrath, but Jesus taught that faith is the open door into God's love.

## p138.025
The apostles were moved by how deeply Jesus respected everyone he met. Even in the middle of an important conversation, he would go over to a woman carrying a heavy load and speak kindly to her, or he would play with a child who came up to him. To Jesus, the one person right in front of him was the most important. Jesus also clearly taught that in the kingdom of heaven women enjoy rights equal to men's.

## p138.026
9. Five Months of Testing
   HEAD: ['9: Cinco Meses de Prueba']

## p138.027
The five months of taking turns between fishing and ministry were a hard test for the apostles, but they held on to the end. During this time, living close to Jesus, the apostles came to think of him as their best friend in the world. The Urantia Book says that what held the apostles together later, in the dark hours of the cross, was not his wonderful teaching or miracles, but this very friendship and loyalty.

## p138.028
Meanwhile almost all of Jesus' family except Ruth turned away from him, and they nearly came to think he was out of his mind. During this time the apostles also arranged to provide for their families, so that they could give themselves to the Master's work without worry.

## p138.029
10. Organization of the Twelve
   HEAD: ['10: La Organización de los Doce']

## p138.030
The apostles divided up the duties among themselves. Andrew became the chairman who led the whole group, and Peter, James, and John became the personal companions who stayed by Jesus' side. Philip was the steward who provided food, Nathaniel looked after the needs of the apostles' families, Matthew managed the finances, and Thomas arranged the travel and lodging.

## p138.031
The Alpheus twins kept order among the crowds, Simon took care of the Wednesday rest and play, and Judas Iscariot kept the money bag. Having finished these preparations, the apostles were officially ordained the following January as ambassadors of the kingdom of heaven. Just as these men, once ordinary fishermen and tax collectors, became a team by sharing out their roles, we too can accomplish great things together when each of us faithfully does the work given to us in our own place.

## p139.001
Introduction
   HEAD: []

## p139.002
Jesus shattered the apostles' dreams of getting ahead many times. Even so, only one of them ever abandoned him. This shows how attractive and upright Jesus' life was. The apostles learned about the kingdom of heaven from Jesus, and Jesus learned a great deal about human nature from them. The twelve men each had a different personality. And they were not uneducated, as people often think. Except for the Alpheus twins, all of them had finished the synagogue schools; they simply had not received the higher education of the rabbis.

## p139.003
1. Andrew, the First Chosen
   HEAD: ['1: Andrés, el Primer Elegido']

## p139.004
Andrew was the first apostle chosen and the leader of the apostolic group. At thirty-three he was the oldest of the twelve, and the most able. He was not a good preacher, but he was excellent at sharing the gospel with people one by one, and he brought his brother Simon Peter to Jesus. Even when his brother became more famous, he was not jealous at all. When two thousand people came to believe through Peter's preaching at Pentecost, Andrew said, “⟦Q 139:1.6⟧I could not do that, but I am glad I have a brother who could⟦/Q⟧”.

## p139.005
Andrew had the sharpest eye of all for judging people, and he was the first to notice that something was going wrong in Judas' heart. He was calm and dependable, but his weakness was that he was slow to praise his fellows. He admired Jesus' steady sincerity. Later, while preaching the gospel in many lands, he was crucified, and even while hanging there for two days he kept proclaiming the good news.

He asked more questions than anyone, and he acted so quickly that when he saw the risen Jesus he jumped into the water and swam to him. He took deeply to heart Jesus' gentleness, especially the teaching to forgive “⟦Q 139:2.5⟧not only seven times but seventy times and seven⟦/Q⟧”.

## p139.008
Peter was brave when attacked head-on, but he shrank back when he was mocked. That is why, at a servant girl's words, he ended up denying his Lord. But after he knew he had been forgiven, the fire of the kingdom burned brightly in his heart, and he became the greatest preacher of the twelve. His wife also worked faithfully alongside him. Peter ended his life in Rome, counting it an honor to die on a cross like his Lord.

## p139.009

   HEAD: ['3: Santiago Zebedeo']

James was the older of Zebedee's two sons, and Jesus called him and his brother John the “⟦Q 139:3.1⟧sons of thunder⟦/Q⟧”. When he got angry, he flared up like fire, and he also had the weakness of going silent for days. But he could see the many sides of any problem, and of the twelve he understood the real meaning of Jesus' teaching best. He admired Jesus' warm love, which made no difference between great and small, rich and poor.

## p139.011
James was a humble worker and a good balance for Peter. He was the first of the apostles to be martyred, by the sword of Herod Agrippa. He carried himself with such dignity to the very last moment that even the man who had accused him was moved and became a disciple of Jesus.

## p139.012
4. John Zebedee
   HEAD: ['4: Juan Zebedeo']

## p139.013
John was the youngest apostle, twenty-four years old, and he was a trustworthy man who took care of the affairs of Jesus' family. With Peter and James, he became one of the three personal aides who helped Jesus at his side. The Urantia Book says it is hard to say that Jesus loved one apostle more than the others. John had a hidden conceit and a narrow mind, but he changed greatly by living with Jesus.

## p139.014
John was deeply moved by Jesus' love and unselfishness. So this “⟦Q 139:4.6⟧son of thunder⟦/Q⟧” at last became the “⟦Q 139:4.6⟧apostle of love⟦/Q⟧”. When he was old and no longer had the strength to preach, he kept repeating only, “⟦Q 139:4.6⟧My little children, love one another.⟦/Q⟧” He was the only apostle who followed Jesus all the way on the night he was arrested, and he was the first to fully believe in the resurrection. Later he was exiled to the island of Patmos, then looked after the churches at Ephesus, and he died peacefully at the age of one hundred and one.

Crowds looking for healing and comfort came to Bethany too, so Jesus went back and forth between Gethsemane and Bethany. This time he had them teach only personally, with no public preaching. In Bethany, Jesus and the twelve quietly kept their first Passover without blood. John's apostles stayed in Jerusalem working with Abner, and the two groups kept on good terms with each other.

## p147.008
3. At the Pool of Bethesda
   HEAD: ['3: En el Estanque de Betesda']

## p147.009
One Sabbath, John took Jesus to the pool of Bethesda. The pool was a hot spring that bubbled up from time to time, and people believed that whoever got into the water first when it moved would be healed. John hoped that if Jesus saw the sick people and worked a miracle, all Jerusalem would believe. Jesus asked John not to try to turn him from the path he had chosen, but he still gathered the sick people and spoke words of comfort to them.

## p147.010
Jesus told them that they were all children of God and that God loved them. Being freed from bodily illness is wonderful, he said, but being healed from spiritual sickness and moral weakness is an even greater thing. Hearing these words, a man who had lain helpless for a long time joyfully picked up his bed and walked away. He had only waited for someone to help him and had never thought of getting up on his own. Yet getting up by himself was the one thing he needed to do to be healed.

## p147.011
4. The Rule of Living
   HEAD: ['4: La Regla del Vivir']

## p147.012
That evening Nathaniel asked a question. What if a person with evil intentions misused the rule that says to treat others the way you want to be treated? Jesus explained six levels of understanding this rule. There is the level of the flesh, which thinks only of one's own wants; the level of the feelings, which adds sympathy and pity; the level of the mind, which adds sound judgment and self-respect; the level of brotherly love, which is devoted to the welfare of one's neighbors; and the moral level, which sees right and wrong through the eyes of a fair third party.

## p147.013
And at the highest, spiritual level, the rule means to treat people the way God would treat them. Jesus said, “⟦Q 147:4.9⟧I would, therefore, that you should do to all men that which you know I would do to them in like circumstances⟦/Q⟧”. The apostles were greatly amazed by these words and talked about them for a long time.

     VERSE: «6. El nivel espiritual. Por último llegamos al nivel más elevado de todos, el del discernimiento del espíritu y de la interpretación espiritual que nos impulsa a reconocer en esta regla de vida el mandato divino de tratar a todos los hombres así como concebimos que Dios los trataría. Ése es el ideal del universo en cuenta a las relaciones humanas. Ésta es pues vuestra actitud frente a todos estos problemas cuando vuestro deseo supremo es hacer por siempre la voluntad del Padre. Deseo por ello que hagáis para todos los hombres lo que sabéis que yo haría en circunstancias semejantes».

## p147.014
5. Visiting Simon the Pharisee
   HEAD: ['5: La Visita a Simón el Fariseo']

## p147.015
Simon, a Pharisee, invited Jesus to dinner. While they were there, a woman whom people pointed at as a sinner came in. She had believed the gospel and given up her old life, but people still looked down on her. The woman wet Jesus' feet with her tears, wiped them with her hair, and anointed them with ointment. Simon thought to himself that if Jesus were a prophet, he would have known what kind of woman this was.

## p147.016
Jesus asked which of two people would love more, one forgiven a large debt or one forgiven a small one. Then he said that this woman, who had been forgiven much, loved that much, and he told her, “⟦Q 147:5.5⟧Woman, go in peace; your faith has saved you.⟦/Q⟧” He also said that no one can shut the door of the kingdom of heaven in the face of someone who wants to come in by faith.

## p147.018
6. Returning to Capernaum
   HEAD: ['6: El Regreso a Capernaum']

## p147.019
At the end of April the group went back to Capernaum. Meanwhile, the leaders in Jerusalem decided to put Jesus on trial on religious charges, and they sent six spies to follow him. On a Sabbath, when the hungry apostles rubbed grain in their hands and ate it as they walked through the wheat fields, the spies accused them of breaking the Sabbath law.

## p147.020
Jesus gave the example of David, who when hungry ate the bread that only the priests were allowed to eat, and he said it was better to look after people's health and well-being than to keep the Sabbath. And he declared “⟦Q 147:6.4⟧that the Sabbath was made for man and not man for the Sabbath⟦/Q⟧”. Rather than condemn the old traditions, Jesus showed the true way in a positive way.

## p147.021
7. Back in Capernaum
   HEAD: ['7: De Vuelta en Capernaum']

## p147.022
In Capernaum, when the leader of the spies asked why Jesus did not make his disciples fast, Jesus answered that people do not fast while the bridegroom is with them. Just as no one sews a piece of new cloth onto an old garment, and new wine is put into new wineskins, they should not bring too much of the old order into the gospel. At the same time, he said, they should keep what is true even if it is old, throw away what is false even if it is new, and bravely accept what is new and true.

## p147.023
8. The Feast of Spiritual Goodness
   HEAD: ['8: La Fiesta de la Bondad Espiritual']

## p147.024
That night Jesus quoted the words of Isaiah as he taught the apostles. The fast that pleases God is not making the body suffer. It is setting the oppressed free, sharing bread with the hungry, and welcoming the homeless. When we do this, our light will shine in the darkness like the morning.

## p147.025
Jesus taught that it is faith, not making the body suffer, that keeps us safe in the kingdom of heaven. His last words that night were these: “⟦Q 147:8.5⟧Grow in grace by means of that living faith which grasps the fact that you are the sons of God while at the same time it recognizes every man as a brother.⟦/Q⟧” Even if we fall a little short today, if we are moving step by step toward God, that is what matters most.
