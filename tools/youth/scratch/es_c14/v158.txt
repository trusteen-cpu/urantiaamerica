## p158.001 Introduction
   HEAD: {'en': 'Introduction', 'official': []}
## p158.002 At sunset on August 12, Jesus and the apostles reached the foot of Mount Hermon, where they stayed for two days to prepare their hearts. It was near the place where Jesus had once gone up the mountain alone and officially brought to an end the Lucifer rebellion, the uprising of a heavenly leader against God. Jesus wished that all the apostles could see what was about to happen, but they were not yet spiritually ready to meet heavenly beings. So he decided to take only Peter, James, and John, the three who always went with him when he prayed apart.
## p158.003 1. The Transfiguration
   HEAD: {'en': '1. The Transfiguration', 'official': ['1: La Transfiguración']}
## p158.004 On the morning of August 15, Jesus climbed Mount Hermon with the three apostles. It is meaningful that this special event took place in gentile country, on a gentile mountain. That day Jesus could have laid everything down and gone back to his place as ruler of the universe, but he chose to follow the Father's will to the end and finish his life on earth.
## p158.005 During the night the three apostles fell asleep, and when they woke up, they saw Jesus talking with two shining beings, and his face and form were glowing with heavenly light. Peter thought they were Moses and Elijah, but they were really Gabriel and the Father Melchizedek. Startled, Peter blurted out that they should build three tents there. Then a silvery cloud covered them, and they heard a voice say, “⟦Q 158:1.10⟧This is my beloved Son; give heed to him.⟦/Q⟧” Jesus touched the apostles, who had fallen on their faces, and told them not to be afraid.

## p158.006 2. Coming down the Mountain
   HEAD: {'en': '2. Coming down the Mountain', 'official': ['2: El Descenso de la Montaña']}
## p158.007 On the way down the mountain, Jesus told them not to tell anyone what they had seen that day until the Son of Man had risen from the dead. The apostles, who had just seen his glory, were deeply troubled by this talk of death. Peter was so frightened by those words that he changed the subject and asked about Elijah, and Jesus answered that Elijah had already come but people had not received him. Only then did the apostles understand that Jesus was speaking of John the Baptist.
## p158.008 Jesus asked them to keep silent because he did not want to encourage the apostles' mistaken idea that he was a Messiah who would save the nation by miracles. And these three were chosen not because they were better than the other apostles, but because they were the ones appointed to go with him whenever he prayed alone.
## p158.009 3. Meaning of the Transfiguration
   HEAD: {'en': '3. Meaning of the Transfiguration', 'official': ['3: El Significado de la Transfiguración']}
## p158.010 The Urantia Book explains what the transfiguration meant in heaven. Gabriel brought word that the Eternal Son accepted Jesus' mission on earth as fully accomplished, and through the Father Melchizedek came the witness that the Infinite Spirit was also satisfied. But God the Father did not say that the mission was finished; he only bore witness, saying, “⟦Q 158:3.4⟧This is my beloved Son; give heed to him.⟦/Q⟧” So Jesus decided, following the Father's will, to live his human life to its natural end. For the apostles, this event was a sign that Jesus had entered the last stage of his earth life.

## p158.016 Jesus took the boy by the hand and told him to get up, and he commanded the spirit to come out and never enter him again. The boy was healed both of his bodily illness and of his spirit's torment, and from then on he was never sick again. The three apostles who had tasted joy on the mountain came right down to face their companions' failure. The apostles' lives often swung back and forth like this between joy and discouragement.
## p158.017 6. In Celsus’ Garden
   HEAD: {'en': '6. In Celsus’ Garden', 'official': ['6: En el Jardín de Celsus']}
## p158.018 That evening in Celsus' garden, Thomas asked Jesus to teach them why they had failed. Jesus pointed out that instead of praying and waiting, they had been arguing about who would sit in the highest place. And he said that the greatest one in the kingdom is the one who humbles himself and serves his brothers. He taught that spiritual greatness is not in showing off power but in understanding love, the kind of love God has. They failed, he said, because their motives were not pure and were not founded on love.
## p158.019 Jesus asked whether they would always need to see miracles before they would believe. And he said that the Son of Man had now entered the last stage, and that he would be killed but would rise again. The apostles were so sad and afraid that they could not ask him anything, but after the resurrection they remembered all of these words.
## p158.020 7. Peter’s Protest

## p158.021 The next day, as the apostles were walking back with Jesus by a quiet road, Andrew asked him whether his words about dying were a parable. Jesus said it was not a parable, and he said plainly that the Son of Man must be rejected in Jerusalem, be killed, and rise again. Then Peter grabbed Jesus by the shoulder and cried out that such a thing must never happen. Peter said it out of love for Jesus, but Jesus felt in those words a temptation to give up the Father's will. So he rebuked him sternly, saying, “⟦Q 158:7.4⟧Get you behind me.⟦/Q⟧” and told him to set his mind not on the ways of men but on the will of God.

## p158.023 8. At Peter’s House
   HEAD: {'en': '8. At Peter’s House', 'official': ['8: En la Casa de Pedro']}
## p158.024 When they arrived at Peter's house in Capernaum, Jesus asked what they had been talking about so much on the way. The apostles could say nothing, because once again they had been talking about who would be the greatest. Jesus called one of Peter's little children, set the child in the middle of them, and said that whoever humbles himself like this child is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven. He also said that whoever receives one such little child receives him, and he urged them never to look down on the little ones. It is good to remember that true greatness lies not in wanting to be lifted up but in wanting to serve from a lowly place.
## p159.001 Introduction
   HEAD: {'en': 'Introduction', 'official': []}
## p159.002 At Magadan Park nearly a hundred evangelists and disciples, including the women's corps, were waiting. Jesus paired one apostle with one evangelist to form twelve groups and sent them out for four weeks to teach in the cities and villages of the Decapolis. Jesus promised to visit the groups often during that time. On this tour, nothing out of the ordinary, such as miracles of healing, happened at all.
## p159.003 1. The Sermon on Forgiveness
   HEAD: {'en': '1. The Sermon on Forgiveness', 'official': ['1: El Sermón del Perdón']}
## p159.004 At Hippos, Jesus taught about forgiveness. When a shepherd loses one of his hundred sheep, he leaves the ninety-nine and searches for that one until he finds it. Jesus said that God does not just wait for sinners to repent; he goes out looking for them even before they think of repenting. He also said that when a brother does wrong, you should first talk with him quietly, just the two of you. If that does not work, go with one or two friends, and if that still does not work, take it to the whole community. One person should not judge another alone, but the community should judge fairly with its shared wisdom.
## p159.005 When Peter asked whether he should forgive his brother up to seven times, Jesus answered, “⟦Q 159:1.4⟧Not only seven times but even to seventy times and seven.⟦/Q⟧” Then he told a story. A king took pity on a servant who owed him the enormous debt of ten thousand talents and canceled the whole debt. But that servant threw a fellow servant who owed him only a hundred denarii into prison. When the king heard this, he was very angry. The lesson is that someone who has been greatly forgiven by God should gladly forgive others too.

## p159.009 3. Instruction for Teachers and Believers
   HEAD: {'en': '3. Instruction for Teachers and Believers', 'official': ['3: La Instrucción para Maestros y Creyentes']}
## p159.010 At Edrei, Jesus told those who teach the gospel what principles they should follow. They should always respect a person's personality and never drag people in by force or clever talk. They should not appeal to fear or pity but to the spirit of God within the person. They should not destroy a person's self-respect, and rather than only pointing out faults, they should generously recognize what deserves praise. Frightening people into believing is a low method, he said, and a loving father does not frighten his children.
## p159.011 Jesus said that strong feelings are not the same as being led by the spirit of God. Faith, like the sail of a ship, does not add a load to life but adds power, and the only struggle a believer has is the struggle against doubt. He also told them not to picture him as a man of sorrows, because this gospel is good news overflowing with joy. He told them not to sink into self-pity but to live bravely.
## p159.012 Above all, he said that believing does not mean you will escape accidents or troubles. Jesus said, “⟦Q 159:3.13⟧I do not promise to deliver you from the waters of adversity, but I do promise to go with you through all of them.⟦/Q⟧”

## p159.013 4. The Talk with Nathaniel
   HEAD: {'en': '4. The Talk with Nathaniel', 'official': ['4: La Conversación con Natanael']}
## p159.014 At Abila, Nathaniel quietly asked Jesus about the Scriptures, because Jesus seemed to treat them differently from the rabbis. In The Urantia Book, Jesus explains that the Scriptures were written by people and reflect the thinking and understanding of the people of their time. So some parts do not show God's love well, and he told Nathaniel not to believe such records as the one saying that the God of love commanded the killing of all one's enemies. At the same time, he said clearly that the Scriptures were the finest collection of religious wisdom and spiritual truth in the world in that age.
## p159.015 Jesus said that nothing that has passed through human hands can be completely free of error. The authority of truth lies not in the letters of long ago but in the spirit that lives now. Nathaniel's eyes were opened, yet he was also greatly amazed, and he kept this conversation in his heart for a long time.
## p159.016 5. The Positive Nature of Jesus’ Religion
   HEAD: {'en': '5. The Positive Nature of Jesus’ Religion', 'official': ['5: La Naturaleza Positiva de la Religión de Jesús']}
## p159.017 At Philadelphia, when James asked how to choose the good passages of Scripture, Jesus gave examples such as “⟦Q 159:5.3⟧The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want.⟦/Q⟧” and “⟦Q 159:5.4⟧You should love your neighbor as yourself.⟦/Q⟧” Jesus taught that God is a loving Father who cares for his children, and so all people are brothers to one another. Worshiping God and serving people was the whole of Jesus' religion.
   AUTO#0 159:5.3 [whole] El Señor es mi pastor; nada me faltará.
   AUTO#1 159:5.4 [whole] Ama a tu prójimo como a ti mismo.
## p159.018 Jesus' religion did not stop at ‘do not’; it was a religion of actively doing good. The saying about turning the other cheek does not mean just taking whatever comes; it means working actively to lead the wrongdoer to a better way. Jesus described three ways to respond to evil: paying back evil with evil, simply putting up with it without complaint, and overcoming evil with good. The third way was the one Jesus wanted. The saying that if someone forces you to carry a load one mile, you should go two miles, means the same thing.
## p159.019 Instead of difficult words, Jesus taught with simple comparisons such as salt, leaven, and little children. He brought religion down from heaven to earth and placed it close to people's lives.
## p159.020 6. The Return to Magadan
   HEAD: {'en': '6. The Return to Magadan', 'official': ['6: El Retorno a Magadán']}
## p159.021 The Decapolis mission was quite successful. Hundreds of people entered the kingdom, and the apostles and evangelists gained the valuable experience of working on their own without Jesus beside them. On September 16 they all gathered again at Magadan Park to talk over their future plans. David's messengers did the important work of bringing news from the whole region and collecting money to help the apostles' families. Jesus and the apostles rested for a week before their final work, and this was their last rest. The teachings of this paper, to forgive, to reach out first, and to overcome evil with good, can be used just as they are in our own friendships today.
