## p156.001 Introduction
   HEAD: {'en': 'Introduction', 'official': []}
## p156.002 On June 10, Jesus and his group arrived near Sidon on the Mediterranean coast and stayed at the home of Karuska, a wealthy woman who had once been cared for at the Bethsaida hospital. They spent almost two and a half weeks in Sidon and the surrounding area.
## p156.003 The Sabbath they spent there was very quiet. The disciples thought over the teachings about religion that Jesus had given them on the way, but none of them yet fully understood what they meant.
## p156.004 1. The Syrian Woman
   HEAD: {'en': '1. The Syrian Woman', 'official': ['1: La Mujer Siria']}
## p156.005 Norana, a Syrian woman who lived nearby, came with her daughter of about twelve, who suffered from convulsions. The disciples tried to send her away, saying Jesus needed rest, but Norana said she would not leave until she had seen him. She asked Thomas why he would send away someone who had come to the Master for help.
## p156.006 When Simon Zelotes spoke harshly to her, saying that because she was a gentile, the children's bread could not be thrown to the dogs, Norana did not get angry but answered cleverly. She said she was a believing dog, and even dogs may eat the crumbs that fall from the children's table. Jesus, who had heard this conversation through a window, came out and said, “⟦Q 156:1.7⟧O woman, great is your faith⟦/Q⟧”. From that hour her daughter was well.

## p156.007 The next day Jesus told the apostles that they had now seen with their own eyes that gentiles too could have saving faith, and that if the children of Abraham would not believe, the Father's kingdom would be taken by the gentiles.
## p156.008 2. Teaching in Sidon
   HEAD: {'en': '2. Teaching in Sidon', 'official': ['2: La Enseñanza en Sidón']}
## p156.009 As they crossed a bridge on the way into Sidon, Jesus said, “⟦Q 156:2.1⟧This world is only a bridge; you may pass over it, but you should not think to build a dwelling place upon it.⟦/Q⟧” Here Jesus taught the disciples every morning, and in the afternoons and evenings the disciples preached the gospel in Sidon. Many gentiles joyfully accepted the gospel.

## p156.010 The Urantia Book says that in many ways these gentiles understood Jesus better than the Jews did. They saw not only that Jesus was like God but also that God was like Jesus, and that God does not favor any person or nation over another. The Urantia Book says that people did not fail to understand Jesus; they were afraid to understand him.
## p156.011 Jesus taught about spiritual progress. A person cannot stand still; he either moves forward or slides back. He told them to forget the past and press forward, and not only to stop doing evil but to learn to do good. Jesus greatly enjoyed Norana's sense of humor, and he was sorry that people who took themselves too seriously lacked humor.
## p156.012 3. The Journey up the Coast
   HEAD: {'en': '3. The Journey up the Coast', 'official': ['3: El Viaje Costa Arriba']}
## p156.013 At the end of June, the group went north along the coast and taught for a week at Porphyreon and Heldua, and there too many people entered the kingdom of heaven. During this time Jesus went to Beirut to meet a believer named Malach.
## p156.014 After that the group went back through Sidon and down south to Tyre. By now the apostles and evangelists were used to working among the gentiles, and they were greatly surprised to see how eagerly the gentiles listened to the gospel and how willingly they believed.
## p156.015 4. At Tyre
   HEAD: {'en': '4. At Tyre', 'official': ['4: En Tiro']}
## p156.016 For two weeks at Tyre, the apostles and evangelists went out two by two and taught in the city and the area around it, and the people of this port city, who spoke many languages, gladly listened and were baptized. Jesus spoke in public only once, about the Father's love for all mankind, and on that occasion even the doors of the temple of Melkarth were opened to him.
## p156.017 Many of the people who made the purple dye that made Tyre famous also believed. When the shellfish used to make the dye grew scarce, they moved far away, even to the ends of the world, to find new beds of them, and wherever they went they spread the gospel that God is our Father and all people are brothers.
## p156.018 5. Jesus’ Teaching at Tyre
   HEAD: {'en': '5. Jesus’ Teaching at Tyre', 'official': ['5: La Enseñanza de Jesús en Tiro']}
## p156.019 At Tyre, Jesus told the story of the white lily. The lily keeps its roots in the dark mud, yet lifts its pure white flower high into the sunlight. In the same way, even though a person's roots are in an animal nature, by faith he can lift his spiritual nature into the light of heavenly truth. Jesus, who had been a carpenter, also told them not to be like a foolish carpenter who wastes his time shaping worm-eaten wood, but to lay a strong foundation of character.
## p156.020 When Nathaniel asked why we pray about being led into temptation when God does not lead us into it, Jesus explained that people often fall into temptation because of their own selfishness and animal urges. Rather than trying to hold down desires by willpower alone, he said, when we come to love something higher and better with all our hearts, the lower things are forgotten on their own. “⟦Q 156:5.5⟧be not overcome by evil but rather overcome evil with good⟦/Q⟧”.

## p156.021 Jesus taught many other things as well. He told them not to be discouraged by the fact that they were imperfect human beings, and not to judge their souls by a single mistake. If we love only those who love us, our lives become narrow, and true self-respect grows along with love for our neighbors. The power not to hold a grudge is the strength of character, and defeat is a mirror that shows us our true selves. Difficulties only make the zeal of those who love truth burn hotter.
## p156.022 6. The Return from Phoenicia
   HEAD: {'en': '6. The Return from Phoenicia', 'official': ['6: El Regreso de Fenicia']}
## p156.023 At the end of July, Jesus and the twelve apostles left Tyre and returned to the Sea of Galilee by way of Ptolemais, Jotapata, Ramah, and Zebulun. The evangelists went back by a different road. Near Kheresa and in the park where the five thousand had been fed, Jesus met with about fifty people who were left of the many believers from Capernaum.
## p156.024 While Jesus was away, his enemies thought the movement was finished, and their opposition died down. In fact, people resented the move to close the synagogues, and Herod sent word that he would not arrest Jesus outside of Galilee. The sincere believers who had been sifted out and remained through the trials began to gather again. In this way Jesus prepared for the last year, when he would preach the religion of the spirit of God within the heart. Just as he said, this world is not a home to stay in but a bridge to cross over, so we too can keep our eyes on a higher goal and move forward step by step.

## p157.002 Before leaving for Caesarea-Philippi with the twelve apostles, Jesus arranged to meet his family on Sunday, August 7, at the Zebedee boatshop in Capernaum. His mother, Mary, and his brothers and sisters planned to come. But a group of Pharisees from Jerusalem came to Mary's house to find out where Jesus was, and they camped there and waited. Because of this the family could not leave the house, and when Jesus heard about it, he did not go to visit them. So, through no one's fault, Jesus and his family once again missed seeing each other.

The next day Jesus asked the apostles whether they still held to their confession of the day before, and Peter answered again, “⟦Q 157:4.4⟧We believe that you are the Son of the living God.⟦/Q⟧” By this time Peter was naturally becoming the spokesman for the twelve.

## p157.014 Jesus said that this belief was not human knowledge but a revelation that the Father's spirit had given deep in their hearts. And he said that on the rock of this spiritual truth he would build the brotherhood of the kingdom of heaven, a living temple of spiritual fellowship. Each person who realizes that he is a son of God becomes a living stone in this temple. On this day Jesus began to trust the faith of the apostles, all except one.
## p157.015 5. The New Concept
   HEAD: {'en': '5. The New Concept', 'official': ['5: El Nuevo Concepto']}
## p157.016 What was new in Peter's confession was that it clearly recognized Jesus as the Son of God, a divine being. The Messiah the Jews of that time were waiting for was an anointed man, not the Son of God. Jesus had tried for a while to change people's ideas about the Messiah little by little, but he found it was not easy. So now he decided to declare boldly that he was both the Son of Man and the Son of God, and to build the kingdom of heaven on this truth, in which human nature and divine nature are joined in one.
## p157.017 6. The Next Afternoon
   HEAD: {'en': '6. The Next Afternoon', 'official': ['6: La Tarde Siguiente']}
## p157.018 As Jesus' popularity dropped, the apostles' purse ran empty too, but because David Zebedee had prepared ahead of time, the messengers brought the money they needed. The Urantia Book divides Jesus' earth life into four stages: his childhood, when he only dimly knew who he was; his youth, when he gradually came to understand his divine nature and mission; the time after his baptism, when he worked as a teacher and healer; and the last stage, which began at Caesarea-Philippi and went on to the cross. From now on Jesus appeared not simply as a teacher but as the Son of God who shows us the Father.
## p157.019 Jesus told the apostles some astonishing things. He said that anyone who would follow him must be ready to make sacrifices, and that he had come not to be served but to serve and to seek out the lost. He said, “⟦Q 157:6.13⟧He who has seen me has seen the Father.⟦/Q⟧” and also that his kingdom was not of this world and that he must soon leave this world. The apostles listened quietly, bewildered, but they did not understand all he meant.

## p157.020 7. Andrew’s Conference
   HEAD: {'en': '7. Andrew’s Conference', 'official': ['7: Los Diálogos de Andrés']}
## p157.021 That evening Andrew had a deep talk with each of the apostles, but he was so worried about the attitude of Judas Iscariot that late at night he told Jesus about it. Jesus said there was nothing more to be done, that Andrew should keep trusting Judas, and that he should not speak of this to the other apostles. Judas had been piling up disappointment and resentment over the death of John the Baptist, Jesus' rebukes, Jesus' refusal to become king, the empty purse, and other things. His great mistake was that whenever he went off alone to pray, instead of talking with God, he sank into fear, doubt, and the wish to get even.
## p157.022 The other apostles faced the same difficulties, but because they loved Jesus, they stayed with him to the end. On the morning of August 12, Jesus told the apostles to get ready to go up the mountain. There he meant to begin his last work as the Son of God. This story shows that when hard times come, the way to keep faith is not to stay in complaint and doubt but to open our hearts to God.
