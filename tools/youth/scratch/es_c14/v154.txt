## p154.001 Introduction
   HEAD: {'en': 'Introduction', 'official': []}
## p154.002 On the same Saturday night that Jesus preached in the Capernaum synagogue, representatives of the Sanhedrin in Jerusalem were in Tiberias pressing Herod Antipas, the ruler of Galilee, to arrest Jesus. But Herod refused. He had received reports that Jesus had refused to be made king by the crowd, and Chuza, an official of his court whose wife was in the women's corps, had told him that Jesus had no interest in politics and only wanted to build a spiritual brotherhood.
## p154.003 Herod was also troubled by guilt and superstitious fear over killing John the Baptist, so he did not want to be mixed up in harming Jesus. When the Jewish leaders threatened to accuse him before Caesar, Herod threw them out. As this week went by, Jesus prepared his disciples for the scattering that would soon come.
## p154.004 1. A Week of Counsel
   HEAD: {'en': '1. A Week of Counsel', 'official': ['1: Una Semana de Asesoría']}
## p154.005 During the first week of May, Jesus held meetings with his trusted disciples at Zebedee's house in the morning, afternoon, and evening. About a hundred people were willing to follow Jesus openly without fearing the opposition of the Pharisees. On Friday of that week, the Capernaum synagogue closed its doors to Jesus and his followers, and Jairus, the ruler of the synagogue, stepped down and openly took Jesus' side.
## p154.006 Fewer than a hundred and fifty people came to the last meeting by the sea. This was the lowest point of public interest in Jesus. But from this time on, new followers more deeply rooted in true faith began to appear, slowly but steadily. The uncertain period of expecting a material kingdom was over, and the spiritual gospel began to be preached more clearly.
## p154.007 2. A Week of Rest
   HEAD: {'en': '2. A Week of Rest', 'official': ['2: Una Semana de Descanso']}
## p154.008 The Sanhedrin in Jerusalem passed a decree that no synagogue in Palestine should receive Jesus or his followers. Each synagogue had always run its own affairs, so this was an abuse of power never seen before, and five members of the Sanhedrin resigned. Only the synagogue at Hebron refused to obey, claiming its right to self-rule, and a short time later it burned down.
## p154.009 Jesus gave the disciples a week of rest and told them to go home and rest, to play or go fishing, and to pray for the kingdom of heaven. During this time Nathaniel and James were sick for three days with a bad stomach upset. Jesus did not heal them supernaturally; he stayed by their side all night and cared for them himself. The Urantia Book says that Jesus never once used a miracle for his family or his close disciples, because our souls grow not through comfort but through struggling with difficulties and problems.
## p154.010 3. The Second Tiberias Conference
   HEAD: {'en': '3. The Second Tiberias Conference', 'official': ['3: La Segunda Reunión en Tiberias']}
## p154.011 In the middle of May, a second conference was held at Tiberias. The Jewish leaders reported that almost every synagogue had closed its doors to Jesus. At first Herod kept refusing, but after pressure from many sides, he finally agreed that if the Roman governor did not object, the Sanhedrin could arrest Jesus and take him to Jerusalem for a religious trial.
## p154.012 On the night of May 21, when word came that the Romans did not object, Herod signed the order allowing the arrest. Herod knew very well that Jesus could not get a fair trial before his enemies in Jerusalem.
## p154.013 4. Saturday Night in Capernaum
   HEAD: {'en': '4. Saturday Night in Capernaum', 'official': ['4: El Sábado por la Noche en Capernaum']}

Some said he was a harmless religious fanatic, some said he was a dangerous agitator who would start a rebellion, some said he was in league with the devil, and some said he was out of his mind.

The Urantia Book says that some people still say this today, but that a far better civilization could be built on Jesus' teachings, and someday it will be. The world, it says, has never yet seriously tried to live out Jesus' teachings.
## p154.016 5. The Eventful Sunday Morning
   HEAD: {'en': '5. The Eventful Sunday Morning', 'official': ['5: El Memorable Domingo por la Mañana']}
## p154.017 Before dawn on Sunday, May 22, urgent news reached Bethsaida that Herod had allowed the arrest. David Zebedee immediately called all the disciples to an emergency meeting at seven in the morning. Jesus urged the disciples to keep on with the work of the kingdom, seeking God's guidance no matter what happened. He said the twelve apostles and twelve of the evangelists should go with him, and the women should stay at Bethsaida until he sent for them.
## p154.018 David said, “⟦Q 154:5.3⟧I am neither a teacher nor a preacher, but it is in my heart to do this⟦/Q⟧”, and he promised that whatever happened, he would never stop his work of carrying messages. Jesus spoke words of farewell to about a hundred believers. Even with danger right in front of him, his face was brighter and more cheerful than usual as he encouraged their faith, hope, and courage.

In the family, only his sister Ruth wholeheartedly believed in Jesus' mission, and she said she would rather see her brother face death than stop preaching.

Then, pointing to his disciples, he said, “⟦Q 154:6.5⟧whosoever does the will of my Father who is in heaven, the same is my mother, my brother, and my sister⟦/Q⟧”. When Mary heard this, she collapsed.

The Urantia Book says that to everyone who feels misunderstood and unappreciated, Jesus is a friend who understands.

A small boat carrying David's messengers followed them and kept them in touch. From this time on, Jesus truly “⟦Q 154:7.2⟧had not where to lay his head⟦/Q⟧” and began a wandering life, moving to Caesarea-Philippi and the coast of Phoenicia.

Jesus' family suffered in confusion and was relieved only when Ruth heard the news that Jesus was safe. Even when the people closest to him did not understand him, Jesus did not hate them but treated them with love. We too can learn not to close our hearts at such moments.
## p155.001 Introduction

## p155.002 Having crossed the lake to escape arrest, Jesus and his group of twenty-four spent their first night in the familiar park south of Bethsaida-Julias. Before they went to sleep, Jesus gathered the disciples and told them of his plan to go through Batanea and northern Galilee to the coast of Phoenicia.

But he said the rest of the psalm, about God being angry and the Son breaking the nations with a rod of iron, was a misunderstanding of the mission of the Son of Man. Jesus' kingdom is founded on love, proclaimed in mercy, and built by unselfish service. Jesus told them to serve not out of fear but with faith, and to rejoice not with trembling but with confidence.

## p155.006 2. The Evangelists in Chorazin
   HEAD: {'en': '2. The Evangelists in Chorazin', 'official': ['2: Los Evangelistas en Corazín']}
## p155.007 The next day Peter went to Chorazin with the twelve evangelists, and Jesus went to Caesarea-Philippi with the other apostles. Chorazin was the place in Galilee where the fewest people had accepted the gospel. The evangelists preached spiritual truth more earnestly than healing, but for two weeks they reaped almost no fruit.
## p155.008 This time was a “⟦Q 155:2.2⟧baptism of adversity⟦/Q⟧” for the evangelists. Without the joy of winning people, they began instead to look more seriously at their own souls and spiritual growth. On the day they met again at Caesarea-Philippi, Jesus told the parable of the sower once more and taught them the meaning of what looks like failure on the outside.

## p155.009 3. At Caesarea-Philippi
   HEAD: {'en': '3. At Caesarea-Philippi', 'official': ['3: En Cesarea de Filipo']}
## p155.010 The two weeks at Caesarea-Philippi were a hard test for the apostles too. Hardly anyone new came to believe, but they learned a great deal from talking with Jesus every day. They learned that when truth is hardened into rigid creeds and used as boundary lines to divide people, it loses its living power. They also learned that it is good to teach people first to love the brother they can see before loving the God they cannot see.
## p155.011 Jesus said that if faith leans only on material things, it will be lost as knowledge of nature grows, but a spiritual religion is not shaken even as science advances. True religion is the sincere loyalty a person gives to his highest and truest convictions; it gives life a high purpose and hope and fills a person with courage. Jesus always warned of the danger of symbols and ceremonies taking the place of living religious experience.
## p155.012 4. On the Way to Phoenicia
   HEAD: {'en': '4. On the Way to Phoenicia', 'official': ['4: En el Camino a Fenicia']}
## p155.013 The group left Caesarea-Philippi and headed for Sidon on the coast of Phoenicia. On the way, while they ate lunch in the shade of a rock, Peter asked why they had to run from their enemies if God was with them. Thomas asked what the difference was between the religion of the Jerusalem leaders and their own religion, since both served the same God. Jesus said it would help everyone more to answer Thomas's question, and he would talk about it after lunch.
## p155.014 5. The Discourse on True Religion
   HEAD: {'en': '5. The Discourse on True Religion', 'official': ['5: El Discurso Sobre la Verdadera Religión']}
## p155.015 Jesus explained three kinds of religion. The first is primitive religion, which comes from fear. The second is the religion of civilized intellect, which leans on the authority of tradition; this is the religion of the mind. The third is true religion, the direct experience of God's goodness and beauty; this is the religion of the spirit. The religion of the mind is propped up by the authority of the church, but the religion of the spirit stands on human experience.
## p155.016 The religion of authority looks easy and safe, since all it asks is that you agree with it in your head. But in return for that comfort, a person gives up the most wonderful adventure there is: searching for truth himself and finding God directly. The religion of the spirit means effort, struggle, faith, decision, love, loyalty, and progress. Jesus called each of his disciples by name and asked whether he would go back to the easy way or go with him on the new way, even though it was hard.
## p155.017 When they all stood up to answer, Jesus stopped them and told them not to be swept along by their feelings but to find their true answer alone before the Father. After they had prayed, the disciples begged to hear more.
## p155.018 6. The Second Discourse on Religion
   HEAD: {'en': '6. The Second Discourse on Religion', 'official': ['6: El Segundo Discurso Sobre la Religión']}
## p155.019 Jesus went on. The Father did not speak only to the prophets of old; he is still giving out truth today. He hoped that people would move beyond the authority of tradition to the experience of finding God directly within themselves. He told them not merely to quote the prophets of old but to become living prophets themselves. Jesus also urged them never to look down on the forefathers of Israel, who had searched for God long and patiently.
## p155.020 The religion of authority divides people by demanding that they all believe exactly the same things, but the religion of the spirit unites people through the experience of the same spirit, even when they think differently. This is because the same divine spirit lives in every person. Also, there are no separate holy jobs and worldly jobs; for a person led by the spirit, every task is sacred. Jesus said that growing in the ability to feel God's presence matters more than knowing the fact of God.
## p155.021 He told them not to try to force proof on others that they know God. The proof is twofold: the fruits of the spirit that ripen in everyday life, and a whole life staked on the search for God. Religion is not a shelter to hide in out of fear; it is an eternal adventure of discovering the God who has come looking for us. The teenage years are a good time to move beyond a faith handed down by others and begin to find a living faith of one's own.
