p132.001 Introduction
p132.002 On their third day in Rome, Jesus, Gonod, and Ganid met the emperor Tiberius. After they left, the emperor pointed to Jesus and said that if he had that man's kingly bearing, he would have been a true emperor. Rome was a city of two million people, and people from every part of the empire lived there together. The main reason Jesus had come on this journey was that he wanted to get to know these many different kinds of people.
p132.003 The most important thing Jesus did during his six months in Rome was meeting the city's religious leaders. He chose thirty-two leaders of the Stoics, the Cynics, and the mystery religions such as Mithraism, and he usually met with them one at a time. Jesus never once attacked their mistakes. Instead he looked for the truth in what they taught and made it shine more brightly, and then that truth pushed out the wrong ideas by itself.
p132.004 Thirty of these people later played an important part in establishing Christianity in Rome. The Urantia Book names three things as the most important reasons Christianity spread so quickly in Europe: choosing Peter as an apostle, the talk with Stephen, and preparing these thirty people ahead of time. But none of them ever knew that the man they called “⟦Q 132:2.1⟧the scribe of Damascus⟦/Q⟧” was Jesus.

p132.005 1. True Values
p132.006 Jesus talked all night with Angamon, the leader of the Stoics, and taught him that the standard of true values must be found not in material things but in the spiritual world. A scientist is a person who studies the facts of matter, and a scientist who claims that matter is all there is has already started doing philosophy.
p132.007 If material progress keeps going while moral and spiritual growth does not grow along with it, civilization can be put in danger. Jesus said that scientists and religious people should both prove their worth by helping people, and that they should not fight each other.
p132.008 2. Good and Evil
p132.009 With Mardus, the leader of the Cynics, Jesus talked about good and evil. Jesus said it is lazy to take society's customs, or the religious traditions other people follow, as the standard of goodness just as they are. The true standard is the spiritual values shown by the spirit God has sent into our minds. A person who just follows what others say without thinking is in danger of becoming “⟦Q 132:2.4⟧an intellectual parrot⟦/Q⟧”.
   Q#0 132:2.4 en='an intellectual parrot' auto=None how=None

Une telle âme est destinée à devenir un perroquet intellectuel, un automate social et l’esclave de l’autorité religieuse.
p132.010 Goodness is not so much something we already have as a goal we keep moving toward. If an experience makes us feel beauty more deeply, recognize truth more clearly, and love our neighbors more, it is a good experience. The possibility of choosing evil proves that we are beings who can choose freely, but that does not mean we actually have to do evil to find out.
p132.011 3. Truth and Faith
p132.012 Nabon, the chief priest of the Mithraic religion, tried to win Jesus over to his own religion, but in fact Jesus was preparing him to accept the gospel. Jesus taught that truth cannot be defined in words; it can only be shown in a life. Knowledge comes from science, wisdom from philosophy, and truth from the spiritual experience of religion.
p132.013 Because people are afraid of change, they tend to freeze science and truth in place. But truth cannot become our own without faith. Faith awakens the spark of God within us and makes it a power that will survive forever. Growing spiritually means choosing the good more and more, freely and gladly, on our own.
p132.014 4. Personal Ministry
p132.015 In Rome Jesus personally met more than five hundred people. He mostly asked questions and let the other person talk. He listened all the way through to people weighed down by worries and burdens, then offered suggestions that really helped, and let them know that God was their loving Father.
p132.016 After hearing Jesus, Claudius, a wealthy slave owner, set one hundred and seventeen slaves free the very next day. Jesus told a Greek physician that his patients had not only bodies but also minds and souls. And for a poor man who had been falsely accused, Jesus spoke in court, saying, “⟦Q 132:4.8⟧Justice makes a nation great⟦/Q⟧”, and the man was set free.

p132.017 5. Counseling the Rich Man
p132.018 A rich man asked Jesus what he should do with his wealth. Jesus told him that this advice was for him alone and that he should not force it on other rich men. Then he told him first to look at where his wealth had come from. There were ten kinds: what he had inherited, what he had discovered, what he had gained by trade, what he had gained by unfairly using other people's work, interest, what he had gained by his talents, what had come to him by chance, what had been stolen, what others had entrusted to him, and what he had earned by his own labor.
p132.019 Jesus said that what a man earns honestly is truly his own, but whatever he gained by cheating or exploiting others he should hurry to give back to its rightful owners. Wealth gained by talent or by chance should also be thought of not as his alone, but as something entrusted to him for the sake of others. When it was hard to decide, Jesus advised him to decide in favor of those in need. The rich man promised to start doing just that the next day.
p132.020 6. Social Ministry
p132.021 One day Jesus and Ganid found a little boy who was lost and crying, and they spent several hours helping him find his mother. Jesus said that most people are like this child: they are not far from safety, yet they spend their lives crying in fear. Those who know God should think of guiding them not as a duty but as a privilege. From then on, all his life long, Ganid looked for lost children and brought them home.
p132.022 The two of them helped the family of a widow whose husband had died in an accident, and they also found a job for her oldest son. When Gonod suggested that Jesus write a book, Jesus answered that his mission was not to write a book but to live a life for all generations.
p132.023 7. Trips about Rome
p132.024 While traveling around Rome, Jesus did not say a single word to one man. When Ganid asked why, Jesus answered that the man was not hungry for truth. You cannot force the knowledge of God on someone who is not looking for God, he said; our job is to live so that the Father shows through our lives.
p132.025 When Gonod asked about the Buddha, Jesus said that the Buddha was a great man, but “⟦Q 132:7.4⟧an orphan prophet⟦/Q⟧” who lost sight of the Father in heaven early on. When Ganid suggested that the two of them start a new religion together, Jesus answered that a religion is not made; it appears in the lives of people who reveal God. The Urantia Book says that at that very moment, without Ganid knowing it, a new and eternal religion was being made with Jesus. When a person becomes a partner with God, great things happen. Our ordinary lives too can become a channel that shows God to others.

p133.001 Introduction
p133.002 When Jesus left Rome, he did not say goodbye to anyone. The people who knew him waited for him for a while, and later they formed small groups that remembered his teachings; these groups continued until the first Christian preachers came to Rome. The three travelers walked along the Appian Way and met all kinds of people.
p133.003 On the road, Ganid asked about India's caste system, the system that divides people into ranks from birth. Jesus answered that people differ from one another in many ways, but before God they all stand in the same place. In God's eyes there are only two groups: those who want to do his will and those who do not. Jesus said, “⟦Q 133:0.3⟧God is truly no respecter of persons⟦/Q⟧”, and he told Ganid to make no distinctions at all among brothers who worship together.

p133.004 1. Mercy and Justice
p133.005 Near Tarentum a big young man was beating a small boy. Jesus rescued the boy and held the attacker until the little boy could run away. But when Jesus let the bully go, Ganid rushed at him and started hitting him, and Jesus stopped him right away. Ganid asked: if mercy tells us to rescue a weak child, doesn't justice tell us to punish the one who did wrong?
p133.006 Jesus explained that showing mercy is a personal matter, but justice that punishes is the work of society and government. Since people disagree about what punishment is right, it is better to leave it to a group that can fairly look at all the facts. When asked what he would do if someone attacked him, Jesus answered that he would do everything he could to stop the attack and to reason with the attacker, but he would not be the first to punish his brother, and he fully trusted the heavenly Father's protection.
p133.007 2. Embarking at Tarentum
p133.008 On the docks at Tarentum a man was beating his wife. Jesus gently tapped him on the shoulder and asked to speak with him privately, and he kindly asked what had happened to make a strong man beat his wife. Softened by Jesus' kind face, the man was sorry for what he had done and promised never to do it again.
p133.009 Jesus told the man that his wife was an equal partner who shared life with him, and that how lovingly a man cares for his wife and children shows how high he has grown. As they boarded the ship, they looked back and saw the couple, tears in their eyes, holding each other. Gonod, who heard all this, also decided to rebuild his family life when he got back to India.
p133.010 3. At Corinth
p133.011 In Corinth, Ganid learned about Jewish family life by watching the home of Crispus, the ruler of the synagogue, and Jesus met with Crispus more than twenty times to teach him a better way of faith. Later, when Paul came there, Crispus and his whole family accepted the gospel.
p133.012 One evening two women who had been making a living in a wrong way because of their hard situation came up to them, and Ganid sharply tried to drive them away. Jesus gently corrected him: “⟦Q 133:3.6⟧Who are we that we should sit in judgment on these women?⟦/Q⟧” We do not know what sorrows and circumstances they have been through, he said, and he asked whether Ganid saw anything evil in their faces. Ganid apologized with tears.

p133.014 4. Personal Work in Corinth
p133.015 In Corinth, Jesus spoke to many people, giving each one exactly the words that person needed. He told a Roman centurion that serving God and being loyal to Caesar do not clash. He told a builder to build a spiritual character in his soul the way he builds a building. He told a judge to remember that he too would someday stand before the judgment seat, and so to judge with justice and mercy.
p133.016 To a boy who had run away from home, he said, “⟦Q 133:4.11⟧there are two things you cannot run away from⟦/Q⟧”, namely God and yourself, and he encouraged him to face life bravely. He comforted a prisoner who was about to be executed, telling him that if he was truly sorry, he had no need to fear the judgment of God, who knows his real heart.

p133.017 5. At Athens—Discourse on Science
p133.018 In Athens, Jesus talked about science with a Greek philosopher. Science can measure how energy behaves, but it cannot tell us what energy is. Science deals with physical things, religion deals with eternal values, and true philosophy tries to connect the two.
p133.019 Jesus gave an interesting example. If one man can shear a sheep in ten minutes, then by arithmetic ten men could shear it in one minute, but in real life they would only get in each other's way. On the other hand, when people work together in harmony, with their hearts united, they produce much more power than simply adding them up. In other words, there are things in life that cannot be counted with numbers alone.
p133.020 6. At Ephesus—Discourse on the Soul
p133.021 In Ephesus a scholar asked Jesus what the soul is. Jesus answered that the soul is the part of a person that reflects on itself, recognizes truth, and perceives spirit. Choosing between right and wrong, knowing God, and wanting to become like him are the marks of the soul.
p133.022 When a person's mind makes its first moral choice, the spirit of God comes, and at that very moment the soul is born. The soul cannot be proved by science, but anyone who lives aware of right and wrong knows by experience that he has a soul. Jesus said, “⟦Q 133:6.5⟧A stagnant soul is a dying soul⟦/Q⟧”, and he taught that the soul grows through moral thinking and spiritual activity.

p133.023 7. The Sojourn at Cyprus—Discourse on Mind
p133.024 While they were resting in the mountains of Cyprus, Ganid suddenly came down with a severe fever. Jesus nursed him so gently and skillfully that Ganid's father, Gonod, was amazed, and while Ganid was getting better, Jesus told him many things about nature.
p133.025 After that the two of them talked about the human mind. Unlike animals, people are conscious of themselves, and they make thoughts by adding their own interpretation to what their senses tell them. Jesus said that when a mind tries to serve good and evil at the same time, it falls into great conflict. The happiest and most unified mind is one wholly given to doing the will of the Father in heaven. True peace comes not by making easy compromises, but by overcoming evil with the power of good.
p133.026 8. At Antioch
p133.027 Antioch was the third largest city in the Roman Empire. As the journey was coming to an end, Jesus was often quiet and lost in thought. Here Ganid went to see an Indian worker who complained so much that he had nearly been fired, and they talked for a long time. Ganid helped him greatly by sharing the proverb “⟦Q 133:8.3⟧Whatsoever your hand finds to do, do that with all your might.⟦/Q⟧” Ganid had already begun to put Jesus' teachings into practice.

p133.029 On the way across the desert, Ganid watched Jesus load the camels and drive one himself, and he exclaimed, “⟦Q 133:9.1⟧Teacher, is there anything that you cannot do?⟦/Q⟧” They visited Ur, the home of Abraham, and Susa, and finally they parted at Charax.

Like Ganid, who met a good teacher and learned and grew alongside him, we too make a teaching truly our own when we practice what we learn in our lives.
